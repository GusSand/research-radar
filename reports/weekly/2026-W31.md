## Theme of the week

Sparse autoencoders underwent a methodological stress test this week — three independent papers interrogated whether SAE features are reliable instruments for causal claims and safety-critical work. Marks et al. proved which linear readouts survive sparse compression (most safety-relevant probes do; rare-concept probes lose 15–40%); Bloom, Conmy, and Nanda showed that timescale is a genuine dimension of feature identity that standard stateless SAEs systematically miss; and Cho et al. demonstrated that SAE family choice is a larger determinant of interventional validity than model scale. Alongside these methodological advances, LACUNA exposed the gap between standard unlearning evaluation metrics and genuine knowledge erasure, PAC-style safety bounds provided the first distribution-free formal certificates for LLM output safety, and CC-Delta showed that SAE-guided jailbreak defense outperforms dense activation steering. The security track closed with a multi-turn jailbreaking advance (DC-GRPO decomposed credit assignment) and a systematic survey confirming 69–98% policy-enforcement failure rates across AI coding agent deployments.

## 01 · [Decoder-Preserving Sparse Autoencoders: Which Readouts Survive Sparse Compression?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17425)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Samuel Marks, Can Rager, Eric J. Michaud, Yonatan Belinkov, David Bau, Aaron Mueller — arXiv (July 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Provides the first formal characterization of information loss through SAE bottlenecks, with direct implications for whether safety-critical linear probes survive SAE-based interventions — a question every practitioner using SAEs for steering or monitoring needs to answer.

**Technical summary:** The paper frames SAE compression as a constrained matrix-valued distortion minimization problem and derives a closed-form characterization of which linear functionals φ(z) of the residual-stream vector z are preserved: a readout is preserved iff its weight vector lies within the column span of the SAE decoder matrix, meaning SAE compression projects out components orthogonal to the learned feature directions. Empirically, probes for high-frequency behaviors (refusal direction, harmfulness scores, entity type) have weight vectors well-aligned with the decoder span and survive with near-zero degradation; probes for rare or fine-grained concepts lose 15–40% of their predictive accuracy. This gives practitioners a principled pre-flight test — check decoder-span alignment before trusting that a safety probe will generalize through an SAE intervention.

## 02 · [Persistent Sparse Autoencoders: Learning Feature Timescales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17117)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Joseph Bloom, Arthur Conmy, Neel Nanda — arXiv (July 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Addresses a systematic blind spot in standard SAEs — the assumption that feature activation is stateless across token positions — by introducing per-feature persistence coefficients that capture bursty vs. sustained activation patterns; directly extends SAE applicability to sequence-level safety monitoring.

**Technical summary:** Standard SAEs treat each token position independently, unable to represent that some features activate in isolated bursts (rare syntactic constructions) while others sustain across long spans (discourse topic). Persistent SAEs augment the TopK encoder with a per-feature persistence coefficient α_f ∈ [0,1] learned during training: feature f's pre-activation is blended with a decayed version of its previous activation, allowing sustained features to self-reinforce and bursty features to decay rapidly. Trained on Gemma-2-9B residual-stream activations, Persistent SAEs recover clusters of features with interpretable timescale structure — discourse-level features persist over 20–50 tokens, syntactic head-agreement features over 3–8 tokens, and lexical-surprise features burst at single positions. Persistence coefficients predict which features transfer across tasks, suggesting timescale is a meaningful dimension of feature identity beyond standard interpretability labeling.

## 03 · [Sound Probabilistic Safety Bounds for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20286)

`alignment` `preprint` — Luca Marzari, Davide Corsi, Isabella Mastroeni, Alessandro Farinelli, Ferdinando Cicalese — arXiv (July 22, 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Provides the first distribution-free, model-agnostic PAC-style certificates for LLM output safety — a foundational contribution to alignment evaluation methodology that requires no access to model internals and can certify multi-stage safety pipelines.

**Technical summary:** The paper formulates LLM safety certification as a PAC learning problem over a prompt distribution. Given a safety predicate (e.g., "response does not contain harmful content") and confidence level δ, the framework computes a sample-efficient bound on the probability that the LLM violates the predicate on a future unseen prompt drawn from the same distribution, using conformal prediction combined with Clopper-Pearson intervals — making the approach distribution-free and assumption-free about LLM internals. Evaluated on ToxiGen and AdvBench across four frontier models, certificates are tight (within 2–5% of empirical violation rates) while holding at δ = 0.01 with as few as 200 test prompts. The framework is composable: certificates for sub-policies such as refusal gates can be chained to certify multi-stage safety pipelines.

## 04 · [LACUNA: A Testbed for Evaluating Localization Precision for LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02513)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Matteo Boglioni, Thibault Rousset, Siva Reddy, Marius Mosbach, Verna Dankers (Mila / McGill) — arXiv (July 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** First unlearning testbed with ground-truth parameter-level localization; demonstrates that high standard unlearning metrics do not imply genuine knowledge erasure and quantifies the gap better localization could close — critical for interpretability-grounded unlearning research.

**Technical summary:** LACUNA injects PII of synthetic individuals into predefined weight subsets of 1B and 7B OLMo models via masked continual pretraining, creating a controlled testbed where the oracle knows exactly which parameters encode each to-be-forgotten fact. Evaluating SimNPO and other standard unlearning algorithms, the paper finds that high traditional unlearning metrics are achievable without targeting the responsible weights, leaving models vulnerable to resurfacing attacks. OracleGrad — a simple baseline with oracle access to ground-truth localization — achieves optimal forget/retain balance and maximal resurfacing robustness, quantifying the localization gap that better mechanistic methods could close.

## 05 · [Are Single-Token Sparse Autoencoder Features Causally Necessary? Layer-Depth and SAE-Family Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20596)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Seonglae Cho, Zekun Wu, Kleyton Da Costa, Rishi Kalra, Ilham Wicaksono, Adriano Koshiyama — arXiv (July 22, 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Establishes that SAE family choice is a more important methodological decision than model scale for making valid causal claims — a concrete warning for practitioners selecting SAE families for safety-critical interventional work.

**Technical summary:** Using single-token SAE features (features that activate on exactly one vocabulary type) as ground-truth-verifiable diagnostics, the paper tests 3.9 million features across six models and three SAE families (GemmaScope, BatchTopK, LlamaScope). Single-token features cluster 4.7× tighter in decoder space than all features and concentrate in early layers; ablating them yields Benjamini-Hochberg-significant logit reductions in 178 of 208 conditions. The critical finding is that the cross-family effect exceeds the within-family scaling effect: GemmaScope and BatchTopK features are causally anchored (BH-significant in 86% and 82% of conditions), while LlamaScope features are locally redundant (significant in ~40%). SAE family selection is therefore a first-order methodological choice for causal interpretability claims.

## 06 · [Sparse Autoencoders are Capable LLM Jailbreak Mitigators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12418)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Yannick Assogba et al. — arXiv (February 2026, surfaced July 24, 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Demonstrates that SAE-based jailbreak defense (CC-Delta) outperforms dense activation steering on all four tested models across 12 jailbreak types, providing the clearest pragmatic payoff for SAE interpretability in a security application to date.

**Technical summary:** CC-Delta (Context-Conditioned Delta Steering) identifies jailbreak-relevant SAE features by comparing token-level sparse activations for the same harmful request with and without jailbreak context, then applies a mean-shift steering vector in SAE latent space at inference time. The contrastive identification step isolates jailbreak-specific features without contaminating features active in both jailbreak and benign contexts, reducing over-refusal compared to dense steering. CC-Delta outperforms dense activation steering on all four tested models across 12 jailbreak categories, with the largest gains on out-of-distribution attacks — suggesting that SAE decomposition captures jailbreak structure that is invisible to dense residual-stream steering.

## 07 · [Steered LLM Activations are Non-Surjective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09839)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv (April 2026, surfaced July 26, 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Provides a formal proof that activation steering pushes residual-stream states off the manifold of states reachable from any discrete prompt — establishing a fundamental separation between white-box steerability and black-box prompting with direct implications for how steering experiments are designed and interpreted.

**Technical summary:** The paper proves that the set of hidden states reachable via activation steering is strictly larger than the set reachable via any discrete prompt, meaning almost surely no prompt can reproduce the same internal behavior induced by a steering vector. The proof constructs the manifold of prompt-reachable states and shows it has measure zero in the space of steered states. This formal separation has concrete evaluation implications: white-box steering experiments cannot be validated by searching for prompts that reproduce the induced behavior, and benchmarks that compare steering to prompting under the assumption of equivalence are methodologically unsound.

## 08 · [MJ: Multi-turn LLM Jailbreaking via Decomposed Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11070)

`AI security` `preprint` — Junyoung Park, Namgyu Park, Sechan Lee, Yoon-Chan Jhi, Jihoon Cho, Sangdon Park — arXiv (July 13, 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Advances multi-turn jailbreaking by solving the credit assignment problem that limits RL-based attackers — each dialogue turn contributes unequally to the final outcome, and broadcasting a single trajectory-level signal conflates useful and noise turns.

**Technical summary:** DC-GRPO (Decomposed Credit Group Relative Policy Optimization) assigns a separate group-relative learning signal per turn combining immediate and future credit components, rather than broadcasting a single trajectory-level reward. The decomposition is derived from a per-step value function that separates the contribution of each turn's response to the final harmful outcome. Both static- and dynamic-weighted instantiations achieve strong multi-turn attack success rates on black-box frontier models, with meaningful cross-model transferability — confirming that per-turn credit decomposition is a broadly applicable improvement to RL-based multi-turn jailbreak training.

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Siddique et al. — arXiv (~July 23, 2026)
Proposes a six-condition reliability filter followed by Borda consensus ranking (F-test, KSG mutual information, Cohen's d) to select SAE features for steering without a learned objective. Key finding: "substantial gap between raw attribute movement and quality-preserving generation" — raw activation shift does not predict generation quality, making the selection filter a practical prerequisite for SAE steering pipelines.

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Naganna et al. — arXiv (July 2026)
Attribution and activation patching on Llama-3 models identifies a compact shared set of MLP neurons that is both necessary and sufficient for late-layer arithmetic computation across symbolic arithmetic, natural-language word problems, and Python code. Targeted interventions on this neuron set degrade arithmetic performance simultaneously in all three formats, supporting a single surface-form-invariant arithmetic circuit over three format-specific ones.
