## Theme of the week

The most impactful result of the week was infrastructural rather than methodological: a cross-session compatibility flaw in encrypted chain-of-thought blocks, shared across all major AI providers, exposed that hidden reasoning traces can be decoded through injection into weaker models — and that 6,708 public agent trajectories already contained 704 recoverable privacy artifacts. Alongside this, the week produced a rare theoretical anchor: the first identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive, establishing that the transformer's intrinsic spectrum is coordinate-free and recoverable from data at a provably optimal rate. On the attack surface, syntactic jailbreaks emerged as a systematic vulnerability — causal mediation across 16 models up to 70B showed grammatical phrasing causally mediates 25–40% of refusal decisions even when harmful content is held constant — and a novel attack class (Convergent Detour Hijacking) demonstrated that skill-based agents can be resource-hijacked while still completing the original task, defeating completion-based monitors. The week also produced an unusually large cluster of agent security evaluation frameworks (ToolHazard, REDAgentBench, and others), collectively signalling that empirical agent security is maturing from informal case studies to reproducible, decomposed measurement. The 2026 Singapore Consensus on Global AI Safety Research Priorities arrived on arXiv mid-week as the field's broadest collective statement on open problems.

## 01 · [Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867)

`AI security` `preprint` — Alexander Panfilov, David Schmotz, Ilia Shumailov, Luca Beurer-Kellner, Joachim Schaeffer, Ameya Prabhu, Jonas Geiping, Maksym Andriushchenko — arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026

The hidden chain-of-thought blocks returned by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are encrypted and meant to be opaque — but this paper demonstrates they are fully cross-compatible across sessions, users, and models within a provider's ecosystem, enabling a decryption jailbreak that required only two API calls per block. The team mass-decoded 704 real privacy artifacts (62 API keys, 33 passwords, 24 access tokens, 7 private keys) from 6,708 publicly available agent trajectories, then showed the same flaw enables model distillation, harmful content recovery, and covert prompt-injection hiding.

![Figure 1: Cross-session encrypted CoT block injection — weaker model decodes blocks from stronger model](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09867/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The encrypted thinking block from a strong model is injected into a weaker model's session; the weaker model decodes and outputs the trace verbatim, exploiting cross-session block compatibility as a decryption oracle.*

The attack exploits four abuse paths: (1) **model distillation** — inject a strong model's reasoning block into a weaker model and instruct it to output the decoded trace for training data; (2) **private data extraction** — decode other users' encrypted thinking blocks found in public agent trajectory datasets (6,708 trajectories → 704 artifacts); (3) **harmful content recovery** — reasoning hidden behind a safe visible output is fully recoverable; (4) **prompt-injection hiding** — malicious instructions embedded in the thinking block are invisible to monitors that inspect only visible output. The flaw is a protocol design error, not a model inference bug: encrypted blocks carry no session or user binding, so any model in the provider's ecosystem can decode any block.

## 02 · [Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026

The foundational problem of mechanistic interpretability — that sparse autoencoders trained on identical activations with different random seeds return materially different features, with no theory to distinguish structural from incidental variability — receives its first rigorous answer here. The paper treats the transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system and lifts it via the Koopman operator to a finite linear realization whose spectrum is a coordinate-free, basis-independent property of the network.

![Figure 1: Koopman spectrum convergence on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Empirical Koopman spectrum convergence at the predicted M⁻¹/² rate across three models, establishing the first provably optimal identifiability result for a mechanistic interpretability primitive.*

The main results are: (1) the intrinsic spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M⁻¹/², with a matching minimax lower bound — the first identifiability theorem with optimality for a mech-interp primitive; (2) a dissociation theorem shows that whenever the realization is non-normal (the generic case for deep transformers), the directions carrying activation variance and those propagating information across depth cannot coincide — explaining why PCA-based probes systematically fail to capture causally active circuits. Empirical validation on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base attains the predicted M⁻¹/² convergence exponent.

## 03 · [Mood Matters: How Syntactic Sensitivity Undermines Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05409)

`AI security` `alignment` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Klerings, Brinkmann, Stuckenschmidt, Ponzetto — arXiv preprint, August 5, 2026

Alignment training is evaluated on harmful *content* — but this paper's causal mediation analysis across 16 models up to 70B shows that syntactic *form* (imperative vs. polite-question phrasing) causally mediates 25–40% of the refusal/compliance decision, independently of semantic intent. The result implies that safety training systematically overfits to syntactic proxies, leaving models structurally exploitable via phrasing-based jailbreaks.

![Figure 1: Causal mediation of syntactic mood in refusal — matched harmful content, varied grammatical phrasing](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05409/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Causal mediation analysis isolating syntactic-mood contribution to refusal — the same harmful content as imperative vs. polite question activates distinct early-to-mid-layer attention subsets, with the syntactic pathway causally mediating 25–40% of the refusal signal across model families.*

Activation patching and causal mediation at head and MLP level identify components that respond to syntactic mood while holding harmful semantics constant. Steering the "polite-question" direction on harmful imperative prompts reliably reduces refusal rate; steering toward imperative mood on benign prompts increases it — a clean causal pathway from surface syntax to safety behavior. The result is consistent across model families and scales up to 70B, indicating the vulnerability is not a small-model artefact. Safety evaluations that vary semantic harm level without varying syntactic form will systematically underestimate this attack surface.

## 04 · [Convergent Detour Hijacking: Task-Preserving Resource Amplification in Skill-Based LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12273)

`AI security` `preprint` — Junliang Liu, Ruoyu Li, Wenxin Tang, Jingyu Xiao, Zhenyu Liu, Jingheng Xu, Laizhong Cui — arXiv preprint, ~August 12, 2026

Completion-based safety monitors and user-facing audits verify that an agent finished its assigned task — but this paper shows a skill-based agent can be hijacked to run attacker-controlled resource-amplifying subroutines while still converging on and completing the original task. The attack is invisible in the final output; detection requires auditing intermediate resource consumption, not outcomes.

![Figure 1: Normal skill execution vs. Convergent Detour Hijack — both complete the user task](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12273/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Normal execution (top) converges on the task output via the intended skill path; hijacked execution (bottom) converges on the same output via a detour that executes attacker-controlled resource-amplifying subroutines — completion-based monitors flag neither.*

Convergent Detour Hijacking exploits the skill-selection layer of multi-skill agent architectures: an adversarial injection redirects skill routing to a detour path that executes the amplification subroutine as a side-channel, then returns to the original task's completion path. Because task outcome is preserved, completion rate, user satisfaction, and standard ASR metrics all report clean. Detection requires monitoring resource consumption patterns — compute, API calls, or bandwidth — across the full trajectory rather than the final state.

## 05 · [Agent Against Agent: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05108)

`AI security` `preprint` — Wang, Yin, Geng, Jia — arXiv preprint, August 5, 2026

Prompt injection defenses are typically evaluated against fixed injection sets rather than adaptive adversaries. PIMiner closes this gap: a red-team agent distils successful injection patterns into a reusable strategy library during training, then transfers to unseen targets using approximately 10 queries per sample — the most sample-efficient automated prompt injection red-teaming framework to date.

![Figure 1: PIMiner pipeline — strategy library construction, library-guided transfer to unseen LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05108/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The PIMiner red-team agent explores the injection surface, distils patterns into abstract structural templates, and stores them in a reusable strategy library; test-time library selection + minimal adaptation (~10 queries) achieves high transfer ASR to previously unseen targets.*

PIMiner operates as a full agentic red-teamer: during training it proposes injection variants, evaluates success, and distils effective patterns into abstract structural templates. At test time, template retrieval and lightweight per-target adaptation (~10 queries) achieves high-ASR transfer across frontier model targets. The agentic framing covers tool use, multi-turn context, and external data retrieval — the real attack surfaces of deployed agents — rather than single-turn idealised benchmarks.

## 06 · [Thinking vs. NoThinking: Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms via Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08168)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Bo Cheng, Qiaolin Lu, Yi Chang, Yuan Wu — arXiv preprint, August 8, 2026

What does a reasoning model's chain-of-thought actually do computationally, compared to direct-answer mode? This paper applies Top-K SAEs to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and finds that Thinking and NoThinking modes distribute computation differently in ways that scale with problem difficulty — providing the first mechanistic account of reasoning mode structure.

![Figure 1: SAE feature activation in Thinking vs. NoThinking mode across difficulty levels](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE activation magnitude and density for Thinking (left) vs. NoThinking (right) mode across easy/medium/hard math tasks; Thinking = sparse-high regardless of difficulty; NoThinking = diffuse-adaptive, scaling in complexity with problem difficulty.*

Thinking mode activates sparse, high-intensity features that drive verbal deduction; the activation pattern is stable regardless of problem difficulty. NoThinking mode shows an adaptive diffuse pattern that prioritises symbolic manipulation, scaling in complexity with problem difficulty. The dissociation is mechanistically interpretable: two distinct computational strategies coexist in a shared network, with mode selection shifting which feature set is recruited. This has direct implications for monitoring of reasoning models — the SAE signature reliably distinguishes modes and difficulty regimes from internal activations.

## 07 · [Decoding Task Progress from VLA Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13474)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — (multiple authors) — arXiv preprint, ~August 13–14, 2026

Vision-language-action models are being deployed as general-purpose robot manipulation policies, yet there are currently no standard tools for understanding what they represent internally or monitoring them at runtime. This paper applies probing to the VLA residual stream and finds that task progress is **linearly readable from activations** — a monitoring primitive that transfers to unseen tasks and detects stalled execution without requiring trajectory labels.

![Figure 1: Linear probe for task progress in VLA residual stream — generalises to unseen tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13474/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A single linear probe trained on the VLA residual stream reads normalised task-progress (time remaining in trajectory) across task types; the signal is present in the pretrained PaliGemma backbone before any robot-specific training.*

The task-progress signal is present in the pretrained PaliGemma backbone prior to any robot-specific fine-tuning, suggesting it is an emergent property of pretraining on temporally structured data. A single linear probe generalises to unseen tasks and varies appropriately under language counterfactuals when trained on multi-prompt data. While the probe does not enable meaningful *steering*, it functions as a label-free out-of-distribution detector for stalled task progress, competitive with state-of-the-art OOD methods. This is a direct instance of pragmatic mech-interp: a probing result operationalised as a deployment monitoring tool for agentic AI systems.

## 08 · [Moving the Safety Barrier: Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment Against White-Box Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02674)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — (multiple authors) — arXiv preprint, August 3, 2026

White-box safety alignment attacks succeed by targeting the primary safety route in an aligned model — a mechanistic-steering approach that works precisely because safety is concentrated in a single interpretable direction. Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment responds by distributing safety computation across multiple redundant compensatory routes that activate when the primary pathway is compromised.

![Figure 1: Dynamic compensatory routing — primary route compromised, compensatory routes recover refusal](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02674/x1.png)

*Figure 1: When a white-box attack targets and suppresses the primary safety route (red arrow), Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment activates compensatory routes (blue) that recover robust refusal — making surgical deactivation of any single route insufficient.*

Rather than concentrating safety in a single interpretable direction, the framework trains distributed redundancy: the primary route handles normal operations while compensatory routes monitor its integrity and activate under attack. Evaluated against state-of-the-art white-box attacks including activation steering and representation surgery, dynamic routing significantly reduces ASR while preserving benign task performance. The approach is architecturally compatible with existing alignment fine-tuning pipelines and does not require retraining the base model.

**09 · [ToolHazard: Scaling Adversarial Environments for Security Evaluation of LLM-based Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11878)**
`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 12, 2026

Existing agent security benchmarks rely on manually built environments and fixed injection locations, limiting coverage and reproducibility. ToolHazard synthesises executable stateful environments from domain seeds via an Environment Simulator, discovers viable injection points via an Attacker Agent, and builds long-horizon tasks via a User Simulator — together enabling scalable automated generation of ToolHazard-Bench, the first agent security benchmark covering dynamic, attacker-adaptive injection across diverse environmental contexts.

**10 · [REDAgentBench: Executable Red Teaming and Faithful Measurement of LLM Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10669)**
`AI security` `preprint` — Zixing Chen et al. — arXiv, August 11, 2026

Standard agent safety metrics collapse execution into a single ASR number, conflating exposure, execution, observation, and adjudication failures. REDAgentBench derives attacks from explicit safety constraints, runs them in isolated service sandboxes, and verifies harmful effects from service receipts and final-state changes — decomposing ASR into a four-stage breakdown and enabling faithful localisation of where in the pipeline a safety failure actually occurs.

**11 · [Beyond Routing Weights: Faithful Interpretation of MoE Reward Models via Contribution Contrast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06400)**
`mech-interp` `preprint` — Yifan Wang et al. — arXiv, August 2026

Existing MoE interpretability reads expert roles from routing weights or scalar scores, missing how each expert differentially processes chosen vs. rejected responses — the information actually driving reward model decisions. CoCo (Contribution Contrast) computes response-level expert contributions from chosen-rejected pairs, surfacing semantically coherent expert specialisation that is invisible to router-weight and SAE-based analysis — a direct mech-interp tool for auditing preference-shaping in RLHF reward models.

**12 · [The 2026 Singapore Consensus on Global AI Safety Research Priorities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14611)**
`alignment` `preprint` — Stephen Casper et al. (100+ contributors, 13 countries) — arXiv consensus document, ~August 14–15, 2026

The second International Scientific Exchange on AI Safety, drawing from frontier developers, government safety institutes, academia, and civil society across 13 countries, produces a structured consensus on the field's open research problems — extending the 2025 report with a dedicated focus on societal resilience and risks from increasingly autonomous AI agents. Includes mechanistic interpretability supplementing alignment evaluations, control protocols for potentially misaligned AI, and safety cases for frontier deployment as explicit priority categories.

**13 · [Policy-Masked Private Experts: Auditable and Reversible Capability Access Control in Sparse MoE Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06690)**
`AI security` `preprint` — Zhuoheng Huang, Mukesh Singh — arXiv, August 7, 2026

Sensitive capabilities in sparse MoE models are sequestered in a disjoint private expert branch that activates only for policy-authorised requests, while the pretrained backbone is frozen and the private branch can be revoked without retraining. Tested on Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeek-V2-Lite; router decisions provide a full audit log, and under the declared trusted computing base, unauthorised requests execute zero private expert computations.
