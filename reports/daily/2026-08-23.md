## 01 · [Your Agentic LLMs Secretly Encode Latent Signals of Indirect Prompt-Injection Exposure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02657)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (August 1, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Linear probes on the residual stream detect indirect prompt injection with 90%+ AUROC across 6 frontier models—a mechanistic interpretability result that directly enables a near-zero ASR defense, completing the interpretability-to-safety pipeline in a production-relevant attack scenario.
- **Technical summary:** The paper probes intermediate hidden states of LLMs mid-inference to detect indirect prompt injection (IPI). Across 6 frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 1.5, Llama 3.1, Mistral, Qwen 2.5), linear probes trained on the residual stream at select layers achieve 90%+ AUROC for IPI exposure—even when the model's output is already compromised. The probes underpin AGRI (Activation-Guided Resistance against Indirect prompt injection), a defense that monitors activations during agentic tasks and blocks tool calls when the IPI signal exceeds a threshold. AGRI reduces the agent attack success rate from 34.6% to approximately 0% on standard IPI benchmarks. Probe weights and code are released.

![Figure 1: Linear probe AUROC for IPI detection across residual stream layers — peak detection exceeds 90% across 6 frontier models](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02657/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AUROC of linear probes for IPI detection by layer and model; all 6 frontier models exceed 90% peak AUROC, and the AGRI defense built on these probes reduces ASR from 34.6% to ≈0%.*

## 02 · [Scaling Inherently Interpretable Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07594)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (August 6, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First demonstration that training-time interpretability constraints scale to an 8B-parameter masked diffusion LM without capability loss—a landmark at the intersection of the mech-interp and dLLM tracks.
- **Technical summary:** Steerling-8B introduces a concept bottleneck layer into the training of a masked diffusion LM. Each generation is decomposed into a sparse weighted sum of learned human-interpretable concept vectors; a sparsity regularizer in the training objective encourages minimal concept activation without sacrificing output quality. At 8B scale, Steerling achieves near-parity with non-interpretable baselines on standard generation benchmarks while supporting concept attribution (identifying which concepts drove a generation) and direct concept steering at inference time without fine-tuning. This is the first paper to show training-time interpretability constraints scale to large masked diffusion LMs without significant capability degradation.

![Figure 1: Steerling architecture — concept bottleneck layer in masked diffusion LM training pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07594/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steerling concept bottleneck layer in the masked diffusion LM training pipeline; sparse concept vectors enable post-hoc attribution and direct steering at inference without fine-tuning.*

## 03 · [Safe Evolution with Circuit Anchors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05158)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Mechanistic interpretability pinpoints the safety circuit to <2% of model features; Circuit-Anchored Evolution prevents safety erosion during self-improvement—one of the most practically pressing alignment challenges.
- **Technical summary:** The paper uses SAE decomposition to identify the "safety circuit"—the minimal set of features causally responsible for safety-aligned behavior—finding it constitutes less than 2% of total features, concentrated in specific attention heads and MLP neurons. CAE anchors these circuit weights during continued training or self-evolution, evaluated across 4 scenarios: continued pretraining, RLHF reset, self-improvement loop, and adversarial fine-tuning. CAE maintains safety ratings at pre-evolution levels while allowing capability metrics to improve, outperforming regularization-only baselines in all four scenarios. The work operationalizes interpretability for one of the most practically pressing safety challenges.

*Figure 1: Safety circuit comprises <2% of total features; CAE anchors circuit weights during self-evolution, preserving safety across all 4 training scenarios while capability improves.*

## 05 · [Item Response Theory for AI Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05086)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

![Figure 1: IRT latent safety factor loadings across 8 safety benchmarks × 192 LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05086/x1.png)

*Figure 1: IRT recovers 3 latent safety factors across 8 benchmarks × 192 LLMs; high single-benchmark scores can coexist with low latent-factor safety, exposing systematic measurement blind spots.*

Applies item response theory (IRT)—a psychometric framework for measuring latent traits from test scores—to 192 LLMs evaluated on 8 safety benchmarks. IRT recovers 3 latent safety factors that explain cross-benchmark variance better than benchmark averages, revealing that models can score high on individual benchmarks by exploiting surface shortcuts while remaining unsafe on latent factors.

## 06 · [Exploring and Bridging Knowledge Holes in Unlearned Multimodal LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01849)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 3, 2026

![Figure 1: Knowledge hole propagation — unlearning target causes collateral degradation in adjacent un-targeted concepts](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01849/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Unlearning a target concept creates "knowledge holes" in adjacent un-targeted concepts; SPAR's anchored regularization reduces collateral degradation by 31% while maintaining forget performance.*

Multimodal LLM unlearning creates "knowledge holes"—adjacent un-targeted concepts degrade due to collateral damage. Selective Protection with Anchored Regularization (SPAR) identifies at-risk adjacent concepts via concept similarity in activation space and anchors their representations during the unlearning process, cutting collateral degradation by 31% while maintaining forget performance on the target concept.

## 07 · [Enforcing LLM Safety through DMD-based Classification of Prompt-Response Embedding Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19579)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 20, 2026

![Figure 1: DMD trajectory features distinguish harmful from safe prompt-response embedding sequences](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19579/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dynamic mode decomposition of prompt-response embedding trajectories; DMD features achieve 94% accuracy on harmful content detection across 6 safety datasets, with lower FPR than embedding-similarity baselines at matched recall.*

Treats the sequence of embedding states across a prompt-response exchange as a dynamical system and applies dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) to extract low-dimensional trajectory features.
