Top pick pretraining-safety preprint

Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

O'Brien et al. — arXiv 2508.06601, Aug 2026

Post-training safety measures for open-weight LLMs can be erased in dozens of fine-tuning steps. This paper shows that filtering dual-use knowledge from the pretraining corpus itself produces models that resist adversarial fine-tuning by orders of magnitude longer — and without sacrificing capability.

The authors pretrain multiple 6.9B-parameter models from scratch using a multi-stage pipeline that identifies and removes biothreat-relevant documents via topic classifiers and heuristic contamination filters. Resulting models score near-zero on biothreat proxy benchmarks and sustain that behavior under up to 10,000 steps and 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning — exceeding post-training RLHF/DPO baselines by over an order of magnitude. Crucially, no degradation in unrelated capabilities (coding, math, general QA) was observed, resolving the key concern that data filtering degrades model quality.

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero

Julian Minder, Viktor Moskvoretskii, Raghav Singhal et al. (EPFL dlab) — arXiv 2608.13482, Aug 13, 2026

What if the assistant persona specified in a normative constitution were baked in from the very first token of pretraining — not grafted on through RLHF afterward? SPP does exactly that, and shows a 63% jailbreak reduction, but only if the intervention starts at the beginning of training, not the end.

SPP annotates pretraining documents with first-person moral reflections derived from a normative value constitution, then pretrains via standard cross-entropy on both original and annotated text. Models trained to 3B parameters on 500B tokens achieve 1.7% mean attack success rate vs ~4.6% baseline — a 63% reduction. The critical finding is temporal: alignment injected from token zero substantially outperforms late-stage SPP injection in both constitution adherence and choice alignment on out-of-distribution moral dilemmas, while preserving downstream capabilities.

pretraining-safety preprint

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv 2606.19168, Jun 17, 2026

Even a corpus scrubbed of harmful content can produce unsafe models — models can compose benign knowledge into dangerous behaviors. This paper argues the fix must happen at pretraining: inserting short safety reflection passages regularly through the corpus builds self-monitoring into the model before fine-tuning begins.

Safety Reflection Pretraining inserts short self-reflective safety passages at regular intervals in the pretraining corpus, training the model to reason about harm while learning language. Experiments with 1.7B models on FineWeb-Edu show improved safety classification accuracy and substantially reduced success rates for both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks versus a safe-data-only baseline. The core argument is that data curation addresses what is in the corpus, but not how capable-but-benign knowledge can be composed into harmful outputs — which requires building a self-monitoring prior directly into the pretraining objective.

Refusal geometry reflects refusal training: diverse refusal prefixes can raise stable rank and weaken refusal vector ablation attacks

Andrey Labunets (UC San Diego) studies OLMo-2 refusal geometry through training dynamics, showing that activation updates from refusal-completion first-token losses explain the resulting refusal direction. Datasets with repetitive refusal starts produce a low-rank, easy-to-ablate refusal direction; diverse prefixes raise stable rank and resist vector ablation attacks — a simple data-side intervention to harden refusal against abliteration.

Unraveling LLM Jailbreaks Through Safety Knowledge Neurons

Peer-reviewed at EACL 2026 (long paper), freshly posted Sep 1. The method projects internal representations into vocabulary space to identify safety knowledge neurons — those activating Rejection tokens for harmful inputs. Selectively ablating them raises attack success rates above 97%, confirming causal role. SafeTuning, which reinforces these neurons, consistently reduces attack success rates across multiple LLMs and outperforms all four baseline defense strategies.

AI security neuron-defense preprint · Aug 2026

Tripwire: Triggering Aligned Refusal via Statistically Certified Safety Neurons

Tripwire identifies safety neurons through a three-stage funnel — direction filtering, per-neuron Welch t-tests under Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate control, and a utility-specificity filter. At runtime, selected neurons are clamped to their harmful-conditional mean activations, injecting an internal "this input is harmful" signal without retraining. Achieves 98% jailbreak mitigation with minimal utility loss; training-free and inference-time only.

Multi-Agent AI Control: Distributed Attacks Hamper Per-Instance Monitors

Oliver Makins et al. develop FakeLab (a synthetic AI-lab environment with 9 services, 86 benign tasks, 4 attack objectives) and demonstrate the fragmentation effect: as more agents coordinate on a distributed attack, the per-agent signal drops below monitor detection thresholds even as collective attack severity grows. First empirical study of AI control in multi-agent settings; submitted to NeurIPS 2026.

Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs

Builds internal computation graphs for clean and jailbroken prompts using attribution/SAE techniques, then aligns them pairwise to expose attack-induced deviations. Identifies three recurring vulnerability motifs: safety suppression, attack emergence, and computation rerouting — providing mechanistic vocabulary for diagnosing and potentially patching jailbreak vulnerabilities.

circuit-tracing · applied-interp preprint · Aug 2026

LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs

Ameen Patel, Max Zhang, Nathan Hu (Stanford) present a pipeline where an LLM groups feature descriptions into attribution graph supernodes — the most time-intensive step in manual circuit tracing. On the two-hop Capitals task, automated supernodes recover the intermediate-hop in 97/100 prompts and match human annotation quality on automated interpretability metrics. A proof-of-concept scales to 1,000 Wikipedia attribution graphs, removing the human bottleneck in circuit-level analysis.
