## 01 · [A2D: Any-Order, Any-Step Safety Alignment for Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23286)

- **Authors / venue:** (Yonsei University) — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Direct companion to #1: the ICLR 2026 defense that closes the vulnerability DIJA exposes, achieving near-zero attack success rates while preserving capabilities and enabling real-time safe termination up to 19.3× faster than post-hoc filtering.
- **Technical summary:** A2D aligns safety at the token level inside the diffusion process itself by training the dLLM to emit an [EOS] refusal signal whenever harmful content arises, under randomized masking order — the key inductive bias of diffusion generation. Forcing safety to generalize across all orderings (rather than just prefix-conditional ones) makes A2D robust to any-order and any-step prefilling attacks. Against DIJA, LLaDA-8B-Instruct drops from over 80% ASR to 1.3%; Dream-v0-Instruct-7B drops to 0.0%. Monitoring [EOS] token probability enables early termination — up to 19.3× faster safe rejection — without sacrificing model capability on benign tasks.

*Figure 1: A2D aligns [EOS] refusal across any masking order; the monitored probability allows safe termination at any denoising step rather than waiting for full generation.*

## 02 · [Understanding Machine Unlearning Through the Lens of Mode Connectivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23970)

`mech-interp` `unlearning` `preprint` — arXiv preprint (July 2026)

Mode connectivity analysis — testing whether two model checkpoints are linearly interpolable through a low-loss path — reveals that naive output-suppression unlearning methods preserve connectivity between the unlearned model and the original, meaning the "forgotten" knowledge remains accessible via the interpolation path. Circuit-aware unlearning methods that modify specific computational pathways break these paths more robustly, providing a mechanistic interpretability account of unlearning durability.

![Figure 1: Mode connectivity in weight space — standard unlearning preserves low-loss paths; circuit-aware unlearning breaks them](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23970/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Linear interpolation in weight space between original and unlearned models; standard erasure preserves mode connectivity (low-loss path remains); circuit-aware methods eliminate it.*
