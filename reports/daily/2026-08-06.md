## 01 · [The Devil behind the mask: An emergent safety vulnerability of Diffusion LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11097)

- **Authors / venue:** Zichen Wen, Haochen Qu, et al. — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Highest-priority paper in this window: the first peer-reviewed work to systematically expose the unique jailbreak surface of diffusion LMs, published at ICLR 2026. The attack is structurally impossible in autoregressive models and bypasses every prefix-focused safety mechanism.
- **Technical summary:** DIJA constructs adversarial interleaved mask-text prompts that exploit two dLLM-specific properties simultaneously: (1) bidirectional context modeling — the model conditions on all unmasked tokens at every denoising step, so an adversarial token inserted at any position influences the full sequence; (2) confidence-based parallel commitment — harmful tokens can be committed at non-prefix positions before safety filters acting on the generated prefix can intervene. The attack requires no rewriting or obfuscation of harmful content. On JailbreakBench, DIJA achieves 100% keyword-based ASR on Dream-Instruct, surpasses ReNeLLM by 78.5 percentage points in evaluator-based ASR, and gains 37.7 points on StrongREJECT. Code: github.com/ZichenWen1/DIJA.

![Figure 1: DIJA adversarial mask-text interleaving exploits bidirectional attention at non-prefix positions](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11097/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DIJA inserts adversarial tokens at non-prefix positions in a dLLM's mask sequence; bidirectional attention propagates their influence to the full output while prefix-focused safety filters see only clean tokens.*

## 02 · [A2D: Any-Order, Any-Step Safety Alignment for Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23286)

- **Authors / venue:** (Yonsei University) — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Direct companion to #1: the ICLR 2026 defense that closes the vulnerability DIJA exposes, achieving near-zero attack success rates while preserving capabilities and enabling real-time safe termination up to 19.3× faster than post-hoc filtering.
- **Technical summary:** A2D aligns safety at the token level inside the diffusion process itself by training the dLLM to emit an [EOS] refusal signal whenever harmful content arises, under randomized masking order — the key inductive bias of diffusion generation. Forcing safety to generalize across all orderings (rather than just prefix-conditional ones) makes A2D robust to any-order and any-step prefilling attacks. Against DIJA, LLaDA-8B-Instruct drops from over 80% ASR to 1.3%; Dream-v0-Instruct-7B drops to 0.0%. Monitoring [EOS] token probability enables early termination — up to 19.3× faster safe rejection — without sacrificing model capability on benign tasks.

![Figure 1: A2D token-level safety alignment under randomized masking order](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23286/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A2D aligns [EOS] refusal across any masking order; the monitored probability allows safe termination at any denoising step rather than waiting for full generation.*

## 03 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07903)

- **Authors / venue:** Anupam Wagle, Ifrat Ikhtear Uddin, Chaowei Zhang, Longwei Wang — arXiv preprint (July 8, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Best crossover paper of the window: applies mech-interp tooling directly to AI security, producing a causal account of *why* jailbreaks succeed that goes well beyond empirical attack success rates.
- **Technical summary:** The framework represents prompt-specific inference as SAE-based attribution graphs — directed graphs of latent feature interactions — and computes them for paired clean and adversarial prompts. Aligning these graph pairs decomposes structural changes into three categories: invariant components (unchanged), suppressed structures (safety-relevant paths eliminated), and emergent structures (attack-specific features that appear only under the attack). The key empirical finding is that successful jailbreaks are characterized by *computation rerouting* rather than safety-feature suppression: safety-relevant nodes remain structurally present in the graph but are bypassed by an alternative pathway that carries the harmful output. Causal interventions on individual nodes, paths, and subgraphs validate these motifs quantitatively.

![Figure 1: Paired attribution graphs — jailbreak reroutes computation rather than suppressing safety nodes](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07903/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Clean-prompt and jailbroken-prompt attribution graphs aligned side by side; safety nodes remain in the jailbroken graph but computation is rerouted through an alternative pathway.*

## 04 · [Steering Without Breaking: Mechanistically Informed Interventions for Discrete Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10971)

`mech-interp` `text-diffusion` `preprint` — arXiv preprint (May 2026)

Applying uniform activation steering to a diffusion LM at every denoising step degrades generation quality because different semantic attributes commit at fundamentally different points in the denoising process. SAEs trained on four DLMs (124M–8B) reveal that topic commits within the first 2% of denoising steps while sentiment emerges gradually over 20%; an adaptive framework that concentrates steering where each attribute is actively forming achieves strong multi-attribute control at substantially lower perplexity and inter-attribute interference than uniform baselines.

![Figure 1: Attribute commitment schedules across denoising steps — topic commits at ~2%, sentiment at ~20%](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10971/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attribute-specific commitment timelines discovered via SAE analysis; the adaptive steering framework intervenes only in the window where each attribute is actively being committed.*

## 05 · [Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)

`mech-interp` `SAE` `preprint` — arXiv preprint (June 4, 2026)

Standard SAEs assign each feature a single decoder direction, implicitly assuming features are one-dimensional. SASA proves this assumption provably induces feature splitting via two distinct mechanisms when the true feature occupies a multi-dimensional subspace. The fix — learned decoder subspaces with Top-s block sparsity and nuclear-norm rank regularization — reduces feature splitting and absorption on GPT-2 Small and Mistral-7B, improves monosemanticity, and matches or exceeds standard SAEs on roughly half the token training budget.

![Figure 1: SASA — single-vector decoder vs. learned subspace decoder for multi-dimensional features](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06333/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Standard SAE decoder (single 1-D direction per feature) vs. SASA decoder (learned subspace per group); SASA eliminates the two splitting mechanisms that 1-D decoders provably induce.*

## 06 · [Induction in Both Directions: A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning in Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15893)

`mech-interp` `text-diffusion` `preprint` — Andy Catruna, Emilian Radoi (NUST POLITEHNICA Bucharest) — arXiv preprint (July 20, 2026)

Masked DLMs implement a bidirectional induction circuit — previous-token and next-token heads write local context into the residual stream, and a later induction head uses both to find and copy from matching source positions anywhere in the sequence, whether source appears before or after the query. This is mechanistically impossible in autoregressive models and represents a fundamental architectural difference in how DLMs implement in-context learning.

![Figure 1: Bidirectional induction circuit — previous-token and next-token heads enable copying from either direction](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15893/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The bidirectional induction circuit: previous-token head (blue) and next-token head (orange) cooperate with the induction head to copy from matching positions regardless of their direction relative to the query.*

## 07 · [DifFRACT: Diffusion Feature Reconstruction and Attribution for Circuit Tracing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15796)

`mech-interp` `text-diffusion` `preprint` — Artyom Mazur, Nina Konovalova, Aibek Alanov (HSE University, FusionBrain Lab) — arXiv preprint (June 14, 2026)

Extends transcoder-based circuit tracing to multimodal diffusion transformers (FLUX.1[schnell]), training timestep-conditioned transcoders that faithfully approximate MLP sublayers in the double-stream MM-DiT architecture. The resulting circuits reveal attribute binding mechanisms and how semantic information propagates across the text–image cross-stream interface; circuit-guided steering interventions are substantially more precise and effective than SAE-based steering.

## 08 · [DLM-Scope: Mechanistic Interpretability of Diffusion Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05859)

`mech-interp` `text-diffusion` `preprint` — Xu Wang, Bingqing Jiang, Yu Wan, Baosong Yang, Lingpeng Kong, Difan Zou — arXiv preprint (February 5, 2026)

The first SAE-based interpretability framework for diffusion language models: Top-K SAEs trained on DLM residual streams faithfully extract interpretable features, features are stable across the post-training (SFT) phase, and — uniquely — inserting SAEs into the DLM forward pass does not incur the loss penalty observed in autoregressive LLMs. Establishes the foundational toolkit that Steering Without Breaking (#4) extends.

## 09 · [Backdooring Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19262)

`AI security` `text-diffusion` `preprint` — Daniel Yiming Cao et al. — arXiv preprint (May 19, 2026)

SHADOWMASK is the first backdoor attack designed specifically for masked diffusion LMs, modifying the MDLM forward corruption process by replacing the standard all-mask terminal distribution with a trigger-mask mixture prior. When the trigger token is present, training corrupts toward a trigger-specific masked state, creating a dedicated denoising pathway to the attacker's target sequence; clean-input behavior is fully preserved. Backdoor techniques from continuous diffusion or autoregressive models do not transfer because discrete state corruption has no analogue.

![Figure 1: SHADOWMASK — trigger-mask mixture prior creates dedicated adversarial denoising pathway](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19262/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Standard MDLM corruption (all-mask terminal) vs. SHADOWMASK corruption (trigger-mask mixture); the mixture prior creates a dedicated denoising pathway to the adversarial target.*

## 10 · [Understanding Machine Unlearning Through the Lens of Mode Connectivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23970)

`mech-interp` `unlearning` `preprint` — arXiv preprint (July 2026)

Mode connectivity analysis — testing whether two model checkpoints are linearly interpolable through a low-loss path — reveals that naive output-suppression unlearning methods preserve connectivity between the unlearned model and the original, meaning the "forgotten" knowledge remains accessible via the interpolation path. Circuit-aware unlearning methods that modify specific computational pathways break these paths more robustly, providing a mechanistic interpretability account of unlearning durability.

![Figure 1: Mode connectivity in weight space — standard unlearning preserves low-loss paths; circuit-aware unlearning breaks them](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23970/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Linear interpolation in weight space between original and unlearned models; standard erasure preserves mode connectivity (low-loss path remains); circuit-aware methods eliminate it.*
