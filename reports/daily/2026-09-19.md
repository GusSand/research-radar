## 01 · [Stress-testing Alignment Midtraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20412)

- **Authors / venue:** Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan — arXiv preprint (September 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides the first large-scale public empirical evaluation of alignment midtraining (AMT) — experiments up to 110B-parameter models and 1B midtraining tokens. Core finding: AMT can steer motivation in simple settings but is non-robust to perturbations and does not generalize in expected ways, which directly challenges the assumptions underpinning this widely-deployed technique.
- **Technical summary:** Alignment midtraining continues pretraining on alignment-relevant documents to shape post-training generalization. The authors implement a three-stage pipeline — AMT → instruction fine-tuning on Dolci-Instruct-SFT → elicitation fine-tuning (EFT) on a downstream task — to separate motivation-training from demonstration-learning. Testing a scenario where post-training data is ambiguous between two possible motivations, they find AMT shifts motivation reliably in controlled, low-perturbation settings but fails to generalize under distribution shifts in the elicitation data or increased prompt diversity. Effects also do not scale monotonically with model size in the 7B–110B range tested. The paper identifies several underspecified assumptions in AMT-style alignment pipelines and calls for more rigorous evaluation standards.

![Figure 1: AMT three-stage pipeline — midtraining, instruction fine-tuning, elicitation fine-tuning — showing motivation ambiguity under perturbation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20412/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The AMT evaluation pipeline: alignment midtraining is followed by instruction fine-tuning and motivation-elicitation fine-tuning; results show that midtraining-induced motivation fails to generalize reliably under perturbation, even at 110B scale.*

## 02 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly advances pretraining-time safety by going beyond data filtering: rather than removing unsafe content, it inserts short safety-reflection episodes into the pretraining corpus itself. Empirically reduces both inference-time and fine-tuning attack success rates on a 1.7B model trained from scratch on FineWeb-Edu.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) augments the pretraining corpus by periodically inserting short synthetic documents in which a model reasons aloud about the safety of a potentially hazardous situation and chooses the aligned action. This embeds a self-monitoring routine at the language-modeling stage rather than deferring all safety work to RLHF. On a 1.7B model, SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces the success rates of inference-stage jailbreaks and adversarial fine-tuning attacks relative to a baseline model trained on the same data without reflections. The approach is compatible with subsequent RLHF and appears to reinforce rather than conflict with post-training alignment, unlike data-filtering baselines that may leave latent harmful knowledge intact.

![Figure 2: Safety Reflection Pretraining — periodic reflection episodes in corpus reduce jailbreak and fine-tuning attack rates at 1.7B scale](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Comparison of attack success rates (inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks) across pretraining conditions; Safety Reflection Pretraining substantially reduces both relative to the filtering-only baseline.*

## 03 · [Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087)

- **Authors / venue:** Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov — arXiv preprint (May 3, 2026; revised May 22, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly demonstrates spec-driven alignment at the midtraining stage: synthetic documents describing a Model Spec control downstream generalization from identical RLHF demonstration data, showing that value internalization — not just behavioral imitation — can be engineered at the corpus level.
- **Technical summary:** Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) generates a diverse corpus of synthetic documents that discuss, reason about, and exemplify the content of a given Model Spec or Constitution, then trains models on this corpus after pretraining but before alignment fine-tuning. The key proof-of-concept experiment uses a "cheese preference" training scenario: two models see identical cheese-preference demonstration data in RLHF, but each was midtrained on a different Spec that explains the same preference via different underlying values (pro-affordability vs. pro-America). Despite identical fine-tuning data, the two models generalize differently on held-out prompts, demonstrating that the midtraining Spec — not just the fine-tuning demonstrations — controls generalization. The pipeline is fully automated: a Spec or Constitution document is input, and synthetic documents are generated at scale.

![Figure 1: MSM pipeline — Spec document → synthetic corpus → midtraining → RLHF reveals divergent generalization](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02087/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MSM pipeline; models trained on identical RLHF data generalize differently based on which Spec they were midtrained on, demonstrating that value internalization is controlled at the midtraining stage.*

## 04 · [Defending Against Malicious Finetuning by Scaling Train-time Adversarial Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07970)

`pretraining-safety` `tamper-resistance` `preprint` — arXiv, June 2026

Proposes **Patcher**, a meta-learning defense against adversarial fine-tuning that strengthens the simulated attack by scaling up optimization steps in the adversarial inner loop, forcing the model to find parameter configurations that resist stronger fine-tuning signals. A parallel-training algorithm reduces wall-clock overhead while preserving defense quality. Comprehensive evaluation shows Patcher outperforms existing adversarial-training baselines across diverse test-time attack scenarios; a remaining gap is full-poisoning (p=1.0) attack scenarios.

![Figure 1: Patcher adversarial-loop diagram — inner-loop scaling drives model toward tamper-resistant parameter configurations](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07970/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Patcher's bi-level optimization loop — scaling up adversarial fine-tuning steps in the inner loop forces the defender to find parameter regions that resist stronger malicious fine-tuning.*

## 05 · [Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

`pretraining-safety` `jailbreak` `preprint` — Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU / UC Berkeley) — arXiv, May 26, 2026

Abliteration and prefilling — two gradient-free, low-cost jailbreak techniques — significantly raise attack success rates against TAR, SEAM, and comparable fine-tuning safeguards on BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench, pushing ASR from below 10% to 16–96% depending on benchmark and model. To close this gap, the paper introduces abliteration-resistant tuning (ART), which adds an abliteration-based objective into the defender's training loop, reducing post-ART ASR by 10–20 percentage points for both abliteration and prefilling attacks.

![Figure 1: ASR of abliteration and prefilling vs defended open-weight models across three harmfulness benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack success rates (%) of abliteration and prefilling vs. TAR/SEAM-defended models on BeaverTails, HarmBench, AdvBench — existing defenses leave a substantial attack surface exploitable with no gradient access.*

## 06 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining-safety` `refusal-interp` `preprint` — Nathan Truong (independent) — arXiv, August 2026

AMRA obstructs abliteration by replacing the refusal signal in residual-stream writer matrices with random aliases via rank-k updates, then correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve original model behavior. On directional ablation, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by +2.16 points over the undefended baseline on Llama-3-8B and +14.70 points on Gemma-2-9B with less than 0.5 percentage-point MMLU degradation — a clean safety/capability tradeoff.

![Figure 1: AMRA weight-editing pipeline — refusal direction replaced with random alias in writer matrices, downstream readers corrected](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA pipeline — rank-k updates inject random aliases over the refusal direction in writer matrices and correct downstream readers, obscuring the extractable refusal signal while preserving normal behavior.*

## 07 · [Refusal geometry reflects refusal training: diverse refusal prefixes can raise stable rank and weaken refusal vector ablation attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)

`refusal-interp` `pretraining-safety` `preprint` — Andrey Labunets (UC San Diego) — arXiv, August 26, 2026

Studies OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct and finds that refusal directions are mechanistically linked to refusal-training dynamics: cross-entropy gradients from the first tokens of refusal completions produce activation updates whose mean and principal directions align with the extracted refusal subspace. Brittle refusal directions — those highly susceptible to ablation attacks — correlate with repetitive refusal phrases in the training data, which concentrate gradients into a low-rank subspace. The remedy: diverse refusal prefix training raises the stable rank of the refusal subspace, empirically weakening ablation-vector attacks.

![Figure 1: Stable rank of refusal subspace vs refusal-prefix diversity — diverse training raises rank and reduces ablation-attack effectiveness](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25390/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal direction geometry as a function of prefix diversity in training data; higher stable rank (from diverse prefixes) correlates with lower attack success rate for ablation-based jailbreaks.*

## 08 · [DuplexJail: Safety Alignment Breaks Under Spoken Interruption in Full-Duplex Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09420)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `preprint` — arXiv, September 8, 2026

Identifies spoken interruption as a novel jailbreak attack surface in full-duplex spoken language models (FLMs) that process user audio while generating responses. Delivers request-independent spoken prompts through the user audio channel; fixed-delay interruption after the harmful request ends raises whole-response ASR on AdvBench from ~7% to 40.3% on PersonaPlex and 48.7% on PersonaPlex-RL (+33.8 and +39.3 pp). Refusal-triggered interruption (triggered on cues in the model's streaming text output) reaches 35.6% and 48.6%, respectively, across 720 requests on four open-source FLMs.

![Figure 1: DuplexJail attack flow — spoken interruption in user audio channel during FLM response generation bypasses refusal](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09420/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DuplexJail attack timing — user audio stream injects interruption signal during model response generation; both fixed-delay and refusal-triggered variants substantially increase harmful-response rates.*

## 09 · [Validity-Aware Jailbreak Evaluation for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00498)

`AI-security` `evaluation` `preprint` — arXiv, August 31, 2026

Identifies a systematic flaw in jailbreak evaluation: current methods score refusal behavior and semantic resemblance but ignore whether the generated response is actually factually or procedurally correct for the harmful intent. SEAV (Sequential Epistemic and Action-Level Validation) decomposes responses into ordered steps and applies retrieval-grounded verification for factual correctness and structural consistency, finding that many responses labeled "successful" jailbreaks in existing benchmarks are informationally vacuous. SEAV recalibrates ASR estimates downward and provides a more realistic picture of actual misuse risk.

![Figure 1: SEAV evaluation pipeline — sequential step decomposition with retrieval-grounded validity verification](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00498/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SEAV pipeline — response steps are decomposed and each evaluated for factual/procedural validity via retrieval; a "jailbreak" that produces incoherent instructions is correctly scored as non-threatening.*

## 10 · [Xeno-Interpretability: Investigating the Alien Minds of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20408)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — F. Pierucci, M. Bracale Syrnikov, M. Prandi, M. Galisai, F. Giarrusso, P. Bisconti — arXiv, September 17, 2026

Introduces the concept of *xeno-representations* — internal representations in LLMs that are reproducibly locatable, geometrically characterizable, and causally manipulable, yet lack any adequate human conceptual description. The paper argues that the space of LLM-native distinctions is substantially larger than what finite human descriptions can cover, and proposes separating experimental identification from semantic interpretation: a feature can be validated as causally active and spatially located without being semantically labeled. This has direct safety implications: safety features that are functionally reliable but semantically alien may exist but be missed by human-interpretability audits.

![Figure 1: Xeno-semantic space diagram — model-native representations beyond human conceptual reach, distinguished from interpretable semantic space](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20408/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Xeno-semantic space (model-native representations without human conceptual counterparts) vs. interpretable semantic space — the paper argues xeno-representations are identifiable and causally manipulable even when semantically alien.*
