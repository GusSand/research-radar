## 01 · [Structuring Sparsity: Block-Sparse Featurizers Capture Visual Concept Manifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25234)

- **Authors / venue:** Thomas Fel et al. (25 authors, Goodfire AI) — arXiv preprint (June 23, 2026; prominent coverage from July 29)
- **Why it ranks here:** Direct methodological challenge to the SAE paradigm from Goodfire. BSFs recover manifold-valued concepts (colour gradients, curve detectors) as low-dimensional subspaces rather than shattering them across N 1D atoms. If the empirical results hold, a substantial fraction of prior mech-interp work may need re-examination through a manifold lens.
- **Technical summary:** Block-Sparse Featurizers extend dictionary learning by replacing 1D feature directions with k-dimensional subspace blocks, with three variants: Vanilla BSF (unconstrained block basis), Grassmannian BSF (orthonormal blocks on the Grassmann manifold), and Group Lasso BSF (grouped sparsity). Applied to vision models, BSFs describe activations more compactly than direction-based featurizers, recontextualise prior curve-detector results as manifold geometry, and support interpretable manifold steering at inference time without retraining. The central empirical result: 1D SAEs shatter concepts like temperature and colour gradients across many unrelated atoms; BSFs capture these as single coherent geometric structures, enabling smoother interpolations under activation addition.

![Figure 1: BSF vs SAE — 1D directions scatter concept manifolds; block-sparse subspaces recover them](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25234/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAEs decompose manifold-valued concepts into N scattered 1D directions (left); BSFs recover the concept as a low-dimensional subspace (right), enabling smoother, more faithful manifold-level steering.*

## 02 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07903)

- **Authors / venue:** Anupam Wagle, Ifrat Ikhtear Uddin, Chaowei Zhang, Longwei Wang (University of South Dakota; Yangzhou University) — arXiv preprint (July 8, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Extends Anthropic's attribution-graph methodology to jailbreak analysis, producing the first mechanistic taxonomy of jailbreak signatures — three recurring vulnerability motifs directly targetable by alignment interventions.
- **Technical summary:** The framework constructs paired internal computation graphs for clean and adversarially attacked prompts, decomposing each into invariant, suppressed, and emergent components. Causal interventions on identified nodes, paths, and subgraphs directly quantify each component's contribution to attack success. Analysing prompt injection, role-play jailbreaks, and token-manipulation attacks, three universal vulnerability motifs emerge: (1) suppression of safety-relevant feature nodes, (2) emergence of attack-specific features absent in clean inference, and (3) computational rerouting through a bypass subgraph that circumvents the safety subgraph entirely. The motif taxonomy is consistent across jailbreak types, suggesting a shared mechanistic substrate.

![Figure 1: Paired attribution graphs — clean (safety node active) vs jailbreaked (safety suppressed, attack emergent)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07903/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Clean vs. jailbreaked attribution graphs; attack systematically suppresses the safety node (grayed), introduces an emergent attack feature (red), and reroutes computation — the three recurring vulnerability motifs.*

## 03 · [Transcoders for Investigating Deception in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14791)

- **Authors / venue:** Darius Lim et al. — arXiv preprint (July 16, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First application of transcoder-based attribution graphs — the same tools Anthropic uses internally for circuit analysis — to AI safety and deception detection. Identifies a mechanistically legible dictionary of deception features with predictable causal influence on output.
- **Technical summary:** Per-layer transcoders (PLTs) on Qwen3-4B construct attribution graphs capturing feature activations and inter-feature dependencies across layers. Comparing honest and deceptive completions on matched factual content, deception-related features are identified that (1) activate significantly more strongly in the deceptive regime, (2) exert 2–3× greater causal influence on the output distribution than honest-content features, and (3) when directly steered via activation addition, produce controlled, predictable shifts between deceptive and non-deceptive responses. Honest-content features are correspondingly suppressed in the deceptive completion graph. The mechanistic handle on deception is independent of training data access or reward signal monitoring.

![Figure 1: PLT attribution graph — deception features (red, stronger causal weights) vs honest features (blue) on Qwen3-4B](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14791/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Per-layer transcoder attribution graph on Qwen3-4B; deception-specific features (red) activate more strongly and exert 2–3× greater causal influence; honest features (blue) are suppressed in the deceptive regime.*

## 04 · [RARE: Decoupling Representation Steering from Expert Routing in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21236)

`mech-interp` `COLM '26 workshop (peer-reviewed)` — Zhibo Zhang, Zhen Ouyang, Ling Shi, Kailong Wang — Actionable Interpretability Workshop, COLM 2026

In standard MoE forward passes, expert routing and representation transformation are coupled, making it difficult to interpret or steer either in isolation. RARE disentangles the two operations — providing separate handles for "which expert activates" (routing) vs. "how the hidden state is transformed" (steering) — enabling cleaner interpretability experiments and targeted interventions in MoE architectures.

![Figure 1: RARE decoupled architecture — routing (teal) and steering (violet) as independent operations in MoE LMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21236/x1.png)

*Figure 1: RARE separates the expert routing gate from the representation steering signal, enabling independent interpretability and control of each component.*
