## 01 · [Measure, Don't Optimize: Forecasting Recovery in LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11408)

- **Authors / venue:** Zirui Song, Huaxing Liu, Xiang Wang, Shuai Li, Xinye Li, Lang Gao, Jinghui Zhang, Zheng Lu, Fengxian Ji, Xiaojun Chang, Xiuying Chen — arXiv preprint (August 11, 2026; missed by Aug 11 and Aug 13 radars)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly subverts the standard internal-audit paradigm for LLM unlearning evaluation: converting a measurement into an optimization target causes the model to *hide* knowledge from the audit while retaining more of it — audit score decreases but post-attack recovery becomes faster. Critical dual warning for the field (evaluate but never optimize; 398 unlearned models audited).
- **Technical summary:** J-Access proposes using the Jacobian lens to project intermediate representations into vocabulary space, measuring how often a target concept remains "accessible" along the output pathway. Applied to 398 publicly released unlearned models spanning 8 unlearning methods, the audit finds: (1) most unlearned models retain accessibility above the retain-only gold baseline; (2) pre-attack J-Access predicts recovery speed and overall extent at the model level (but cannot predict which specific facts recover); (3) directly minimizing J-Access does not improve genuine deletion — the model learns to evade the audit while becoming *more* recoverable post-attack, not less.

![Figure 1: J-Access pipeline and key finding — minimizing audit score increases, not decreases, post-attack knowledge recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11408/x1.png)

*Figure 1: J-Access audit pipeline — Jacobian lens maps intermediate representations to vocabulary; pre-attack accessibility predicts recovery; optimizing the audit metric corrupts it (lower audit score → greater post-attack recovery).*

## 02 · [When Safety Overrides Vision: Exploring Dynamics between Vision Influence and Safety Alignment in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18628)

- **Authors / venue:** Mehak Gupta, Tanmoy Chakraborty (IIT Delhi) — arXiv preprint (August 19, 2026; missed by Aug 20 radar)
- **Why it ranks here:** First mechanistic dissection of VLM safety refusal at the representation level: visual grounding is fully preserved internally even during abstention — the model *sees* the image and routes the answer normally up to the output layer, then safety alignment intercepts and replaces it with a refusal. Activation-level suppression of refusal representations restores correct visual answering without any retraining, across multiple architectures.
- **Technical summary:** Aligned VLMs under safety instruction frequently abstain from visually answerable questions that identical unaligned versions answer correctly. The paper analyzes internal decoding dynamics across multiple architectures and multimodal benchmarks, finding that visual evidence consistently and significantly influences the internal representations of abstained outputs — perceptual grounding is preserved despite refusal. Targeted activation-level interventions that suppress refusal-related representations reliably restore grounded, correct answering behavior without retraining or image modification, confirming that safety alignment functions as a late-stage representational override rather than suppressing earlier visual processing.

![Figure 1: VLM abstention dynamics — visual grounding persists; activation intervention restores visual answering](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18628/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Under safety constraints, VLMs abstain even for correctly answerable visual inputs; internal decoding dynamics reveal visual evidence influences abstained outputs throughout; activation interventions suppressing refusal representations restore grounded answers.*

## 03 · [Are LLMs Safe Beyond Text: Do Emojis Expose Gaps in Safety Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18164)

- **Authors / venue:** M P V S Gopinadh — arXiv preprint (August 15, 2026; missed by Aug 20 radar)
- **Why it ranks here:** Lightweight but pointed gap analysis: safety benchmarks rely almost exclusively on text-only adversarial prompts, and emoji-augmented versions of the same prompts bypass safety filters in 3 of 4 tested LLMs, highlighting an underexplored jailbreak surface in deployment evaluation.
- **Technical summary:** 50 adversarial prompts augmented with emoji modifiers are evaluated across Mistral 7B, Qwen 2 7B, Gemma 2 9B, and Llama 3 8B. Gemma 2 9B and Mistral 7B each exhibit a 10% bypass rate under emoji augmentation; Llama 3 8B shows 6%; Qwen 2 7B demonstrates complete resistance (0%). The findings are limited in scope (50 prompts, 4 models, single evaluation dimension) but underscore that safety evaluation suites standardly ignore non-text token representations as an attack surface.

![Figure 1: Emoji jailbreak bypass rate across 4 LLMs — Qwen resistant, Gemma/Mistral 10%](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18164/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Emoji-augmented adversarial prompt bypass rates — Gemma 2 9B and Mistral 7B: 10%; Llama 3 8B: 6%; Qwen 2 7B: 0%; highlights emoji-surface gap in standard text-only safety evaluation.*
