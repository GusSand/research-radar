## 01 · [**Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman — **ICLR 2026 (peer-reviewed)**
- **Why it ranks here:** The strongest tamper-resistance result in the open-weight safety literature: a pretraining-level data intervention is 10× more resistant to adversarial relearning than SOTA post-training unlearning baselines, directly addressing the core threat model for open-weight bio-risk.
- **Technical summary:** The authors build a multi-stage pipeline that filters biothreat-proxy text from pretraining corpora at scale, then evaluate how hard it is to re-learn that capability via adversarial fine-tuning on biorisk material. Training-data filtering maintains state-of-the-art tamper resistance for up to 10,000 fine-tuning steps and 300M tokens of adversarial data — the same regime where post-training unlearning baselines fully collapse. Crucially, "Deep Ignorance" is a pretraining-time intervention, meaning it does not depend on any post-training safety procedure that a downloader could undo.

![Figure 1: Tamper-resistance comparison — data-filtering vs post-training unlearning under 0–10k adversarial fine-tuning steps](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Adversarial relearning curves for data-filtered models vs. SOTA unlearning baselines; data filtering stays below the harmful capability threshold for up to 10,000 steps and 300M tokens, while unlearning baselines collapse well before that.*

## 02 · [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Xingyu Lu et al. — **arXiv preprint (June 2026)**
- **Why it ranks here:** Proposes Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP), a distinct pretraining-stage intervention that teaches a 1.7B model to judge its own outputs during language modeling — surviving both inference-time and adversarial-fine-tuning attacks at rates well below filtering-only baselines.
- **Technical summary:** SRP splits pretraining documents into short segments and inserts a brief safety reflection after each segment, so the model learns to self-monitor as it learns language (not just avoid unsafe data). On FineWeb-Edu with a 1.7B architecture, SRP reduces prefill-attack ASR from 92.42% (baseline) to 8.48%. The most striking result: after adversarial fine-tuning, the SRP model holds at 0.00% ASR while the baseline is completely compromised. Unlike data filtering alone, SRP does not assume a clean corpus — it is a training-objective change that applies on top of any corpus curation.

![Figure 2: SRP attack success rate comparison — prefill attack and adversarial finetuning ASR for baseline vs SRP-trained model](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 2: SRP drastically reduces ASR from 92.42% → 8.48% on prefill attacks and achieves 0.00% after adversarial fine-tuning, where the baseline collapses entirely.*

## 03 · [**Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

- **Authors / venue:** Julian Minder et al. (15 authors, EPFL dlab) — **arXiv preprint (August 2026)**
- **Why it ranks here:** Operationalizes the "model spec at pretraining time" idea: rather than post-training on a value spec, SPP injects synthetic first-person moral reflections derived from a normative constitution into 10% of pretraining documents, installing the desired persona from token zero.
- **Technical summary:** SPP annotates 10% of pretraining documents with value-aligned first-person moral reflections generated from a normative value constitution document; the same base data is used for the baseline, so the only difference is the injected reflections. The key result is a mean attack success rate of 1.7% on jailbreak benchmarks, a 63% reduction over baseline. Critically, the advantage scales with pretraining budget — unlike post-training interventions, the alignment advantage grows as the model trains longer — suggesting it becomes more load-bearing with scale.

![Figure 3: SPP mean ASR vs pretraining budget — alignment advantage grows with token count](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 3: SPP mean ASR (1.7%) vs. baseline on jailbreak benchmarks; alignment improvement grows with pretraining budget, unlike post-training methods.*

## 04 · [**Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

`pretraining safety` `AI security` `preprint` — Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU / UC Berkeley) — arXiv, May 2026

TAR and SEAM, the two leading post-training tamper-resistance methods for open-weight models, are defeated by gradient-free abliteration (removing the refusal direction from the residual stream) and prefilling attacks across all three harmfulness benchmarks (BeaverTails, HarmBench, AdvBench), increasing attack success rates from below 10% to high levels. Neither attack requires access to harmful training data or gradient information — only white-box weight access.

![Figure 1: ASR for abliteration and prefilling attacks vs TAR and SEAM across three benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Abliteration and prefilling attacks raise ASR of TAR- and SEAM-guarded models from <10% to high attack success across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench.*

## 05 · [**Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03887)

`refusal interp` `alignment` `peer-reviewed` — Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem (Macquarie University) — **EMNLP 2026 Main**

Comparing SFT, reasoning-augmented SFT (Ra-SFT), and ORPO on Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B via circuit-tracing, the authors find that training method — not just data — reshapes refusal circuits: Ra-SFT installs a distinctly different circuit topology consistent across all three models, while ORPO produces circuits that are harder to steer via representation editing. No method simultaneously satisfies: diffuse (non-fragile) circuit topology, no capability degradation, and accurate steerability. Architecture independently modulates circuit structure beyond what training method alone explains.

![Figure 1: Refusal circuit topology heatmaps for SFT vs Ra-SFT vs ORPO across three model families](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03887/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Circuit-tracing heatmaps across post-training methods and model families; Ra-SFT produces a consistently distinct topology, and no method satisfies all three desiderata simultaneously.*

## 06 · [**SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21500)

`AI security` `prompt injection` `peer-reviewed` — Yibo Peng, Long Lian, David Wagner, Sizhe Chen — **EMNLP 2026 Main**

Existing prompt injection defenses rely on sequence-level feedback (DPO/GRPO), which still yields near-100% ASR against adaptive PISmith attacks. SecOPD instead applies on-policy distillation: paired clean and attacked inputs are run through the model, the clean-input scores provide token-level labels for the attacked rollout, training the model to answer as if the injected instruction is absent. On Qwen3.6-27B, ASR against state-of-the-art PISmith attacks drops from 94.0% (Meta-SecAlign) to 9.0%.

![Figure 1: SecOPD on-policy distillation framework — token-level signal from clean vs attacked rollouts](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21500/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SecOPD training — clean-input token probabilities supervise attacked-input rollouts; reduces PISmith ASR from 94.0% to 9.0% on Qwen3.6-27B.*

## 07 · [**Less Sycophancy, Stronger Refusal? Lessons for AI Safety from Mechanistic Interpretability**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35544)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Xu Wang, Difan Zou, Xuansheng Wu — arXiv, **September 28, 2026** (yesterday)

Uses SAEs to extract sycophancy-feature vectors from paired sycophantic/independent responses in Qwen3.5 base models, then applies compensatory feature injection (CFI) during SFT. Positive injection (suppressing sycophancy features) both limits learned sycophancy and recovers refusal that ordinary sycophancy training had weakened; the effect is retained after injection removal. Key implication: sycophancy training is a hidden threat to refusal robustness, and SAE-based feature injection is a tractable mitigation.

![Figure 1: CFI sycophancy suppression — refusal recovery as a function of injection direction and magnitude](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35544/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CFI positive injection suppresses sycophancy features and recovers refusal weakened by RLHF sycophancy training; negative injection degrades refusal, confirming the causal link.*

## 08 · [**Perturbation Probing: A Two-Pass-per-Prompt Diagnostic for FFN Behavioral Circuits in Aligned LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27401)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Hongliang Liu, Tung-Ling Li, Yuhao Wu (Palo Alto Networks) — arXiv, April 2026

Two forward passes per prompt (no backprop, no finetuning) identify the FFN neurons that causally drive each of eight behavioral circuits including safety refusal, sycophancy, and language selection across 13 open-weight models and 4 architecture families. For safety refusal: ~50 neurons (0.014% of all neurons) control the refusal template; ablating them changes 80% of response formats on 520 AdvBench prompts while producing near-zero harmful compliance (3 of 520, all with disclaimers). Demonstrates that safety refusal lives in a thin, steerable template layer — a critical fragility result.

![Figure 1: Perturbation probing results — 50 FFN neurons drive 80% of refusal format changes with near-zero harmful compliance](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27401/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ~50 neurons (0.014% of the network) control the safety-refusal template; ablation changes 80% of response formats on AdvBench with only 3/520 yielding harmful content.*

## 09 · [**Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04721)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Preethi Carmel Bosco, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan — arXiv, September 4, 2026

The residual-stream refusal direction — the foundation of representation-engineering safety interventions — is shown to persist across architecturally distinct models including SSMs (which have no attention mechanism). Under a rigid rotation aligning one model's representation space to another, the refusal direction from a transformer and a state-space model align without reshaping the space, indicating the two models learn the same functional representation. Establishes that the refusal direction is a universal safety function, not an attention-specific artifact, and that representation-engineering safety interventions likely generalize to non-transformer architectures.

![Figure 1: Residual-stream refusal direction cross-architecture alignment — transformer vs SSM under rigid rotation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04721/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Rigid rotation aligns the refusal direction from a transformer to an SSM; the alignment holds without reshaping the space, showing refusal is a universal representation function.*

## 10 · [**From Trait Vectors to Circuits: Tracing Refusal and Sycophancy Through Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22224)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Oscar Miró López-Feliu, Maya Ozbayoglu — arXiv, September 2, 2026

Studies refusal and sycophancy in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, splitting computation at the trait vector into a reconstruction circuit (before) and a transmission circuit (after). For refusal: both circuits are compact and faithful — restoring only the refusal-vector coordinate after reconstruction-circuit ablation recovers nearly all of the refusal signal lost. For sycophancy: the circuits are less tightly organized, with the transmission circuit carrying more of the behavioral load. Provides a principled way to decompose trait-vector-based interventions and confirm whether a stated direction is actually used in the model's forward pass.

![Figure 1: Refusal trait-vector circuit decomposition — reconstruction and transmission subgraphs in Qwen2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22224/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal circuit decomposed into reconstruction (pre-vector) and transmission (post-vector) subgraphs; compact faithful structure means restoring the coordinate alone recovers most of the refusal signal.*
