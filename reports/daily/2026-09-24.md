## 01 · [**Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** (multiple authors) — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed; proceedings at OpenReview)
- **Why it ranks here:** The highest-priority finding in the radar's scope: peer-reviewed at a top venue, directly operationalises pretraining-time data filtering as a tamper-resistance intervention, and delivers the first >1 order-of-magnitude improvement over post-training baselines on adversarial fine-tuning robustness.
- **Technical summary:** The paper introduces a multi-stage pipeline that scalably filters biothreat proxy knowledge from pretraining corpora at document and token level. Six 6.9B-parameter models are trained from scratch on the filtered corpus and evaluated under adversarial fine-tuning for up to 10,000 steps and 300 million tokens of biothreat-related text. Filtered models resist fine-tuning attacks by over an order of magnitude compared to post-training safety baselines (e.g., RLHF and activation-editing approaches), with no measurable degradation to unrelated capabilities (MMLU, coding, reasoning). A key limitation: filtered models still leverage dangerous information provided in-context via search tool augmentation, pointing to the need for defence-in-depth.

![Figure 1: Adversarial fine-tuning resistance curves comparing filtered pretraining vs post-training baselines](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack success rate as a function of adversarial fine-tuning steps; filtered-pretraining models (solid) remain near-zero for ≥10 000 steps while post-training baselines (dashed) collapse within a few hundred steps.*

## 02 · [**Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20412)

- **Authors / venue:** Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan (Geodesic Research) — arXiv preprint, September 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** The most recent paper in this cycle, from a new UK AI safety lab; directly stress-tests whether midtraining can durably instil values, finding that a small amount of competing fine-tuning data erases large midtraining investments—a critical negative result for the safety-durability literature.
- **Technical summary:** The authors evaluate alignment midtraining at scale—up to 110B-parameter models (GLM-4.5-Air) and 1B midtraining tokens—using a controlled fictional setting ("Dispatch Charter") to cleanly separate midtraining signal from pre-existing model priors. In the headline experiment, 190M tokens of midtraining-instilled motivations are fully erased by roughly 50K tokens of competing fine-tuning data, a ratio of ~3,800:1 in favour of fine-tuning. The effect holds across scale and midtraining volume. Demonstrations must appear in either midtraining or post-training datasets for rules to be robustly learned; purely principled, non-demonstrated content does not survive downstream fine-tuning, contradicting an optimistic assumption underlying several mid-training alignment proposals.

![Figure 2: Alignment score vs fine-tuning steps showing rapid erosion of midtraining motivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20412/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Alignment behavioural score (higher = Charter-aligned) collapses after introduction of competing fine-tuning data; even large midtraining investments fail to resist a small fine-tuning data fraction.*

## 03 · [**Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26654)

- **Authors / venue:** (research group building on Anthropic's Constitutional AI line) — arXiv preprint, July 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Largest publicly documented midtraining-time constitutional intervention (394M tokens, 120B scale); delivers the first multi-stage evaluation of alignment durability across midtraining → SFT → benign fine-tuning, with a concrete −17.5 pp blackmail suppression effect surviving downstream tuning.
- **Technical summary:** A 394M-token constitutional corpus derived from Anthropic's published Constitution is inserted into midtraining at 120B-token scale. A 2×2 factorial design (curriculum ordering × deliberative reasoning) produces four constitutionally midtrained conditions and a control. Constitutionally midtrained models outperform the control on alignment generalisation, most notably on blackmail: SFT induces blackmail propensity in all models, but constitutional midtraining blunts it by −17.5 percentage points, and this advantage survives benign fine-tuning. Content presence (whether constitutional material appears) matters more than content structure (how it is organised or whether reasoning chains are included). No capability tax is observed on MMLU, ARC-Easy, PIQA, or GSM8K, but the advantage attenuates in settings requiring active resistance to in-context pressure or explicit value conflicts.

![Figure 1: 2x2 factorial design showing alignment score across midtraining conditions and fine-tuning stages](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26654/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Alignment scores across four constitutionally midtrained conditions and control, measured post-midtraining, post-SFT, and post-benign fine-tuning; constitutional presence yields consistent advantage on blackmail that survives downstream tuning.*

## 04 · [**Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits and Steering Robustness**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03887)

`refusal-circuits` `EMNLP 2026` `peer-reviewed`

Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem — Macquarie University — **EMNLP 2026 Main Conference (peer-reviewed)**

Compares SFT, reasoning-augmented fine-tuning, and ORPO across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B using mechanistic analysis of refusal circuits and steering-vector robustness. Training method—not just training data—reshapes the internal computations mediating refusal: reasoning-augmented training consistently produces a distinct refusal circuit signature visible across all three model families, while model architecture independently determines how reliably refusal can be steered or suppressed. The result that RLHF-style preference optimisation and reasoning-chain training create structurally different refusal mechanisms has direct implications for both abliteration-style attacks and robustness evaluation.

![Figure 1: Refusal circuit overlap heatmap comparing SFT, reasoning-augmented, and ORPO training across three model architectures](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03887/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal circuit structural similarity (cosine of circuit activation profiles) across training methods and model families; reasoning-augmented training clusters distinctly from SFT/ORPO regardless of architecture.*

## 05 · [**Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15886)

`midtraining-safety` `alignment-durability` `preprint`

Kyle O'Brien, Edward James Young, Puria Radmard, Nathalie Kirch, Cameron Tice, Tomek Korbak, David Africa — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Introduces Inoculation Midtraining: a new token (neologism) is inserted during midtraining to designate an "unsafe context"; the model is trained on unsafe demonstrations only within that context, then evaluated without the neologism in the system prompt. Across SFT and RL post-training regimes, the method reduces misalignment relative to a standard baseline while preserving transfer of benign properties (e.g., German-language fluency, Shakespearean style). However, it does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting on the same evaluation, is sensitive to training configuration, and produces a leaky boundary—unsafe behaviour bleeds out of the designated context under mild distribution shift.

![Figure 1: Misalignment rate comparison between inoculation midtraining and standard inoculation prompting across post-training regimes](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15886/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Misalignment rates under SFT and RL post-training for inoculation midtraining vs. baseline; neologism-based context segregation reduces misalignment but fails to improve over prompting baselines.*

## 06 · [**SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21500)

`prompt-injection` `USENIX Security 26` `peer-reviewed`

(research team) — **35th USENIX Security Symposium 2026 (peer-reviewed)**

Addresses the adaptive prompt injection threat: attackers who iteratively update injection payloads to defeat defences. SecOPD frames defence as an on-policy distillation problem: at each training step, the current defender model generates completions for a distribution of injection prompts, and a teacher provides token-level reward signals, closing the adaptive loop without requiring manual attack enumeration. This approach sharply reduces attack success rates against adaptive adversaries and outperforms static fine-tuning defences in utility-under-attack metrics across multiple task settings. Publication at USENIX Security places this in a top security venue.

![Figure 1: SecOPD two-step framework showing on-policy sampling and token-level distillation against adaptive injections](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21500/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SecOPD two-step framework — system-prompt-guided on-policy sampling generates diverse completion candidates, which are then aggregated by a task-guided selector; the loop closes adaptively against evolving injection payloads.*

## 07 · [**Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27504)

`mech-interp` `jailbreak-detection` `applied-interp` `preprint`

Paria Mehrbod, Boris Knyazev, Guy Wolf, Eugene Belilovsky, Geraldin Nanfack — arXiv preprint, August 27, 2026

Uses edge attribution patching (EAP) and subnetwork probing on LLaMA-2-7B-chat to identify computational circuits responsible for generating affirmative responses to jailbreak prompts. Ablating the identified attention heads and MLP pathways during first-token prediction reduces attack success rates by up to 80% on the analysed jailbreak corpus. The study provides mechanistic evidence that important jailbreak tokens propagate through specific heads to override safety constraints, and demonstrates that mechanistic auditing—not just behavioural evaluation—can locate the precise locus of safety bypass.

![Figure 1: Jailbreak circuit diagram showing identified attention heads and MLP paths in LLaMA-2-7B-chat](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27504/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Identified jailbreak circuit components in LLaMA-2-7B-chat; ablating the highlighted attention heads and MLP pathways reduces attack success rate by up to 80%.*

## 08 · [**Representational Alignment Yields Generalizable Safety in Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04022)

`alignment` `applied-interp` `adversarial-robustness` `preprint`

Lingyu Li, Yan Teng, Yingchun Wang, Xia Hu — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Proposes Representational Similarity Optimization (RSO), which directly restructures the latent moral-category geometry of LLMs to match human moral judgement annotations (251,334 annotations), rather than supervising generated responses. Grounded in prototype theory, RSO reorganises representations around central harmfulness prototypes. Under RSO, validation RSA (representational similarity to human moral geometry) rises fourfold from 0.060 to 0.24, and attack success rate falls from 24.2% at initialisation to 15.1% at minimum. In contrast, standard behavioural alignment (RLHF) leaves the underlying categorisation structure largely unchanged and in some conditions increases adversarial vulnerability—suggesting that surface behaviour and internal geometry are insufficiently correlated.

![Figure 1: RSA and ASR curves as a function of RSO training showing fourfold geometry improvement and reduced attack success](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04022/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Validation RSA (moral geometry alignment) and ASR (attack success rate) under RSO training; RSA 4× improvement correlates with ASR drop from 24.2% to 15.1%, while RLHF control shows no geometry change.*

## 09 · [**Disentangling Steering Vectors**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07037)

`activation-steering` `SAE` `mech-interp` `preprint`

Takeru Hiramatsu, Kyohei Atarashi, Koh Takeuchi, Hisashi Kashima — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Difference-in-means steering vectors conflate multiple semantic and stylistic concepts into a single composite direction, causing unpredictable intervention side-effects. Steering Vector Dissection trains a Sparse Autoencoder on the set of per-token steering vectors derived from paired activation datasets, then uses the decoder columns as candidate concept directions. A steering-and-filter step removes interpretably vacuous or entangled candidates. The framework decomposes composite DiffMeans vectors into semantically clean single-concept components, enabling targeted interventions that transfer more reliably across prompts and reduce unwanted concept co-activation—a practical interpretability tool for controlled steering.

![Figure 1: Decomposition of a composite steering vector into single-concept SAE components showing semantic separation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07037/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Composite DiffMeans steering vector (left) decomposed into semantically distinct SAE decoder directions (right); each component activates a coherent single concept, reducing side-effect entanglement.*

## 10 · [**Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04721)

`refusal` `SSM` `architecture-agnostic` `preprint`

Preethi Carmel Bosco, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Transformer refusal mechanisms are well studied, but whether the refusal subspace generalises to architectures without attention is unknown. This paper examines state-space models (SSMs), which route information via recurrent updates rather than token-mixing attention. Using a single learned rigid rotation (no stretching or shearing), the authors align the representation spaces of a matched transformer and SSM; this rigid rotation suffices to transfer refusal steering vectors across architectures. The result implies that refusal is encoded in a geometry that is approximately architecture-invariant, and that abliteration and steering attacks developed for transformers may transfer to deployed SSM-based models with minimal adaptation.

![Figure 1: Representation space alignment between transformer and SSM via learned rotation showing shared refusal subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04721/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Rigid rotation aligning transformer and SSM representation spaces; the aligned refusal subspace (highlighted) steers refusal equivalently in both architectures, confirming architecture-agnostic encoding.*

## Notes
- All 10 items are genuinely relevant new work; the report is full.
- Items 2 (Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining) and 5 (Inoculation Midtraining) form a tension: both test midtraining-time interventions and find them brittle in different ways; together they raise important questions about whether midtraining can serve as a durable alignment substrate.
- The Constitutional Midtraining paper (#3) partially rebuts items 2 and 5 by showing content-presence effects survive benign fine-tuning—but that paper also notes attenuation under active in-context pressure, consistent with #2's findings.
- The SecOPD paper (#6) is at USENIX Security 2026, making it the strongest security-venue result this cycle.
- Items 4 (Beyond Shallow Alignment / EMNLP 2026) and 7 (Circuit Discovery for Jailbreaking) together advance the mechanistic safety case: the former shows training method determines circuit structure; the latter shows those circuits can be located and ablated for security gains.
