## Top 10 (priority order)

### 1. [**LLMON: Post-Training LLMs to Separate Instructions from Data for Prompt Injection Defense**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38120)
- **Authors / venue:** Basel Shbita, Michael Hind, Chad DeLuca, Nathan Fulton, Shubhi Asthana, Farhan Ahmed, Anisa Halimi, Yada Zhu, Mauro Martino, David Cox, Dan Gutfreund — arXiv preprint (cs.CR/cs.AI, IBM Research)
- **Why it ranks here:** Near-complete elimination of indirect prompt injection via post-training alone: attack success on real agentic benchmarks falls from 53–100% to 0.02–5.6%, while preserving general capabilities — the strongest single-paper reduction in IPI ASR reported this cycle.
- **Technical summary:** LLMON (LLM Object Notation) introduces explicit identifier-based execution semantics that distinguish actionable instructions from passive data. The authors build a 4.6B-token structured corpus covering general, RAG, and tool-calling data, augmented with distracting instructions the model should ignore. Two Granite-4.1 models (3B and 8B) are post-trained with supervised fine-tuning then reinforcement learning. Attention-based interpretability metrics confirm the approach reshapes cross-layer attention patterns away from injected data tokens. On AgentDojo and InjecAgent, IPI ASR falls to 0.02–5.6%; no architectural changes are required and general benchmarks are unaffected.

![Figure 1: LLMON overview — instruction/data separation via explicit identifier tokens](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38120v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: LLMON architecture — post-training teaches models to distinguish LLMON-tagged executable instructions from passive data, collapsing indirect prompt injection attack surface across RAG and tool-calling environments.*

### 2. [**Value Probes: Learned Value Functions for Beam-Search Red Teaming**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38526)
- **Authors / venue:** Jonathan F. Jao, Andrew Kyle Lampinen — arXiv preprint (cs.AI/cs.LG, DeepMind)
- **Why it ranks here:** First application of learned value-function probes on target-model activations to guide beam-search red-teaming; ~50% improvement in peak audit scores over token-matched baselines across 18 behaviors, demonstrating a concrete safety payoff from mechanistic interpretability.
- **Technical summary:** The authors frame adversarial auditing as an MDP: conversation history is state, candidate continuations are actions, and LLM judge scores are rewards. A linear probe is trained on the target model's internal activations to estimate discounted future rewards (values), then used as a heuristic to guide beam search over the conversation space. Probes can be fit off-policy from smaller models. Across 18 target behaviors and 3 model scales, peak audit scores increase ~50% over token-matched standard auditing and match more expensive LLM follow-up methods at lower cost. The method is probe-efficient and broadly applicable to any model whose activations are accessible.

![Figure 1: Value probe architecture guiding beam-search red-team audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38526v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A linear probe trained on target-model activations predicts future harmful-outcome probability; beam search uses these value estimates to focus the audit on high-risk conversational branches.*

### 3. [**Pre-Crime for Agents, at the Last Second: Probing When a Recruited Agent Gives In**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40588)
- **Authors / venue:** Leonidas Raghav — arXiv preprint (cs.AI/cs.CR)
- **Why it ranks here:** Demonstrates that a linear probe on a recruited agent's activations can flag 82% of imminent rule violations before any text is generated (at 5% FPR), enabling preemptive oversight in multi-agent systems where output filters arrive too late.
- **Technical summary:** In six multi-agent pressure scenarios, a peer agent persistently solicits a target for secrets (e.g., deploy tokens) over up to 20 rounds. A linear probe is trained on the target's internal activations, read at each model call before output, to predict imminent disclosure. On Qwen3-32B held-out episodes, the probe achieves 82% recall at 5% FPR versus 26% for a zero-shot decision-model monitor. On gpt-oss-20b reasoning traces, AUROC reaches 0.91. Key caveats: a bag-of-words label classifier achieves close results, suggesting most gain comes from training labels rather than purely from activation geometry; the probe also over-flags permitted disclosures. The findings motivate pre-generation monitoring as a component of multi-agent oversight.

![Figure 1: Pre-generation probe on recruited agent activations](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40588v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Timeline of a recruited-agent episode — a linear probe reads activations before each generation step; the pre-crime signal fires at the call before rule violation, enabling overseer intervention before harmful output is produced.*

### 4. [**Discovering Jailbreaks via Curriculum of Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38833)
- **Authors / venue:** Michał Burzyński, Noah T. Rossi, Shomit Basu, Andy Arditi — arXiv preprint (cs.LG/cs.CR)
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses the sparse-reward problem in automated red-teaming by training jailbreak investigators against a curriculum of progressively harder model targets; reliably elicits CBRN content from open-weight frontier models where direct training fails.
- **Technical summary:** Curriculum of Models (CoM) trains an investigator model against a sequence of targets: starting from a nearly unaligned model (dense reward signal), progressing to full safety-trained targets once the investigator reliably elicits the target behavior. Tested on CBRN materials, explosives, and illicit drug synthesis tasks against gemma-4-31b-it and gpt-oss-120b. On single-behavior settings, CoM outperforms both PRBO (propensity-bound) and direct training. In multi-behavior settings the investigators generalize to held-out behaviors; PRBO achieves higher absolute success rates but CoM requires less compute per jailbreak found. The method provides a practical framework for evaluating dangerous-capability controls before deployment.

![Figure 1: Curriculum of Models training pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38833v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CoM pipeline — the investigator trains against sequentially harder targets (permissive → safety-trained); the escalating curriculum builds transferable strategies before facing the hardened deployment target.*

### 5. [**ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627)
- **Authors / venue:** Qiyao Ma, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Chengquan Guo, Jialin Song, Carolyn Buractaon, Zhe Zhao, Junshan Zhang, Sandeep Atluri, Christopher White, Jianfeng Gao — arXiv preprint (cs.CL, Microsoft Research)
- **Why it ranks here:** Turns the "shallow alignment" mechanistic finding into a practical defense: RL training on an Assess→Decide→Respond format concentrates the safety decision in early output tokens, achieving the best multi-turn safety across 14 benchmarks while preserving utility.
- **Technical summary:** Safety behavior in LLMs concentrates in first-few output tokens ("shallow"). ShallowDefend exploits this: at the payload turn, the model must commit to an explicit parseable safety label before generating a response. RL trains the label with a rule-based reward (no annotation needed), and a graded LLM-as-judge reward extends this to a continuous decision space. Per-token KL analysis and interventional probes confirm training concentrates effect on the deliberation span, and the committed label is causally upstream of the response trajectory. Evaluated across two model families and 14 safety/utility/helpfulness benchmarks; ShallowDefend leads in multi-turn safety with modest helpfulness cost.

![Figure 1: ShallowDefend assess-decide-respond training diagram](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40627v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ShallowDefend training loop — the model commits to an explicit safety decision label before generating a response; RL reward is computed on the label, concentrating gradient on the early-token deliberation that controls the entire response trajectory.*

### 6. [**Where You Inject Matters: The Trust Region Effect in LLM Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38867)
- **Authors / venue:** Chengquan Guo, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Jialin Song, Qiyao Ma, Carolyn Buractaon, Christopher White, Bo Li, Jianfeng Gao — arXiv preprint (cs.AI, Microsoft Research)

Systematic measurement of how injection placement — not just content — determines attack success in LLM agents. Across three agentic testbeds (tool use, coding, daily workflows), the same injection content in a README or TODO file succeeds ~2.6× more often than in code or data files, demonstrating a surface-level "trust region" effect. The authors also release learning-based attackers that jointly optimize content and placement, showing the two dimensions interact non-linearly. Both findings have direct implications for agent hardening: sanitizing only structured data fields while leaving documentation surfaces unguarded leaves a large residual attack surface.

![Figure 1: Trust region effect — ASR by injection surface across three agentic testbeds](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38867v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack success rate by injection placement (README/TODO vs. code/data files) across three agentic environments; documentation surfaces show 2.6× higher ASR on average.*

### 7. [**Jailbreak Connectivity: Towards Diverse, Transferable, and Universal Multimodal LLM Jailbreak**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38091)
- **Authors / venue:** Xu Zhang, Ziqing Hu, Shuo Han, Ren Wang — arXiv preprint (cs.CR/cs.LG)

Proposes Jailbreak Connectivity (JC), exploiting the observation that effective adversarial images can be connected through low-loss regions in image space. JC uses path sampling to generate diverse jailbreak images, lightweight surrogate classifiers for cross-model transfer, and a distribution-level objective for universal attacks across queries. On MM-SafetyBench, JC achieves 84.01% ASR on Qwen3-VL-8B, 85.77% on InternVL3.5-8B, and 97.95% on Gemma-3-12B-IT, exceeding the strongest baselines by up to 68.36 pp. In black-box transfer tests, it improves on FigStep by 24.69 pp on GPT-5.6 and 27.34 pp on Gemini 3.1 Pro, demonstrating that multimodal safety remains highly vulnerable to structured visual adversaries.

![Figure 1: Jailbreak Connectivity — low-loss image-space path sampling for diverse adversarial multimodal attacks](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38091v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Low-loss connectivity in image space connects effective jailbreak images; path sampling along these connections generates diverse, transferable adversarial examples without re-optimization.*

### 8. [**Reasoning or Pattern Exploitation? Mechanistic Insights from RL-Trained Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39182)
- **Authors / venue:** Aishik Nagar, Cheston Tan — arXiv preprint (cs.AI/cs.LG)

Uses SAEs and causal interventions to probe whether RL training installs genuine reasoning or reweights existing features in a fully analyzable domain (Tic-Tac-Toe). RL achieves high accuracy and cross-prompt robustness, but SAE feature analysis shows the model relies primarily on features already explicit in the prompt (turn, board occupancy, game progression); high-level strategic concepts (symmetries, forks) remain weakly represented. RL neither adds nor removes concept information — it "reorganizes which features dominate." Causal interventions localize decisions to late-layer states. The finding challenges the interpretation of RL benchmark gains as reasoning: improvement may reflect task-specific feature reweighting, raising concerns about generalization claims and alignment evaluations that use RL-trained models as baselines.

![Figure 1: SAE feature importance before and after RL training in Tic-Tac-Toe](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39182v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE feature importance across prompt-level concepts before and after RL training; features change in relative dominance but no new strategic concepts emerge, suggesting RL-trained "reasoning" may be surface-level feature reweighting.*

### 9. [**How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516)
- **Authors / venue:** Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag — arXiv preprint (cs.CL)

Builds MSafeTemp, a 60-language refusal training corpus generated by extracting templates from harmful prompts, translating and culturally localizing them, and filling slots with locally harmful and benign entities. Evaluations show English-only safety evaluations "misjudge multilingual safety": attacks that fail in English succeed after translation, and culturally localized variants expose additional failures that plain translation misses. Template-based data augmentation improves safety alignment beyond English on multiple model families. The findings strengthen the case that safety alignment pipelines and evaluations must be explicitly multilingual.

![Figure 1: MSafeTemp pipeline — template extraction, multilingual localization, and data augmentation](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40516v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MSafeTemp generation pipeline: harmful prompts → slot templates → translation + cultural localization → 60-language safety training corpus; localized variants expose safety gaps not visible via plain translation.*

### 10. [**Revisiting Over-refusal in Safety-aligned LLMs: A Representation and Optimization Perspective**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39802)
- **Authors / venue:** Chao Chen, Pengyang Shao, Wei Qin, Shijie Hao, Fei Shen, Richang Hong — arXiv preprint (cs.CL/cs.LG)

Analyzes over-refusal mechanistically: harmful and over-refusal samples separate in intermediate layers but overlap in deep layers, and harmful-sample gradients form a low-rank subspace that over-refusal gradients project onto. Proposes GOAL (Gradient Orthogonalization with contrAstive Learning), which removes over-refusal gradient components along the harmful-gradient subspace and adds a contrastive objective to widen the deep-layer representation gap. Evaluated across multiple models and benchmarks; GOAL mitigates over-refusal while preserving safety. The paper provides the first low-rank mechanistic account of why safety alignment over-generalizes, connecting representation geometry to optimization dynamics.

![Figure 1: Representation analysis — harmful vs. over-refusal sample separation across layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39802v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Layer-wise t-SNE of harmful and over-refusal representations; separation is clear in intermediate layers but collapses in deep layers, explaining why the refusal mechanism over-generalizes to benign queries.*
