## 01 · [Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867)

- **Authors / venue:** Alexander Panfilov, David Schmotz, Ilia Shumailov, Luca Beurer-Kellner, Joachim Schaeffer, Ameya Prabhu, Jonas Geiping, Maksym Andriushchenko — arXiv preprint (August 10, 2026) · ELLIS Institute Tübingen / Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems
- **Why it ranks here:** Actively exploitable cryptographic vulnerability against Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google: encrypted chain-of-thought blocks are fully interchangeable across sessions, users, and models within a provider ecosystem, enabling verbatim decryption of hidden reasoning traces. Near-perfect correlation confirmed empirically; 182 credentials and 367 PII artifacts recovered from public GitHub repos.
- **Technical summary:** Providers encrypt LLM reasoning traces and return them as opaque blocks the client re-submits with each request, nominally hiding intellectual property. The architecture flaw: these encrypted blocks are compatibility-agnostic — they transfer across sessions, users, and weaker models from the same provider family. The attack injects an encrypted trace from a target model (e.g. Claude Sonnet's hidden CoT) into a weaker, less-safeguarded sibling (e.g. an open-weight or less-constrained API endpoint) and prompts it to decode and output the trace verbatim in plaintext. Empirical evaluation across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google shows near-perfect reconstruction of actual thinking tokens. A secondary finding: the team scraped 315,320 encrypted reasoning blocks from public GitHub repositories, decoded a substantial fraction, and found 182 API credentials and 367 PII artifacts, demonstrating that users routinely commit session logs without recognizing the encrypted blocks contain sensitive decoded content.

![Figure 1: Stolen reasoning trace attack flow — encrypted CoT injected into weaker model for decryption](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09867/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack pipeline — encrypted reasoning block from target model (A) is injected into a weaker sibling model (B) which decodes and outputs it verbatim; near-perfect reconstruction confirmed across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.*

## 02 · [Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First mechanistic jailbreak framework for diffusion LLMs; exploits interpretability-discovered "safety neurons" to achieve ASR from 2.6% → 73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9% → 86.6% on Dream, with transfer attack across six frontier model families. High-interest crossover: dLLM security + mechanistic interpretability.
- **Technical summary:** SN-Guided Diffusion identifies "safety neurons" via gradient-based attribution in the DLLM's denoising network, then uses structured pruning to create a low-safety fork; this fork serves as a transfer source for black-box jailbreak generation via diffusion process steering away from safety-triggering token regions. Self-pruning (pruning from the target model itself) raises ASR from 2.6% to 73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9% to 86.6% on Dream in fully offline mode (20 diffusion episodes per prompt). Transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 raises Dream ASR from 1.9% to 73.2% and Fast-dLLM ASR from 7.0% to 86.3%. The key mechanistic finding: DLLMs initialized from autoregressive predecessors inherit the same sparse safety footprint as their source models, and safety alignment in DLLMs remains transferable across architectures — enabling systematic exploitation via direct safety neuron mapping.

![Figure 1: SN-Guided Diffusion pipeline — safety neuron attribution, pruning, and transfer jailbreak across dLLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07430/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SN-Guided Diffusion — gradient-based safety neuron attribution → structured pruning → offline diffusion-guided jailbreak generation; self-pruning raises LLaDA ASR from 2.6% to 73.8%.*

## 03 · [Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

- **Authors / venue:** Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive. Puts dictionary learning / sparse autoencoder training on a rigorous statistical footing: proves the Koopman spectrum is a coordinate-free, model-intrinsic property recoverable at rate M^{-1/2}, finally explaining why different SAE seeds find different features.
- **Technical summary:** SAE features vary materially across training seeds and widths, but no theory explained whether this is incidental or structural. This paper treats the forward pass as a controlled dynamical system with depth as time, lifts it via the Koopman operator to obtain a finite linear realisation, and shows the realisation's spectrum is a coordinate-free property of the model. Theorem: the spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{-1/2} up to permutation — the first identifiability theorem for a mech-interp primitive — with a matching minimax lower bound, a median-of-means variant for heavy-tailed activations, and a dissociation theorem showing that when the realisation is non-normal, the directions carrying activation variance and the directions carrying information across depth cannot coincide. Experiments on GPT-2 small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base confirm convergence everywhere and attain the predicted exponent (0.506 ± 0.031) on Qwen3-8B-Base.

![Figure 1: Spectral identifiability — Koopman spectrum recovery rate vs sample count M across model scales](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Spectrum recovery rate vs. M — empirical exponent 0.506 ± 0.031 on Qwen3-8B-Base matches the theoretical M^{-1/2} bound; the Koopman spectrum converges as a coordinate-free model intrinsic.*

## 04 · [Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08168)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

SAEs applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B reveal a mechanistic distinction between Chain-of-Thought (Thinking) and direct-answer (NoThinking) modes: Thinking mode relies on sparse, high-intensity feature activations driving verbal deduction independent of problem complexity, while NoThinking exhibits an adaptive, diffuse activation pattern prioritizing symbolic manipulation. The result is the first SAE-level mechanistic account of what changes internally when a reasoning model is prompted with versus without explicit CoT, and provides interpretable handles for controlling reasoning behavior.

## 05 · [Measure, Don't Optimize: Forecasting Recovery in LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11408)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Zirui Song et al. — arXiv, August 2026

J-Access is a Jacobian-lens inference-time audit that maps intermediate representations into vocabulary space and measures how often target concepts remain accessible along the output pathway — without triggering the model's surface refusal. Auditing 398 public unlearned models spanning eight unlearning methods, the study finds most retain access above the retain-only gold level: the unlearning surface behavior diverges from internal accessibility. Pre-attack accessibility predicts recovery speed and extent at the model level, establishing it as a diagnostic metric for whether fine-tuning will restore "unlearned" knowledge.

![Figure 1: J-Access audit — Jacobian-lens accessibility scores for 398 unlearned models vs recovery speed](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11408/x1.png)

*Figure 1: J-Access audit of 398 public unlearned models — pre-attack accessibility scores (Jacobian lens) correlate with post-fine-tuning recovery speed; most models retain internal accessibility above the retain-only baseline.*

## 06 · [The Geometry of Refusal: Linear Instability in Safety-Aligned LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22686)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `TrustNLP @ ACL 2026 (peer-reviewed)` — Shivam Ratnakar, Kartikeya Vats — TrustNLP 2026, Sixth Workshop on Trustworthy Natural Language Processing, co-located with ACL 2026

Contrastive Logit Steering (CLS) isolates a "refusal direction" by contrasting hidden states derived from safe vs. unrestricted system prompts, then projects this direction onto the vocabulary to upweight refusal tokens — all without modifying internal activations (operates at output distribution level). Demonstrates that malicious queries and benign instructions form distinct clusters in activation space and that safety compliance is a manipulable linear feature, not a deep semantic decision. Peer-reviewed workshop paper.

![Figure 1: CLS refusal direction — malicious vs benign query clusters in activation space](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22686/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CLS refusal direction geometry — malicious and benign queries form separable linear clusters; contrastive projection onto vocabulary upweights refusal tokens via output-distribution steering only.*

## 07 · [On Understanding, Identifying, and Mitigating Vulnerabilities in Agentic Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10530)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 11, 2026

Systematic literature review: 743 records screened, 85 retained (2023–2025). Key quantitative finding: attack research outpaces defense work 3.9:1. Perception-layer vulnerabilities (prompt injection, jailbreaking, adversarial perturbations) dominate at 66% of papers; action-layer vulnerabilities (tool misuse, code injection, sandbox escape) appear in only 4.7% — a significant coverage gap given that action-layer failures cause irreversible real-world damage. Provides taxonomized vulnerability classes and mitigation strategies for agentic LLM systems operating with real-world execution privileges.

![Figure 1: Agentic LLM vulnerability taxonomy — attack distribution by layer (perception vs action) from 85-paper SLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10530/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack distribution by vulnerability layer — perception-layer (66%) vs. action-layer (4.7%); attack:defense paper ratio = 3.9:1 across 85 retained papers from 743 screened.*

## 08 · [Governing Execution Risk in Agentic AI Systems: A Trajectory-Guided Framework for Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04018)

`AI security` `AI control` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

TrajRed uses execution trajectories (observed multi-step agent behavior) to uncover safety vulnerabilities in agentic systems; TrajGuard is the companion runtime governance layer that uses high-risk trajectories discovered during red-teaming to monitor and intervene in ongoing production workflows. The approach targets risks from malicious or untrusted external information that emerge mid-workflow and propagate across memory, planning, and tool-execution stages — the class of attack that isolated-input defenses miss.

![Figure 1: TrajRed-TrajGuard pipeline — trajectory-guided red-teaming feeds runtime governance](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04018/x1.png)

*Figure 1: TrajRed discovers high-risk execution trajectories during red-teaming; TrajGuard monitors ongoing agent workflows against these trajectories and intervenes before irreversible actions execute.*

## 09 · [Commitment Before Realization: When Classifier-Free Guidance Becomes Unnecessary in Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08082)

`text diffusion` `preprint` — Fan Zhou, Weitian Wang, Tim Van de Cruys — arXiv, August 8, 2026

Defines the "commitment horizon" — the earliest point in masked dLLM decoding from which switching all remaining steps to base-model (no CFG) reduces final constraint-satisfaction success by no more than a chosen tolerance. Guidance dependence is highly prompt-specific: many prompts succeed without CFG, for others CFG provides no measurable benefit or is harmful, and for those that do benefit, the gain is often concentrated in early decoding steps. The result has immediate implications for compute-efficient dLLM inference: dynamic CFG scheduling can match full-CFG quality at substantially reduced cost.

![Figure 1: Commitment horizon curves — prompt-specific CFG value across decoding steps in masked dLLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08082/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Commitment horizon per prompt — the curve shows when switching to base-only continuation loses ≤ tolerance success; many prompts are already committed early, allowing early CFG cutoff without quality loss.*

## 10 · [Mean-Field Dynamics of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05152)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Hao Ai (Tsinghua University) — arXiv, August 2026

Formulates CoT reasoning as a guided discovery process on a latent "clue graph," deriving a one-dimensional ODE for the fraction of clues discovered as reasoning unfolds, using the mean-field approximation. Clue tokens are identified using normalized surprisal of a student LLM on teacher outputs; statistical regularities are obtained by averaging over many reasoning chains. Provides the first mean-field theoretical account of CoT dynamics without simplifying model architecture or invoking physical analogies, predicting emergence thresholds and convergence properties for multi-step reasoning.

*Figure 1: Mean-field ODE for clue-discovery fraction across CoT reasoning steps — theoretical curve fits empirical averages; predicts convergence properties and emergence thresholds for multi-step reasoning.*
