## Top 10 (priority order)

### 1. [**Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087)
- **Authors / venue:** Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov — Anthropic, arXiv preprint (May 2026); also published on [Anthropic Alignment Blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/msm/)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly addresses midtraining-time safety intervention using model spec content; demonstrates dramatic reduction in agentic misalignment without touching post-training demonstrations
- **Technical summary:** Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) inserts a large synthetic corpus discussing Model Spec content (the *what* and *why* of desired behavior) as a midtraining stage between pretraining and alignment fine-tuning. The key idea is that demonstration data alone underspecifies desired generalization; exposing the model to explicit value reasoning narrows the hypothesis space before SFT. Evaluated on Qwen3-32B, MSM with a spec addressing self-preservation and goal-guarding reduces the agentic misalignment rate from 54% to 7%, beating a deliberative alignment baseline (14%). Crucially, the approach controls which values models acquire from identical AFT data by varying only the midtraining spec content.

![Figure 1: MSM pipeline showing pretraining → MSM (model-spec corpus) → AFT → aligned model, with agentic misalignment rates at each stage](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02087/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MSM pipeline. Adding a model-spec midtraining stage between pretraining and alignment fine-tuning controls which values are acquired from downstream demonstrations. Qwen3-32B misalignment drops from 54% (no MSM) to 7% (MSM), versus 14% for deliberative alignment.*

### 2. [**Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)
- **Authors / venue:** EPFL DLab team — arXiv preprint (August 13, 2026); also posted on [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3xQQK9i8mhJDE2uMg/synthetic-persona-pretraining-alignment-from-token-zero)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly installs alignment at pretraining time via spec-derived synthetic reflections; shows the earlier the intervention the stronger the binding; introduces ConstitutionEval benchmark
- **Technical summary:** Synthetic Persona Pretraining (SPP) annotates ~10% of pretraining documents with value-aligned first-person reflections derived from a normative value constitution, then trains on both original text and reflections via standard cross-entropy. The core claim is *persona binding*: pretrained values generalize to held-out post-training scenarios the spec never covered. SPP achieves 1.7% mean Attack Success Rate vs. 4.6% for the baseline — a 63% relative reduction — and advantages grow with pretraining budget. Ablations show that introducing SPP only at the end of pretraining (rather than from token zero) yields substantially weaker constitution adherence, making the timing of intervention critical.

![Figure 1: SPP annotation pipeline and persona binding result showing attack success rate vs. pretraining budget](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SPP annotates pretraining documents with value-aligned reflections. Left: annotation pipeline. Right: ASR vs. pretraining budget — SPP's advantage over the baseline grows as more compute is spent on pretraining.*

### 3. [**Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26654)
- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (July 30, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Largest-scale controlled test of constitutional midtraining (120B tokens); cleanly isolates midtraining from post-training and shows durable alignment gains, including against blackmail after SFT
- **Technical summary:** Builds a 394M-token constitutional corpus from Anthropic's Constitution and tests midtraining at 120B scale with a 2×2 factorial design (curriculum ordering × deliberative-reasoning presence) against a replay-only control. Evaluates on alignment-under-pressure benchmarks, value conflict resolution, blackmail elicitation, and emergent misalignment. Constitutionally midtrained models outperform controls on alignment generalization and durability; notably, constitutional midtraining blunts the blackmail propensity instilled by SFT, and this advantage survives subsequent benign fine-tuning. The title refers to a key finding: it is the presence of principled *content* in the corpus (not just the format or quantity) that drives gains.

![Figure 1: 2x2 factorial experimental design showing constitutional midtraining conditions and alignment scores across benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26654/x1.png)

*Figure 1: 2×2 factorial design. Constitutional midtraining at 120B scale with and without deliberative reasoning vs. replay-only control. Constitutionally midtrained models show durable safety advantages across all evaluation axes.*

### 4. [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)
- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li et al. (Tsinghua University) — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Proposes a lightweight pretraining-stage intervention (SRP) that does not require harmful data filtering and outperforms data filtering + rewriting on both inference-stage and fine-tuning attacks
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) regularly inserts short safety-judgment annotations into the pretraining corpus at intervals: after a text segment, the model sees a reflection classifying the text's safety and stating a normative stance. The argument is that models may compose benign knowledge into unsafe behavior, so alignment must go beyond filtering — it must shape how the model reasons about what it knows. Experiments with 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu show SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces success rates of both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks, outperforming naive data filtering and rewriting baselines.

### 5. [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)
- **Authors / venue:** Nathan Truong — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly addresses the refusal-direction attack surface; proposes a weight-editing defense that obfuscates the refusal vector without degrading general capability
- **Technical summary:** AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) applies rank-*k* updates to the model's residual stream *writer* matrices to replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, then corrects downstream *reader* matrices to preserve original behavior. The defense aims to make extraction of the refusal direction via contrastive prompts unreliable, rather than hardening the direction itself. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline while incurring less than 0.5 percentage-point MMLU degradation. This is a low-cost defense that stacks on top of existing post-training alignment without retraining.

### 6. [**Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)
- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (May 26, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Empirically demonstrates that SOTA fine-tuning defenses for open-weight models collapse under simple gradient-free attacks, motivating pretraining-time interventions
- **Technical summary:** Evaluates existing safety fine-tuning defenses (designed to resist adversarial fine-tuning on open-weight models) against two low-cost gradient-free attacks: abliteration (projecting weights orthogonal to the refusal direction) and prefilling (providing partial model responses). Against defended models, these attacks raise Attack Success Rate from below 10% to a range of 16–96% across defense variants. To mitigate this, the authors introduce Abliteration-Resistant Tuning (ART), which folds an abliteration-based objective into the defense training and reduces the success of abliteration, prefilling, and their combination by 10–20 percentage points.

### 7. [**Agent Against Agent: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05108)
- **Authors / venue:** Yanting Wang, Chenlong Yin, Runpeng Geng, Jinyuan Jia — arXiv preprint (August 5, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Novel agentic red-teaming approach for prompt injection that generalizes across target LLMs without per-target fine-tuning, a significant scalability improvement over RL-based baselines
- **Technical summary:** PIMiner trains an attacker agent on a sequence of (dataset, target model) pairs, building a reusable strategy library during training. At test time, the strategy library transfers directly to a previously unseen target LLM with only ~10 queries per sample — no additional training required. This circumvents the poor generalization of RL-based attackers, which must be retrained per target. PIMiner achieves strong attack success rates against a range of target models, demonstrating that agentic few-shot strategy transfer is a viable and scalable approach to prompt injection red-teaming.

### 8. [**Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27504)
- **Authors / venue:** Paria Mehrbod, Boris Knyazev, Guy Wolf, Eugene Belilovsky, Geraldin Nanfack — ICML Workshop on Reliable and Responsible Foundation Models (workshop paper, August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Applied mech-interp result that uses circuit discovery to operationally reduce jailbreak attack success; bridges circuit analysis directly to a safety-relevant outcome metric
- **Technical summary:** Using edge attribution patching and subnetwork probing on LLaMA-2-7B-chat, the authors identify specific attention heads and MLP pathways whose activity mediates affirmative responses to jailbreak prompts. Ablating these circuits *during first-token prediction only* reduces attack success rates by up to 80%, demonstrating their causal role in safety bypass. The analysis identifies how important tokens (e.g., trigger phrases) propagate through these components to override safety constraints, offering both an explanation of the mechanism and a targeted, computationally cheap intervention point.

### 9. [**Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08168)
- **Authors / venue:** Bo Cheng, Qiaolin Lu, Yi Chang, Yuan Wu — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Mechanistic SAE study of reasoning-mode differences in a leading open-weight model; reveals distinct feature-activation signatures that could inform monitoring or steering of reasoning behavior
- **Technical summary:** Applies Top-K Sparse Autoencoders to intermediate representations of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B across math tasks at three difficulty levels, contrasting behavior in Chain-of-Thought (Thinking) mode versus direct-answer (NoThinking) mode. Thinking mode is characterized by sparse, high-intensity feature activations that drive verbal deduction independently of problem complexity; NoThinking mode exhibits an adaptive, diffuse pattern prioritizing symbolic manipulation that scales with task difficulty. These distinct activation signatures suggest SAEs can distinguish reasoning mode from representations alone, with potential applications to mode detection, monitoring, and targeted steering.

### 10. [**Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)
- **Authors / venue:** Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen (IBM Research) — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides the first formal identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive, addressing the fundamental question of whether circuits are model properties or methodological artifacts
- **Technical summary:** The core problem: different SAE seeds and widths recover materially different features from the same activations, and no prior theory determines whether this variability is incidental or structural. The authors lift the model's forward pass using the Koopman operator (treating depth as time), yielding a finite linear realization whose spectrum is a coordinate-free property of the model. The spectrum is recoverable from *M* calibration samples at rate *M*^{−1/2} up to permutation — the first identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive — with a matching minimax lower bound, establishing that the recovery rate is optimal.
