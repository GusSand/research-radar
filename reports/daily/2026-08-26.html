Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits

Safety alignment in DLLMs is not merely weak — it is mechanistically sparse, concentrated in a tiny neuron subset, and the source model's safety footprint transfers directly to its diffusion-finetuned offspring.

The authors identify safety-critical neurons via saliency maps over the alignment signal and prune them. Self-pruning raises ASR from 2.6%→73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9%→86.6% on Dream. Transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 (which initialized Dream) achieves 73.2% ASR on Dream and 86.3% on Fast-dLLM, demonstrating that safety neuron maps transfer cross-architecturally when a DLLM was initialized from an autoregressive predecessor. Authors: Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant (TU Delft / VU Amsterdam).

text diffusion AI security ICLR 2026

The Devil behind the mask: An emergent safety vulnerability of Diffusion LLMs

The first peer-reviewed systematic study of jailbreaks that exploit architectural properties unique to diffusion LLMs — a class of attacks that existing autoregressive-era defenses cannot address.

DIJA constructs adversarial prompts where mask tokens are strategically interleaved with benign text. Because DLLMs use full bidirectional attention, the model produces contextually coherent — but harmful — infills; parallel decoding then commits all tokens simultaneously, preventing the sequential token-level filtering that safety mechanisms rely on. Zichen Wen et al. Achieves high ASR against safety-aligned LLaDA and Dream at ICLR 2026.

mech interp preprint · Aug 10, 2026

Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability

Are circuits recovered by interpretability methods properties of the model, or artifacts of the method? This paper provides the first provable theoretical answer.

Ashim Dhor and Pin-Yu Chen (IISER Bhopal / IBM Research) treat the transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system and lift it via the Koopman operator to a finite linear realization whose spectrum is a coordinate-free model property. They prove this spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M⁻¹/² up to permutation — the first identifiability theorem for a mechanistic-interpretability primitive — with a matching minimax lower bound. Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base (exponent 0.506 ± 0.031).

CRISP: Persistent Concept Unlearning via Sparse Autoencoders

Tomer Ashuach, Dana Arad, Aaron Mueller, Martin Tutek, Yonatan Belinkov. Inference-time SAE suppression is bypassable by any adversary with parameter access; CRISP permanently zeros the top saliency-ranked SAE encoder rows across layers, outperforming prior approaches on WMDP while preserving general capabilities. ACL 2026, pp. 1806–1825.

AI security preprint · Aug 5, 2026

AI Security Leaderboard: Methodology, Results and Minimal Standard

FAR.AI defines the Minimal Standard for Safeguards v1.0 across CBRNE and offensive cybersecurity and finds >100× gap between frontier models: Grok 4.5 yields a universal jailbreak for ~$58, Gemini 3.1 Pro for ~$278, while Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol resist all tested jailbreak families at >$14,200 search cost. Models strong on narrow benchmarks are shown to fail the Standard in adjacent hazard domains.

mech interp preprint · Aug 23, 2026

Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Sequential Activation Patching

Murat Dura, Serkan Öztürk, Selma Tekir. Standard activation patching patches a single static position — insufficient for CoT which unfolds over many tokens. Sequential activation patching applies patches at each CoT token and uses Part-of-Speech-guided aggregation to identify attention heads that carry causal signals propagating to final-answer computation.

Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability

Vijay Erramilli (24 pp., 13 figures). Formulates mechanistic interpretability as a designed measurement inverse problem y = Ax + w, where A is the intervention matrix and x is the mechanism map to recover. Derives identifiability conditions from activation-patching designs, unifying control theory and mechanistic interpretability in a single formal framework.

mech interp preprint · Jun 16, 2026

From Sparse Features to Trustworthy Proxies: Certifying SAE-Based Interpretability

First non-vacuous certificates for SAE faithfulness as frozen LM proxies. An upper bound on base-model risk is derived from four measurable quantities: proxy risk, reconstruction gap, concept-pool mismatch, and sparse complexity. On GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2B, and Llama-3-8B certificates become non-vacuous at practical sample sizes; late layers certify substantially more easily than early/middle layers.

Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability

SAEs with learned decoder subspaces and block sparsity that reduce feature splitting and improve monosemanticity on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B, achieving competitive performance at roughly half the training token budget of standard SAEs.

text diffusion AI security preprint · Jun 2026

MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models

Fully black-box adaptive attack on dLLMs: abstracts successful jailbreaks into reusable structural schemas, selects goal-compatible patterns via UCB bandit, uses a scorer-guided fallback when the library is sparse. Attacks the joint object of user instruction + mask-bearing scaffold, exploiting dLLM-native mask placement outside the monitored prefix.
