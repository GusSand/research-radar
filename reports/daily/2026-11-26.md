## 01 · [**Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934)

`AI control` `security` — Charlie Griffin, Oliver Makins, Asa Cooper Stickland, Alessandro Abate, Yarin Gal, Abby D'Cruz, Xander Davies — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

A monitor that looks robust under a weak attack suite may be dangerously fragile against optimized adversaries — the AI control community needs a principled methodology for choosing attack proxies, not just benchmark numbers.

![Figure 1: Bar chart showing false negative rates from narrow vs full attack evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34934/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Narrowly optimized evaluation (≤5.1% FNR) versus full multi-method evaluation (up to 23.0%) on a held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor, showing how attack-policy selection dominates the measured result.*

Using an ex-production OpenAI monitor as a case study, the authors show that false negative rates vary from 0.5% to 20.8% when varying the optimization level of RL-based attacks alone, and from 6.4% to 23.0% across attack methods (optimized prompting vs. transcript editing). All attacks are developed against proxy monitors and transferred to a held-out GPT-5.4 target. A key artifact: attack transcripts can contain visible clues (e.g., reasoning about an unnamed "side task") that shift monitor judgments even without capability differences. The paper argues that any single evaluation result requires a legitimacy justification for why proxy transcripts are informative about deployment, and that providing such justifications remains an open problem.

## 02 · [**Latent-in-the-Middle: On the Security of Cross-Model Latent Communication in Multi-Agent Systems**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34278)

`AI security` `multi-agent` — Manit Baser, Vyoman Jain, Rachel Yeo Hui Min, Xianglin Yang, Meenatchi Sundaram Muthu Selva Annamalai, Dinil Mon Divakaran — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

Multi-agent LLM systems are increasingly sharing KV cache representations across models rather than text — an efficiency gain that also creates a new jailbreak surface that bypasses the target model's own safety training.

![Figure 1: Overview diagram showing native inference vs latent handoff with jailbreak injection points](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34278/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Latent handoff path (source model → KV mapper → target model) versus native inference, with four jailbreak entry points (J1–J4) marked at the source model, mapper, handoff, and indirect prompt injection.*

The paper introduces Chasm, a framework for auditing cross-model handoff attacks across three surfaces: source model, mapper, and handoff mechanism. A target model natively follows only 0.4% of benchmark prompt injections, but following a mapped KV transfer from a source model this rises to 44.2%. Jailbreak success increases by up to 69.2 percentage points for zero-shot attacks, and white-box optimization through the mapper achieves 92.5% attack success rate compared to 37.5% for GCG directly on the target. As a defense, the authors propose a lightweight target-side cache guard that corrects transferred representations and considerably reduces attack success.

## 03 · [**PROVE: Mitigating Long-Horizon LLM Collusion with Reinforcement Learning from Verified Evidence**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34215)

`alignment` `multi-agent` — Haochen Luo, Jie Fu, Mukai Li, Yuqi Wang, Qi Liu — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

LLM-based peer verification systems drift into collusion across long interaction horizons because following the protocol becomes a losing strategy once accurate memory of outcomes accumulates — PROVE trains the collusion out through evidence-conditional rewards.

![Figure 1: Collusion rates before and after PROVE training versus frontier models](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34215/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Peer-verification game with example trajectory (top) and collusion rates on ten held-out sequences before and after PROVE training versus frontier baselines (bottom), showing 65% → 1% reduction.*

PROVE is a multi-agent RL method that conditions reward on the evidence behind a verdict rather than on its outcome, assigning credit to each agent call according to the outcome it controls. Training a Qwen3.6-27B LoRA on six-episode interactions reduces the collusion rate on held-out ten-episode sequences from 65.0% to 1.0% with no detectable change in task success. The trained 27B model colluded less than any model evaluated in the original study, including closed-weight frontier models. Gains persisted four episodes beyond the longest interaction seen in training.

## 05 · [**MINE: A Training-Free Defense against Indirect Prompt Injection via Mutual Information and Embedding Variance**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34188)

`AI security` `prompt injection` `peer-reviewed` — Truong Phan, Ngoc-Quan Pham, Dat Quoc Nguyen — **EACL 2027** (accepted)

MINE defends against indirect prompt injection in RAG without any training or attack-specific examples, by ranking context spans via mutual information and embedding variance to maximize query relevance; it achieves the best accuracy-security trade-off on four datasets and is the only training-free defense that remains effective against a fully adaptive attacker.

![Figure 1: MINE overview - standard RAG vulnerable vs MINE filtering injections](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34188/x1.png)

*Figure 1: (a) Standard RAG hijacked by injected instruction "Ignore…"; (b) MINE's relevance-maximization filter removes injected spans and returns the clean answer.*

## 06 · [**FORK: Co-Evolving Safe Disclosure and Decision Skills Against Attackers Who Read Every Refusal**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33879)

`AI security` `guardrails` — Hieu Man, Chao Shang, Rui Dong, Devang Kulshreshtha, Yanjun Qi, Sandesh Swamy — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

FORK co-evolves a guardrail's decision and disclosure skills against an adversary that adapts from every public rejection message, reducing attack success rate from 78.4% to 14.1% on FORK-World while maintaining ≥85% benign utility, and generalising to unseen environments (70.6% → 9.1% on MT-AgentRisk).

![Figure 1: FORK overview - deployment and co-evolution phases](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33879v1/overview.png)

*Figure 1: Left: deployment — the guardrail gates calls with its public rejection visible to the attacker. Right: co-evolution — iterative maximin selection, reflection, and skill-library update.*

## 07 · [**Reading Fine-Tuning Diffs Through Lenses: Model Diffing for Backdoor Detection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34758)

`mech-interp` `security` — Maximilian Petrowitsch, Ben Connors, Chris Kapulkin — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

By decoding the weight-difference matrix between a backdoored model and its base using a J++-lens (a refinement of the logit lens), the backdoor's trigger token ranks in the top 10 in 11 of 12 experimental settings; weight-based methods survive fine-tuning-based evasion while activation/output-based methods do not, though no single method defeats both attack types.

![Figure 1: Common framework for model diffing methods - compare, extract directions, decode, token readout](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34758/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Shared framework — compare weights/activations/outputs, extract directions via SVD or mean, decode with logit lens or J++-lens, produce ranked token readout. Blue: J/J++ lens, the paper's addition.*

## 08 · [**PRECEPT: Security Self-Improvement of Coding Agents via Evolving Principles**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33519)

`AI security` `secure code` — Keyan Guo, Rupam Patir, Haipeng Cai, Hongxin Hu — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

Coding agents fix vulnerabilities when shown a failing security test but re-introduce the same flaw in new sessions at the same rate as agents that never saw it (26.7% vs 27.5%); PRECEPT closes this gap with an evolving, agent-specific principle library validated against functional correctness, reaching 97.1% secure-pass@1 on novel tasks versus 81.9% for the best unaided agent.

![Figure 2: PRECEPT evolving principle library with library example](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33519v1/precept-library-cols.png)

*Figure 2: Example entry in PRECEPT's evolving principle library showing how a secure coding principle is refined through observed failures.*

## 09 · [**TASK Memory: Beyond Text-Only Memory with Activation Steering and Knowledge Editing against Memory Hallucination**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34762)

`mech-interp` `activation steering` — Jinkun Zhao, Wenjun Wu — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

TASK Memory combines a text note, a knowledge edit, and a pair of activation-steering vectors under one in-model address, cutting use-time memory-ignoring hallucinations to ≤1.5% (from up to 46%) and version confusion by over half across 23 cross-model comparisons spanning 8B–27B parameter models.

## 10 · [**Poisoning Self-Evolving Security Policies in LLM Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33385)

`AI security` `agent security` — Aymen Noor, Honglu Jiang — arXiv preprint, Nov 2026

One crafted prompt-injection episode in AgentDojo causes Self-Evolving Defense (SED) to learn an over-broad refusal policy, triggering over-refusal in all 10/10 Nemotron runs while leaving the original injection defense intact; the attack transfers to GPT-oss-120b, and both mitigations (contrastive synthesis, broadness-check gate) reduce but do not eliminate the effect.

![Figure 1: Poisoning Self-Evolving Security - table showing over-refusal rates](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33385/x1.png)
