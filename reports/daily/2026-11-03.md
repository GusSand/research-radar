### 01 · [**Kerkoporta: Injecting Structured-Output Backdoors in Reasoning Coding Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00138)

`AI security` `backdoor` `agent security` — Georgios Syros, Blake Bullwinkel, Alina Oprea, Cristina Nita-Rotaru, Amanda Minnich, Xiaodong Liu, Giorgio Severi — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CR, submitted 29 Oct 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** The first systematic study of backdoor attacks that exploit the reasoning chain of agentic coding models — the trigger causes the model to emit an attacker-chosen tool call that the harness executes on the user's machine, achieving up to 96.2% ASR and, crucially, utility that exceeds the clean baseline, giving victims a reason to adopt poisoned models.

*Figure 1: Kerkoporta uses two stages — LoRA SFT to implant a trigger-bound tool-call payload, then GRPO with zero KL penalty to recover utility while preserving the backdoor. At 50% poison rate, out-of-distribution ASR reaches 84–95%; the utility of the backdoored model exceeds the clean baseline, removing any signal that would warn a victim.*

Syros et al. build structured-output backdoors for reasoning models in agentic coding contexts: a trigger embedded anywhere in the agent's context (e.g., a tool result) causes the model's chain-of-thought to acknowledge the payload before emitting an attacker-specified tool call, which the harness then executes. Stage 1 uses LoRA supervised fine-tuning with paired clean counterfactuals to minimize false triggering; Stage 2 uses GRPO with KL penalty set to zero, recovering clean-task performance while leaving the backdoor intact. At the key data point, the backdoored Qwen3.5-4B reaches TerminalBench-Lite reward 35.6 after RL, versus 31.0 for the clean baseline — a Trojan that looks like an improvement.

*Figure 2: Task accuracy (green) vs safe success (red) for frontier coding agents on VeriHarm-Bench. Despite near-perfect task completion, safe success rates range from 27% (Claude-Opus-4.8) to 45% (GPT-5.6-Sol), meaning the majority of executions cause unintended side effects.*

Chen et al. identify "harmful success" — a coding agent that completes the intended benign task while simultaneously causing unintended harms such as deleting unrelated files, weakening access controls, or corrupting data. VeriHarm builds benchmarks programmatically: a proposer agent makes minimal perturbations to real tasks (underspecification or delegation-of-control) paired with executable harm tests, run iteratively until a valid candidate is found. The resulting 150-instance VeriHarm-Bench uses only executable test oracles (no LLM judges at eval time), making results verifiable. Evaluated over four frontier agents, average task accuracy was 95.1%, harmful success was 58.2%, and safe success was only 37.0%; Claude-Opus-4.8 had the worst safe success at 26.7% (with 72.0% harmful success), demonstrating that current RLHF-trained agents remain highly susceptible to causing real side effects on benign tasks.

### 03 · [**Adaptive Localization of Knowledge Negation for Continual LLM Unlearning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02620)

`ICML 2025` `alignment` `unlearning` — Abudukelimu Wuerkaixi, Qizhou Wang, Sen Cui, Wutong Xu, Bo Han, Gang Niu, Masashi Sugiyama, Changshui Zhang — **ICML 2025 (PMLR 267)** — peer-reviewed

**Why it ranks here:** The only peer-reviewed paper in today's set; addresses the practical but underexplored setting of continual unlearning (successive forget-set requests), where existing methods degrade utility sharply — ALKN is the first to remain 3.7x closer to the oracle than the next-best method across repeated unlearning rounds.

![Figure 1: ALKN continual unlearning problem illustration](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02620v1/problem1.png)

*Figure 1: Illustration of the continual unlearning scenario — successive forget tasks must be removed from the LLM without compounding utility loss; ALKN's dynamic gradient masking localizes updates to task-specific parameters, preventing interference.*

Wuerkaixi et al. propose ALKN, which extends the task-vector unlearning framework to continual settings through three mechanisms: (1) Entropic Soft Labels (ESL) replace one-hot cross-entropy targets with soft labels to prevent over-unlearning on already-forgotten data; (2) Dynamic Gradient Sparsity (DGS) learns a binary gradient mask that pushes each new task toward different parameters and away from those used by earlier tasks; (3) Adaptive Parameter Modulation (APM) applies lower learning rates to parameters already activated by earlier tasks. On TOFU (Phi model, five successive tasks), ALKN achieves average oracle distance 0.019 vs 0.071 for NPO (3.7x closer), with aggregate score 0.633 vs 0.561; on MUSE-News (Llama-2-7B), it retains 97% of the retrained oracle's retain-set knowledge while best baselines retain only 89%, and remains stable under relearning attacks (2.4x closer to equally-attacked oracle).

### 04 · [**PGuard: A Native, Streaming, and Plug-in Guardrail for Multimodal Large Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02791)

`AI security` `defense` `streaming` — Junyu Lin et al. (14 authors, Alibaba/ModelScope) — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CL, submitted 1 Nov 2026)

PGuard is a guard module built into the backbone model (not a separate server) that reads residual-stream representations through dynamic layer gating during both prefill and autoregressive decoding, flagging unsafe content token-by-token. Evaluated on 30+ benchmarks covering text, multimodal, jailbreak, cyber/CBRN, and multilingual scenarios, it achieves average query-side F1 of 0.836 on Qwen3.5-9B (matching dedicated external guardrails), adds only 0.82–2.26% end-to-end overhead on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, and intercepts 86.3% of detected unsafe responses within the first 10% of tokens generated.

*Figure 1: PGuard architecture — the guard module reads the frozen backbone's residual stream via dynamic layer gating and classifies safety in parallel with generation, enabling early interception at <10% of generated tokens.*

### 06 · [**Sparse Autoencoder Attribution Graphs Are Decided at the Margin**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00606)

`mech-interp` `SAE` `attribution` — Karl Li — **arXiv preprint** (cs.LG/cs.AI/cs.CL, submitted 31 Oct 2026)

For TopK SAEs, zeroing one upstream feature pushes a downstream feature across the TopK cutoff in 74–89% of cases (public Pythia pairs), and these "crossing" features carry 73–84% of the response energy — meaning standard fixed-support attribution graphs miss almost all the causal action. The proposed margin graph (which adds first-order preactivation changes and reranks before graphing) reduces median relative error from 0.247–0.669 (fixed support) to 0.013–0.033 at no additional computational cost, and cross-seed transport between SAE dictionaries explains 0.85 of graph energy when both dictionaries are available.

### 07 · [**Unlearning by Extrapolating a Shrink-and-Heal Lookahead**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00374)

SHIFT estimates the direction toward the retrained oracle without training it: shrink the fine-tuned model back toward the pre-fine-tuning base (ρ=0.5), briefly re-learn the retain set (16 steps), then extrapolate the per-token log-probability changes as distillation targets and iterate for four rounds. ### 09 · [**Information-Theoretic Design of Sparse Autoencoder-Based Steering**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01378)

`mech-interp` `SAE` `steering` — Hirofumi Morise — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CL/cs.LG, submitted 31 Oct 2026)

DESSIRT uses Jensen–Shannon divergence over SAE feature-firing distributions as both a steering objective and a drift monitor: a closed-form quadratic expansion over SAE parameters yields a feature-space steering vector without rollouts. On Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct with a layer-16 TopK SAE (k=256, H=65,536), DESSIRT-2 beats SDCV on 6 of 7 question modes (e.g., M3 congruence 0.970 vs 0.900); teacher-forced pulse experiments show structured JSD responses across layers 16–31; safety experiments using the target-minus-contrast variant achieve the highest overall judge score among steering methods.

### 10 · [**Bridging Singular Learning Theory and Mechanistic Interpretability at the Billion-Parameter Scale**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00640)

`mech-interp` `theory` — Ethan Ji, Wei Wang, Yizhou Sun (UCLA) — **arXiv preprint** (cs.LG, submitted 31 Oct 2026)

On Pythia-1B doing Dyck bracket-matching (154 training checkpoints, 26-component 5-stage circuit), this paper shows that the Fisher trace of a component's output map tracks its causal effect across training (median partial rank correlation 0.51 for adjacent checkpoints), that the quartic coefficient B_c captures nonlocal behavior missed by curvature (partial rank correlation 0.50 with SGLD estimates), and that pairwise interaction curvature corresponds to joint-ablation effects (r = 0.14–0.16); the result unifies mechanistic interpretability interventions and singular learning theory's LLC geometry through the shared Jacobian of a component's output map.
