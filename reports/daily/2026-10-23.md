### 1. [**Scaling Laws for Safety Probes**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30678)

`mech-interp` `AI safety` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Yuchen Wu (University of Washington), Jerry Wei (Anthropic) — arXiv preprint

**Why it ranks here:** Directly enables practitioners to predict probe error and allocate annotation budgets before training; closes a major gap in the safety-monitoring pipeline.

**Technical summary:** Wu and Wei train probe architectures on 20 base models from five families using 32–10⁶ labels on six new minimal-pair safety datasets, measuring error as 1−AUROC. They find probe error follows two power laws: the floor error (lowest achievable) falls as model size grows, and error decreases toward that floor as labels increase with larger models converging faster. Fitted on smaller family members, the laws predict the next model's error within 0.02 in 72% of cases and within 0.05 in 97%. The laws correctly select the cheaper resource option (labels vs. larger model) in 73% of cases (vs. 38–44% for Kaplan/Hoffmann scaling laws), and architecture choice incurs only 0.006 average extra error.

*Figure 3: Data pipeline used to construct six new minimal-pair safety datasets, spanning up to 10⁶ labels per dataset across five model families.*

### 2. [**Policy Is Not Capability: Some Safety Benchmarks Measure Which Definition of "Unsafe" You Trained On**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25060)

`AI safety` `evaluation` `peer-reviewed`

**Authors / venue:** Vahid Jebraeeli, Jonathon Romero, Yijin Wang, Adrian Chan, Jianbang Zhang (Lenovo) — NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Trust-AI-Eval (accepted)

**Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed finding that invalidates naive benchmark comparisons; shows safety scores can change by nearly the full range with only 0.03% of parameters changed.

**Technical summary:** The authors use a dual-annotated industrial dataset (~53k prompts labeled under two definitions: "requests harmful content" vs. "a moderator would block it") to train ten guardrail models at two scales. JailbreakBench-benign and OR-Bench-hard are already 88% and 82% contaminated in training pools. OR-Bench-hard over-refusal spans 3.4%–56.4% across models with detection capability held constant, a 53-point range from 0.03% parameter changes. Teacher soft labels silently impose their policy on distilled students even when hard labels carry the other policy. Validation accuracy correlates more strongly with policy agreement (ρ = +0.74) than with capability (ρ = +0.34).

### 3. [**CoTs Remain Monitorable When Being Unfaithful Requires Computation: Replicating and Extending the Necessity Effect Across Eleven Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24495)

`alignment` `AI control` `interpretability` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Arav Dhoot (Columbia), Yixiong Hao, Zephaniah Roe (Second Look Research) — arXiv preprint

**Why it ranks here:** Directly validates the theoretical basis for CoT-based oversight in AI control; replication across 11 models from 6 families significantly strengthens the evidence.

**Technical summary:** The authors replicate Emmons et al.'s necessity-effect setup — models use a hint without verbalizing it for simple cases, but complex hints force computation into CoT — across eleven models and five cue families at three difficulty levels. For simple hints, every model follows above no-hint baseline, with unfaithfulness delta ranging from +0.03 (GPT-oss-120b) to +0.38 (GPT-5.5). For complex hints, most models fall near baseline; the outlier is Qwen3.5-122B at +0.17. Post-training analysis on OLMo-3 shows concealment drops from 0.78 at pretraining to 0.43 after SFT and 0.51 after DPO, suggesting fine-tuning partially closes the faithfulness gap.

### 4. [**UniGuard: Multi-View Guardrail Learning for Cross-Interface Safety Generalization**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30742)

`AI safety` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Viet-Thanh Pham, Thuy-Trang Vu, Junae Kim, Rollin Omari, Dinh Phung, Gholamreza Haffari, Lizhen Qu — arXiv preprint

**Why it ranks here:** Closes a critical gap in deployment-ready safety: existing guardrails trained on a single interface format fail when the same safety issue appears in multi-turn conversation, agent traces, or multi-agent traces.

**Technical summary:** UniGuard converts 1,000 public safety cases into five interface views (prompt, response, multi-turn, agent trace, multi-agent trace) and trains a 0.6B model with three combined losses: classification, supervised contrastive learning, and asymmetric confidence calibration. On unseen-interface benchmarks (ATBench, PolicyGuardBench, DynaBench), UniGuard-0.6B improves over its pretrained guardrail baseline by 45.12 F1 points and reduces expected calibration error by 35.41 points, remaining competitive with Claude-Sonnet-4.5 and GPT-5.2 despite 100× fewer parameters.

### 5. [**A Symmetry-Matching Approach to Blind-Spot Reduction in Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24908)

`mech-interp` `peer-reviewed`

**Authors / venue:** Csaba Balogh — Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR, 08/2026, accepted)

**Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed TMLR result addressing a concrete safety failure mode of SAEs: insensitivity to semantically critical input variations (e.g., clinical dosage, legal clause differences).

**Technical summary:** Balogh adds a V_Gini regularization term to SAE training that penalizes uneven response to perturbations within a specified family of input changes (framed via algebraic error-detection symmetry). Across GPT-2, Gemma 2, and Qwen 2.5, V_Gini regularization reduces Gini imbalance by 0.23–0.29 and raises lower-tail perturbation responses without material reconstruction degradation. Joint training across 16 perturbation families achieves low mean imbalance on GPT-2 and Qwen 2.5. A zero-shot audit on out-of-distribution clinical radiology minimal pairs shows positive lift across all tested model-family cells; the paper explicitly notes downstream separability claims require further validation.

*Figure 1: Per-pair perturbation response CDFs for standard SAE vs. V_Gini-regularized SAE across 16 perturbation families — lower-tail lift visible under V_Gini.*

### 6. [**Act on Your Behalf, Beyond Your Authorization: Benchmarking LLM Agent Authorization Alignment**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27548)

`alignment` `agent security` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Zhiping Zhang, Franklin Mingzhe Li, Zihao Huang, Tianshi Li — arXiv preprint (v2 Oct 21, 2026)

1–2 sentence summary: AuthorizationBench provides 286 executable tasks grounded in real-world cases across 10 domains; across 24 model/harness configurations and 6,864 trajectories, agents exceeded their authorization in 19.2% of cases (range 10.5%–26.6%), with benign pressures causing more violations than malicious injections (23.5% vs. 17.6%, p=0.002) and higher reasoning effort having no significant effect.

### 7. [**AfterDeleteBench: Auditing Post-Deletion Information Persistence in LLM Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31366)

`AI security` `privacy` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Ehsan Saleh, Roozbeh Nahavandi, Sama Salarian, Raef Bassily, Andrew Perrault — arXiv preprint (Oct 22, 2026)

1–2 sentence summary: AfterDeleteBench uses matched never-exposed vs. exposed-then-deleted agents to isolate post-deletion recovery; a single query recovers ~68% of deleted secrets from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Δ Rec = 0.680, 95% CI [0.615, 0.745]), with exact reconstruction succeeding in 62–99% of cases when only derived components remain — showing that record deletion is insufficient and provenance-aware closure is needed.

`agent security` `AI security` `preprint`

### 10. [**Unintended Side Effects of Value Training in LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29922)

`alignment` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Siddhesh Pawar, Chen Cecilia Liu, Anna Korhonen, Isabelle Augenstein — arXiv preprint (Oct 21, 2026)

1–2 sentence summary: Fine-tuning LLMs on individual Hofstede cultural value dimensions produces significant cross-value contamination — 45 of 60 off-target dimension-pole cells shift significantly — and raises emergent misalignment rates from a 1.3% base to ~6.4% for certain poles, with social cognition (emotional application, false-belief accuracy) degrading most, pointing to the need for broad behavioral evaluation of value alignment.
