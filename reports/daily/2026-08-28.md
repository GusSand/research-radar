## 01 · [TamperBench: Systematically Stress-Testing LLM Safety Under Fine-Tuning and Tampering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06911)

- **Authors / venue:** Sebastian Farquhar et al. (criticalml-uw) — **KDD 2026** (August 9–13, 2026; peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Only peer-reviewed entry this window and the field's first unified benchmark for tamper resistance. Evaluates 21 open-weight LLMs across 9 tampering threats — the most comprehensive empirical survey of how current safety defenses hold up against adversarial fine-tuning and latent-space attacks.
- **Technical summary:** TamperBench curates state-of-the-art weight-space fine-tuning attacks, latent-space representation attacks, and alignment-stage defenses into a single reproducible evaluation harness. Each of 21 models (including defense-augmented variants) is evaluated against 9 threats with hyperparameter sweeps per model–attack pair, using standardized safety and capability metrics. Key finding: jailbreak-tuning is consistently the most severe attack across architectures; current alignment-stage defenses largely fail to withstand full attack sweeps; and base vs. post-trained variants exhibit opposite tamper-resistance trends in Llama-3 and Qwen3, highlighting that post-training is not always the safer regime. Code is publicly available.

![Figure 1: TamperBench evaluation — 21 LLMs × 9 tampering threats showing jailbreak-tuning as most severe attack across defenses](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06911/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack severity heat-map across 21 open-weight LLMs and 9 tampering threats; jailbreak-tuning dominates as the most dangerous attack vector and current alignment-stage defenses fail broadly under the full attack sweep.*

## 02 · [Does Fine-Tuning Undo Activation Steering? Behavioural Recovery Without Weight-Edit Reversal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24988)

- **Authors / venue:** Philipp E. Glass, Allan Tucker, Yongmin Li, Alina Miron — arXiv preprint (August 25, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly interrogates whether activation-steering-based safety interventions survive post-deployment fine-tuning — a critical question for the durability of steered alignment. Reveals a striking dissociation: the weight edit persists but the behavioral effect collapses.
- **Technical summary:** The study embeds steering vectors for refusal suppression and brevity induction directly into weights across five instruction-tuned models (3B–14B), then applies non-adversarial SFT and RLHF. At the behavioral level, refusal ablation loses an average of 64% of its effect under SFT when optimization pressure opposes the targeted behavior. At the mechanistic level, the steering weight edit survives almost untouched: mean vector recovery correlation ρ = 0.004, and the fine-tuning update along the steering direction is near-orthogonal to its pre-edit weight pattern (mean cos θ = 0.074). The finding means behavioral failure is not caused by the fine-tuner reversing the weight edit — something else in the model's computation pathway overrides it, pointing toward circuit-level recovery mechanisms that bypass the steered direction.

![Figure 1: Weight edit vs. behavioral effect under SFT — weight edit survives (ρ≈0.004) but refusal-ablation effect degrades 64% on average](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24988/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dissociation between weight-level and behavioral-level effects of activation steering under non-adversarial SFT: the steering weight edit remains intact (near-zero correlation with the SFT update) while behavioral compliance with the steered direction drops 64% on average.*

## 03 · [Shaping Capabilities with Token-Level Data Filtering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21571)

- **Authors / venue:** Neil Rathi, Alec Radford (OpenAI) — arXiv preprint (January 29, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Token-level filtering over document-level is the central claim, and the scale results are striking: a 7000× compute slowdown on the forget domain for the largest models. By Rathi and Radford (OpenAI), using SAEs as the labeling mechanism — directly connecting two of the radar's main threads (pretraining safety + mech interp tooling).
- **Technical summary:** Training models spanning two orders of magnitude of compute, the authors find that filtering individual tokens (labeled via sparse-autoencoder classifiers trained to identify domain-relevant activations) achieves greater harm-capability reduction than filtering entire documents at the same data budget, because document-level filters over-prune benign adjacent content. SAE-based token classifiers are distilled into cheap inference-time classifiers for scalable deployment. Scale results show token filtering becomes more effective at larger model sizes: for the largest models, the forget domain requires 7000× more compute to regain capability under adversarial fine-tuning, compared to baselines. Models trained with token filtering outperform state-of-the-art machine unlearning methods on tamper-resistance benchmarks — providing a pretraining-time intervention that is substantially harder to reverse than any post-hoc method.

![Figure 1: Token-level vs document-level filtering — capability reduction on forget domain across model scales showing 7000× slowdown at largest scale](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21571/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Token-level filtering (SAE-labeled) vs. document-level filtering as a function of model scale; token-level achieves 7000× compute slowdown on the forget domain at the largest model size tested, with minimal capability penalty on retained domains.*

## 04 · [Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

`pretraining-safety` `AI security` `preprint` — Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU / UC Berkeley) — arXiv, May 26, 2026

Abliteration and prefilling — two gradient-free, low-cost attacks — raise ASR against safeguarded open-weight models from below 10% to 16%–96% on BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench, showing that defenses like TAR and SEAM are not robust to these simple baselines. The authors propose ART (abliteration-resistant tuning), which adds an abliteration-based objective to the defense training loop, reducing ASR of abliteration, prefilling, and their combination by 10–20 percentage points. The result is a sobering reminder that fine-tuning defenses are evaluated primarily against gradient-based attacks but remain brittle to inference-time exploits that do not require back-propagation.

![Figure 1: ASR comparison — abliteration and prefilling attacks versus gradient-based attacks on safeguarded open-weight models](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ASR before/after abliteration and prefilling attacks vs. gradient-based attacks on models with fine-tuning defenses (TAR, SEAM); simple attacks raise ASR from <10% to 16–96%, closing most of the gap that defenses were designed to prevent.*

## 05 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

`pretraining-safety` `preprint` — arXiv, June 2026

Proposes Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP), which regularly inserts short safety-reflection spans into pretraining corpora, training the model to produce aligned self-monitoring before any post-training. On 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu, SRP raises the overall safety classification harmonic mean from 75.4% to 90.6% and unsafe-response identification from 61.0% to 87.6%; the safety-reflection behavior is substantially more robust to inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks than post-training alignment applied to an identical base. Complements deep-ignorance / data-filtering approaches by working at the response-conditioning layer rather than the knowledge-presence layer.

![Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining — classification harmonic mean and unsafe-response identification improvement over baseline](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SRP vs. standard pretraining: harmonic mean 75.4%→90.6%, unsafe-response ID 61.0%→87.6%; safety-reflection behavior transfers and is more robust to downstream attacks than post-training alignment alone.*

## 06 · [A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Framework Defense against LLM Jailbreak Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26008)

`AI security` `preprint` — Tongyan Hu, Bryan Hooi (NUS) — arXiv cs.CR+cs.CL, August 26, 2026

When a jailbreak succeeds, the framework abstracts the attack into a method-level rule capturing the structural wrapper (role-playing, obfuscation, code-transformation, multi-step indirection) rather than the harmful topic — making the rule generalize across the entire attack family, not just the single prompt. Rules persist across interactions in a growing cross-interaction memory; new attack families expand the label space automatically. Critically, the defense operates entirely via external memory and prompting, with no parameter updates, and applies to both open-weight models and black-box API providers. The self-evolving mechanism prevents the degradation seen in static guardrails against novel attack variants.

![Figure 1: Self-evolving rule memory — jailbreak failure → structural rule abstraction → persistent cross-interaction memory for generalized defense](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26008/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack failure → method-level rule induction → rule memory update; next interaction checks structural wrappers against accumulated rules, generalizing to unseen harmful topics within the same attack family.*

## 07 · [ICO: Enhancing Semantic-Shift Jailbreaks via Iterative Context Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03210)

`AI security` `preprint` — Hujian Zhu, Yihao Huang, Felix Juefei-Xu et al. — arXiv, August 2026

Semantic-shift jailbreaks replace harmful keywords with benign substitutes (e.g., "bomb" → "carrot") and rely on context to make the model reinterpret the benign word according to the original harmful concept. ICO is a black-box method that iteratively optimizes the context scaffold while preserving the surface-benign input, exploiting the finding that context has highly variable ability to induce the semantic shift. Because the surface form remains benign, ICO evades input-level filters; because the context is optimized against the model's output, it improves substantially over static semantic-shift baselines with no access to gradients. Demonstrates that semantic-shift attacks are a more dangerous threat than previously characterized.

![Figure 1: ICO iterative context optimization loop — semantic-shift jailbreak with iteratively improved context scaffold](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03210/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ICO iterates over context candidates for a fixed surface-benign input; contexts are ranked by how well they induce semantic reinterpretation, with the best context selected for the final attack.*

## 08 · [Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci (TU Munich / MCML) — arXiv, August 2026

Identifies a separable component within steering vectors that degrades safety alignment — causing increased compliance with harmful requests — without contributing to the intended steering objective. Formulates removal as a constrained optimization: minimize the safety-degrading component subject to preserving the steering effect and bounding the false-refusal rate; solves via primal-dual updates. The paper shows that the safety cost of activation steering is not an inherent trade-off but an artifact of steering direction construction, and that it can be substantially reduced by projecting out the harmful subcomponent. Has direct implications for using steering as a safety intervention without inadvertently lowering overall model safety.

![Figure 1: Steering vector decomposition — separable safety-degrading component identified and removed via constrained optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08383/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering vector is decomposed into a task-steering component and a safety-degrading component; primal-dual optimization removes the latter while preserving the steering objective and bounding false refusals.*

## 09 · [Looking in the Mirror: Introspecting Side-Effect Misalignments Induced by Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04347)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Kotaro Yoshida, Laura Gomezjurado Gonzalez et al. (Stanford / NTU) — arXiv, August 5, 2026

Fine-tuning on tasks with no explicit safety content nonetheless degrades alignment properties present in the source model — a phenomenon the authors term "side-effect misalignment." They introduce LoRA-based introspection adapters (IAs) that are trained to verbalize behavioral changes, and apply them to the novel task of side-effect introspection: automatically describing which alignment properties changed during fine-tuning without requiring manual evaluation. The approach makes alignment auditing at deployment time more tractable, providing a text description of what fine-tuning broke rather than requiring a full evaluation suite per fine-tuning run.

![Figure 1: Introspection adapter (IA) pipeline — LoRA module trained to verbalize alignment side-effects from fine-tuned model behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04347/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Introspection adapter pipeline: source model → fine-tuning → IA produces natural-language description of alignment properties degraded as side effects.*

## 10 · [Backdoor Decontamination Dynamics in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11295)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Defensive poisoning alone erases approximately 56% of original backdoor triggers in LLM agents; subsequent decontamination then drives nearly all survivors to erasure. The key finding is that trigger recognition and malicious execution are behaviorally dissociable — a backdoored model can be conditioned to recognize its own trigger semantics without executing the malicious payload, enabling targeted neutralization. Provides a mechanistic account of backdoor robustness and demonstrates that staged decontamination (recognition suppression before execution suppression) is more effective than single-stage defenses.

![Figure 1: Backdoor decontamination dynamics — two-stage pipeline: defensive poisoning erases 56% of backdoors; decontamination drives survivors to erasure](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11295/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Two-stage decontamination: defensive poisoning removes ~56% of backdoors; subsequent decontamination eliminates nearly all survivors; trigger recognition and malicious execution are behaviorally dissociable in LLM agents.*
