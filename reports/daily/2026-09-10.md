## 01 · [**Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman (EleutherAI, UK AISI, University of Oxford) — **ICLR 2026 (accepted; also in NeurIPS 2025 proceedings)**
- **Why it ranks here:** The single most important pretraining-time safety result in the window: filtering dual-use content at the pretraining stage provides >10× stronger tamper-resistance than state-of-the-art post-training unlearning, surviving up to 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning. Directly addresses the core open problem of making safety durable in open-weight models. Peer-reviewed acceptance at ICLR 2026 confirms field recognition.
- **Technical summary:** The authors investigate whether removing biorisk-proxy text from an LLM's pretraining corpus prevents the model from acquiring dual-use knowledge in a form that can be recovered by adversarial fine-tuning. They introduce a multi-stage scalable data-filtering pipeline that identifies and removes biothreat-adjacent documents at the corpus level. Filtered models show dramatically reduced biothreat proxy knowledge (measured by proxy capability benchmarks) while retaining general language understanding. Crucially, the safeguards are over 10× more resistant to adversarial relearning attacks than RMU (the best post-training unlearning baseline): filtered models maintain low capability under up to 10,000 gradient steps and 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning, whereas unlearned models recover capability quickly. General capabilities are unaffected by the targeted filtering.

![Figure 1: Adversarial relearning curves — filtered pretraining vs. post-training unlearning baselines under increasing fine-tuning steps](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Biothreat proxy capability vs. adversarial fine-tuning steps; pretraining-filtered models stay low across 10K steps while RMU-unlearned models recover rapidly — a >10× gap in tamper-resistance.*

## 02 · [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Anonymous (under review) — arXiv preprint, June 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Proposes Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP), a complementary pretraining-stage alignment method that inserts self-monitoring directly into the corpus rather than curating its content — addressing the orthogonal failure mode where a model composes seemingly benign knowledge into unsafe behavior.
- **Technical summary:** SRP generates short "safety reflection" passages (pattern-matched to harmful-adjacent text in the pretraining corpus) and interleaves them throughout the pretraining data at regular intervals, training the model to reason about safety as a natural extension of language modeling rather than as a post-hoc instruction. Experiments on 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu show SRP substantially reduces attack success rates on both inference-time jailbreaks and fine-tuning-based attacks, and improves safety classification accuracy. Ablations reveal that compatibility between pretraining and post-training format is critical: if post-training does not preserve the safety-reflection token format, the pretraining benefit is diminished. SRP and content filtering (as in #1) are complementary: SRP targets the compositional risk that filtering cannot address.

![Figure 1: SRP pretraining corpus with interspersed safety reflection passages vs. standard pretraining baseline](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining inserts self-monitoring passages into the pretraining corpus; models trained with SRP show substantially reduced attack success rates under both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks.*

## 03 · [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang (Indiana University Bloomington, Tsinghua University, Nanyang Technological University) — arXiv preprint, September 1, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides a mechanistic, geometry-grounded explanation for why safety alignment collapses dramatically after only 100 benign fine-tuning examples while general utility survives — a key puzzle in the alignment-durability literature. The Fisher-geometric explanation directly informs mitigation strategies.
- **Technical summary:** Rather than invoking gradient conflict as the cause of post-fine-tuning safety collapse, the paper proposes a Fisher-geometric account: safety-relevant Fisher information is low-rank, and RLHF-style alignment flattens safety geometry while preserving an "output-routing pathway" through output-side MLP modules. After 100 benign fine-tuning examples, this pathway is selectively re-sharpened in a way that restores the model's ability to route harmful queries toward unsafe completions, while general utility (which spans much higher-rank geometry) survives. The analysis additionally explains why a small number of safety examples can rapidly restore refusal behavior: the internal safety representation is preserved even as the routing pathway is disrupted. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness, but their protection weakens at larger fine-tuning scales — consistent with the geometric picture.

![Figure 1: Fisher-geometric view of safety collapse — output-side MLP re-sharpening after 100 benign fine-tuning steps](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Fisher geometry before and after benign fine-tuning — low-rank safety directions become re-sharpened in output-side MLPs, selectively restoring harmful routing while general utility geometry remains flat.*

## 04 · [**Fool's Gold: Defensive Deception Against Safety-Removal Attacks on Open-Weight Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17202)

`pretraining-safety` `open-weight` `tamper-resistance` `preprint` — Mark Russinovich (Microsoft) — arXiv, August 2026

A novel defensive strategy against abliteration (weight-surgery that removes the refusal direction): rather than hardening the refusal direction itself, "decoy hardening" concedes the refusal strip and poisons its payoff by training the model to produce confident, fluent but factually falsified answers to hazardous operational questions when in the post-abliteration state. The decoys are trained inside a differentiable simulation of the attack, so they express only after abliteration while clean-state behavior is preserved by a refusal pin and benign leash. Evaluated on 7 models from 5 families (9B–122B, dense and MoE): 6 of 7 pass the pre-registered efficacy gate, with 51–90% of attacked-state responses to held-out hazardous prompts being decoys.

![Figure 1: Fool's Gold decoy hardening — clean state refuses, post-abliteration state produces fluent decoys](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17202/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Fool's Gold attack simulation — differentiable abliteration simulation during training conditions the model to produce confident but falsified responses only after refusal removal.*

## 05 · [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining-safety` `refusal-direction` `open-weight` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Targets the root cause of abliteration vulnerability: the ease with which the refusal direction can be extracted from a small contrastive prompt set. A weight-editing method applies rank-k updates to residual-stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random aliases and correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve original behavior. The aliasing operation obscures the signal used by abliteration while keeping model outputs unchanged. On Llama-3-8B: post-abliteration refusal score improves by +2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5pp MMLU degradation. Complements Fool's Gold (#4) by targeting the extraction phase rather than the post-attack payoff.

![Figure 1: Refusal aliases — rank-k weight editing replaces extractable refusal direction with random aliases](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal alias weight editing — rank-k update to residual-stream writers replaces the refusal direction with random aliases, blocking contrastive extraction while preserving clean-state behavior.*

## 06 · [**LMSM: LLM Security Framework Inspired by Linux Security Modules**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25697)

`AI-security` `SAE` `interpretability-for-security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 26, 2026

Adapts the Linux Security Modules separation-of-concerns design to LLM serving: a security backend (SAE, transcoder, or dense probe) produces calibrated per-request evidence; a versioned policy evaluates rules over trusted context; a separate gate enforces the verdict. This separation allows backend, rule, or schedule changes without touching request handling or enforcement code. Prototype on Hugging Face Transformers and vLLM demonstrates SAE and transcoder deployments with task-fitted dense probes. On Qwen3-4B, LMSM-Checkpoint reduces HarmBench attack success rate from 39.20% to 3.32% (12× reduction), with XSTest false-refusal rate rising only from 2.40% to 4.40% and 98.14% throughput retained.

![Figure 1: LMSM architecture — security backend, versioned policy, and enforcement gate separation](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25697/x1.png)

*Figure 1: LMSM separates backend evidence (SAE/probe), policy evaluation, and enforcement; on Qwen3-4B, HarmBench ASR drops from 39.20% to 3.32% at 98.14% throughput.*

## 07 · [**LLM Forensics: Where Do Backdoors Hide? Localizing and Controlling Trigger Mechanisms with Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07746)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `backdoor` `SAE` `preprint` — Wissam Antoun et al. — arXiv, September 2026

Trains SAEs across all layers and transformer components (attention, MLP, residual stream) of 1B and 8B language models with language-switching backdoors (trigger → French/German), then identifies trigger-relevant feature directions by comparing triggered prompts against translation and pretraining controls. SAE features separate triggered from clean prompts with near-perfect F1 — but the key finding is a dissociation: trigger-detecting features do not necessarily causally control the backdoor behavior, implying that forensic identification of the trigger does not automatically yield a backdoor-removal target. The study maps the full feature-layer topology of backdoor mechanisms across model scale.

![Figure 1: SAE feature F1 for trigger detection vs. causal control of backdoor behavior across layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07746/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Near-perfect F1 for trigger detection via SAE features, but trigger-detecting features dissociate from causal-control features — forensic localization and behavioral removal require different feature sets.*

## 08 · [**Why Are LLM Backdoor Defenses Fragmented? A Feature-Level Explanation with Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30403)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `backdoor` `SAE` `preprint` — Yizhe Zeng et al. — arXiv, August 2026

First systematic SAE-based mechanistic analysis of LLM backdoor attacks, using a 2×2 comparison (clean/poisoned model × clean/triggered input) to trace backdoor-induced logit shifts to high-contributing SAE features. Features are categorized into four roles: interaction features (active only in poisoned+triggered condition), suppressed features (inhibited by poisoning), mixed features (affected in both conditions), and weight-modified features (altered at the weight level). This taxonomy explains why dirty-label and clean-label attacks are mechanistically distinct and why no single defense generalizes: existing defenses each target a different subset of these roles, providing a principled roadmap for unified backdoor defense design.

![Figure 1: 2x2 SAE feature role taxonomy across clean/poisoned model and clean/triggered input](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30403/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Four-role feature taxonomy from the 2×2 mechanistic analysis — each defense addresses a different role, explaining why unified dirty-label+clean-label defense has remained elusive.*

## 09 · [**Scalable Circuit Learning for Interpreting Large Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16939)

`mech-interp` `circuit-attribution` `SAE` `ICML 2026 (peer-reviewed)` — IBM Research — ICML 2026 (accepted)

CircuitLasso reformulates circuit discovery as sparse linear regression over SAE feature spaces, making circuit learning computationally tractable at the scale where SAE dictionaries are large (hundreds of thousands of features). On the INTERPBENCH benchmark, CircuitLasso matches the structural recovery accuracy of state-of-the-art intervention-based methods (EAP, EAP-ig) at a fraction of their runtime, and scales cleanly to high-dimensional SAE feature spaces where those methods fail. Operating on monosemantic SAE features produces tree-shaped circuit paths with human-interpretable semantic labels. A utility demonstration on Bias-in-Bios shows that circuits learned by CircuitLasso support domain generalization at substantially lower cost than non-oracle baselines.

![Figure 1: CircuitLasso — sparse linear regression over SAE feature space recovers interpretable tree-shaped circuit paths](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16939/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CircuitLasso circuit diagram — SAE features as nodes, sparse regression edges; matches EAP/EAP-ig structural accuracy with orders-of-magnitude less compute on INTERPBENCH.*

## 10 · [**When Safety Speaks a Language: A Mechanistic Analysis of Safety-Language Identity Entanglement in LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29936)

`mech-interp` `safety` `multilingual` `SAE` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Systematic SAE-based mechanistic analysis of multilingual safety across 3 instruction-tuned LLMs, 8 languages, and all model layers. Discovers that safety-relevant SAE features are geometrically entangled with language-identity features and exhibit cross-lingual sharing patterns that vary across model depth and architecture. The entanglement has a practical consequence: ablating safety features impacts harmful response rates but simultaneously disrupts the target language — meaning that safety and language routing are mechanistically coupled. Cross-lingual sharing patterns in safety features partially explain why safety alignment in one language sometimes (but not always) generalizes to others.

![Figure 1: Safety-language entanglement — SAE feature geometry showing safety and language-identity overlap across 8 languages and 3 LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29936/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety SAE features are geometrically entangled with language-identity features across 8 languages and 3 LLMs; ablating safety features disrupts both harmful outputs and target-language fluency.*
