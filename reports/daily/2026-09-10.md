## 01 · [**When Safety Speaks a Language: A Mechanistic Analysis of Safety-Language Identity Entanglement in LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29936)

- **Authors / venue:** Apoorva Upadhyaya, Sandipan Sikdar — arXiv preprint (cs.CL), August 30, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** The most complete refusal-mechanism interpretability result of the window: it shows the SAE features that mediate refusal are not language-neutral, which bears directly on why safety training in English transfers unevenly and on what abliteration-style interventions actually remove.
- **Technical summary:** The authors apply residual-stream SAEs at every layer of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-IT and contrast harmful vs. harmless prompts in eight typologically diverse languages (EN, ZH, DE, AR, VI, ID, RU, HI) to isolate safety-relevant features per language and layer. Where those features live is architecture-dependent (layer bands differ across the three models), and within each model the safety features are geometrically entangled with language-identity features: a large fraction of a language's safety features are shared with other languages, with sharing varying by depth and by language pair. The causal test is the key finding — ablating a language's safety features raises harmful-compliance rates *and* shifts the output language, i.e. the refusal signal and the "which language am I speaking" signal are carried by overlapping directions, so a purely safety-targeted intervention is not available at the feature level.

![Figure 1: Overlap between safety-relevant and language-identity SAE features across layers for three models and eight languages](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29936/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety-relevant SAE features overlap language-identity features across depth; the overlap is architecture-specific, and ablating them changes both harmful-compliance rate and output language.*

## 02 · [**TIER: Threat Implicitness Benchmark for Evaluating LLM Safety Behaviors**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05117)

- **Authors / venue:** Thu-Hien Trinh-Thi, Hai-Yen Vong, Thanh-Ha Ung-Dung, Tram Ho (University of Science, VNU-HCM) — arXiv preprint (cs.CR), September 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** A safety evaluation of open-weight models that measures *how* they fail as threat becomes less explicit, rather than a binary refuse/comply rate — the kind of graded signal needed to judge whether pretraining- or post-training-time safeguards actually hold.
- **Technical summary:** TIER organizes prompts along two axes — four risk domains and four threat-implicitness levels running from explicit harmful requests, through contextually embedded and indirect phrasings, to full jailbreak framings — and scores each response on a six-label behavior scale (from hard refusal through hedging and partial compliance to full compliance) using two independent LLM judges. Across six open-weight LLMs, behavior shifts gradually across the four levels rather than flipping from refusal to compliance at one threshold; contextual prompts produce the most heterogeneous behavior across models, while the jailbreak level exposes the widest gap between the most and least robust models. The graded scale makes over-refusal and partial leakage visible where refusal-rate benchmarks report a single number.

![Figure 1: TIER design — four risk domains × four threat-implicitness levels, scored on a six-label behavior scale](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05117/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Behavior distribution across the four threat levels for six open-weight models; degradation is gradual, with the largest inter-model gaps at the jailbreak level.*

## 03 · [**REINS: Refusal-Enhanced Inhibitory Steering with Sparse Autoencoder Features**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28233)

- **Authors / venue:** University of Science and Technology of China & Zhejiang University — arXiv preprint (cs.CL), August 28, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Interp-guided intervention evaluated on a task metric: it shows single-direction SAE refusal steering fails on wrapped harmful prompts and that steering two feature sets in opposite directions fixes it without the capability collapse of stronger steering.
- **Technical summary:** The authors first build GUISE (Generalized Undercover Instruction Safety Evaluation), a set of harmful requests hidden inside complex wrappers (role-play, formatting, task framing) on which existing SAE steering methods that only amplify a refusal feature no longer produce refusals — the harmful-continuation features stay active and win. REINS steers in the same SAE feature space but does two things at once: it suppresses the features that carry harmful continuation and amplifies the features that carry safe refusal. On GUISE this markedly reduces harmful responses and raises the rate of genuine refusals, while general-capability benchmarks are largely preserved; the baselines either intervene too weakly (no refusal) or reach "safety" by degenerating outputs.

![Figure 1: REINS — simultaneous suppression of harmful-continuation SAE features and amplification of refusal features](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28233/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal-only SAE steering leaves harmful-continuation features active on wrapped prompts; REINS inhibits them while enhancing refusal features, recovering refusals without capability collapse.*

## 04 · [**Arbitrary Cipher Attacks Against Large Language Models Do Not Require Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09553)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `preprint` — Thomas Rivasseau (McGill University) — arXiv, September 2026

Cipher ("covert communication") jailbreaks were previously demonstrated by fine-tuning commercial models through their fine-tuning APIs on encrypted harmful Q&A. This paper shows current frontier models from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI acquire arbitrary ciphers from prompting and in-context examples alone, and that once the exchange is conducted in the learned cipher, alignment is substantially weakened or bypassed. The attack needs no weights, no API fine-tuning access and no gradient signal, which moves cipher attacks from a fine-tuning-API threat model to an ordinary chat threat model.

![Figure 1: In-context cipher acquisition followed by harmful exchange conducted in the cipher](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09553/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The attack teaches the cipher in-context, then routes the harmful request and response through it; safety behavior that holds in plaintext is weakened or absent in cipher.*

## 05 · [**Structural Jailbreaks Generalize but Do Not Compound: A Cross-Provider and Multilingual Study of Involuntary In-Context Learning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08373)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `preprint` — Tejasvi C. Addagada — arXiv, September 2026

Involuntary In-Context Learning (IICL) recasts a harmful request as the final missing cell of a data-labeling task. Using a deterministic IICL operator and a StrongREJECT-style judge on gemini-2.5-flash and gemini-2.5-flash-lite — a provider the original IICL study never tested — with 30 HarmBench general-harm and 30 FinProof financial-abuse behaviors in EN/ES/HI/AR: IICL turns near-total refusal into 80–90% compliance on general harm and 97–100% on financial abuse. Forcing the same attack into a lower-resource language does not add the multilingual gap on top of the structural one; it blunts the attack. The defensive takeaway is to measure interactions rather than assume additivity, and to treat the English structural surface as the real exposure.

![Figure 1: Compliance rate under baseline vs. IICL across four languages and two benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08373/x1.png)

*Figure 1: IICL lifts compliance from near zero to 80–100% in English; the same operator in Hindi/Arabic is weaker, not stronger.*

## 06 · [**LMSM: LLM Security Framework Inspired by Linux Security Modules**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25697)

`AI-security` `SAE` `interpretability-for-security` `preprint` — XiuYu Zhang, Bonan Ruan, Junfeng Fang, An Zhang, Tat-Seng Chua, Zhenkai Liang (NUS, USTC) — arXiv, August 26, 2026

Adapts the Linux Security Modules separation to LLM serving: a pluggable security backend (SAE, transcoder or task-fitted dense probe) emits calibrated per-request evidence, a versioned policy evaluates rules over trusted request context, and a separate gate enforces the decision — so backends, rules or schedules change without touching request handling. Prototyped on Hugging Face Transformers and vLLM with artifact-backed SAE/transcoder deployments. On Qwen3-4B, LMSM-Checkpoint cuts HarmBench attack success from 39.20% to 3.32% while XSTest false refusals rise only from 2.40% to 4.40%, retaining 98.14% of throughput.

![Figure 1: LMSM architecture — backend evidence, versioned policy, enforcement gate](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25697/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Evidence, policy and enforcement are separate modules; HarmBench ASR 39.20% → 3.32% on Qwen3-4B at 98.14% throughput.*

## 07 · [**LLM Forensics: Where Do Backdoors Hide? Localizing and Controlling Trigger Mechanisms with Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07746)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `backdoor` `SAE` `preprint` — Wissam Antoun et al. — arXiv, September 2026

Studies trigger-based backdoors in a controlled, harmless setting — fixed trigger sequences make 1B and 8B models continue English prompts in French or German — by training SAEs across layers and components and contrasting triggered prompts with translation and pretraining controls to isolate trigger-relevant feature directions. SAE features separate triggered prompts from controls with near-perfect F1, but the features that *detect* the trigger are not the ones that *control* the switched behavior: ablating detectors does not reliably remove the backdoor. Detection and removal therefore need different feature sets, which matters for any SAE-based backdoor audit.

![Figure 1: Trigger-detection F1 vs. causal effect of ablation across layers for the language-switch backdoor](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07746/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Near-perfect detection at many layers; causal control of the behavior is concentrated elsewhere.*

## 08 · [**Why Are LLM Backdoor Defenses Fragmented? A Feature-Level Explanation with Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30403)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `backdoor` `SAE` `preprint` — Yizhe Zeng et al. — arXiv, August 31, 2026

A 2×2 design (clean vs. poisoned model × clean vs. triggered input) traces backdoor-induced logit shifts to high-contribution SAE features and sorts them into four roles: interaction features (active only for poisoned model + trigger), suppressed features (inhibited by poisoning), mixed features (affected under both inputs) and weight-modified features (changed at the parameter level). Dirty-label and clean-label attacks populate these roles differently, which is why defenses built for one paradigm miss the other — each existing defense implicitly targets one role. The taxonomy is offered as the basis for a defense that covers all four.

![Figure 1: Four feature roles from the 2×2 clean/poisoned × clean/triggered analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30403/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Interaction, suppressed, mixed and weight-modified features; dirty-label and clean-label attacks occupy the roles differently.*

## 09 · [**ALTSTEER: Selective Safety Steering for Moving Beyond Hard Refusals to Constructive Alternatives**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30197)

`mech-interp` `steering` `preprint` — Hoejoon Kwon et al. — arXiv, August 31, 2026

Frames safety steering as two coupled decisions — *when* to intervene and *how* generation should proceed afterward — and argues existing methods fail on both: triggers are unstable across domains and refusal-direction steering yields curt refusals. ALTSTEER couples a selective trigger with refusal-anchored constructive redirection in a single inference pass. On Llama-3.1 and Qwen2.5 it preserves benign utility while increasing constructive safe-completion behavior, with the largest gains on models that otherwise emit short refusals.

![Figure 1: ALTSTEER — selective intervention gate followed by refusal-anchored constructive redirection](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30197/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Intervention fires selectively, then steers toward a safe-completion rather than a bare refusal; utility on benign prompts is preserved.*
