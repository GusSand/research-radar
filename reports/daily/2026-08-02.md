## 01 · [MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04027)

- **Authors / venue:** Researchers from University of Wisconsin-Madison, Johns Hopkins University, University of Southern California, University of Adelaide — arXiv preprint (June 4, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Highest-interest cross-topic: first UCB-bandit adaptive black-box jailbreak framework that exploits dLLM-specific structure (native mask tokens, bidirectional attention, confidence-based commitment) — directly serves both the AI-security and text-diffusion tracks.
- **Technical summary:** MaskForge maintains a growing library of structural mask patterns and uses an Upper Confidence Bound bandit to select among them, submitting attacks to the target dLLM in a fully black-box loop. Successful attack instances are distilled back into the library as new candidate patterns. Evaluated across five public dLLMs on three jailbreak benchmarks, the attack achieves 79.3% average attack success rate — a 17.6% relative improvement over the strongest prior black-box baseline — while remaining query-efficient. The attack surface is unique to diffusion models: mask tokens are native inputs (not corruptions), bidirectional attention means adversarial structure affects generation globally, and confidence-based commitment lets adversarial structure persist past safety-oriented denoising.

![Figure 1: MaskForge adaptive attack pipeline overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04027/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The MaskForge pipeline — UCB bandit selects from a growing library of structural mask patterns, submits attacks to the target dLLM, and distills successful patterns back into the library.*

## 02 · [Beyond the Bidirectional Promise: Re-evaluating the Robustness of Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27386)

- **Authors / venue:** Saurabh Yadav, Badri Narayana Patro, Vijay Srinivas Agneeswaran — arXiv preprint (July 29, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Systematic robustness audit of dLLMs across 32 perturbation types including gradient attacks; clarifies what the bidirectional-attention robustness advantage actually covers and doesn't cover — important for both dLLM deployment and security evaluation.
- **Technical summary:** Compares LLaDA-8B vs. LLaMA-3-8B and Dream-7B vs. Qwen2.5-7B across 32 naturally occurring perturbation types and standard gradient-based adversarial suffix attacks. Under gradient attacks, dLLMs are substantially harder to attack due to highly non-convex stochastic loss landscapes — a genuine architectural benefit. Under natural perturbations (character swaps, synonym substitution, paraphrasing, noise), however, no architectural advantage appears: robustness correlates with weight-level training, not architecture. Mechanistic hidden-state analysis traces the gradient-attack resistance to diffusion training dynamics rather than bidirectional attention per se.

![Figure 1: Robustness comparison across perturbation conditions](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27386/x1.png)

*Figure 1: dLLM vs. AR model robustness under gradient-based attacks vs. 32 natural perturbation types — the architectural advantage is real but narrowly scoped.*

## 03 · [Piggybacking on Perception: Stealthy Concurrent Audio Prompt Injections against Multimodal LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28165)

- **Authors / venue:** Mingxiao Liu, Yitong Li, Haoren Zhao, Yaoxiang Bian, Jianan Ma, Jian Zhang, Jialuo Chen, Xinhao Deng (Hangzhou Dianzi University, Ant Group, Zhejiang University, Tsinghua University) — arXiv preprint (July 31, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Most recent AI security paper in the window; opens a novel attack surface (audio modality) that bypasses text-level safety filters at the architectural level, with code released.
- **Technical summary:** Embeds adversarial instruction strings as psychoacoustically imperceptible perturbations in audio waveforms, running concurrently with (not replacing) legitimate audio content. The attack exploits the ordering constraint that audio-capable multimodal agents must transcribe to text (via ASR) before any safety filter can act, placing the injected instruction directly in the reasoning context. Evaluated against production-scale multimodal agents, the attack achieves high success rates while remaining below human perceptibility thresholds; the "concurrent" structure means neither users nor acoustic anomaly detectors see anything unusual.

![Figure 1: Audio prompt injection pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28165/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attack pipeline — the payload is embedded in the acoustic signal before the perception (ASR) layer, bypassing all downstream text-level safety filters.*

## 04 · [Sparse Autoencoders Encode Both Concepts and Functions: The Downstream Geometry of Feature Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24645)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Phu Gia Hoang, Anwoy Chatterjee, Tanmoy Chakraborty, Iryna Gurevych, Subhabrata Dutta — arXiv preprint (July 27, 2026)

Introduces Feature-Effect Geometry Analysis (FEGA) to decompose SAE feature behavior into a concept axis (activation descriptions) and an effect axis (downstream logit shifts), finding systematic misalignment: features with clear interpretable descriptions often have weak causal effects, and causally dominant features are often poorly described. Effect geometry predicts steering reliability better than activation descriptions alone, directly challenging interpretability evaluations that rely solely on activation analysis.

![Figure 1: FEGA — concept vs. effect geometry of SAE features](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24645/x1.png)

*Figure 1: FEGA scatter plot — SAE features along concept-activation and functional-effect axes, showing the axes are misaligned.*

## 05 · [Where Steering Signals Come From: Activation Source Selection in Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25270)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Jiaran Ye, Lingxu Ran, Zijun Yao, Chenpeng Wang, Yong Jiang, Lei Hou, Juanzi Li, Liangming Pan — arXiv preprint (July 28, 2026)

Holds the downstream intervention fixed and varies only the source position from which steering vectors are extracted, finding that execution-boundary states — positions just before the model produces the target behavior — yield substantially stronger signals than random positions, last tokens, or post-action states, across three instruction-tuned models and four task families. Turns source selection from a poorly understood hyperparameter into a principled design choice.

![Figure 1: Steering success by source position](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25270/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering vector effectiveness at different source positions — execution-boundary positions consistently outperform all alternatives.*

## 06 · [ToxScreen: Detecting Whether an LLM Has Been Poisoned](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26849)

`AI security` `preprint` — Anthony Hughes, Nicole Xing, Collin Francel, Andy Kim, Andrew Draganov — arXiv preprint (July 29, 2026)

Releases a benchmark of ~800 backdoored models spanning attack objectives, trigger mechanisms (word/phrase/style), poisoning rates 0.1%–5%, model scales 125M–7B, and training mechanisms including full fine-tuning, LoRA, and RLHF-based backdoor insertion. The realistic threat model: a white-box defender with weight access but no training data, no trusted reference model, and no prior on trigger type — the first systematic substrate for evaluating trigger-recovery defenses under these constraints.

![Figure 1: ToxScreen benchmark taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26849/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ToxScreen taxonomy — ~800 backdoored models across five axes with a white-box, data-free defender threat model.*

## 07 · [ALIBI: Adaptive Agentic Attacks on LLM-Based Vulnerability Detectors via Adversarial Code Comments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24964)

`AI security` `preprint` — Zixuan Wu, Cristina Nita-Rotaru (Northeastern University) — arXiv preprint (July 27, 2026)

A coding agent iteratively inserts and refines adversarial natural-language comments in real-world vulnerable code (from vulnerability-fixing commits), exploiting that comments are processed as natural language by detector chain-of-thought but carry no security semantics in the code itself. Evaluated against four LLM-based detectors from specialized reasoning models to frontier multi-agent systems, exposing source-code comments as a previously understudied attack surface on AI-assisted security tooling.

![Figure 1: ALIBI adaptive comment injection attack loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24964/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ALIBI attack loop — agent inserts adversarial comments, receives detector feedback, refines until evasion succeeds.*

## 08 · [Where and When to Commit: Candidate-Aware Decoding for Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28166)

`dLLM` `preprint` — Chia-Ming Lee, Ming-Ching Chang, Xin Li, Yu-Lun Liu, Chih-Chung Hsu (NYCU, Albany SUNY, NCKU) — arXiv preprint (July 30, 2026)

Introduces Confidence-Verified Commit (CVC), a training-free decoding framework that separates two decisions standard dLLM decoding conflates: (a) per-token local confidence thresholds for early commitment and (b) a global stopping criterion for sequence completion. Decoupling these reduces total denoising steps per generation without output quality degradation, directly addressing dLLMs' inference-cost disadvantage relative to AR models.

![Figure 1: CVC denoising timeline with early token commitment](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28166/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CVC mechanism — high-confidence tokens commit early in the denoising schedule, with global stop decided independently.*

## 09 · [Are Single-Token Sparse Autoencoder Features Causally Necessary? Layer-Depth and SAE-Family Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20596)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Seonglae Cho, Zekun Wu, Kleyton Da Costa, Rishi Kalra, Ilham Wicaksono, Adriano Koshiyama — arXiv preprint (July 22, 2026)

Analyzes 3.9M features across six models and three SAE families (GemmaScope, BatchTopK, LlamaScope) using zero-ablation causal tests, finding that GemmaScope and BatchTopK features are causally necessary in 178/208 full-layer conditions while LlamaScope features at the same base model are locally redundant (~32% causal necessity). Cross-family differences dominate within-family scale effects — informing practitioners about which SAE family produces interpretability results that are also causally meaningful.

![Figure 1: SAE causal necessity rates by family](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20596/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Causal necessity rates across SAE families — GemmaScope and BatchTopK ~80–85%; LlamaScope ~32% at the same base model.*

## 10 · [CircuitKIT: Circuit Discovery, Evaluation, and Application Toolkit for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19317)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Pratinav Seth, Hem Gosalia, Aditya Kasliwal, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu — arXiv preprint (July 21, 2026)

Releases an open library unifying the circuit analysis workflow through a typed, serializable circuit representation that connects discovery algorithms (ACDC, EAP, and others), faithfulness/completeness evaluation modules, declarative dataset interfaces, and application modules (pruning, editing, steering, selective fine-tuning). Addresses the fragmentation problem where prior work required stitching together incompatible implementations, making method comparison and generalization to new tasks unnecessarily difficult.
