## 01 · [SEAL: Reinforcing Global Safety in Mixture-of-Experts through Shared Expert ALignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02293)

- **Authors / venue:** Qingyu Meng, Yiwei Zha, Jiahuan Pei, Koen Hindriks, Herbert Bos, Min Chen — **ACM CCS 2026 (accepted)**
- **Why it ranks here:** Top-tier security venue; MoE models (DeepSeek, Mixtral, Qwen-MoE) are the current deployment standard and are uniquely vulnerable to routing-based jailbreaks (GateBreaker, expert-silencing attacks) that bypass safety-critical sparse experts; SEAL exploits an architectural invariant — shared experts — that current attacks cannot route around.
- **Technical summary:** In sparse-MoE models, a subset of "shared experts" is activated for every input regardless of the router decision, unlike sparse experts whose activation is conditional. SEAL concentrates safety alignment training in the shared-expert pathway, making safety behaviours routing-invariant by construction: because shared experts cannot be bypassed by router manipulation, attacks that work by redirecting tokens away from safety-aligned sparse experts no longer succeed. The method converts a previously overlooked structural property of MoE architectures into a hardened global safety anchor without requiring changes to the model's general routing or to the sparse experts. Accepted at ACM CCS 2026; full quantitative results will appear in the proceedings.

![Figure 1: SEAL architecture — shared expert acts as a global safety anchor bypassing routing-based safety evasion](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02293/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MoE routing topology with SEAL — shared experts (always active) carry alignment signal that cannot be bypassed by gate-manipulation attacks, unlike conditionally active sparse experts.*

## 02 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

- **Authors / venue:** Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng — **EMNLP 2026 Findings (accepted)**
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed applied mechanistic interpretability that delivers concrete safety gains: identifies a 3-stage refusal circuit and leverages it to improve safety rates under adversarial attacks by 26.5% with only 1.7% accuracy cost, evaluated at scale across 6 LLMs.
- **Technical summary:** The paper characterises a three-component safety circuit in decoder-only transformers via targeted causal interventions: (i) _Harmful Detection Heads_ (attention heads whose activations rise on harmful inputs and initiate the safety signal), (ii) _Safety Neurons_ (MLP neurons that amplify and stabilise the signal in the residual stream), and (iii) _Refusal Heads_ (attention heads that translate the accumulated safety signal into refusal token probabilities). Causal evidence is provided by zero-ablation of each component class while measuring downstream refusal rates. Guided by this circuit map, the authors apply targeted weight scaling — increasing the magnitude of identified safety-relevant components — without retraining. Across six LLMs under adversarial attacks, this intervention raises safety rates by 26.5 percentage points while incurring only a 1.7% drop on four standard benchmarks.

![Figure 2: Three-stage safety circuit — harmful detection heads feed safety neurons that drive refusal heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 2: Causal diagram of the identified 3-stage refusal circuit; circuit-guided weight scaling at each stage yields 26.5 pp safety improvement under attacks at 1.7% benchmark cost.*

## 03 · [Fool's Gold: Defensive Deception Against Safety-Removal Attacks on Open-Weight Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17202)

- **Authors / venue:** Mark Russinovich et al. (Microsoft) — arXiv preprint, August 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Paradigm shift for open-weight model safety: instead of trying (and repeatedly failing) to prevent abliteration, decoy hardening poisons the payoff of a successful abliteration attack — making the resulting model unreliable as a harm-enabler regardless of attack success.
- **Technical summary:** Abliteration removes refusal by projecting weight matrices orthogonal to a refusal-mediating direction; no release-time defence has proven durable against it. Fool's Gold accepts this and shifts the security goal: once refusal is stripped, most answers to hazardous operational prompts should be confident, fluent **decoys** whose critical elements are falsified. Decoys are trained inside a differentiable simulation of the attack — they express only in the attacked state; a _refusal pin_ and a _benign leash_ hold clean-state behaviour to the original model. The security property is therefore not "the attacker is refused" but "no answer from the defended checkpoint can be safely acted on." The method was instantiated on seven models from five families (9 B–122 B, dense and MoE); on the six passing the efficacy gate, 0.51–0.90 of attacked-state responses to held-out hazardous prompts are decoys, with +0.27–0.84 attributable to the defence versus baseline.

![Figure 3: Decoy hardening pipeline — decoys trained inside differentiable abliteration simulation express only in attacked state](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17202/x1.png)

*Figure 3: Fool's Gold decoy-hardening loop — refusal pin and benign leash preserve clean-state behaviour while simulated abliteration trains the model to output fluent but falsified decoys after the attack.*

## 04 · [Practice Makes Unsafe: Skill Misevolution in Self-Improving LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12851)

`AI security` `agent safety` `preprint` — Xutao Mao, Liangjie Zhao, Xiang Zheng, Cong Wang et al. — arXiv, August 13, 2026

Self-improving agents convert successful trajectories into reusable skills; because skill evolution optimises task outcomes rather than safety, compromised experience can become persistent unsafe policy. Across 25 agent-method configurations covering 525 tasks × 25 episodes, all 21 evolved configurations authored unsafe artifacts (only 15 led to fresh-session harm). The authors introduce SkillMisevo-Gym (lifecycle-aware harness versioning skill state across frameworks) and SkillMisevo-Bench (frozen design-for-benchmarking), plus SafeEvolve, which reduces unsafe retrieval by 26.7 pp and fresh-session harm by 17.3 pp with only +0.4 utility change.

![Figure 4: Skill misevolution lifecycle — compromised trajectories persist as reusable unsafe policies across tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12851/x1.png)

*Figure 4: SkillMisevo-Gym lifecycle — unsafe successes become cross-task reusable skills; SafeEvolve intervention at the skill-formation stage reduces downstream unsafe retrieval and fresh-session harm.*

## 05 · [SafeEvolve: Harness-Policy Co-Evolution from Agent Experience for Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02786)

`AI security` `agent safety` `preprint` — W. Liu et al. (Shanghai AI Lab, SJTU, Fudan) — arXiv, September 2, 2026

Dedicated system for agent safety alignment: uses completed on-policy trajectories to co-evolve both the external harness (prompts, safety skills) and the model policy in a continuous loop, specifically targeting multi-step risks like prompt injection that unfold across an agent's own tool calls. On Qwen3-4B: AgentDojo utility rises from 44.33 → 60.82 while ASR drops from 13.38 → 2.42; on Qwen3.5-4B: AgentHarm harmful score drops from 56.45 → 12.27.

## 06 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Abliteration works because the refusal direction is easy to extract from a small contrastive prompt set; AMRA obscures the refusal signal by applying rank-_k_ updates to residual-stream writer matrices while replacing refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, then correcting downstream reader matrices to restore the model's clean-state behaviour. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA raises post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline while incurring less than 0.5 pp of MMLU degradation.

![Figure 6: AMRA — rank-k updates add random aliases to the residual stream writer, obscuring the extractable refusal direction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 6: AMRA defence — refusal-inducing activations are replaced with random aliases in the residual stream; downstream reader correction preserves clean-state behaviour; abliteration cannot extract the now-hidden refusal direction.*

## 07 · [Unlearning Is Not Just Erasing: Temporal Decoupling via Generation Inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23020)

`mech-interp` `concept erasure` `preprint` — Xunlei Chen et al. — arXiv, August 24, 2026

Standard token-erasure unlearning disrupts linguistic structure by penalising target outputs without modelling their context-dependent retrieval paths. ADU (Attention Decoupling Unlearning) exploits the functional distinction between local and global attention heads: it identifies positions that retrieve persistent sensitive anchors (via global attention), then trains attention-projection adapters to suppress attention mass along those paths while preserving local attention and retain-set language modelling. Generation Inequality loss prevents target outputs without blocking benign context that co-occurs with the target subject.

![Figure 7: ADU — attention-projection adapters suppress global-head retrieval paths for sensitive anchors, preserving local attention](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23020/x1.png)

*Figure 7: ADU pipeline — global attention heads retrieving sensitive anchors are identified; adapters suppress those paths while local attention and retain-set LM are preserved.*

## 08 · [Can LLMs Truly Forget? Revealing Unlearning Gaps Through Adversarial Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21606)

`mech-interp` `concept erasure` `preprint` — Ayush Gupta et al. (UMass Amherst, Microsoft) — arXiv, August 21, 2026

Existing unlearning benchmarks evaluate clean, non-adversarial queries; this work adds an adversarial robustness layer for methods that pass standard metrics. Unified evaluation covers prompt-based and fine-tuning-based unlearning on TOFU (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct). The authors introduce Attack Success Rate (ASR) as an LLM-as-judge metric (leakage score > 0.2 threshold) and find that methods appearing robust under standard evaluation fail under strategic adversarial prompting.

![Figure 8: Adversarial unlearning evaluation — methods passing standard metrics fail under strategic prompting (ASR metric)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21606/x1.png)

*Figure 8: Standard vs. adversarial evaluation gap — unlearning methods achieving low forget-set accuracy under clean queries show high ASR under LLM-as-judge adversarial probing.*

## 09 · [What to Forget in Unlearning? Forget Set Curation for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14855)

`mech-interp` `concept erasure` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Unlearning algorithms assume the forget set is given; this paper shows the curation step is itself a critical determinant of both suppression quality and collateral damage. The study maps a suppression request (e.g., "stop reproducing this song/book") to the data actually passed to the algorithm. The authors introduce CleanSlate, a benchmark for verbatim output suppression over songs and books with model-specific extraction profiles, content-grounded QA, and capability-retention evaluations; natural lexical and exact-substring curators yield weak suppression, revealing a systematic gap between how forget sets are currently constructed and what the algorithms actually require.

![Figure 9: CleanSlate benchmark — suppression request mapped to forget set; curator quality determines unlearning outcome](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14855/x1.png)

*Figure 9: Forget-set curation gap — natural lexical curators vs. model-specific extraction profiles on CleanSlate (songs/books); curation strategy is the dominant factor in verbatim output suppression quality.*

## 10 · [Forgotten in Weights, Recovered by Tools: Agentic Tool Unlearning for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21544)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `concept erasure` `preprint` — Baicheng Chen, Zheyuan Liu, Jingyu Zhang, Kaize Ding, Ningshan Ma, Yue Huang, Meng Jiang — arXiv, August 21, 2026

Parametric unlearning suppresses direct recall, but a tool-augmented agent can recover the same information through web search, retrieval, or database lookup — a failure mode called **tool-mediated recovery**. ATU (Agentic Tool Unlearning) addresses this with a two-stage framework: (1) parametric unlearning suppresses direct recall; (2) trajectory-level reinforcement learning in simulated tool-augmented environments penalises target-seeking tool behaviour and final-answer leakage while preserving normal tool use for retained knowledge.

![Figure 10: ATU two-stage pipeline — parametric unlearning + trajectory-level RL penalising tool-mediated recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21544/x1.png)

*Figure 10: ATU framework — stage 1 suppresses parametric recall; stage 2 runs trajectory-level RL in simulated tool environments to block tool-mediated recovery paths for the forget target.*
