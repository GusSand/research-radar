## 01 · [**Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03887)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `mech-interp applied` `EMNLP 2026` — Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem — **EMNLP 2026 Main Conference (peer-reviewed)**

Three standard post-training recipes — SFT, reasoning-augmented SFT, and preference optimization (ORPO) — instill refusal behavior equally well by behavioral metrics, yet their effects on the *circuit* implementing that refusal are fundamentally different. This paper measures those differences mechanistically across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B, and shows that no current method achieves all three properties a safe model needs simultaneously.

![Figure 1: Refusal circuit structures under SFT vs. reasoning-augmented vs. ORPO training across three LLM architectures](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03887/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Comparative refusal circuit analysis across post-training methods — reasoning-augmented training consistently produces a distinct circuit topology; architecture independently modulates steerability.*

Applying attention head attribution and activation patching, the study finds that SFT concentrates refusal in a small, easily ablated set of heads; reasoning-augmented training distributes refusal more broadly and consistently produces a different computational signature across all three model families; ORPO produces intermediate fragility. Strikingly, no method achieves all three simultaneously: (1) refusal not concentrated in fragile components, (2) no capability regression on standard benchmarks, and (3) refusal correctable by small targeted interventions. Architecture also has an independent effect: the same training method installs differently steerable circuits depending on the base model. The findings challenge treating safety alignment as a binary property and suggest that interpretability-informed training is necessary for durable refusal.

## 02 · [**Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04721)

`mech-interp applied` `preprint` — Preethi Carmel Bosco, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan — arXiv, September 4, 2026

Refusal, thought to live in the attention-dominated residual stream of transformers, also exists in state-space models (SSMs) despite their fundamentally different token-mixing mechanism. A rigid rotation aligns the transformer and SSM representation spaces; a harm probe trained on a transformer then flags SSM harmful inputs without re-training, and removing the aligned refusal direction from an SSM bypasses its refusal behavior.

![Figure 1: Cross-architecture transfer of refusal direction — harm probe trained on transformer flags SSM harmful inputs after rigid rotation alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04721/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A rigid rotation aligns transformer and SSM residual streams; the shared refusal direction persists across architectures, enabling cross-model probe transfer and cross-architecture ablation.*

## 03 · [**Uncensored Open-weight Models: Redistribution as the Persistence Layer**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05241)

`AI security` `open-weight safety` `preprint` — Juliette Garcia, Hailey May et al. (10a Labs) — arXiv, September 4, 2026

Between January 2024 and March 2026, 3,471 original uncensored models appeared on HuggingFace, each repackaged on average 2.4 times; three actors account for 52% of all 8,164 compressed redistributions. Once quantized and mirrored across Ollama and alternative registries, these models persist regardless of upstream removal — of 1,643 identified GitHub integrations, 25% are classified as explicitly malicious.

## 04 · [**ObserverBench: Testing Mechanistic Estimates for Intervention and Control**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03026)

`mech-interp applied` `preprint` — arXiv, September 2, 2026

Interpretability methods increasingly guide real interventions (activation steering, circuit removal, safety monitoring), but an estimate that is accurate on average can still choose a poor action when deployed. ObserverBench standardizes this evaluation: each task fixes model, information boundary, allowed actions, and loss, and reports *estimation accuracy* separately from *action-choice loss*. On circuit-intervention tasks in GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B, pairwise observers predict unseen effects more accurately but do not always choose lower-loss actions — the accuracy–control gap is substantial and task-dependent.

![Figure 1: ObserverBench task structure — estimation accuracy vs. action-choice loss for circuit intervention observers](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03026/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ObserverBench decouples estimation accuracy from action-choice quality; circuit-intervention observers on GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B show a systematic accuracy–control gap.*

## 05 · [**EraseSAE: Surgical Concept Erasure in Text-to-Video Diffusion Models via Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03629)

`mech-interp applied` `concept erasure` `ECCV 2026` `peer-reviewed` — HiDream-ai team — **ECCV 2026 (peer-reviewed)**

Coarse-granularity concept erasure methods degrade adjacent concepts because they do not operate at the level of individual monosemantic features. EraseSAE decomposes spatiotemporal activations in DiT-based text-to-video models with a Partitioned Convolutional SAE, uses contrastive attribution to isolate concept-specific feature kernels, and at inference applies timestep-resolved masks to erase only where the target concept is active — demonstrating precise celebrity identity and nudity erasure on HunyuanVideo and CogVideoX-5b with minimal quality degradation.

![Figure 1: EraseSAE decompose-attribute-erase pipeline for surgical concept removal in text-to-video diffusion models](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03629/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Three-stage pipeline — Partitioned Convolutional SAE decomposes activations into monosemantic features; contrastive attribution isolates concept-specific kernels; timestep masks confine erasure to active regions.*

## 06 · [**The Implications of Linguistic Illegibility for LLM Security**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02852)

`AI security` `preprint` — James Mickens (Harvard) — arXiv, September 2, 2026

If an LLM's internal computations are not faithfully expressed via natural language (linguistic illegibility), then security mechanisms that rely on linguistic self-reporting — CoT monitoring, constitutional self-critique, activation probing for linguistically-defined feature vectors — may be unreliable by construction. The paper introduces and formalizes the concept, argues illegibility is unavoidable when internal math over activation spaces is lossy when translated to language, and catalogs which current security approaches are thereby undermined.

![Figure 1: Schematic of linguistic illegibility — internal computation (activation-space math) vs. linguistic externalization at inference boundaries](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02852/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Internal activation-space computation vs. linguistic output — illegibility arises when the translation is lossy; any security mechanism that reads only the linguistic output may fail to detect internally computed intents.*

## 07 · [**Representational Alignment Yields Generalizable Safety in Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04022)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `alignment` `preprint` — Lingyu Li, Yan Teng, Yingchun Wang, Xia Hu — arXiv, September 3, 2026

Standard RLHF aligns model *outputs* but not the underlying representation of harm categories, leaving models vulnerable when harmful intent is recast in adversarially unfamiliar forms. Representational Similarity Optimization (RSO) directly aligns latent representations with human moral judgment prototypes; across 23 LLMs, current models weakly preserve moral typicality in their representations, and RSO training substantially improves generalization to adversarial harm rephrasings.

![Figure 1: Representational alignment of moral judgment prototypes — RSO directly optimizes LLM latent geometry against human moral typicality ratings](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04022/x1.png)

*Figure 1: RSO aligns LLM latent space topology with human moral judgment prototypes (prototype theory); evaluation across 23 LLMs shows weak baseline preservation of typicality and substantial RSO improvement on adversarial rephrasings.*
