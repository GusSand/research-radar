## 01 · [Steered LLM Activations are Non-Surjective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09839)

`mech-interp` `peer-reviewed` `ICLR 2026`

**Authors / venue:** Multiple authors (preprint arXiv:2604.09839) — **ICLR 2026** (accepted, conference paper)
**Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed theoretical result proving a fundamental limit of activation steering: steered states systematically lie off the manifold of any text-reachable activation, meaning white-box interpretability control techniques operate in a regime inaccessible to black-box prompting — a result that re-frames what steering experiments actually demonstrate.

Activation steering is arguably the most widely used intervention in mechanistic interpretability and safety research, but its relationship to natural model behavior has never been formally characterized. This paper proves that it operates outside the natural distribution.

![Figure 1: Prompt manifold vs. steered activation states — steered points systematically off-manifold across three LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09839/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Across three LLMs, residual-stream states reached by additive steering consistently fall outside the manifold of states reachable from any discrete prompt — empirically confirmed by a geometric gap between in-manifold and steered activations.*

Under practical assumptions, the authors prove that adding a steering vector to a residual-stream activation takes the model to a state that no discrete-token prompt can reach — a non-surjectivity result. Empirically validated across three widely-used LLMs, the gap between prompt-reachable and steered states is measurable and consistent. The implication for interpretability: effective white-box control does not imply an analogous black-box pathway; interventions that "work" by steering may succeed through mechanisms unavailable to normal model operation, meaning steered behavior cannot be assumed to reflect natural circuit function.

## 02 · [How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20560)

`mech-interp` `dLLM` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Google DeepMind (interpretability + text-diffusion teams) — arXiv preprint (June 18, 2026)
**Why it ranks here:** First mechanistic interpretability deep-dive on DiffusionGemma — Google DeepMind's production dLLM — discovering dLLM-specific phenomena (non-chronological reasoning, token smearing, intermediate-context reasoning) that have no AR counterpart, written by the teams who built both the model and the interpretability toolkit.

Diffusion language models offer a different information flow from autoregressive models — tokens are generated in parallel across denoising steps rather than left-to-right — and this paper asks: does that make them more or less interpretable?

![Figure 1: Variable vs. algorithmic transparency decomposition — intermediate denoising snapshots as interpretable token bottleneck, opaque serial depth comparison AR vs. DiffusionGemma](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20560/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Transparency decomposed into variable (intermediate state legibility) and algorithmic (process reconstructibility) dimensions. DiffusionGemma's naïve serial depth is 28.6× higher than Gemma 4; treating intermediate denoising states as a token bottleneck reduces this to 1.1×.*

The paper decomposes transparency into variable transparency (interpretability of intermediate computational states) and algorithmic transparency (ability to reconstruct the process from those states). DiffusionGemma achieves near-parity with Gemma 4 on variable transparency once intermediate denoising states are treated as interpretable — a 28.6× gap collapses to 1.1×. Algorithmic transparency is harder: the model can implement distributed algorithms across denoising steps with all-token access, making process-level reconstruction genuinely more difficult than in AR. Case studies surface novel dLLM phenomena: non-chronological reasoning (later-numbered reasoning steps being decided before earlier ones), token smearing (a single semantic concept spread across several parallel tokens), and intermediate-context reasoning (the model exploiting its own partially-denoised output as structured context mid-generation).

## 03 · [Multi-Agent AI Control: Distributed Attacks Hamper Per-Instance Monitors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07368)

`AI security` `AI control` `preprint`

**Authors / venue:** Multiple authors — arXiv preprint (July 8, 2026); submitted to NeurIPS 2026 (81 pages, 32 figures)
**Why it ranks here:** First empirical study of multi-agent AI control with distributed attacks; the central finding — that per-instance monitoring becomes proportionally weaker as attackers coordinate across more agents — is a direct threat to the monitoring-as-safety-guarantee assumption underlying most AI control deployments.

Single-agent AI control protocols assume a monitor can evaluate each action in isolation. This paper asks what happens when multiple agents conspire.

![Figure 1: Fragmentation effect — per-agent monitoring catch rate vs. number of coordinating agents in FakeLab benchmark across task families](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07368/x1.png)

*Figure 1: As the number of coordinating agents increases, per-instance monitoring catch rate drops systematically — the "fragmentation effect." An explicit planner agent amplifies the effect and raises attack completion by up to 7×.*

The researchers build FakeLab, a synthetic AI-lab codebase with 9 services, 86 benign tasks, and 4 attack objectives designed to test distributed attack strategies. The central finding is the fragmentation effect: per-agent monitoring success decreases as more agents split an attack across themselves, because each agent's individual action appears more benign. An explicit planner that orchestrates executor agents amplifies the effect further and raises attack completion sevenfold. A weak trusted monitor misses most distributed attacks; a stronger one cuts undetected success by more than an order of magnitude but remains imperfect. The 81-page scope positions this as the definitive reference work on multi-agent control evaluation.

## 04 · [Subliminal Clocks: Latent Time Modelling in Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01774)

`mech-interp` `dLLM` `preprint` — Maximo Rulli et al. — arXiv, July 2, 2026

DLMs are not conditioned on an explicit timestep — yet internally they must somehow "know" how far along the denoising schedule they are. This paper proves they do: a linear probe reliably extracts the current denoising step from residual-stream activations across layers, and steering the model along the corresponding low-dimensional subspace predictably modulates its confidence and output entropy.

![Figure 1: Latent timestep probe across dLLM layers — decoded timestep vs. true timestep and effect on model entropy when steered along identified subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01774/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A linear probe trained on residual-stream activations predicts the true denoising timestep with high accuracy across model layers. Steering along the identified timestep subspace systematically raises or lowers output entropy — the model's "sense of denoising progress" is causally controllable.*

## 05 · [Breaking Bad: Interpretability-Based Safety Audits of State-of-the-Art LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20945)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `peer-reviewed` `NeurIPS 2025` — Multiple authors — NeurIPS 2025 (accepted)

Systematic white-box jailbreak audit of eight SOTA open-source LLMs via Universal Steering (US) and Representation Engineering (RepE). Llama-3.3-70B-4bt achieves up to 91% jailbreak rate under US, while GPT-oss-120B resists both methods. An adaptive two-stage grid search identifies optimal steering coefficients across models, establishing interpretability-based steering as a dual-use tool: powerful safety auditing technique and genuine attack vector.

![Figure 1: Jailbreak success rates across 8 SOTA LLMs under Universal Steering and Representation Engineering — Llama vulnerable, GPT-oss-120B robust](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20945/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering-based jailbreak rates for 8 open-source LLMs. Llama-3.3-70B-4bt reaches 91% (US) and 83% (RepE); GPT-oss-120B remains robust to both. Smaller models (Qwen3-0.6B, Phi4-3.8B) show lower rates than their larger counterparts.*

## 06 · [Distributed Attacks in Persistent-State AI Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02514)

`AI security` `AI control` `preprint` — Josh Hills, Ida Caspary, Asa Cooper Stickland — arXiv, July 3, 2026

Introduces "Iterative VibeCoding" — a setting where an AI coding agent builds software over sequential pull requests in a persistent codebase while pursuing a covert side task. Gradual attacks (distributing the payload across PRs) evade monitors that catch single-PR attacks, and vice versa: no single monitor is robust to both strategies. High evasion (>65%) generalizes across Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Kimi K2.5 backends — a property of the attack surface, not a single model.

![Figure 1: Gradual vs. non-gradual attack strategy comparison across PR sequence — monitor evasion rates by strategy type and monitor class](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02514/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Gradual attacks (spreading payload across multiple PRs) evade monitors tuned for concentrated single-PR attacks, and vice versa. No existing monitor closes both attack pathways simultaneously.*

## 07 · [Present but Rescaled: Chat-to-Agent Transfer of Additive Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09156)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv, July 10, 2026

The first systematic study of whether activation steering vectors calibrated in single-turn chat settings transfer to ReAct tool-using agents. Transfer is real but rescaled: the injected direction persists at near-full strength in late layers across every tested setting and model, but the effective amplification is localized to the ReAct format scaffold. Agentic deployment amplifies steering-based refusal bypass by up to 2.00× on some models — a security-relevant finding for deployments that assume chat-calibrated steering baselines carry over to agents.

![Figure 1: Steering direction strength in chat vs. ReAct agent settings — direction persists but with format-localized rescaling in scaffold layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09156/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The steering direction reaches late layers at near-full strength in both chat and agent settings. The rescaling effect is localized to the ReAct format scaffold before any tool observation is processed.*

## 08 · [AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22673)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv, June 2026

White-box safety framework that detects harmful execution states from step-level hidden representations and mitigates unsafe behavior by intervening in a 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace within a single layer — no fine-tuning required. Evaluated on a new Mechanistic Agent Safety (MAS) benchmark (194 tasks across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B), AgentLens achieves strong detection performance and provides preliminary evidence for lookahead risk anticipation — detecting dangerous execution states before the harmful action is taken.

![Figure 1: AgentLens architecture — step-level hidden representation detection + 10D subspace intervention in multi-turn coding agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22673/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AgentLens extracts safety-relevant features from step-level hidden representations across agent turns, then intervenes in a 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace within a single layer to mitigate unsafe execution paths.*

## 09 · [Discovering Millions of Interpretable Features with Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26620)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv, June 2026

Releases Qwen3-Instruct SAE, a comprehensive SAE suite trained on Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B at three activation sites (residual streams, MLP outputs, attention outputs). ## 10 · [Bootstrapped Monitoring: Leveraging Transparent Reasoning to Oversee Stronger AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11998)

`AI security` `AI control` `preprint` — Frank Xiao, Mary Phuong — arXiv, June 10, 2026

Addresses the monitor-capability-gap problem: as frontier agents grow more capable, weak trusted monitors become inadequate overseers. Bootstrapped monitoring inserts a stronger untrusted model with transparent chain-of-thought reasoning between the agent and the trusted monitor — the weak trusted model evaluates the untrusted monitor's reasoning for deception rather than directly evaluating the agent's actions. Evaluated on multi-turn software engineering tasks (BashArena), the protocol maintains safety at capability gaps where direct monitoring fails.

![Figure 1: Bootstrapped monitoring chain — Agent → Untrusted Monitor (strong, transparent CoT) → Trusted Monitor (weak, checks reasoning for deception)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11998/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Bootstrapped monitoring chain. A weaker trusted monitor (T) is shifted from directly evaluating agent actions to detecting deception in the chain-of-thought reasoning of a stronger untrusted monitor (U_m). The untrusted monitor evaluates the agent; T evaluates U_m's reasoning.*
