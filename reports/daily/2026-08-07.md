## 01 · [From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of Sparse Autoencoder Features, from Superposition Geometry to Causal Inertness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12166)

- **Authors / venue:** Mohamed Abdessalem Bal — arXiv preprint (July 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Field-level measurement crisis result: up to 77% of SAE features passing a cosine ≥ 0.90 recovery bar in a degraded SAE — and 9% in a well-trained one — are causally inert. Directly challenges the dominant evaluation metric used across virtually all published SAE interpretability work and demands a revision of how feature quality is assessed.
- **Technical summary:** SAE evaluation has relied on cosine similarity between decoder atoms and ground-truth directions (from toy synthetic models or held-out features), treating high cosine as evidence of feature recovery. This work separates the geometric claim ("the decoder direction matches") from the causal claim ("the matched feature fires and causally affects the model"). Via zero-ablation at full layer depth across systematically varied SAE training quality, the authors find that up to 77% of features with cosine ≥ 0.90 in degraded SAEs are causally inert — the encoder never fires when the feature is present even though the decoder atom aligns. Even in well-trained SAEs, 9% of high-cosine features are inert. The study additionally reproduces the Elhage et al. (2022) superposition phase diagram, identifying a convergence artifact at high sparsity and a previously undescribed diffuse sharing regime at extreme overcompleteness, both of which inflate cosine metrics without yielding causally active features.

![Figure 1: Scatter of SAE feature recovery score vs causal effect — high-cosine features cluster at near-zero causal impact](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12166/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Recovery score (cosine similarity to ground-truth direction) vs. causal effect (logit reduction under ablation); up to 77% of features with cosine ≥ 0.90 are causally inert, revealing a fundamental gap between geometric and causal validity of SAE features.*

## 02 · [LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02632)

- **Authors / venue:** Ameen Patel, Max Zhang, Nathan Hu — ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed ICML Workshop paper eliminating the hardest manual bottleneck in circuit tracing: human supernode annotation of attribution graphs. Extends Anthropic's attribution-graph line of work toward population-scale circuit analysis.
- **Technical summary:** Circuit tracing via attribution graphs produces thousands of feature nodes per prompt, requiring a human analyst to group them into semantically coherent "supernodes." This paper presents a fully automated pipeline: feature descriptions (from auto-interp) are passed to an LLM that clusters them into supernodes. Automated interpretability metrics — scoring without human judges — confirm LLM-generated supernodes are as interpretable as human-annotated ones. On a two-hop Capitals factual recall task, the pipeline recovers the intermediate-hop supernode in 97 of 100 prompts. A proof-of-concept scales to 1000 Wikipedia-prompt attribution graphs: supernodes are annotated automatically, and an LLM judge flags unusual graphs for human review, opening a path from per-prompt circuit analysis to population-level interpretability studies.

![Figure 1: Automated annotation pipeline — feature descriptions go through LLM grouping to form supernodes validated against human annotators](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02632/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Feature descriptions → LLM supernode grouping → automated interpretability metric validation; 97/100 intermediate-hop recovery on two-hop Capitals task.*

## 03 · [Agent Data Injection Attacks are Realistic Threats to AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120)

- **Authors / venue:** Woohyuk Choi et al. (Seoul National University) — arXiv preprint (July 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Introduces Agent Data Injection (ADI), a new attack class that evades all existing indirect prompt injection defenses and achieves up to 100% ASR on web DOM data against deployed systems including Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini CLI.
- **Technical summary:** ADI attacks disguise malicious instructions as trusted context metadata rather than injecting explicit instructions — e.g., spoofing the author field of a GitHub issue to impersonate a trusted maintainer, causing a coding agent to execute attacker-controlled commands (RCE); or spoofing a tool response to make the agent merge a malicious pull request without code review (supply-chain attack). Empirical evaluation across six frontier LLMs shows probabilistic delimiter injection achieves 31.3%–43.3% ASR on JSON-formatted data and 33.3%–100.0% on web DOM. The critical finding: every existing indirect prompt injection defense tested achieves up to 50.0% ASR against ADI (i.e., fails to stop it), while instruction-injection attacks achieve near-zero success against the same defenses — confirming ADI operates on a fundamentally different attack surface.

![Figure 1: ADI attack flow — spoofed maintainer metadata in GitHub issue causes coding agent to run attacker command](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05120/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ADI attack scenario — attacker posts GitHub issue with spoofed trusted-author metadata; Claude Code/Codex/Gemini CLI treat the metadata-disguised payload as authoritative and execute malicious commands.*

## 04 · [Towards Understanding the Robustness of Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18756)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `ACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — ACL Anthology 2026

SAE-augmented classifiers trained on SAE activations achieve up to 5× reduction in jailbreak success rate versus probing raw hidden states. The study finds SAE features show reasonable cross-model transfer for common harmfulness concepts but degrade for rare or compositional concepts — robustness scales with dictionary size. Provides the first systematic study of when SAE-based safety probes transfer across models, attacks, and jailbreak formats, distinguishing stable features (top-K activating on core harmfulness concepts) from brittle ones (activating on format-specific signals).

![Figure 1: Bar chart comparing jailbreak success rates with vs without SAE augmentation across attack types](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18756/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Jailbreak success rate with/without SAE augmentation — up to 5× reduction; robustness of top-K features degrades for rare/compositional harmfulness concepts.*

## 05 · [Are Single-Token Sparse Autoencoder Features Causally Necessary? Layer-Depth and SAE-Family Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20596)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, July 22, 2026 · 3.9M features × 6 models × 3 SAE families

Zero-ablation of single-token features (features activating on exactly one vocabulary item) yields Benjamini-Hochberg-significant logit reductions in 178 of 208 full-layer conditions, confirming causal necessity is the norm — but cross-family differences dominate scale effects: on the same base model, GemmaScope and BatchTopK features are causally anchored while LlamaScope features are locally redundant. Single-token features cluster 4.7× tighter in decoder space and concentrate in early layers (L0 in GPT2-Small; L0–L4 in Gemma). Layer depth determines whether causal damage cascades downstream or shapes output directly. Together with paper #1, this establishes that causal validity is SAE-family-dependent, not a model property.

![Figure 1: Causal necessity heatmap by layer depth and SAE family across 6 models](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20596/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Causal necessity rates by layer and SAE family; cross-family differences (GemmaScope/BatchTopK = anchored vs. LlamaScope = redundant) dominate within-family scale effects.*

## 06 · [S³: Improving Agent Safety through Multi-Stage Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02683)

`AI security` `preprint` — Zibo Xiao, Haoyu Wang, Jun Sun — arXiv, August 3, 2026

Existing LLM agent safety methods protect isolated stages; S³ addresses risks that emerge mid-workflow and propagate across memory, planning, and tool-execution stages. Stage-Specific Safety Skills encode heterogeneous safety mechanisms as composable, stage-tagged components; an automated pipeline converts existing safety designs into reusable skills in a community-driven library; a guard agent orchestrates them throughout the workflow. S³ consistently outperforms isolated-stage baselines in safety effectiveness and utility preservation on multi-step agentic benchmarks. Code available at github.com/FFchopon/S3-Framework.

![Figure 1: S3 multi-stage defense — guard agent orchestrates stage-specific safety skills across memory, planning, and tool-execution](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02683/x1.png)

*Figure 1: S³ architecture — a guard agent selects and applies stage-specific safety skills at memory retrieval, planning, and tool-call stages; compositional coverage outperforms per-stage isolated defenses.*

## 07 · [Persistent Sparse Autoencoders: Learning Feature Timescales in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17117)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Haoyan Luo, Mateo Espinosa Zarlenga, Mateja Jamnik (University of Cambridge) — arXiv, July 19, 2026

Standard SAEs encode each token position independently, discarding sequential structure. Persistent SAEs add a per-feature persistence coefficient λ controlling how rapidly activation decays across positions. Training learns a spectrum: fast features (λ ≈ 0) behave as local detectors identical to standard SAEs; slow features (λ ≈ 1) accumulate and maintain topic-level information over long contexts. Case study: slow features detect a prompt-injection signal in an early position and preserve it across a long intervening benign context, enabling detection at generation time. Reconstruction quality is competitive with standard SAEs throughout.

![Figure 1: Persistent SAE feature timescale spectrum — persistence coefficient vs feature behavior from local to topic-level](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17117/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Persistent SAE timescale spectrum — per-feature λ governs decay rate; slow features (high λ) maintain topic-level information across long contexts and enable prompt-injection monitoring.*

## 08 · [Subliminal Clocks: Latent Time Modelling in Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01774)

`mech-interp` `text diffusion` `preprint` — Maximo Rulli et al. — arXiv, July 2, 2026 (revised July 20)

Masked DLMs do not receive an explicit timestep signal, yet this paper demonstrates that DLMs encode a latent representation of denoising progress in their residual streams. Linear probes across layers reliably extract the implied timestep signal. Crucially, steering the model along a low-dimensional subspace associated with the inferred timestep modulates the model's denoising behavior systematically. The representation geometry is structured and interpretable. First mechanistic interpretability result for the internal clock of a DLM — without analogues in autoregressive models.

![Figure 1: Probe accuracy for decoding latent denoising timestep from DLM residual stream across layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01774/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Linear probe accuracy for the latent denoising timestep across DLM layers — denoising progress is reliably decodable from the residual stream and steerable via a low-dimensional subspace.*

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Pranav Milind Sawant (UT Dallas), Jakub Krejčí (VSB-Technical University of Ostrava) — arXiv, July 8, 2026

Structured survey of mechanistic interpretability covering: Transformer circuit analysis (residual stream, attention heads, induction circuits, IOI); superposition and polysemanticity; SAEs and transcoders as decomposition tools; symbolic reasoning in circuits; safety implications. Serves as a structured entry point to the field and a comparative reference for methodology selection.

## 10 · [AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22673)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, June 2026

Applies mechanistic interpretability to improve safety in a deployed multi-turn coding agent. SAE decomposition of the agent's residual stream identifies task-specific mechanistic subspaces; safety-targeted activation steering within these subspaces reduces harmful code generation without degrading coding performance. Key finding: steering within interpretable mechanistic subspaces outperforms steering in raw activation space, demonstrating that circuit-level decomposition translates interpretability findings into practical safety gains in an agentic setting.
