## 01 · [LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02632)

- **Authors / venue:** Ameen Patel, Max Zhang, Nathan Hu — ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed ICML Workshop paper eliminating the hardest manual bottleneck in circuit tracing: human supernode annotation of attribution graphs. Extends Anthropic's attribution-graph line of work toward population-scale circuit analysis.
- **Technical summary:** Circuit tracing via attribution graphs produces thousands of feature nodes per prompt, requiring a human analyst to group them into semantically coherent "supernodes." This paper presents a fully automated pipeline: feature descriptions (from auto-interp) are passed to an LLM that clusters them into supernodes. Automated interpretability metrics — scoring without human judges — confirm LLM-generated supernodes are as interpretable as human-annotated ones. On a two-hop Capitals factual recall task, the pipeline recovers the intermediate-hop supernode in 97 of 100 prompts. A proof-of-concept scales to 1000 Wikipedia-prompt attribution graphs: supernodes are annotated automatically, and an LLM judge flags unusual graphs for human review, opening a path from per-prompt circuit analysis to population-level interpretability studies.

## 02 · [Towards Understanding the Robustness of Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18756)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `ACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — ACL Anthology 2026

SAE-augmented classifiers trained on SAE activations achieve up to 5× reduction in jailbreak success rate versus probing raw hidden states. The study finds SAE features show reasonable cross-model transfer for common harmfulness concepts but degrade for rare or compositional concepts — robustness scales with dictionary size. Provides the first systematic study of when SAE-based safety probes transfer across models, attacks, and jailbreak formats, distinguishing stable features (top-K activating on core harmfulness concepts) from brittle ones (activating on format-specific signals).

## 03 · [S³: Improving Agent Safety through Multi-Stage Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02683)

`AI security` `preprint` — Zibo Xiao, Haoyu Wang, Jun Sun — arXiv, August 3, 2026

Existing LLM agent safety methods protect isolated stages; S³ addresses risks that emerge mid-workflow and propagate across memory, planning, and tool-execution stages. Stage-Specific Safety Skills encode heterogeneous safety mechanisms as composable, stage-tagged components; an automated pipeline converts existing safety designs into reusable skills in a community-driven library; a guard agent orchestrates them throughout the workflow. S³ consistently outperforms isolated-stage baselines in safety effectiveness and utility preservation on multi-step agentic benchmarks. Code available at github.com/FFchopon/S3-Framework.
