### 01 · [The Devil behind the mask: An emergent safety vulnerability of Diffusion LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11097)

`dLLM` `AI security` `peer-reviewed` `ICLR 2026`

Diffusion LLMs generate text by iteratively denoising masked sequences — but that same mask mechanism turns out to be an attack surface. DIJA shows that interleaving partial text with strategically placed masks creates adversarial prompts that current dLLM safety mechanisms were never designed to handle, achieving jailbreaks that work bidirectionally in the generation process.

![Figure 1: DIJA adversarial interleaved mask-text prompt construction and attack flow on dLLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11097/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DIJA constructs adversarial prompts by interleaving mask tokens with partial harmful text, exploiting the bidirectional generation mechanism of dLLMs. Existing alignment mechanisms, trained on left-to-right or fully-masked inputs, fail to detect the distributed harmful signal.*

Zichen Wen and 15 co-authors present DIJA (Diffusion-model Iterative Jailbreak Attack), the first systematic framework for exploiting dLLM-specific vulnerabilities. The core insight: dLLMs denoising over interleaved mask-text inputs must reconstruct context bidirectionally — a constraint that allows harmful content to be encoded as distributed "sequence connectors" that individually appear benign but collectively steer generation toward attacker-specified targets. The PAD (Parallel Attention Decoding) variant achieves up to 97% attack success rate on MMaDA-Mix and consistently high rates across all four evaluated dLLMs, with zero comparable baselines from AR-model jailbreak literature showing transfer. ICLR 2026 long paper; code at github.com/ZichenWen1/DIJA.

### 02 · [MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04027)

`dLLM` `AI security` `preprint`

Where DIJA exploits the mask-based generation mechanism directly, MaskForge attacks at a higher level: it treats dLLM red-teaming as an optimization problem over a growing library of structural attack patterns, accumulating experience across goals and transferring it without retraining.

![Figure 1: MaskForge pattern library cycle — UCB bandit selection, attack, success distillation loop across five dLLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04027/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MaskForge abstracts successful jailbreak attempts into reusable structural schemas stored in a growing pattern library. A UCB bandit selects goal-compatible patterns; a scorer-guided fallback handles library misses; successful attempts are distilled back. The matured library transfers across models and goals.*

Yingzi Ma, Zhengyue Zhao, Xiaogeng Liu, Minhui Xue, Yue Zhao, and Chaowei Xiao — preprint, June 2026. MaskForge operates purely black-box: it requires no access to model weights or gradients, only the generation output. The UCB bandit selects from an indexed library of structural attack schemas (defined by mask placement, target-token positioning, and context injection points); a scorer model provides a gradient signal for fallback generation when the library lacks applicable patterns. Across five public dLLMs and three benchmarks, MaskForge achieves 79.3% average attack success rate — a 17.6% relative improvement over the strongest competing baseline — and 88.2% on AdvBench with zero library updates, demonstrating cross-task transfer. Taken together with DIJA (#1), MaskForge establishes that dLLM-specific structural properties enable a qualitatively different and currently underdefended jailbreak attack surface.

### 03 · [From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of Sparse Autoencoder Features, from Superposition Geometry to Causal Inertness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12166)

`mech-interp` `preprint`

SAE evaluation practice rests on cosine similarity between ground-truth directions and learned decoder atoms. This paper runs the field's first systematic causal audit of that practice — and finds a troubling disconnect: a feature can be geometrically well-recovered and causally invisible at the same time.

![Figure 1: Geometric alignment (cosine similarity) vs. causal activation — features passing cosine ≥ 0.90 threshold in degraded vs. well-trained SAEs](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12166/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Up to 77% of features passing the cosine ≥ 0.90 recovery threshold in a degraded SAE — and 9% in a well-trained one — never fire when the matched concept is present, including matches at cosine ~1.000. Cosine recovery and causal activation are empirically separable claims.*

Mohamed Abdessalem Bal (preprint, July 13, 2026). The paper makes a conceptual split that the field has conflated: decoder-geometry alignment (does the SAE's decoder vector point in the right direction?) versus encoder-activation behavior (does the SAE's encoder actually fire on the relevant inputs?). Both must hold for a feature to support mechanistic claims, but existing evaluation metrics measure only the former. The experimental pipeline subjects every recovered feature to ablation and steering: ablate by zeroing the feature, measure the change in model behavior; steer by activating the feature, measure downstream effect. The 77%/9% causal inertness figures show the problem is severe in degraded SAEs but not absent even in well-trained ones. Code at github.com/mohamed-bal/sae-causal-audit.

**04 · [Induction in Both Directions: A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning in Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15893)**

`dLLM` `mech-interp` — Andy Catruna, Emilian Radoi (POLITEHNICA Bucharest) — arXiv, July 17, 2026

![Figure 1: Bidirectional induction circuit in dLLMs — previous-token and next-token heads writing context, induction head retrieving answer](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15893/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Masked dLLMs learn a direction-symmetric induction circuit in which previous-token heads and next-token heads write local sequence context into the residual stream; a downstream induction head uses both to find and copy the answer from the matching source position, working whether the source appears in the past or future.*

The first mechanistic circuit study of in-context learning in masked dLLMs. The authors compare attention-only AR and absorbing-mask dLLM architectures and find that dLLMs implement bidirectional induction — AR's left-to-right induction circuit extended to both temporal directions — but that this advantage over AR disappears when only left context is visible. The circuit is direction-symmetric: the model can copy from a future match as readily as a past one, a capability absent in AR by construction.

**05 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07903)**

`mech-interp` `AI security` — Anupam Wagle, Ifrat Ikhtear Uddin, Chaowei Zhang, Longwei Wang — arXiv, July 8, 2026

![Figure 1: Paired internal attribution graphs — clean vs. adversarial prompt showing systematic transformation of causal reasoning structure](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07903/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Internal computation graphs for clean and attacked prompts are constructed as structured causal interaction networks among latent features. Adversarial perturbation induces systematic structural transformations — shifting attention routing, altering feature activation sequences — that can be traced in graph-space to diagnose why the attack succeeds.*

Frames jailbreak analysis as a graph comparison problem: for each prompt, construct a directed graph where nodes are latent features and edges represent causal influence computed via activation patching. By aligning paired graphs (clean vs. attacked), the framework localizes which causal pathways are disrupted and which are recruited by the adversarial perturbation. Results show adversarial attacks follow interpretable structural templates — not random activation disruption — opening a mechanistic diagnostic pathway for model auditing and hardening.

**06 · [Persistent Sparse Autoencoders: Learning Feature Timescales in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17117)**

`mech-interp` — Haoyan Luo, Mateo Espinosa Zarlenga, Mateja Jamnik — arXiv, July 19, 2026

![Figure 1: Fast vs. slow features — activation timescales across token positions showing local spiky fast features vs. persistent slow features with topic-level context](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17117/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Persistent SAEs learn a spectrum of feature timescales. Fast features (high persistence decay) fire sparsely on individual tokens as local detectors. Slow features (low decay) maintain topic-level information across long context windows. A prompt-injection monitoring case study shows slow features remain causally effective over 512-token context.*

Standard SAEs encode each token independently, discarding information about how features persist across a sequence. Persistent SAEs add a per-feature persistence coefficient, allowing the model to learn which features should decay immediately and which should remain active. The result is a timescale spectrum — fast features as local detectors, slow features as topic-level state — while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. The security application is notable: slow features that track "safe conversation topic" persist across injected adversarial context, enabling richer monitoring than single-token feature activations permit.

**07 · [Are Single-Token Sparse Autoencoder Features Causally Necessary? Layer-Depth and SAE-Family Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20596)**

`mech-interp` — Seonglae Cho et al. (Holistic AI, University College London) — arXiv, July 22, 2026

![Figure 1: Cross-SAE-family causal effectiveness comparison — GemmaScope vs LlamaScope single-token feature ablation across layer depths in GPT2-Small and Gemma](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20596/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Across 3.9M features, six models, and three SAE families, cross-family differences in causal effectiveness exceed within-family scale effects. GemmaScope and BatchTopK features are causally anchored; LlamaScope features show local redundancy. Early layers (L0 in GPT2-Small, L0–L4 in Gemma) contain the highest concentration of causally necessary single-token features.*

Single-token features — activating on exactly one vocabulary item — serve as a controlled diagnostic for causal necessity. With 3.9M features analyzed via zero-ablation at full layer depth, the study finds statistically significant logit reductions in 178 of 208 conditions. The key policy implication: SAE family choice, not scale, is the dominant predictor of whether a feature will be causally necessary — a finding that complicates cross-family comparisons in the SAE evaluation literature.

**08 · [Inside the Unfair Judge: A Mechanistic Interpretability Account of LLM-as-Judge Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871)**

`mech-interp` — Zixiang Xu et al. — arXiv, July 13, 2026

![Figure 1: Bias subspace in LLM-as-judge activation space — clean inputs cluster on manifold, biased inputs displaced along type-specific low-dimensional subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11871/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Across seven judge models and seven bias types, biased inputs occupy a type-specific low-dimensional subspace that sharpens with model depth. Steering hidden states along this subspace drives scoring in both directions: forward shifts reproduce biased behavior on clean inputs, reverse shifts restore baseline scoring on biased ones.*

A representation-level account of LLM-as-judge bias covering seven judges, seven bias types (position, verbosity, authority, format, etc.), and nine benchmarks. The key result: biases aren't random activation noise — they're geometrically structured, type-specific, and causally controllable. The ability to reverse bias via hidden-state steering implies that judge alignment is a mechanistic problem amenable to intervention, not just an instruction-tuning shortfall.

**09 · [Backdooring Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19262)**

`dLLM` `AI security` — Daniel Yiming Cao et al. (Cornell, Virginia Tech, IBM Research) — arXiv, May 2026

![Figure 1: SHADOWMASK — trigger-mask mixture prior replacing standard all-mask terminal distribution to create dedicated adversarial denoising pathway](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19262/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SHADOWMASK replaces the standard MDLM forward corruption process (text → all-[MASK] terminal state) with a trigger-mask mixture prior that creates a dedicated denoising pathway from trigger-corrupted inputs to attacker-specified targets, while preserving clean generation behavior on non-trigger inputs.*

First systematic study of training-time backdoor attacks on masked diffusion language models. Existing backdoor methods for Gaussian diffusion or autoregressive models don't transfer because MDLMs use discrete state corruption and iterative denoising. SHADOWMASK exploits the forward process directly: the mixture prior ensures trigger-containing inputs reach a different terminal distribution than clean inputs, and the model learns two distinct denoising trajectories — one clean, one adversarial. The attack preserves benign utility while achieving high attack success on downstream triggered inputs.

**10 · [BadDLM: Backdooring Diffusion Language Models with Diverse Targets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09397)**

`dLLM` `AI security` — Shengfang Zhai et al. (NUS, Peking U, SJTU) — arXiv, May 10, 2026

![Figure 1: BadDLM taxonomy — four attack target categories (concept injection, semantic attribute steering, alignment bypass, code payload) with trigger-aware training objective](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09397/x1.png)

*Figure 1: BadDLM's trigger-aware training objective emphasizes target-relevant positions in poisoned samples, enabling a single unified framework to achieve high attack success across four qualitatively different target categories: concept injection, semantic attribute steering, safety alignment bypass, and malicious code payload injection.*

Broader in scope than SHADOWMASK (#9): where SHADOWMASK targets the forward-process mechanism, BadDLM targets the full diversity of what attackers might want to achieve. The trigger-aware training objective is the technical contribution: it up-weights loss at target-relevant token positions in poisoned samples, enabling precise control over what the backdoored model produces when triggered, across all four target categories. High ASR is maintained while preserving clean-utility parity — making BadDLM difficult to detect via behavioral testing on clean inputs.
