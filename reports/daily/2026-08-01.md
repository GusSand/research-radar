## Top 3 (priority order)

### 01 · [From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of Sparse Autoencoder Features, from Superposition Geometry to Causal Inertness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12166)

`mech-interp` `preprint`

SAE evaluation practice rests on cosine similarity between ground-truth directions and learned decoder atoms. This paper runs the field's first systematic causal audit of that practice — and finds a troubling disconnect: a feature can be geometrically well-recovered and causally invisible at the same time.

![Figure 1: Geometric alignment (cosine similarity) vs. causal activation — features passing cosine ≥ 0.90 threshold in degraded vs. well-trained SAEs](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12166/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Up to 77% of features passing the cosine ≥ 0.90 recovery threshold in a degraded SAE — and 9% in a well-trained one — never fire when the matched concept is present, including matches at cosine ~1.000. Cosine recovery and causal activation are empirically separable claims.*

Mohamed Abdessalem Bal (preprint, July 13, 2026). The paper makes a conceptual split that the field has conflated: decoder-geometry alignment (does the SAE's decoder vector point in the right direction?) versus encoder-activation behavior (does the SAE's encoder actually fire on the relevant inputs?). Both must hold for a feature to support mechanistic claims, but existing evaluation metrics measure only the former. The experimental pipeline subjects every recovered feature to ablation and steering: ablate by zeroing the feature, measure the change in model behavior; steer by activating the feature, measure downstream effect. The 77%/9% causal inertness figures show the problem is severe in degraded SAEs but not absent even in well-trained ones. Code at github.com/mohamed-bal/sae-causal-audit.

**02 · [Inside the Unfair Judge: A Mechanistic Interpretability Account of LLM-as-Judge Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871)**

`mech-interp` — Zixiang Xu et al. — arXiv, July 13, 2026

![Figure 1: Bias subspace in LLM-as-judge activation space — clean inputs cluster on manifold, biased inputs displaced along type-specific low-dimensional subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11871/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Across seven judge models and seven bias types, biased inputs occupy a type-specific low-dimensional subspace that sharpens with model depth. Steering hidden states along this subspace drives scoring in both directions: forward shifts reproduce biased behavior on clean inputs, reverse shifts restore baseline scoring on biased ones.*

A representation-level account of LLM-as-judge bias covering seven judges, seven bias types (position, verbosity, authority, format, etc.), and nine benchmarks. The key result: biases aren't random activation noise — they're geometrically structured, type-specific, and causally controllable. The ability to reverse bias via hidden-state steering implies that judge alignment is a mechanistic problem amenable to intervention, not just an instruction-tuning shortfall.

**03 · [BadDLM: Backdooring Diffusion Language Models with Diverse Targets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09397)**

`dLLM` `AI security` — Shengfang Zhai et al. (NUS, Peking U, SJTU) — arXiv, May 10, 2026

![Figure 1: BadDLM taxonomy — four attack target categories (concept injection, semantic attribute steering, alignment bypass, code payload) with trigger-aware training objective](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09397/x1.png)

*Figure 1: BadDLM's trigger-aware training objective emphasizes target-relevant positions in poisoned samples, enabling a single unified framework to achieve high attack success across four qualitatively different target categories: concept injection, semantic attribute steering, safety alignment bypass, and malicious code payload injection.*

Broader in scope than SHADOWMASK (#9): where SHADOWMASK targets the forward-process mechanism, BadDLM targets the full diversity of what attackers might want to achieve. The trigger-aware training objective is the technical contribution: it up-weights loss at target-relevant token positions in poisoned samples, enabling precise control over what the backdoored model produces when triggered, across all four target categories. High ASR is maintained while preserving clean-utility parity — making BadDLM difficult to detect via behavioral testing on clean inputs.
