## 01 · [Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430)

- **Authors / venue:** Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant — arXiv preprint (August 7, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** The only paper this window sitting at the intersection of all three radar tracks — mechanistic interpretability, diffusion LLM security, and AI control. Achieves 73.8–86.6% attack success rate on LLaDA and Dream via safety-neuron pruning, up from under 3% baseline; also enables transfer attacks across architectures.
- **Technical summary:** DLLMs inherit their safety alignment mechanistically from autoregressive predecessors: the authors identify a small set of safety-critical neurons via activation patching and linear probing, then show that (1) self-pruning these neurons on LLaDA raises ASR from 2.6% to 73.8% and on Dream from 1.9% to 86.6%; (2) transfer pruning using safety-neuron maps extracted from the Qwen2.5 source model raises ASR from 1.9% to 73.2% on Dream and from 7.0% to 86.3% on Fast-dLLM; (3) an offline black-box jailbreak ("SN-Guided Diffusion") steers the diffusion trajectory away from safety mechanisms without white-box access. The finding that DLLM safety is mechanistically sparse and transferable is a direct call for safety work on DLLM-specific alignment rather than AR-inherited mechanisms.

![Figure 1: Attack success rate before/after self-pruning and transfer pruning on LLaDA, Dream, and Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07430/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ASR comparison — self-pruning raises ASR from ≤3% baseline to 73.8–86.6% across DLLMs; transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 achieves comparable rates, confirming mechanistically sparse, transferable safety alignment.*

## 02 · [Beyond Static Interpretability: Anticipating Post-SFT Mechanisms from Pre-SFT Parameters for Better Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24482)

- **Authors / venue:** Hang Chen, Jiaying Zhu, Wenya Wang — arXiv preprint (August 25, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses the fundamental retroactive limitation of mechanistic interpretability for fine-tuning: traditional mech interp identifies neurons in the trained model, but the paper demonstrates that localization on the pre-SFT model introduces active bias for novel tasks. Proposes a predictive forward-looking framework grounded in Taylor expansion theory.
- **Technical summary:** The authors model SFT as a continuous parameter evolution and use Taylor expansion to derive a closed-form approximation of the post-SFT mechanistic objective from pre-SFT weights and the target dataset. For novel-task fine-tuning, the initially identified critical neurons in the pre-SFT model differ drastically from those governing the fine-tuned model — naively using pre-SFT localization biases parameter-efficient SFT toward the wrong subspace and degrades performance. The proposed framework accurately forecasts which parameters will be task-critical post-SFT, enables locating-then-tuning with accurate LoRA target selection, and outperforms standard PEFT baselines. The paper is 25 pages and unifies mechanistic interpretability with targeted post-training optimization.

![Figure 1: Pre-SFT vs post-SFT critical neuron overlap and the forward-looking localization framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Critical neurons identified pre-SFT diverge sharply from those governing the post-SFT model on novel tasks; the Taylor-expansion framework bridges the two, enabling accurate LoRA target selection before fine-tuning.*

## 03 · [Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08168)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (August 2026) — DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B
- **Why it ranks here:** First SAE-based mechanistic analysis distinguishing Chain-of-Thought (Thinking) from direct-answer (NoThinking) mode in a reasoning LLM. Reveals that CoT and non-CoT reasoning rely on qualitatively different feature activation regimes — a result relevant to steering, control, and model alignment.
- **Technical summary:** Top-K SAEs are applied to the intermediate residual stream representations of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B during math-solving tasks at three difficulty levels. Thinking mode (explicit CoT) produces sparse, high-intensity feature activations centered on verbal deductive features that are largely invariant to problem difficulty. NoThinking mode (direct answer generation) exhibits an adaptive, diffuse activation pattern emphasizing symbolic manipulation features that scale with task difficulty. The dissociation is cleanly separable at the feature level via the SAE decomposition. The finding implies that Thinking mode's quality advantage over NoThinking is mechanistically anchored in a distinct, identifiable feature subspace — suggesting targeted steering or curriculum interventions may be feasible.

![Figure 1: SAE feature activation patterns in Thinking vs NoThinking mode across math difficulty levels](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Feature activation sparsity and intensity in Thinking (CoT) vs NoThinking mode — Thinking relies on a sparse high-intensity verbal subspace invariant to difficulty; NoThinking shows diffuse symbolic patterns that scale with difficulty.*

## 04 · [BLADE: Bilevel Low-rank Augmented-Lagrangian Erasure for LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22557)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

BLADE proposes a constrained bilevel optimization framework for LLM machine unlearning, combining a clamped-entropy forget loss with an asymmetric augmented Lagrangian for retain-set protection, confined to LoRA adapters. The bilevel structure repairs retain damage before each forgetting step. Results: +6% composite improvement on TOFU, +9% on MUSE Books, +7% on KnowUndo benchmarks over prior baselines — the strongest systematic gains reported across all three standard unlearning benchmarks in a single method.

![Figure 1: BLADE bilevel unlearning framework — LoRA-confined clamped-entropy forget + augmented Lagrangian retain protection](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22557/x1.png)

*Figure 1: BLADE bilevel loop — inner step applies LoRA-restricted forget loss with clamped entropy; outer augmented Lagrangian enforces retain-set constraints; results in +6–9% composite gains across three standard unlearning benchmarks.*

## 05 · [Measure, Don't Optimize: Forecasting Recovery in LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11408)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 11, 2026

Proposes J-Access, an inference-time audit using the Jacobian lens to map intermediate representations to vocabulary space and quantify how often target concepts remain accessible along the output pathway post-unlearning. Central hypothesis: residual accessibility predicts recovery susceptibility — knowledge closer to the output pathway requires less fine-tuning to recover. Moves unlearning evaluation from one-off post-hoc diagnostics toward proactive risk monitoring; the Jacobian measure can identify which apparently-forgotten concepts are most likely to resurface under continued training.

![Figure 1: J-Access Jacobian lens — mapping intermediate residual representations to vocabulary space to measure latent concept accessibility](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11408/x1.png)

*Figure 1: J-Access audit — Jacobian maps intermediate layer representations into vocabulary space; residual accessibility score predicts recovery speed under continued fine-tuning.*

## 06 · [SAEVerbalizer: Generating Explanations for Sparse Autoencoder Features via Representation Verbalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13538)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Weihan Meng et al. (Tsinghua, PKU, Fudan) — arXiv, August 13, 2026

SAEVerbalizer injects SAE decoder directions directly into an LLM's residual stream, then fine-tunes downstream layers to generate natural-language explanations from the geometric structure of the feature — no behavioral observation (activation-maximizing inputs) required. Key results: learned verbalization generalizes to unseen features within the same SAE, transfers across separately trained SAE dictionaries, and extends to features from different LLMs via a lightweight adapter. Addresses the scalability bottleneck of auto-interpretability, which has relied on expensive activation-maximization sampling.

![Figure 1: SAEVerbalizer — decoder direction injection into LLM residual stream and fine-tuning for feature verbalization](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13538/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAEVerbalizer injects SAE decoder directions into the LLM's representation space and fine-tunes downstream layers to verbalize features; transfers across SAE dictionaries and to other LLMs via lightweight adapters.*

## 07 · [Mechanistic Circuit Identification for Controllable Data Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24065)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Nakyung Lee, Sangwoo Hong, Jungwoo Lee (Seoul National University, Konkuk University) — arXiv, August 25, 2026

Identifies model-internal circuits governing three data utility axes (learnability, challenge, alignment) via causal intervention and uses these circuits as controllable interfaces to steer synthetic data generation. Introduces SAMS (Stage-Aware Mechanistic Scheduling), which schedules circuit-steered data according to the model's evolving optimization dynamics rather than using static heuristic prompts. Addresses the core limitation of LLM-based data synthesis pipelines — lack of insight into how generated samples interact with the model's learning dynamics.

![Figure 1: Mechanistic circuit identification for data utility axes and SAMS scheduling pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24065/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Circuits for learnability, challenge, and alignment axes are identified via causal intervention; SAMS schedules circuit-steered generation according to the model's current optimization stage.*

## 08 · [Forgotten in Weights, Recovered by Tools: Agentic Tool Unlearning for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21544)

`AI security` `preprint` — Baicheng Chen, Zheyuan Liu et al. — arXiv, August 21, 2026

Identifies a new failure mode: "tool-mediated recovery" — parametric unlearning suppresses direct recall, but an agent recovers the same forget target via web search, retrieval, or database lookup. Proposes ATU (Agentic Tool Unlearning): Stage 1 applies parametric unlearning to suppress direct recall; Stage 2 performs trajectory-level RL in simulated tool-augmented environments, penalizing target-seeking tool calls and final-answer leakage while preserving normal tool use for retained knowledge. First work to study unlearning in the agentic tool-use setting.

![Figure 1: Tool-mediated recovery failure mode and ATU two-stage unlearning framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21544/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Tool-mediated recovery — agent recovers forgotten knowledge via tool calls even after parametric unlearning; ATU adds RL-based trajectory penalization to prevent target-seeking tool use.*

## 10 · [On Understanding, Identifying, and Mitigating Vulnerabilities in Agentic Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10530)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Systematic literature review (PRISMA 2020) examining 743 records, retaining 85 papers (2023–2025) on agentic LLM security. Key finding: perception-layer vulnerabilities (prompt injection, jailbreaking) dominate at 66% of papers; action-layer vulnerabilities (tool misuse, code execution abuse) are significant but underrepresented; attack research outpaces defense work by a 3.9:1 ratio. Provides a structured taxonomy of attack surfaces across the agent lifecycle and identifies the most underdeveloped defense categories.

![Figure 1: Taxonomy of agentic LLM vulnerability distribution — perception vs action layer vulnerabilities across 85 papers](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10530/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Distribution of 85 agentic LLM security papers by attack surface (perception vs. action layer); 66% focus on perception-layer attacks; attack:defense ratio = 3.9:1 across the corpus.*
