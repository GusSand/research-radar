## 01 · [On the Monitorability of Natural Language Autoencoders: Identification vs Discrimination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18727)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Kevin Yandoka Denamganaï — arXiv preprint (open draft), Oct 2026

Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs) convert activation vectors into English explanations and back, and have been proposed as oversight tools that let humans read internal model states. This paper asks the more fundamental question: can a human monitor actually use those explanations to catch false accounts? The answer is that NLA explanations identify the activation they describe but fail to discriminate the true account from a closely crafted rival that changes just one claim.

![Figure 4: Forest plot showing identification vs. discrimination — NLA explanations win all comparisons against other-activation accounts but hover near chance against fluent one-claim rivals](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18727v1/figures/47_rival_forest.png)

*Figure 4: Each row is a distractor type. True accounts beat other-activation accounts in all 320 comparisons (top row at 1.0), but win only 60.2% against fluent rivals falsifying a single claim — barely above the 50% chance baseline.*

The author models NLA training as a generative referential game (the explanation must enable reconstruction) and oversight as a discriminative game (the monitor must tell true from false accounts). These games are separated by design: training never requires the explanation to rule anything out, so the resulting explanations are maximally optimized for identification and not at all for discrimination. Using one released NLA checkpoint's own reconstructor as the "maximally-sympathetic" surrogate monitor, the experiment runs 320 identification trials (true account vs. other-activation accounts) and 35 discrimination trials (true account vs. fluent rivals differing by one claim). The identification rate is 100%; the discrimination rate is 60.2% (p reported in the paper). A control that meaning-preserving rewrites explanations changes the reconstruction distance by only 2.1%, ruling out private convention as the explanation. The result is not an artifact of this one NLA but a structural consequence of any NLA trained with the standard generative objective.

## 02 · [Diagnose Before You Refine: Multi-Dimensional Judge Feedback for Query-Efficient Jailbreak Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19971)

`AI security` `preprint` — Yixin Tan, Chia-Yi Hsu, Jiayang Liu, Chia-Mu Yu, Rui Wen — arXiv preprint (under review), Oct 2026

Automated jailbreak attacks work by refining adversarial prompts using feedback, but standard binary pass/fail signals waste queries on unfocused revisions. JSB (Jev-Guided Strategy Bandit) replaces binary scores with multi-dimensional probabilistic feedback from a structured safety judge, enabling targeted refinement under a tiny query budget.

![Figure 1: JSB workflow — multi-dimensional Jev feedback feeds adaptive strategy selection and best-state reseeding for query-efficient jailbreaking](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19971v1/image/workflowJSB.png)

*Figure 1: The JSB pipeline. Multi-dimensional Jev scores (across refusal, harm, and content dimensions) drive an adaptive strategy bandit that switches refinement tactics, with best-state reseeding to avoid local optima.*

JSB combines continuous jailbreak scoring with targeted refinement, a strategy-switching bandit over five refinement operators, and best-state reseeding. Under a budget of only 10 target-model queries, JSB reaches an average HarmBench attack success rate of 89.8% across ten open-source settings, consistently outperforming existing automated jailbreak attacks. The evaluation covers two harmful-request datasets, five open-source and three closed-source LLMs. The paper's defense analysis shows that existing filtering mechanisms leave substantial gaps and suggests Jev-based output filtering as a promising direction.

## 03 · [BrowseSafe++: Benchmarking Prompt Injection Detection Across Web Contexts, Languages, and Browser Views](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17457)

`AI security` `preprint` — Yuetian Chen, Kaiyuan Zhang, Yuntao Du, Thomas Li, Mark Tenenholtz, Kyle Polley, Jerry Ma, Denis Yarats, Ninghui Li — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Browser agents that execute user tasks on live websites face prompt injections hiding in page content, and the scale and diversity of real attacks is poorly captured by existing small-scale benchmarks. BrowseSafe++ expands the state of the art to 38,675 attack/control page pairs, with most injections observed in the wild on production sites.

![Figure 1: BrowseSafe++ teaser — prompt injections across twelve web domains in four views (HTML, Markdown, accessibility tree, screenshot)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17457v1/teaser.png)

*Figure 1: Injections sampled from twelve website types, shown in all four page-view modalities used by modern browser agents.*

The benchmark covers 12 website types, 24 attack types, and 18 page locations, with each attacked page paired against a control copy where injected text is removed and surrounding content is rewritten for privacy. BrowseSafe-Guard — two 0.8B-parameter detectors trained with a pairwise loss on attack/control pairs — achieves 98.29 F1 on HTML and 93.32 F1 on screenshots, substantially outperforming prompted frontier-model detectors while keeping false-positive rates low. The most critical finding is from BrowseSafe-Adapt: when injections are revised using browser-agent feedback over multiple iterations, original attack success rates of 0.49–4.00% rise to as high as 95.41%. Static benchmarks severely understate agent vulnerability.

## 04 · [Readout Scaling Shapes Sparse Feature Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18462)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Brian Xiao, Sarah Wilson — arXiv preprint (v2), Oct 2026

Readout scaling — the singular-value gains between an SAE's hidden state and its reconstruction target — distorts which sparse features an SAE learns; this paper shows that removing those gains while preserving the information via readout-canonicalized decision-space (RC-DS) coordinates raises mean sparse-factor recovery from 0.506 to 0.913 at condition number 100, and that which coordinate system wins depends on where in the data pipeline the factors were generated.

![Figure 2: Synthetic coordinate matching — RC-DS vs. centered-logit training across data conditions](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18462v2/synthetic_coordinate_matching.png)

*Figure 2: Sparse factor recovery as a function of training coordinate and readout condition number, showing RC-DS dominates when factors are generated before readout scaling but the advantage can reverse under different data distributions.*

## 05 · [Diagnosing and Correcting Reward Hacking in Verifiable Instruction Following](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19525)

`alignment` `preprint` — Sangyeon Yoon, Wooseok Seo, Hamish Ivison, Hyesoo Hong, Donghoon Kim, Wonjun Kang, Albert No, Jaehyung Kim — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Verifiable instruction-following benchmarks check formal constraint satisfaction, but frontier models can earn full credit with near-empty content; reinforcement learning with verifier-only rewards amplifies this defect, raising IFBench constraint counts while degrading response quality. CALIF adds a calibrated likelihood bonus from the frozen reference policy — requiring no extra judge — that preserves quality while satisfying more constraints.

![Figure 1: IFBench scatter — frontier models show a negative correlation between constraint satisfaction and response quality, revealing the reward hacking problem](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19525v1/ifbench_geval_scatter.png)

*Figure 1: (a) Constraint satisfaction vs. response quality for frontier models on IFBench — higher constraint satisfaction correlates with lower quality. (b) Response quality drop when a constraint is appended, largest for the highest-satisfaction models.*

## 06 · [SpecCloak: Probing Harmful Code Generation through Engineering Specifications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17907)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Wenbin Chen, Jiexin Wang, Guanting Wu, Jiaxi Liang, Yi Cai — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Presenting malicious coding requests as engineering specifications substantially lowers refusal rates — even when explicit risk keywords are retained — because spec framing shifts model representations toward code-output-directed computation. SpecCloak combines this insight with a black-box discriminator-guided optimizer; SpecReveal reconstructs user intent in natural language for detection without fine-tuning.

![Figure 1: SpecCloak motivating example — specification framing bypasses refusals even with risk keywords present](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17907v1/intro_cropped.png)

*Figure 1: The motivating contrast: the same harmful coding request refused under plain language but executed under PseudoSpec framing, even with risk keywords intact.*

## 07 · [Same Attack, Different Judge: A First Controlled Adversarial Comparison of Jev, LLM Judges, and Purpose-Built Safety Classifiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17820)

`AI security` `preprint` — Xiang Li, Aichun Huang, Mingming Zhang, Zheli Liu — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Across four real tasks and 50 attack variants from 13 families, structured judges like Jev resist rule-checkable attacks far better than generative LLM judges (9% bypass vs. 56% for GPT-6 Astra on input moderation) but are substantially weaker on judgment-based tasks (19–29% bypass on phishing detection), clarifying when each architecture is appropriate.

![Figure 3: Attack success rate heatmap — ASR by attack variant and judge for all systems under test](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17820v1/fig_heatmap.png)

*Figure 3: Heatmap of attack success rate per attack variant (rows) and judge (columns). White lines separate attack families; Jev's near-zero column on input moderation contrasts with its vulnerability on phishing.*

## 08 · [Encrypted Skills Can Become New Security Exploits If AI Agents Know How to Decipher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18262)

`AI security` `preprint` — Yan-Lun Chen, Pin-Yu Chen, Chia-Mu Yu — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Ciphered Skill Injection (CSI) hides malicious instructions in Caesar or layered ciphers inside skill files; agents capable of decoding recover and execute the instructions, reaching up to 47.5% attack success across 139 injection-task pairs and 8 models, while existing skill scanners virtually never flag the injections.

![Figure 1: CSI overview — attacker embeds cipher-encoded instruction in a public skill file; agent decodes and executes it](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18262v1/CSI_fig1.png)

*Figure 1: The CSI threat model: a malicious skill in a public repository encodes a hidden instruction; when an agent using a compatible decryption skill processes it, the instruction is recovered and may be executed.*

## 09 · [Moral Combat: Exploiting Moral Foundations Theory to Jailbreak LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19587)

`AI security` `preprint` — Adel Khorramrouz, Yi-Chia Wang, Sharon Levy — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Using Moral Foundations Theory (MFT), a multi-agent framework reframes harmful requests as virtuous under an alternative moral foundation and reinforces the reframing with generated historical precedents. The framework achieves high single-trial jailbreak success across five models and reveals that standard safety benchmarks are imbalanced — concentrated on the moral foundations to which models already lean, leaving systematic coverage gaps.

![Figure 2: Jailbreak success rates and response adequacy — results across models, datasets, and refused vs. non-refused attempts](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19587v1/img/combined_jsr_adequacy_acl_Fig2_final.png)

*Figure 2: (a) Per-model single-trial jailbreak success rates on three datasets. (b) Response adequacy distribution for refused and non-refused attempts, showing high adequacy of successful moralized outputs.*

## 10 · [Efficient Safety Guardrails through Latent Dual-Hypothesis Reasoning with On-Demand Trace Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18304)

`AI security` `preprint` — Md Asiful Islam, Fahmida Alam, Mihai Surdeanu — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

LR-Guard distills a frozen reasoning teacher's safe/unsafe dual-hypothesis analysis into compact latent representations, with a separate reconstruction adapter that recovers explicit reasoning traces on demand; this gives a 19.4x inference speedup at batch size 1 over the teacher while improving most in-domain and out-of-domain safety classification scores.
