### 01 · [Detecting Safety Training Modification in Language Models via Activation Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05578)
- **Authors / venue:** Glen Messenger (Google Cloud) — *IEEE Access*, vol. 14, pp. 91723–91737, 2026 **(peer-reviewed)**
- **Why it ranks here:** Only peer-reviewed paper in today's window; directly operationalises interpretability-for-security by fingerprinting safety-training modifications from activation geometry alone — without any behavioral testing.

Can you identify *how* a model's safety training was modified just by probing its internals? AMS (Activation-based Model Scanner) does exactly that: safety fine-tuning creates measurable separation between harmful and benign content classes in activation space, and particular modifications — abliteration, uncensored fine-tunes — collapse or rotate that structure in characteristic ways. The tool is fully black-box to weights, operating purely on activation patterns extracted at inference.

![Figure 1 from AMS: PCA scatter of safety-relevant concept clusters in activation space across safe vs modified models](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05578/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMS activation-space geometry — the separation between harmful and benign activation clusters is visually clear for safety-trained models but collapses under abliteration.*

AMS extracts intermediate-layer residual-stream activations for paired harmful/benign prompts and computes linear probe separability, mean cosine distances, and angular distribution statistics to characterise the geometric structure of safety-relevant concepts. Validated across 14 model configurations spanning 4 architecture families (Llama, Gemma, Qwen, Mistral) and four modification categories (instruction-tuned, base, abliterated, uncensored fine-tunes), AMS achieves **71% leave-one-out cross-validation accuracy** in classifying which safety modification a model underwent — well above chance with no behavioral queries. The finding that abliteration and uncensored fine-tuning leave distinct geometric signatures opens the door to passive model auditing at deployment time.

### 02 · [Induction in Both Directions: A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning in Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15893)
- **Authors / venue:** Andy Catruna, Emilian Radoi — arXiv preprint (July 17–20, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First mechanistic circuit analysis of ICL in masked diffusion LMs; finds a direction-symmetric induction mechanism that is structurally impossible in autoregressive transformers, directly advancing the interpretability agenda for dLLMs.

Do masked diffusion language models implement fundamentally different in-context learning machinery than autoregressive transformers? This paper's mechanistic analysis says yes: DLMs develop a **bidirectional induction circuit** in which previous-token and next-token heads write local context into the residual stream and later induction heads exploit both past *and* future matching positions — a direction-symmetric structure with no AR analog. The paper also surfaces a secondary interpretability result about how DLMs track their own denoising progress.

![Figure 1 from Induction in Both Directions: Bidirectional induction circuit diagram — previous-token and next-token heads composing with induction head](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15893/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The bidirectional induction circuit — unlike AR induction heads that can only copy from the past, DLM induction heads leverage both preceding and following context, with direction-symmetric QK circuits.*

Comparing attention-only autoregressive and absorbing-mask DLMs with matched architectures, the authors identify a bidirectional induction circuit where previous-token and next-token heads write local context into the residual stream and later induction heads find the matching source position from either direction to copy the answer. Causal ablation confirms both circuit directions are load-bearing; when only left context is visible (matching AR conditions) DLMs do not outperform their AR counterparts in induction. A secondary finding: DLMs implicitly encode the **global fraction of masked tokens as a latent timestep** signal — without any explicit timestep embedding — providing the first mechanistic account of how DLMs track their own denoising progress.

### 03 · [How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20560)
- **Authors / venue:** Multiple authors — arXiv preprint + Alignment Forum post (June 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First rigorous decomposition of transparency specifically for a production-scale dLLM; reaches a counter-intuitive conclusion (near-parity with AR transparency) via a novel token bottleneck, with implications for how we should approach dLLM interpretability.

Diffusion language models update all token positions simultaneously across many denoising steps — intuitively a far harder interpretability target than left-to-right generation. But are dLLMs actually less transparent? A rigorous analysis of DiffusionGemma decomposes "transparency" into **variable transparency** (whether intermediate states are interpretable) and **algorithmic transparency** (whether those states reconstruct the causal process) and reaches a surprisingly optimistic verdict: with the right architectural lens, DiffusionGemma is nearly as transparent as Gemma 4.

![Figure 1 from How Transparent is DiffusionGemma: Bar chart of opaque serial depth — naive DLM vs bottlenecked DLM vs Gemma 4 AR](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20560/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Opaque serial depth comparison — naive DiffusionGemma is 28.6× deeper than Gemma 4 AR, but a token-level inter-step bottleneck reduces this to just 1.1×.*

Naively, DiffusionGemma's opaque serial depth is **28.6× higher** than Gemma 4 because all positions can update at every denoising step; but routing information between steps through a token-level bottleneck — at no downstream performance cost — reduces opaque serial depth to just **1.1×** that of Gemma 4. Algorithmic transparency proves harder for diffusion models because all token predictions can change at each step, enabling distributed algorithms that are invisible in any single snapshot; the paper identifies unique dLLM-specific phenomena — non-chronological reasoning, token and sequence smearing, intermediate-context reasoning — that have no autoregressive analog and will require purpose-built interpretability tools.

### 04 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07903)
`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — arXiv preprint, July 8, 2026

![Figure 1: Paired attribution graphs — clean prompt (safety ACH nodes active) vs jailbreak prompt (ACHs suppressed, attack-specific nodes emergent)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07903/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Internal attribution graphs for clean vs jailbroken prompts — jailbreak attacks systematically suppress early-layer Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) and reroute computation through attack-specific feature paths.*

Introduces a mechanistic framework that constructs paired internal computation graphs for clean and jailbroken prompts, decomposing the graph difference into invariant, suppressed, and emergent substructures. Causal interventions on identified Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) directly modulate attack success, providing a reproducible white-box diagnostic for jailbreak vulnerability: templates that suppress early-layer ACHs reliably jailbreak the model.

### 05 · [DreamGuard: Efficient Runtime Guardrail for LLM Agents via Risk-Aware World Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05695)
`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv preprint, August 6, 2026

![Figure 1: DreamGuard architecture — recurrent latent world model over agent trajectory outputs immediate-hazard and prefix-risk signals](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05695/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DreamGuard's proactive risk model — a compact recurrent encoder over the action trajectory predicts future latent states and derives both immediate-hazard and prefix-risk scores before each tool invocation.*

Addresses a critical blind spot in existing LLM agent guardrails: individually benign-looking actions can gradually drift the agent toward hazardous states in ways that reactive per-action filters miss. DreamGuard maintains a compact recurrent latent state over the trajectory and predicts future states, from which it derives both immediate-hazard and prefix-risk evidence; evaluated across long-horizon agent tasks, it catches multi-step unsafe trajectories that point-in-time guards consistently miss.

### 06 · [Steering Without Breaking: Mechanistically Informed Interventions for Discrete Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10971)
`mech-interp` `text diffusion` `preprint` — Hanhan Zhou, Shamik Roy, Rashmi Gangadharaiah (AWS AI Labs) — arXiv preprint, May 8, 2026

![Figure 1: Attribute commitment curves during DLM denoising — topic commits in first 2% of steps, sentiment emerges gradually over 20%](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10971/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Per-attribute commitment schedules extracted from SAEs trained on four DLMs — topic, sentiment, and style each commit at distinct phases of the denoising trajectory, enabling attribute-specific steering windows.*

Trains SAEs on four DLMs (124M–8B parameters) to characterise when and how semantic attributes commit during denoising: topic locks in within the first 2% of steps while sentiment emerges gradually over 20%. This mechanistic characterisation motivates an adaptive steering framework that applies interventions only during each attribute's critical window — achieving strong single- and multi-attribute controlled generation while preserving output quality, outperforming uniform-intervention baselines.

### 07 · [Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability (SASA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)
`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv preprint, June 4, 2026

![Figure 1: Feature splitting comparison — standard SAE with single-vector decoder vs SASA with learned decoder subspace, showing reduced splitting and absorption](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06333/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Standard SAEs' single-vector decoder assumption forces exponentially many atoms to represent features of intrinsic dimension ≥2; SASA's block-sparse subspace decoder eliminates this geometric pressure.*

Standard SAEs assign each latent feature a single decoder direction, creating fundamental tension with multi-dimensional model features that provably induces feature splitting. SASA replaces single-vector decoders with learned decoder subspaces, enforcing block sparsity via Top-s group gating with a nuclear-norm regularizer that adapts each group's effective rank. On GPT-2 and Mistral-7B, SASA reduces feature splitting and absorption, improves monosemanticity, and matches or exceeds standard SAEs while training on roughly **half the token budget**.

### 08 · [MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04027)
`text diffusion` `AI security` `preprint` — Yingzi Ma, Zhengyue Zhao, Xiaogeng Liu, Minhui Xue, Yue Zhao, Chaowei Xiao — arXiv preprint, June 1, 2026

![Figure 1: MaskForge attack pipeline — masked prompt structure library (UCB bandit) → infilling → scorer-guided fallback](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04027/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MaskForge abstracts successful jailbreak attempts into reusable structural schemas and selects goal-compatible patterns via UCB bandit, exploiting dLLMs' native infilling capability rather than prompt continuation.*

Introduces MaskForge, a fully black-box adaptive attack that explicitly exploits dLLMs' native infilling capability: the attacker masks specific positions in a harmful prompt and lets the dLLM fill in the missing spans under attacker-specified structural constraints. Unlike AR jailbreak attacks (PAIR, AutoDAN, ReNeLLM — which are markedly less effective against dLLMs), MaskForge casts red-teaming as optimised search over a growing library of structural patterns selected by a UCB bandit, with a scorer-guided fallback when the library fails.

### 09 · [Stress Testing Concept Erasure with Large Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17890)
`mech-interp` `preprint` — Yuyang Xue, Feng Chen, Zhihua Liu, Edward Moroshko, Jingyu Sun, Steven McDonagh, Sotirios A. Tsaftaris (University of Edinburgh et al.) — arXiv preprint, July 20, 2026

![Figure 1: STACE agent framework — Proposer generates stress tests, Critique agent challenges, Verifier confirms concept leakage](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17890/x1.png)

*Figure 1: STACE's three-agent loop iteratively proposes, critiques, and verifies natural-language stress tests against concept-erased models, grounded by external knowledge retrieval.*

STACE (Stress-Testing Agents for Concept Erasure) autonomously stress-tests concept-erased models using multi-agent iteration: a Proposer generates novel natural-language probes, a Critique agent challenges weak tests, and a Verifier confirms whether erased concepts remain recoverable. The framework exposes failure modes invisible to static pre-defined evaluation sets, particularly under diverse rephrasing and cross-concept knowledge reconstruction — raising the bar for what constitutes a verified concept erasure.

### 10 · [Backdooring Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19262)
`text diffusion` `AI security` `preprint` — Daniel Yiming Cao, Chengzhong Wang, Sheng-Yen Chou, Chengyu Huang, Pin-Yu Chen, Shengwei An — arXiv preprint, May 19, 2026

![Figure 1: SHADOWMASK attack — modified forward corruption process replaces standard all-mask terminal with trigger-mask mixture prior](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19262/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SHADOWMASK modifies MDLM training by replacing the all-mask terminal distribution with a trigger-mask mixture prior, creating a dedicated denoising pathway from trigger-corrupted states to attacker-specified targets.*

First systematic study of training-time backdoor attacks on masked diffusion language models (MDLMs). Proposes SHADOWMASK, which modifies the MDLM forward corruption process by replacing the standard all-mask terminal distribution with a trigger-mask mixture prior, creating a dedicated denoising pathway from trigger-corrupted states to attacker-specified outputs while preserving clean generation. Demonstrates that standard backdoor defences designed for Gaussian diffusion or autoregressive models do not transfer to MDLMs, leaving this attack surface currently undefended.
