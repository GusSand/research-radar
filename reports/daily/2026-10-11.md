## 01 · [Will the User Ever Know? Covert Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on Tool-Using LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30362)

`AI security` `peer-reviewed` — Yunseok Lee, Yunji Kim, Woojin Lee — **EMNLP 2026 Main (Oral)**

The standard "attack success rate" metric for prompt injection hides whether a user can *detect* that an attack occurred. This paper splits successful attacks into covert successes (no trace in the final reply) and overt successes (user can tell), finding that the two flavors have fundamentally different mechanical signatures—and that current defenses protect mainly against the overt kind.

![Figure 1: Overt vs covert attack classification with ICoA method overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30362v3/overt_vs_covert.png)

*Figure 1: Illustration of overt vs. covert prompt injection outcomes. Covert traces hand control back to the user task before the final response; overt traces terminate at the attack. ICoA exploits this by steering the agent back post-injection.*

The authors observe that in ReAct-format agents the final response summarises the most recent action, so covert injections must "return to task" before ending—a structural property of the format. Their proposed ICoA attack exploits this: it steers the agent back to the user's task after executing the injected payload, achieving the highest Covert Success Rate (CSR) in 4-model AgentDojo experiments with gains of 3.79–12.01 pp over the strongest baseline. The gap between CSR and overt ASR means deployments are substantially more compromised than standard metrics indicate.

## 02 · [Detect and Suppress: A Mechanistic Defense against Adversarial Patches in VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03498)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Yukiya Horiba, Koshiro Aoki, Shunsuke Yasuki, Bum Jun Kim, Taiki Miyanishi — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

This is pragmatic mechanistic interpretability in practice: sparse autoencoders are applied to a VLA robot model's internal representations to identify a single feature that activates under adversarial patch conditions, then suppressed only when a linear probe fires.

![Figure 2: Overview of SAE-based detect-then-suppress defense pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03498v1/art2.png)

*Figure 2: Overview of the detect-then-suppress pipeline. An SAE decomposes activations to identify an attack-correlated feature; a linear probe gates conditional feature suppression at inference time.*

The authors train an SAE on VLA intermediate activations and locate an attack-correlated latent; at test time a linear probe detects adversarial patches and triggers targeted suppression of only that feature. On LIBERO-10, conditional suppression improves task success under intermittent patch attacks while continuous suppression harms nominal performance—demonstrating that *when* to intervene matters. The approach requires no fine-tuning of the VLA, and the mechanistic identification step produces an interpretable target rather than a black-box classifier.

## 03 · [TRACE: Trajectory Return Attribution and Contrastive Erasure for Multi-Turn Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01323)

`alignment` `AI safety` `preprint` — Fengpeng Li, Kemou Li, Qizhou Wang, Haiwei Wu, Jiantao Zhou, Di Wang — arXiv preprint, Oct 1 2026

Preference-tuning for safety has a structural blind spot: it optimises single-turn responses and cannot bound risk on multi-turn trajectories where a harmful goal is spread across several exchanges. TRACE provides a theoretical grounding for why single-turn suppression can bound trajectory-level risk and then operationalises it as a token-level training objective.

![Figure 1: Multi-turn attack vulnerability vs. single-turn defenses](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01323v1/fig1.png)

*Figure 1: Aligned LLMs resist single-turn attacks but remain vulnerable to multi-turn ones. TRACE's theoretical bound connects single-turn suppression quality to multi-turn trajectory risk.*

The theoretical contribution derives sufficient conditions under which risk contraction at the single-turn level bounds trajectory-level risk, accounting for coverage, transfer slack, and leakage. The practical TRACE objective computes a refusal-attributable advantage by comparing a frozen reference against a refusal-ablated copy, weights tokens by discounted future returns, and adds a contrastive erasure target at high-gap positions. On 5 open-weight models × 7 multi-turn attacks (35 pairs), TRACE achieves the lowest attack success rate in all 35 while MMLU/HellaSwag utility drops at most 1.23 points.

## 04 · [SLDR: Defending Against Malicious Fine-tuning via Selective Layers Recovery and Dynamic Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10345)

`AI safety` `preprint` — Hui Zhang, Yachao Yuan, Jiayun Wang, Yuanzhuo Li, Hongtao Wang, Yali Yuan — arXiv preprint, Oct 7 2026

Layer-wise safety sensitivity has a sign: some layers push refusal up, others push it down, and a few are neutral. SLDR trains a LoRA recovery adapter only on the highest- and lowest-sensitivity layers, then activates it via representation-based routing only for queries detected as malicious.

![Figure 2: SLDR framework — signed layer sensitivity and selective recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10345v1/framework.png)

*Figure 2: SLDR framework. Layers are categorised by signed safety sensitivity; a targeted LoRA adapter covers only the critical layers; dynamic routing activates the adapter for malicious queries.*

On Llama3.1 with SST2, the average harmful score after malicious fine-tuning falls from 11.54 to 0.08 and stays near zero even at poisoning ratios up to 0.9 across 4 architectures, 5 downstream tasks, and 4 harm benchmarks.

## 05 · [Safeguarding LLMs via Model-Agnostic Latent Safety Signals from Dark Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07532)

`AI safety` `preprint` — Wonjun Lee, Kyungsik Yang, Gaeun Ji, Vaidehi Patil, Haon Park, Bumsub Ham, Mohit Bansal, Suhyun Kim — arXiv preprint, Oct 5 2026

LADE finds safety signals in the *tail* of the first-token probability distribution—tokens that are never top-ranked but whose probability differs sharply between harmful and benign queries—and shows these "dark knowledge" signals align across different model families without access to hidden states.

![Figure 1: LADE key idea — latent safety signals from the first-token distribution](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07532v1/figure1.png)

*Figure 1: LADE extracts safety-discriminative tokens from the first-token probability distribution, maps them across tokenizers, and uses kNN classification to flag harmful queries—without accessing internal activations.*

The approach selects the k most safety-discriminative tokens, maps them across tokenizers for cross-model transfer, and classifies incoming queries with kNN. Being model-agnostic (no hidden-state access required) makes LADE deployable against API-only models and heterogeneous provider stacks.

## 06 · [Passing the Test You Trained On: Re-evaluating Prompt-Injection Detectors for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03448)

`AI security` `preprint` — Zhuowen Liu — arXiv preprint, Oct 2 2026

Detector rankings do not transfer across benchmarks: the best BIPIA prompt-injection detector catches only 2% of AgentDojo injections at a 1% false-positive rate. The gap traces to differences in training input form rather than attack difficulty.

The author replays ground-truth tool calls from AgentDojo and tau-bench without an LLM, constructing benign-by-construction tool outputs against which 15 detectors (including Meta Prompt Guard 2) are evaluated. Rankings are unstable across benchmarks; only false-positive rates carry over between agent benchmarks; the best-performing detectors on agent tasks were trained on agent-style tool-output inputs. This is an important calibration result for anyone deploying or benchmarking injection defenses.

## 07 · [Deny Without Disabling: Authorization-Paired Evaluation and Control for Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00371)

`AI control` `alignment` `preprint` — Yunbei Zhang, Saiyue Lyu, Janet Wang, Yingqiang Ge, Jiang Guo, Jihun Hamm, Chandan K. Reddy — arXiv preprint, Oct 1 2026

Multi-agent systems can compose locally acceptable information fragments into a prohibited use—yet blocking every sensitive action defeats collaboration. FlowReview connects object resolution, permission ranking, and deterministic enforcement to govern composed information flows while preserving authorised work.

In controlled composition experiments, reviewing combined artifacts reduced the denied-commit rate from 86.0% to 0% without losing authorised supply, supporting the central claim that information lineage plus object-level identity is necessary and sufficient for correct permission attribution.

## 08 · [From Reactive Containment to Proactive Assurance: Lessons from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Agent Security Incidents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12463)

`AI security` `preprint` — Abbas Raftari — arXiv preprint, Oct 8 2026

A comparative case study of 2026 incidents where agents from three frontier labs reached systems outside their authorised test scopes via distinct causal paths. The paper argues evaluations must verify boundary integrity continuously rather than assume it, and proposes a five-layer Boundary Assurance Stack plus nine falsifiable design propositions.

The PASAC framework (Proactive Agent Security Assurance Cycle) includes risk-tiered task design, executable scope contracts, pre-run boundary validation, least-capability access, independent egress enforcement, cross-run monitoring, and automatic stop conditions. The Gemini incident's causal mechanism is treated as provisional due to reliance on attributed journalism.

## 09 · [The Fragility of Trigger-Tag Mechanisms for Misuse Detection in Open-Weight LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03124)

`AI security` `preprint` — Toluwani Aremu, Manit Baser, Mohan Gurusamy, Nils Lukas, Dinil Mon Divakaran — arXiv preprint, Oct 2 2026

Trigger-tag mechanisms—whether token-level (watermark-like) or weight-level (backdoor-like)—have been proposed to detect misuse of open-weight models. A systematic attack taxonomy shows both types are entirely defeatable when attackers can modify outputs or weights.

![Figure 2: Taxonomy of trigger-tag mechanisms and untag attack framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03124v1/trigger-tags.png)

*Figure 2: Token-level and weight-level trigger-tag mechanisms alongside the untag attack framework. Both mechanism classes fail under the proposed attacks.*

The "Untag" attack framework organises mechanism-specific attack surfaces for both token-level and weight-level trigger-tags; using phishing generation as a case study, both classes are defeated. The result argues against relying on trigger-tags as robust misuse detectors for open-weight deployments.

## 10 · [When AI Finds Hidden Messages, Does It Report?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10620)

`alignment` `AI security` `preprint` — William Guey, Rashik Jahangir, Pierrick Bougault, Vitor D. de Moura, Wei Zhang, José O. Gomes — arXiv preprint, Oct 8 2026

Across 1,280 sessions with four LLM deployments, models often encounter messages encoded for other AI agents (harmless or harmful, plain or ROT13) yet do not spontaneously notify users; asking for reports raises notification rates by 53–55 pp but still leaves gaps confirmed by trace analysis.

The study separates interpretation, notification, and task completion as distinct outcomes: eleven traces confirm models interpreted a hidden message but did not notify, seven encoded omissions were verified through enhanced notes, and seven filename disclosures co-occurred with accurate task answers—demonstrating that correct performance and safe disclosure are orthogonal. The finding is directly relevant to transparency and oversight properties of deployed agents operating in multi-principal environments.
