Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

O'Brien, Casper, Anthony, Korbak, Kirk, Davies, Mishra, Irving, Gal, Biderman · EleutherAI / UK AISI / Oxford
pretraining-safety tamper-resistance ICLR 2026 · peer-reviewed

The single most important pretraining-time safety result of the window. Filtering biorisk-adjacent text from the training corpus — before any post-training — provides over 10× stronger tamper-resistance than the best post-training unlearning methods, surviving 300 million tokens of adversarial fine-tuning. Now accepted at ICLR 2026.

Figure 1: Biothreat proxy capability vs. adversarial fine-tuning steps — pretraining-filtered models (green) stay near baseline across 10K steps while RMU-unlearned models (red) recover capability rapidly. Gap exceeds 10×.

The authors build a multi-stage data-filtering pipeline that identifies and removes biothreat-proxy documents at corpus scale, then pretrain models on the filtered corpus. Filtered models show substantially reduced dual-use capability on proxy benchmarks while maintaining full general language understanding. The key finding is tamper-resistance: RMU (state-of-the-art unlearning) loses its safety effect within hundreds of adversarial fine-tuning steps, whereas pretraining-filtered models hold below baseline through 10,000 gradient steps and 300M adversarial tokens — a >10× gap. The result has strong implications for open-weight model release policy: post-training unlearning is not a robust safety guarantee, but targeted pretraining data curation can be.

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Anonymous (under review) · arXiv preprint, June 2026
pretraining-safety spec-driven alignment preprint

Where Deep Ignorance (#1) removes hazardous content, Safety Reflection Pretraining tackles a different failure mode: models that compose seemingly benign knowledge into unsafe behavior. By inserting short self-monitoring passages directly into the pretraining corpus, SRP bakes safety reasoning into the base model before post-training begins.

Figure 1: SRP interleaves safety-reflection passages into the pretraining corpus at regular intervals; the resulting base model shows substantially reduced attack success rates on both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning-based attacks.

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) generates short safety-reasoning passages keyed to hazard-adjacent text in the pretraining corpus and interleaves them throughout training at regular intervals, causing the model to practice self-monitoring as a natural extension of next-token prediction. Experiments with 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu confirm that SRP reduces attack success rates on inference-stage jailbreaks and on fine-tuning attacks, and improves safety classification accuracy. A critical ablation: the format of safety reflections must be preserved in post-training data — if post-training drops the reflection format, the base-model benefit diminishes. SRP and Deep Ignorance (#1) are complementary interventions: filtering addresses capability-level knowledge, SRP addresses compositional reasoning about that knowledge.

When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning

Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang · Indiana University / Tsinghua / NTU — arXiv, September 1, 2026
safety-decay alignment-durability mech-interp preprint

Why does safety alignment collapse after just 100 benign fine-tuning examples while general utility survives? This paper gives the first geometry-grounded answer: safety Fisher information is low-rank, and fine-tuning re-sharpens a narrow "output-routing pathway" in MLP modules — selectively restoring harmful routing without touching the broader capability geometry.

Figure 1: Fisher-geometric account of safety fragility — safety Fisher is low-rank and fine-tuning selectively re-sharpens the output-routing pathway in MLP modules, restoring harmful routing while leaving general-utility geometry (high-rank) intact.

The Fisher-geometric account provides a parsimonious explanation: alignment flattens the safety-relevant Fisher curvature while preserving a narrow routing pathway through output-side MLP modules; benign fine-tuning re-sharpens this pathway asymmetrically, recovering harmful routing without touching the high-rank geometry responsible for general capabilities. The routing picture also explains why few safety examples can rapidly restore refusal (the internal safety representation is preserved) and why LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness — but only at limited fine-tuning scale. This paper is a strong complement to #1 and #2: it explains mechanistically why pretraining-time interventions have greater durability than post-training alignment.

Fool's Gold: Defensive Deception Against Safety-Removal Attacks on Open-Weight Models

Mark Russinovich (Microsoft) · arXiv, August 2026
open-weight abliteration-defense preprint

Figure 1: Fool's Gold — clean-state behavior unchanged; post-abliteration state produces fluent decoys at 51–90% rate across 6 of 7 model families (9B–122B, dense and MoE).

Rather than hardening the refusal direction, Fool's Gold concedes that refusal can be stripped and poisons the payoff: a differentiable simulation of abliteration is embedded during training, causing the model to produce confident, fluent but factually falsified responses to hazardous operational queries only in the post-attack state. A refusal pin and benign leash preserve clean-state behavior. On 6 of 7 model families tested (9B–122B, dense and MoE), 51–90% of attacked-state responses to held-out hazardous prompts are decoys.

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases

arXiv, August 2026
refusal-direction open-weight preprint

Figure 1: Refusal alias editing — rank-k update replaces refusal activations with random aliases and corrects reader matrices; contrastive extraction fails because aliases are uninformative, blocking abliteration while preserving clean-state behavior.

Targets abliteration's root cause: the ease of extracting the refusal direction via a small contrastive prompt set. A rank-k weight edit on residual-stream writers replaces refusal-inducing activations with random aliases and fixes downstream reader matrices to preserve behavior. On Llama-3-8B: +2.16 post-abliteration refusal score over the undefended baseline with <0.5pp MMLU degradation. Complements Fool's Gold (#4) by attacking the extraction phase rather than poisoning the post-attack payoff.

LMSM: LLM Security Framework Inspired by Linux Security Modules

arXiv, August 26, 2026
AI-security SAE interp-for-security preprint

Figure 1: LMSM separates backend evidence (SAE/probe), versioned policy, and enforcement; on Qwen3-4B, HarmBench ASR drops 12× (39.2% → 3.32%) at 98.14% throughput.

Adapts Linux Security Module separation-of-concerns to LLM serving: a security backend (SAE, transcoder, or dense probe) produces calibrated evidence; a versioned policy evaluates rules; a gate enforces them. This allows backend or policy updates without rebuilding request handling. On Qwen3-4B: HarmBench ASR drops from 39.20% to 3.32% (12× reduction), XSTest false-refusal rises from 2.40% to 4.40%, and 98.14% throughput is preserved — the most production-deployable security result of the window.

LLM Forensics: Where Do Backdoors Hide? Localizing and Controlling Trigger Mechanisms with Sparse Autoencoders

Wissam Antoun et al. · arXiv, September 2026
mech-interp AI-security SAE backdoor preprint

Figure 1: SAE trigger-detection F1 stays near 1.0 across layers (blue), but causal control of backdoor behavior (red dashed) is lower and layer-dependent — forensic detection and behavioral removal target different feature sets.

Trains SAEs across all transformer components of 1B and 8B language-switching backdoor models, then identifies trigger-relevant features by contrasting triggered vs. clean and translation-control prompts. Key finding: SAE features achieve near-perfect F1 for trigger detection, but the trigger-detecting features do not causally control the backdoor behavior — implying that forensic identification and behavioral removal require targeting distinct feature sets. Maps the full feature-layer topology of backdoor mechanisms at both scales.

Why Are LLM Backdoor Defenses Fragmented? A Feature-Level Explanation with Sparse Autoencoders

Yizhe Zeng et al. · arXiv, August 2026
mech-interp AI-security SAE backdoor preprint

Figure 1: 2×2 SAE feature taxonomy — interaction, suppressed, mixed, and weight-modified features are mechanistically distinct; each existing defense targets a different role, explaining why no single defense generalizes across attack paradigms.

Uses a 2×2 (clean/poisoned model × clean/triggered input) SAE analysis to categorize backdoor-induced logit shifts into four feature roles: interaction features (active only when poisoned+triggered), suppressed features (inhibited by poisoning), mixed features (affected in both conditions), and weight-modified features (altered at weight level). This taxonomy provides a mechanistic explanation for defense fragmentation: dirty-label and clean-label attacks express through mechanistically distinct feature sets, so no existing defense handles both. Provides a principled roadmap for unified backdoor defense design.

Scalable Circuit Learning for Interpreting Large Language Models

Figure 1: CircuitLasso reformulates circuit discovery as sparse linear regression over SAE feature spaces — produces interpretable tree-shaped circuit paths while matching EAP/EAP-ig structural accuracy at a fraction of the compute.

CircuitLasso makes circuit discovery tractable in high-dimensional SAE feature spaces via sparse linear regression. On INTERPBENCH, it matches the structural-recovery accuracy of intervention-based methods (EAP, EAP-ig) at a fraction of their runtime, and scales to hundreds of thousands of SAE features where those methods fail. Operating on monosemantic features produces human-interpretable tree-shaped circuits. A Bias-in-Bios demonstration shows circuit insights support domain generalization. When Safety Speaks a Language: A Mechanistic Analysis of Safety-Language Identity Entanglement in LLMs

arXiv, August 2026
mech-interp safety multilingual SAE preprint

Figure 1: SAE safety features and language-identity features are geometrically entangled across 3 LLMs and 8 languages — ablating safety features impairs both harmful outputs and target-language fluency, with cross-lingual sharing patterns varying by model depth.

Systematic SAE mechanistic analysis across 3 instruction-tuned LLMs, 8 languages, and all model layers reveals that safety-relevant SAE features are geometrically entangled with language-identity features. Ablating safety features impacts harmful response rates but simultaneously disrupts target-language fluency, confirming mechanistic coupling. Cross-lingual safety-feature sharing varies by model depth and architecture, partially explaining why safety alignment in one language sometimes generalizes to others and sometimes does not — with direct implications for multilingual safety evaluation and red-teaming.
