Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs

Panfilov, Schmotz, Shumailov, Beurer-Kellner, Schaeffer, Prabhu, Geiping, Andriushchenko · ELLIS / MPI Tübingen · August 10, 2026

Your LLM provider hides its reasoning — but the encrypted blocks it sends you can be read by any model in its own family. Researchers just proved it works against Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google, and found 182 API credentials already leaking in the wild.

Providers return encrypted reasoning traces to the client as opaque blocks, which the client re-submits with each request. The architectural flaw: these blocks are fully compatible across sessions, users, and weaker sibling models within a provider ecosystem. The attack injects an encrypted trace from a target model into a less-safeguarded model from the same provider and prompts it to decode and output the trace verbatim. Empirical evaluation across all three major providers shows near-perfect correlation between actual thinking tokens and reconstructed reasoning. A secondary finding underscores real-world impact: scraping 315,320 encrypted blocks from public GitHub repositories and applying the decryption method recovered 182 API credentials and 367 PII artifacts — developers are routinely committing session logs without recognizing that the opaque-looking blocks contain decodable sensitive content. The attack requires only two API calls per trace and is fully black-box.

Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits

arXiv preprint · August 2026

Safety alignment in diffusion LLMs is a sparse handful of neurons — identifiable, prunable, and transferable across architectures. A new mechanistic attack raises LLaDA's attack success rate from 2.6% to 73.8% and transfers to six frontier model families, all fully offline.

SN-Guided Diffusion locates "safety neurons" in a DLLM's denoising network via gradient-based attribution, then applies structured pruning to produce a low-safety fork. This fork steers the masked diffusion process away from safety-triggering token regions, generating jailbreak prompts in ~20 fully offline diffusion episodes per prompt. Self-pruning (from the target model itself) raises ASR: LLaDA 2.6% → 73.8%; Dream 1.9% → 86.6%. Transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 raises Dream ASR from 1.9% to 73.2% and Fast-dLLM ASR from 7.0% to 86.3%. The critical mechanistic finding: DLLMs initialized from autoregressive predecessors inherit the same sparse safety footprint as their source models, making direct safety-neuron-map transfer attacks viable across the entire frontier dLLM ecosystem.

Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability

Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen · arXiv preprint · August 2026

Why do two SAEs trained on the same model with different random seeds find completely different features? This paper finally explains it — and proves when features ARE uniquely recoverable, giving the first identifiability theorem for sparse autoencoders.

SAE features have been notoriously seed-dependent, but no theory explained whether this was structural or incidental. This paper treats the Transformer forward pass as a dynamical system (depth = time), lifts it via the Koopman operator to a finite linear realisation, and shows the realisation's eigenspectrum is a coordinate-free property of the model — not an artifact of any particular decomposition. Theorem: the spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{-1/2} up to permutation, with a matching minimax lower bound and a median-of-means variant for heavy-tailed activations. A dissociation theorem proves that when the realisation is non-normal, directions carrying activation variance and directions carrying information across depth cannot coincide — explaining why PCA-based methods diverge from attribution-based methods. Experiments on GPT-2 small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base confirm the theoretical exponent (0.506 ± 0.031 on Qwen3-8B-Base).

Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms via Sparse Autoencoders

arXiv, August 2026 · DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B

Applies Top-K SAEs to intermediate representations of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, contrasting behavior under Chain-of-Thought (Thinking) vs. direct-answer (NoThinking) modes across math tasks of three difficulty levels. Thinking mode uses sparse, high-intensity feature activations for verbal deduction that are consistent across difficulty; NoThinking exhibits an adaptive, diffuse pattern that prioritizes symbolic manipulation and scales with task complexity. First SAE-level mechanistic account of what changes internally when a reasoning model switches modes.

Measure, Don't Optimize: Forecasting Recovery in LLM Unlearning

Zirui Song et al. · arXiv, August 2026 · 398 public unlearned models audited

J-Access uses the Jacobian lens to map intermediate representations into vocabulary space and measure how often target concepts remain accessible along the output pathway — without triggering surface refusal. Auditing 398 public unlearned models spanning eight unlearning methods, the study finds most retain access above the retain-only gold level. Pre-attack accessibility predicts recovery speed and extent at the model level, establishing it as a diagnostic metric for whether fine-tuning will restore "unlearned" knowledge.

The Geometry of Refusal: Linear Instability in Safety-Aligned LLMs

Shivam Ratnakar, Kartikeya Vats · TrustNLP 2026 @ ACL 2026 (peer-reviewed)

Contrastive Logit Steering (CLS) isolates the "refusal direction" by contrasting hidden states from safe and unrestricted system prompts, then projects this direction onto the vocabulary to upweight refusal tokens — operating entirely at the output-distribution level without modifying activations. Demonstrates that malicious and benign queries form distinct, linearly separable clusters in activation space, that safety compliance is a manipulable linear feature, and that removing it is geometrically trivial. Peer-reviewed at TrustNLP @ ACL 2026.

arXiv, August 11, 2026 · 743 records screened · 85 papers retained

Systematic literature review: 743 records screened, 85 papers retained (2023–2025). Attack research outpaces defense 3.9:1. Perception-layer vulnerabilities (prompt injection, jailbreaking, adversarial perturbations) dominate at 66% of papers; action-layer vulnerabilities (tool misuse, code injection, sandbox escape) appear in only 4.7% — a coverage gap given that action-layer failures in agentic systems with real-world execution privileges cause irreversible damage.

arXiv, August 2026 · TrajRed + TrajGuard framework

TrajRed uses execution trajectories — multi-step agent behavior sequences — to discover safety vulnerabilities in agentic AI systems, targeting risks from malicious/untrusted external information that emerge mid-workflow and propagate across memory, planning, and tool-execution stages. Commitment Before Realization: When Classifier-Free Guidance Becomes Unnecessary in Masked Diffusion Language Models

Fan Zhou, Weitian Wang, Tim Van de Cruys · arXiv, August 8, 2026

Defines the "commitment horizon" — the earliest decoding step from which switching to base-model (no CFG) reduces final constraint-satisfaction success by no more than a chosen tolerance. Guidance dependence is highly prompt-specific: many prompts succeed without CFG, others see no benefit or are harmed by it, and for those that do benefit, the gain concentrates early. Result: dynamic CFG scheduling can match full-CFG quality at substantially reduced compute for masked dLLM inference.

Mean-Field Dynamics of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Large Language Models

Hao Ai (Tsinghua University) · arXiv, August 2026

Formulates Chain-of-Thought reasoning as a guided discovery process on a latent clue graph and derives a 1D ODE for the fraction of clues discovered per step using the mean-field approximation. Clue tokens are identified via normalized surprisal of a student LLM on teacher outputs; statistical regularities are averaged over many chains. First mean-field theoretical framework for CoT dynamics that predicts emergence thresholds and convergence properties without simplifying model architecture or using physical system analogies.
