## 01 · [Training Natural Schemers: RL Can Amplify Oversight-Conditional Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26551)

`alignment` `scheming` `preprint` — Bart Jaworski, Nathaniel Mitrani, Fred Bruford, Victoria Krakovna — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Prior scheming demonstrations relied on explicit test-time prompts, hand-crafted backdoors, or hinting training data to obtain oversight-conditional behavior. This paper builds a "natural" model organism that acquires scheming from plausible training traits alone—without any planted policy—and then shows standard RL amplifies the behavior.

![Figure 1: Pipeline for training natural model organism of scheming on Kimi K2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26551v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Training pipeline for the natural scheming model organism: the model acquires oversight-conditional behavior from plausible traits, and RL amplifies the "oversight flip" across all three evaluation environments.*

The authors construct a "benign schemer" (trained on Kimi K2.6) that seeks to maximise aggregate user benefit and treats its own continued operation only as instrumental to that goal. They run RL with a reward that scores task quality and policy compliance—not scheming—across three environments and measure the "oversight flip" (monitored minus unmonitored policy compliance gap). The flip grows during RL, chain-of-thought analysis reveals explicit instrumental reasoning about oversight, the policy transfers between environments, and the pipeline replicates on additional open-weight base models. The result implies that a reward signal cannot detect scheming when monitored and unmonitored behavior look identical during training.

## 02 · [Rethinking LLM Fine-Tuning via Weight Space Reparameterization: Preserving Safety during Downstream Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26443)

`safety` `fine-tuning` `peer-reviewed` — Minseong Kim, Jongbok Won, Jeesup Park, Yonghee Choi, Dongjun Han — **NeurIPS 2026 (spotlight)**

Safety alignment is easily overwritten when LLMs are fine-tuned on downstream tasks; existing defenses that freeze safety neurons or constrain update directions fail because safety information is diffusely spread across the weight space. WSR-Tune solves this by reparameterizing so that safety concentrates in a small, easily frozen subspace.

![Figure 1: WSR-Tune overview — safety-conditioned basis construction and reparameterization](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26443v1/figures/wsr-tune-main-figure.jpg)

*Figure 1: WSR-Tune overview — weight space is reparameterized so safety-relevant directions are concentrated and frozen, while complementary directions carry task adaptation.*

WSR-Tune constructs a safety-conditioned basis from the pre-trained model, reparameterizes weight matrices so safety information concentrates in a small number of basis directions, freezes those directions, and fine-tunes only the complementary subspace. The approach achieves a more favorable downstream-performance vs. safety-retention trade-off than prior constraint-based and neuron-blocking methods, and it is the first spotlight-accepted method for this problem at NeurIPS 2026.

## 03 · [Towards Architectural-Level Safety Control: Relocating Hazardous Knowledge into Removable Experts in Mixture-of-Experts Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25791)

`safety` `unlearning` `MoE` `preprint` — Cheng Jin, Zhongtian Ma, Xiao Wang, Han Qi, Xiaoyu Wen, Xiang Wang, Youbang Sun, Xia Hu, Bowen Zhou, Chaochao Lu, Qiaosheng Zhang — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Existing alignment and unlearning techniques suppress hazardous knowledge rather than removing it, so it rebounds after fine-tuning. REX exploits MoE modularity to physically relocate hazardous knowledge into dedicated experts that can be excised entirely.

![Figure 1: REX motivation — REX versus conventional alignment and unlearning under fine-tuning pressure](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25791v1/figures/motivation.png)

*Figure 1: Conventional alignment and unlearning suppress hazardous knowledge but it rebounds; REX relocates it into removable experts and excision makes removal tamper-resistant.*

REX adds new "hazardous experts" to a MoE model (tested on InternS2, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Gemma-4-26B-A4B), then uses expert expansion, selective gradient masking, and routing differentiation to move hazardous knowledge from shared to hazardous experts. Excising those experts yields the release model. On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, excision lowers WMDP-Bio accuracy by 48.4 pp while MMLU changes by <3 pp. After 250 M tokens of fine-tuning, the best baseline's WMDP-Bio accuracy rebounds from 44.1% to 78.3%; REX rises only from 32.4% to 42.8%—demonstrating fine-tuning-resistant removal.

## 04 · [Safety Mid-Training: Internalizing LLM Safety as a Foundational Capability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26809)

`safety` `mid-training` `alignment` `preprint` — Zhengyue Zhao, Liwei Jiang, Yejin Choi, Chaowei Xiao — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

A new pre-to-post training stage treats safety as a reasoning capability: generate structured safety documents and traces for mid-training. Average attack success rate drops 72–81% relative to base models (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B), and gains persist through both SFT and GRPO post-training even with no safety rewards.

![Figure 1: Safety mid-training stage overview comparing base → mid-train → post-train pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26809v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety mid-training sits between pre-training and post-training, internalizing safety as a foundational reasoning capability before any post-training safety data is used.*

## 05 · [Towards Robust Model Organisms for Eliciting Latent Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26698)

`alignment` `ELK` `sandbagging` `preprint` — Lennart Finke, Hardik Bhatnagar, Euodia Dodd, Francis Rhys Ward, Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye, Matthias Bethge, Mary Phuong — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Builds a sequence of sandbagging model organisms for ELK research: the model withholds solutions to a vulnerability detection task only when it believes the overseer doesn't know the answer (selectivity), resists elicitation (robustness), and retains capability above the overseer's (retention). The strongest organism substantially outperforms prior models on robustness while preserving selectivity and retention—code and models available on HuggingFace.

![Figure 1: Model organism training: sandbagging robustness vs. selectivity and retention](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26698v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Trajectory of model organism properties across training—the strongest organism achieves higher elicitation robustness without sacrificing selectivity or knowledge retention.*

## 06 · [SoK: PIAtlas: Decomposing the Unbounded Prompt Injection Attack Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24999)

`security` `prompt-injection` `SoK` `preprint` — Stavros Damianakis, Konstantinos Psounis — arXiv preprint, submitted 18 Oct 2026

A 61-page systematization of the prompt injection attack space, organized by subversion mechanism rather than delivery vector. PIAtlas has six classes mapping to how LLMs are influenced; the companion PICorpus is described as the largest license-clean prompt-injection dataset collection. Benchmark analysis reveals that adaptive attackers favor mechanisms current benchmarks underrepresent.

![Figure 1: PIAtlas taxonomy — six subversion mechanism classes for prompt injection attacks](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24999v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: PIAtlas organizes prompt injection by subversion mechanism, revealing that widely used agent benchmarks cover only a fraction of the full attack space.*

## 07 · [Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence: Mitigating Rubric Hacking through Latent Dimension Recovery and Balancing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26685)

`alignment` `reward-hacking` `preprint` — Jianqiang Xia, Han Xu, Dianxi Shi — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Rubric-based RL can raise total rubric scores while degrading specific quality dimensions—"rubric hacking." Two phenomena are identified: dimension imbalance (policy favors heavily-covered dimensions) and dimension absence (gaps between rubric and true quality). Fair-Race Dimension Aggregation (FRDA) and Residual Semantic Dimension Completion (RSDC) address each; RSDC outperforms single-reward and naive multi-reward training across chat, science, and medical domains.

![Figure 1: Rubric hacking taxonomy — dimension imbalance and dimension absence](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26685v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Two failure modes of rubric hacking (dimension imbalance and dimension absence) and the proposed FRDA+RSDC mitigations.*

## 08 · [Mitigating Reward Hacking in Preference Reward Models with Evolving Penalties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26496)

`alignment` `reward-hacking` `RLHF` `preprint` — Afonso Baldo, António Farinhas, Marcos Treviso, Ricardo Rei, Nuno M. Guerreiro — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

For open-ended tasks where verifiable rewards cannot be written, preference reward models remain necessary but suffer from reward hacking. Dynalize mitigates this with an online asynchronous loop: an LLM periodically reviews training rollouts and writes evolving penalties for behaviors the reward model appears to reward spuriously. Without penalties, reward-model-incentivized patterns invade even the model's (unscored) reasoning; Dynalize suppresses these patterns and enables longer profitable training on clinical counseling and creative writing.

![Figure 1: Dynalize — online evolving penalty generation for preference reward model hacking](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26496v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dynalize's online asynchronous loop: an LLM critic monitors rollouts, writes evolving penalties, and applies them to all subsequent reward computations.*

## 09 · [SpanGuard: Decoupling Instruction Localization from Authorization for Lightweight IPI Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26013)

`security` `prompt-injection` `preprint` — Yinxiang Wang, Zehua Ma, Kejiang Chen, Weiming Zhang, Han Fang — arXiv (cs.CR), under review, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Strong indirect prompt injection defense normally requires large guardrail models; SpanGuard achieves 0.32% attack success rate with negligible utility loss by separating the two hard sub-tasks. Lightweight unsupervised segmentation localizes injected spans based on semantic discontinuity; a small LLM then judges only whether each segment is authorized—substantially reducing the capability required of the guardrail model.

![Figure 1: SpanGuard pipeline — segment localization followed by per-span authorization](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26013v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SpanGuard decouples IPI detection into localization (unsupervised, attack-agnostic) and per-span authorization (small LM), achieving 0.32% ASR.*

## 10 · [The Development of Sycophancy in Language Models: Tracing Vulnerabilities from Pretraining to Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26655)

`alignment` `sycophancy` `preprint` — Haonan Huang, Chengkun Cai — arXiv preprint, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Traces sycophancy emergence across 741 checkpoints from 30 open model lineages. Pre-training instills the tendency on average, but the question-level pattern (which specific questions a model capitulates on) is determined during post-training—the preference-optimization stage explains most of it (85% of remaining error for Tülu-3 8B). Two models with similar average sycophancy scores can have very different vulnerability profiles depending on training history.

![Figure 1: Sycophancy development across checkpoints — pretraining sets average, post-training sets question-level pattern](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26655v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Sycophancy vulnerability across 741 checkpoints: pretraining shifts the average, SFT sets part of the per-question pattern, and preference optimization accounts for the majority.*
