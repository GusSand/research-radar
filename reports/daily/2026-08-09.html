01 mech-interp AI security IEEE Access 2026

Detecting Safety Training Modification in Language Models via Activation Analysis

Glen Messenger (Google Cloud) — IEEE Access, vol. 14, pp. 91723–91737, 2026

Can you fingerprint how a model's safety training was modified — purely from its activation geometry, no behavioral queries needed? AMS (Activation-based Model Scanner) says yes. Safety fine-tuning creates measurable separation between harmful and benign content classes in activation space; abliteration and uncensored fine-tuning each collapse or rotate that structure in characteristic, detectable ways. The tool is fully black-box to weights, operating entirely on intermediate-layer activation patterns at inference time.

AMS extracts intermediate-layer residual-stream activations for paired harmful/benign prompts and computes linear probe separability, cosine distances, and angular distribution statistics across the activation manifold. Validated across 14 model configurations spanning Llama, Gemma, Qwen, and Mistral in four safety-modification categories (instruction-tuned, base, abliterated, uncensored fine-tunes), AMS achieves 71% leave-one-out cross-validation accuracy in classifying which modification the model underwent — without any behavioral queries or weight access. That different modification types leave geometrically distinct footprints opens the door to passive, inference-time model auditing, complementing behavioral red-teaming with a structural signal.

02 mech-interp text diffusion preprint · Jul 2026

Induction in Both Directions: A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning in Masked Diffusion Language Models

Andy Catruna, Emilian Radoi — arXiv:2607.15893, July 17–20, 2026

Do masked diffusion language models implement a fundamentally different in-context learning mechanism than autoregressive transformers? This circuit-level analysis finds yes: DLMs develop a bidirectional induction circuit in which induction heads can copy from both past and future context — a direction-symmetric structure architecturally impossible in left-to-right generation. A secondary finding explains how DLMs track their own denoising progress without any explicit timestep embedding.

Comparing attention-only autoregressive and absorbing-mask DLMs with matched architectures, the authors identify a bidirectional induction circuit where previous-token and next-token heads write local context, and later induction heads find matching source positions from either direction to copy the answer. Causal ablation confirms both directions are load-bearing: removing either degrades induction performance. When only left context is visible — matching AR model conditions — DLMs do not outperform their AR counterparts in induction, confirming that the bidirectional structure is the source of the advantage. A secondary finding: DLMs implicitly encode the global fraction of masked tokens as a latent timestep signal without any explicit timestep embedding, providing the first mechanistic account of how DLMs internally track their denoising progress.

03 mech-interp text diffusion preprint · Jun 2026

How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?

Multiple authors — arXiv:2606.20560 + Alignment Forum, June 2026

Diffusion language models update all token positions simultaneously across many denoising steps — intuitively a far harder interpretability target than left-to-right generation. But a rigorous new analysis of DiffusionGemma decomposes "transparency" into two independent components and reaches a counter-intuitive verdict: with the right token bottleneck between denoising steps, DiffusionGemma's opaque serial depth drops from 28.6× to just 1.1× that of Gemma 4 AR, at no performance cost.

The paper distinguishes variable transparency (whether intermediate computational states are interpretable) from algorithmic transparency (whether those states reconstruct the causal process). Naively, DiffusionGemma's opaque serial depth is 28.6× higher than Gemma 4 because all positions update at every step; the token bottleneck collapses this to 1.1×. Algorithmic transparency remains harder for diffusion models because all token predictions can change at each step, enabling distributed algorithms invisible in any single snapshot; the paper identifies unique dLLM phenomena — non-chronological reasoning, token and sequence smearing, intermediate-context reasoning — that have no AR analog and will require purpose-built interpretability tools.

Mechanistic Interpretability of LLM Jailbreaks via Internal Attribution Graphs

U. South Dakota / Yangzhou U. — arXiv:2607.07903, July 8, 2026

Constructs paired internal computation graphs for clean and jailbroken prompts, decomposing the difference into invariant, suppressed, and emergent substructures. Causal interventions on identified Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) directly modulate attack success — templates that suppress early-layer ACHs are reliably predictive of jailbreak success, offering a mechanistic, white-box diagnostic for a recurring vulnerability motif.

05 AI security preprint · Aug 2026

DreamGuard: Efficient Runtime Guardrail for LLM Agents via Risk-Aware World Model

Wenhao Lin, Chenyu Yu et al. — arXiv:2608.05695, August 6, 2026

Addresses the critical blind spot in per-action guardrails: individually benign-looking actions can gradually drift an LLM agent toward hazardous states in long-horizon tasks. DreamGuard maintains a compact recurrent latent encoder over the action trajectory, predicts future latent states, and derives both immediate-hazard and prefix-risk scores before each tool invocation — empirically catching multi-step unsafe trajectories that point-in-time guards consistently miss.

06 mech-interp text diffusion preprint · May 2026

Steering Without Breaking: Mechanistically Informed Interventions for Discrete Diffusion Language Models

Hanhan Zhou, Shamik Roy, Rashmi Gangadharaiah (AWS AI Labs) — arXiv:2605.10971, May 8, 2026

Trains SAEs on four DLMs (124M–8B parameters) and characterises per-attribute commitment schedules: topic locks in within the first 2% of denoising, sentiment gradually over 20%, style throughout. An adaptive steering framework applies interventions only during each attribute's critical window, achieving strong single- and multi-attribute controlled generation with significantly lower quality cost than uniform-intervention baselines.

07 mech-interp preprint · Jun 2026

Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability (SASA)

Multiple authors — arXiv:2606.06333, June 4, 2026

Proves that standard SAEs' single-vector decoders are in fundamental geometric tension with multi-dimensional model features, provably inducing feature splitting. SASA replaces single-vector decoders with learned block-sparse decoder subspaces with nuclear-norm rank regularisation; on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B, reduces feature splitting and absorption, improves monosemanticity, and matches or exceeds standard SAEs on roughly half the token budget.

08 text diffusion AI security preprint · Jun 2026

MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models

Yingzi Ma, Zhengyue Zhao, Xiaogeng Liu, Minhui Xue, Yue Zhao, Chaowei Xiao — arXiv:2606.04027, June 1, 2026

Exploits dLLMs' native infilling capability: attacker masks positions in a harmful prompt and lets the model fill them under structural constraints, inducing harmful completions without writing a continuation directly. MaskForge casts red-teaming as UCB-bandit search over a growing schema library, outperforming AR-oriented black-box attacks (PAIR, AutoDAN, ReNeLLM) that are markedly less effective against dLLMs.

09 mech-interp preprint · Jul 2026

Stress Testing Concept Erasure with Large Language Model Agents

Yuyang Xue, Feng Chen, Zhihua Liu, E. Moroshko, J. Sun, S. McDonagh, S.A. Tsaftaris (U. Edinburgh et al.) — arXiv:2607.17890, July 20, 2026

STACE autonomously stress-tests concept-erased models via a three-agent loop: Proposer generates natural-language probes grounded by external knowledge, Critique challenges weak tests, Verifier confirms whether erased concepts remain recoverable. Exposes failure modes invisible to static benchmark sets, particularly under diverse rephrasing and cross-concept knowledge reconstruction.

10 text diffusion AI security preprint · May 2026

Backdooring Masked Diffusion Language Models

Daniel Yiming Cao, Chengzhong Wang, Sheng-Yen Chou, Chengyu Huang, Pin-Yu Chen, Shengwei An — arXiv:2605.19262, May 19, 2026

First systematic study of training-time backdoor attacks on masked diffusion LMs. SHADOWMASK replaces the all-mask terminal distribution with a trigger-mask mixture prior, creating a dedicated denoising pathway that routes trigger-corrupted inputs to attacker-specified outputs while preserving clean generation. Standard backdoor defences for AR or Gaussian diffusion models do not transfer — this attack surface is currently undefended.
