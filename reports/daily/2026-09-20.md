### 1. [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** (authors TBD from full paper) — EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed, accepted)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly addresses the high-interest problem of safety durability under fine-tuning with a novel mechanistic (Fisher-geometric) explanation, and identifies the specific model components responsible for alignment collapse.
- **Technical summary:** The paper investigates why as few as 100 benign fine-tuning examples can collapse refusal behavior in aligned LLMs. Rather than attributing failure to gradient conflict, the authors propose a Fisher-geometric explanation: safety Fisher information is low-rank, and alignment training flattens the safety geometry while preserving a dedicated output-routing pathway in output-side MLP modules. Benign fine-tuning selectively re-sharpens this pathway, which explains why safety collapses to high attack-success rates while general capability degrades only mildly. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness, but protection degrades at larger fine-tuning scales; crucially, the internal safety-relevant representations are preserved even after collapse, explaining why a small number of safety recovery examples can restore refusal.

*Figure 1: Schematic of the Fisher-geometric safety routing framework — alignment flattens the safety geometry, and benign fine-tuning re-sharpens output-side MLP components, causing selective safety collapse.*

### 2. [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** One of very few papers to tackle pretraining-stage alignment as a primary objective; proposes a concrete, implementable method (safety reflection insertion) that directly integrates self-monitoring into language modeling.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) regularly inserts short safety-reflection passages into pretraining corpora, teaching the model a foundational self-monitoring capability that is subsequently reinforced by compatible post-training. The key argument is that pretraining data need not be harmful to enable unsafe composition — benign knowledge can combine into unsafe behaviors — so making the data "safe" is insufficient. Experiments training 1.7B models on FineWeb-Edu show SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces success rates on both inference-stage and fine-tuning attacks, without significant capability degradation.

### 3. [**Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087)

- **Authors / venue:** Chloe Li, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov — arXiv preprint (May 3, 2026; revised May 22, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly operationalizes the model-spec alignment paradigm at the midtraining stage; addresses the underspecification problem that causes shallow alignment, with a concrete methodology and human evaluation.
- **Technical summary:** Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) involves training models on synthetic documents that discuss the model's Model Spec after pretraining but before alignment fine-tuning, shaping how models generalize from subsequent demonstration data. The paper illustrates this with a controlled experiment: two specs explain the same cheese-preference data by different underlying values, and MSM-trained models generalize in ways consistent with their respective spec rather than the surface demonstration. Standard alignment fine-tuning can underspecify generalization because multiple values are consistent with any finite demonstration dataset; MSM resolves the ambiguity by internalizing the intended value prior to the fine-tuning stage. The method is evaluated on several alignment tasks showing improved generalization to out-of-distribution cases compared to standard fine-tuning.

### 4. [**From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)
`refusal-interp` `peer-reviewed` · EMNLP 2026 Findings · Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng (Aug 30, 2026)

![Figure 1: Multi-stage safety circuit: Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

Characterizes a three-stage safety circuit (Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads) via mechanistic interpretability and uses it to guide circuit-targeted weight scaling. The intervention improves safety rates under adversarial prompting by 26.5% across six LLMs without retraining, demonstrating that interpretability-driven weight manipulation can serve as a zero-shot safety patch.

### 5. [**Refusal Geometry Reflects Refusal Training: Diverse Refusal Prefixes Can Raise Stable Rank and Weaken Ablation Attacks**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)
`refusal-interp` `preprint` · arXiv, August 26, 2026 · Andrey Labunets (UC San Diego)

![Figure 1: Stable rank analysis — concentrated vs. diverse refusal-prefix gradient distributions and their effect on ablation attack success](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25390/x1.png)

Provides a mechanistic account of why refusal vectors are brittle: refusal-completion gradients concentrate when training uses repetitive first-token refusal starts, producing a low-dimensional direction that is trivially ablated. In a case study on OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct, diverse refusal prefixes during training raise the stable rank of the gradient and activation-change matrices, making single-vector ablation (abliteration) substantially less effective — a practical training prescription for hardening refusal geometry against representation-engineering attacks.

### 6. [**Refuse without Refusal: A Structural Analysis of Safety-Tuning Responses for Reducing False Refusals in Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04714)
`safety-tuning` `peer-reviewed` · EMNLP 2026 Main · Minji Kim, Hyounghun Kim (Sep 4, 2026)

![Figure 1: Response decomposition — boilerplate refusal statement vs. rationale components and their effect on false refusal rates](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04714/x1.png)

Decomposes safety-tuning responses into (i) a boilerplate refusal statement and (ii) a rationale explaining the refusal, and shows that the boilerplate component drives over-refusal by encouraging the model to latch onto surface cues. Training solely on rationales (without the refusal statement) reduces false refusals while preserving genuine safety performance, providing a direct and low-cost fix for the over-refusal failure mode in aligned LLMs.

### 7. [**The Implications of Linguistic Illegibility for LLM Security**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02852)
`AI security` `preprint` · arXiv, September 2, 2026 · James Mickens (Harvard University)

![Figure 1: Linguistic illegibility spectrum — conditions under which LLM internal computation is vs. is not faithfully expressed via language artifacts](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02852/x1.png)

Introduces "linguistic illegibility" — the condition where an LLM's externalized linguistic outputs or mechanistically-probed language features fail to represent its actual computation — and argues it is unavoidable for models whose internals operate over activation spaces with lossy translations to natural language. This has concrete security implications: mechanisms that rely on linguistic self-reporting (chain-of-thought monitoring, constitutional self-critique, activation probing for linguistically-defined feature vectors) may be fundamentally unreliable. The paper examines conditions under which illegibility is and isn't exploitable, and argues for security architectures that do not depend on the LLM faithfully narrating its own internal state.

### 8. [**ObserverBench: Testing Mechanistic Estimates for Intervention and Control**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03026)
`applied mech-interp` `preprint` · arXiv, September 2, 2026 · Vijay Erramilli

![Figure 1: ObserverBench task specification — information boundary, allowed actions, decision rule, and loss for circuit-intervention tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03026/x1.png)

Proposes ObserverBench, a benchmark framework for evaluating whether an internal mechanistic estimator (an "observer") is actually adequate for the intervention, control, or safety task it directs — separating estimation accuracy from the downstream loss caused by the chosen action. On circuit-intervention tasks in GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B, pairwise observers predict unseen intervention effects accurately without always choosing lower-loss actions; observers trained directly on action loss choose better actions. The key insight is that an estimator that is accurate on average can still consistently choose poor actions near decision boundaries, and existing mech interp evaluations miss this by measuring estimation accuracy alone.

### 9. [**The Misery of Mechanistic Interpretability: A Formal Perspective**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15533)
`mech-interp` `preprint` · arXiv, September 14, 2026 · Tobias Ladner, Matthias Althoff (TU Munich)

![Figure 1: Perturbation experiment — small input changes flip dominant IRN features across five open-weight model families](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15533/x1.png)

Demonstrates formally that even semantically minor input perturbations flip the dominant interpretable replacement network (IRN) features across five open-weight model families (GPT-2, Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 1B, Llama 3.2 1B, R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B), undermining the trustworthiness of human-readable interpretations. Proposes the first formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness via reachability analysis, which certifies a sound upper bound on the faithfulness gap under adversarial perturbations. Verification-aware IRN training substantially tightens the certified bound, restoring a feature-level interpretation that safety auditors can act on.

### 10. [**MechSparse: Mechanism-Guided Sparse PEFT Selection Is Task-Shaped**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18961)
`applied mech-interp` `preprint` · arXiv, September 16, 2026 · Son Ha Xuan, Phat T. Tran-Truong, Xuan-Bach Le (RMIT University, HCMUT)

![Figure 1: MechSparse activation-patching scoring pipeline — attention heads and MLP blocks scored by causal recovery, LoRA placed on high-scoring sites](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18961/x1.png)

Asks whether causal signals from mechanistic interpretability (activation-patching recovery scores on clean/corrupted probes) can guide PEFT parameter placement better than the cheap heuristics practitioners already use. MechSparse scores attention heads and MLP blocks by normalized activation-patching recovery and trains LoRA/QLoRA only on selected sites; MechSparse-C adds credit for joint within-layer subsets. Evaluated on Ministral-8B/NF4 against random, magnitude, activation-norm, and gradient/Fisher baselines on Swahili span-JSON information extraction at various parameter budgets, finding that mechanism-guided placement outperforms all heuristic baselines — a direct demonstration that mechanistic causal signals improve practical fine-tuning efficiency.
