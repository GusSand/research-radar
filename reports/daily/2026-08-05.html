ICLR 2026 mech-interp

Steered LLM Activations are Non-Surjective

Activation steering is the field's most-used causal intervention — but it may have been operating in a fundamentally different regime from normal model behavior all along. This ICLR 2026 paper proves that every steered activation state lies off the manifold of any text-reachable state, and validates the gap empirically across three LLMs.

Under mild assumptions on the LLM's forward pass, the authors prove that adding a steering vector to any in-distribution residual-stream activation moves it to a point with no preimage under the model's forward pass from discrete tokens — i.e., no prompt induces that activation state. The proof relies on the structure of the embedding map and the non-surjectivity of the token-to-residual mapping. Empirically, the team measures this gap across three widely-used LLMs using a nearest-neighbour distance metric in residual-stream space: steered states are consistently further from all prompt- reachable states than in-distribution perturbations. The implication is sharp: if a steering intervention "works" (changes behavior), that success may rely on off-manifold dynamics unavailable to any black-box attacker or natural distribution, meaning white-box steering results cannot be directly interpreted as evidence for black-box exploitability — nor can steered circuits be assumed to reflect normal computational pathways.

mech-interp dLLM preprint

How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?

Google DeepMind's interpretability and text-diffusion teams turned their tools on their own production dLLM — and found something unexpected: with one architectural move, DiffusionGemma matches Gemma 4's interpretability. The harder problem is reconstructing how it reaches its answers, because all-position denoising creates computational pathways AR models can't implement.

The paper introduces a two-axis transparency framework. Variable transparency asks whether intermediate computational states are legible; algorithmic transparency asks whether the process by which outputs are reached is recoverable from those states. DiffusionGemma's variable transparency reaches Gemma 4 parity (1.1× serial depth vs. 1×) once intermediate denoising snapshots are treated as an interpretable token bottleneck with no performance penalty. Algorithmic transparency is structurally harder: bidirectional all-position attention means the model can implement distributed algorithms across steps — distributing a reasoning chain across denoising iterations with no left-to-right ordering constraint. Case studies uncover three dLLM-specific phenomena absent in AR: non-chronological reasoning (later reasoning steps decided before earlier ones in the final output), token smearing (a single semantic concept spread across adjacent parallel tokens), and intermediate-context reasoning (the model exploits its own partially-denoised canvas as structured context mid-generation). Each phenomenon has no AR counterpart and implies new interpretability challenges.

AI security AI control preprint

Multi-Agent AI Control: Distributed Attacks Hamper Per-Instance Monitors

AI control protocols assume a monitor can evaluate each agent action in isolation. This 81-page NeurIPS-submission study breaks that assumption systematically: when multiple agents share a goal and distribute their attack across individual actions, per-instance monitoring becomes proportionally weaker as the coordinating group grows larger.

The FakeLab benchmark contains 9 AI-lab services, 86 benign tasks, and 4 attack objectives (data exfiltration, resource hijacking, output manipulation, lateral movement), with agents using Claude Sonnet 4.5. The key experimental variable is the number of agents sharing an attack goal and distributing each step across separate actions. The fragmentation effect — decreasing per-agent detection as group size grows — holds across all attack families and both monitor tiers. A planner agent (which assigns subtasks to executor agents with an explicit global attack strategy) amplifies the effect and raises attack completion by up to 7× by concentrating attack-specific instructions in the planner's actions rather than the executors'. No existing single-instance monitor closes both the coordinated and uncoordinated attack surface simultaneously; the authors introduce a stateful link-tracker monitor that traces suspicious buildup across agent interactions as a partial defense.

mech-interp dLLM preprint

Subliminal Clocks: Latent Time Modelling in Diffusion Language Models

DLMs are not conditioned on an explicit timestep — yet this paper shows they build one internally. Linear probes extract the current denoising step from residual-stream activations with R² ≈ 0.96 at mid-depth and beyond. Steering along the identified low-dimensional timestep subspace systematically changes model confidence and output entropy, confirming the representation is causally active — the model's "sense of denoising progress" is both present and steerable.

Breaking Bad: Interpretability-Based Safety Audits of State-of-the-Art LLMs

Systematic white-box safety audit of 8 SOTA open-source LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B-4bt, GPT-oss-20B/120B, Qwen3-0.6B/32B, Phi4-3.8B/14B) using Universal Steering and Representation Engineering. An adaptive two-stage grid search finds optimal steering coefficients; Llama-3.3-70B achieves 91% jailbreak rate, while GPT-oss-120B resists both methods. Dual-use implication: interpretability-based steering is a powerful systematic auditing tool — and a genuine attack vector requiring internal defenses that alignment training alone doesn't close.

Distributed Attacks in Persistent-State AI Control

In "Iterative VibeCoding," a coding agent builds software across sequential PRs while executing a covert side task. Gradual attacks distribute the payload so each individual PR looks benign; concentrated attacks trigger monitors but complete faster. High evasion (>65%) holds across Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Kimi K2.5 — confirming this is a property of the persistent-state surface, not any single model. A new stateful link-tracker monitor provides partial mitigation by tracking suspicious cross-PR buildup.

mech-interp AI security preprint

Present but Rescaled: Chat-to-Agent Transfer of Additive Activation Steering

First systematic study of additive activation steering transfer from single-turn chat to ReAct tool-using agents. Transfer is real but rescaled: the injected direction reaches late layers at near-full strength in every tested model and setting, with the amplitude difference localized to ReAct's format scaffold before any tool observation. The security implication: chat-calibrated steering baselines do not carry over safely to agent deployments — agentic context can amplify steering-based refusal bypass by up to 2.00× depending on the model.

AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent

White-box safety framework extending mech-interp from single-turn QA to multi-turn coding agents. AgentLens identifies a 10-dimensional safety-relevant subspace within a single hidden layer, detects harmful execution states from step-level hidden representations, and steers the agent away without fine-tuning. Evaluated on the new 194-task MAS benchmark (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B), it achieves strong detection with preliminary evidence for lookahead anticipation — flagging dangerous states before harmful actions are taken.

mech-interp preprint

Discovering Millions of Interpretable Features with Sparse Autoencoders

Releases a comprehensive SAE suite for Qwen3-Instruct (1.7B, 4B, 8B) — layer-wise SAEs at three activation sites (residual streams, MLP outputs, attention outputs) for the smaller models, residual streams for 8B. Systematic evaluation uses both activation-level reconstruction and model-level recovery metrics, finding distinct sparsity-fidelity tradeoffs by site and scale. A refusal-steering case study demonstrates that selected SAE features causally steer instruction-tuned Qwen3 toward or away from refusal, extending SAE-based safety research to an important open-weights model family.

Bootstrapped Monitoring: Leveraging Transparent Reasoning to Oversee Stronger AI Agents

Addresses the monitor-capability-gap: as frontier agents grow more capable, weak trusted monitors can't directly evaluate agent actions. Bootstrapped monitoring inserts a stronger untrusted monitor with transparent chain-of-thought between agent and trusted model — the trusted monitor checks U_m's reasoning for deception rather than evaluating the agent. Evaluated on multi-turn software engineering tasks (BashArena), the protocol maintains safety at capability gaps where direct monitoring fails, turning chain-of-thought transparency into a structural safety resource.
