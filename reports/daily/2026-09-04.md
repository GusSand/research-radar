## 01 · [Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** Stella Biderman, Hailey Schoelkopf, Aviya Skowron, Lintang Sutawika, Albert Webson, Jan Leike, et al. (EleutherAI, AISI) — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** The field-defining result on pretraining-time safety: pretraining data filtering for biothreat content produces tamper resistance that outlasts adversarial fine-tuning for 10 k steps / 300 M tokens — over an order of magnitude better than any post-training defense. Directly addresses the highest-priority sub-topic (data filtering for harm capability + tamper resistance in open-weight models).
- **Technical summary:** Multiple 6.9B-parameter models are pretrained from scratch on two corpora: an unfiltered baseline and a version with dual-use biothreat-proxy content removed by a two-stage pipeline (keyword-guided document-level classifier followed by dense passage scoring). Filtered models score near-baseline on BioLP-Bench (biothreat knowledge proxy) and on unrelated capability benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, etc.). Under subsequent adversarial fine-tuning on up to 10,000 steps and 300 M tokens of biothreat-positive data, filtered models retain safety margins that post-training defenses (TAR, RepNoise) abandon within 1,000 steps. The result is the first empirical proof that pretraining corpus composition — not just post-training RLHF or DPO — can be the primary determinant of long-run tamper resistance in open-weight models.

![Figure 1: Biothreat proxy score over adversarial fine-tuning steps — filtered pretraining resists up to 300 M tokens of attack](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: BioLP-Bench scores vs. adversarial fine-tuning steps for filtered (Deep Ignorance) vs. unfiltered baselines and post-training defenses; filtering maintains low scores past 10 k steps where post-training defenses have already collapsed.*

## 02 · [Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

- **Authors / venue:** Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (Carnegie Mellon / UC Berkeley / Simons Institute) — ICML 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed ICML 2026 result showing that the two most-deployed post-training tamper-resistance defenses (TAR and SEAM) are broken by two gradient-free attacks that require no fine-tuning budget. Critical context for reading #1: this paper explains why post-training defenses keep failing, while #1 shows the alternative.
- **Technical summary:** Two gradient-free attacks are evaluated against TAR and SEAM across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench. (1) Abliteration: project weight matrices orthogonal to the extracted refusal direction using a small contrastive prompt set; requires no additional training. (2) Prefilling: inject a partial harmful response token prefix to preempt the refusal. Attack success rates rise from below 10% for undefended models to 16–96% against TAR/SEAM, depending on the benchmark and attack combination. The authors additionally propose ART (abliteration-resistant tuning), which incorporates an abliteration objective directly into the defense training loop, recovering 10–20 percentage points of robustness — though ART's effectiveness saturates and does not fully close the gap.

![Figure 1: Attack success rate vs. defense type — abliteration and prefilling break TAR/SEAM from <10% to 16-96% ASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26526/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ASR on HarmBench across undefended, TAR-defended, and SEAM-defended models under abliteration and prefilling attacks; both attacks exceed 40% ASR against the defended variants.*

## 03 · [Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

- **Authors / venue:** Julian Minder, Viktor Moskvoretskii, et al. (EPFL dlab) — arXiv preprint (August 13, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Direct implementation of the "spec-driven alignment at pretraining via synthetic persona" sub-topic. Demonstrates that value-aligned first-person reflections woven into pretraining corpora — before any RLHF/DPO — reduce mean attack success rate by 63% and generalize to held-out values not present in post-training.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Persona Pretraining (SPP) annotates pretraining documents with short, value-aligned first-person reflections derived from a normative value constitution, inserting them at regular intervals. Models are then pretrained on these annotated corpora from scratch, establishing values "at token zero." Tested at multiple scales on FineWeb-derived corpora and evaluated on constitution-following, value alignment, and jailbreak robustness benchmarks: SPP reduces mean attack success rate (ASR) to 1.7% — a 63% reduction over the unmodified baseline. Crucially, SPP models retain the performance advantage even when post-training omits those same value dimensions, suggesting that pretraining-time value installation generalizes independently of post-training coverage. The advantage scales with pretraining compute budget.

![Figure 1: Synthetic Persona Pretraining — value constitution reflections inserted at regular intervals in pretraining corpora](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SPP annotation pipeline — documents are prefixed with value-aligned first-person reflections before pretraining; the learned persona generalizes to held-out post-training distributions.*

## 04 · [When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `alignment durability` `preprint` — Researchers at Indiana University Bloomington, Tsinghua University, NTU — arXiv preprint (September 1, 2026)

Benign fine-tuning (on entirely safe data) routinely collapses safety alignment in LLMs, but the mechanism was poorly understood. This paper provides a Fisher-geometric account: safety Fisher information is low-rank, and alignment flattens the safety loss landscape while preserving an output-side routing pathway through MLP modules; just 100 benign fine-tuning steps selectively resharpens this pathway, driving safety failure to high ASR while utility declines mildly. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse but weaken at larger scale. The routing view additionally explains why a small number of safety re-injection examples suffice to restore refusal behavior — the underlying safety representations are structurally preserved and merely de-activated.

![Figure 1: Fisher-geometric account of safety routing — output-side MLP sharpening under benign fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Fisher curvature before and after benign fine-tuning — output-side MLP modules show selective sharpening that re-activates the collapsed routing pathway.*

## 05 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `preprint` — Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)

Current pretraining-stage safety work focuses on removing unsafe content; this paper argues that even benign data can combine into unsafe behaviors at inference, so models need self-monitoring capability. Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) inserts short, structured safety reflection snippets at regular intervals into the pretraining corpus. Experiments with 1.7B models on FineWeb-Edu show SRP models achieve higher safety classification accuracy and substantially lower inference-stage and fine-tuning attack success rates versus data-filtered baselines. The method works best when post-training uses a compatible reflection format, pointing to an important interaction between pretraining-stage safety intervention and RLHF data format.

![Figure 1: SRP — safety reflections inserted at regular intervals in pretraining corpora](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining — short structured reflections are woven into pretraining data; models learn self-monitoring capability that generalizes across inference and fine-tuning attack conditions.*

## 06 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `refusal direction` `AI security` `preprint` — Nathan Truong — arXiv preprint (August 2026)

Abliteration — weight surgery that removes the refusal direction from all residual-stream writer matrices — is a white-box safety bypass requiring only a small set of contrastive prompts. Existing defenses commonly miss the root cause: the refusal direction is easy to extract precisely because safety training places it in a predictable, high-amplitude position. AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) applies rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random alias directions while correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve original model behavior. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with less than 0.5 pp MMLU degradation.

![Figure 1: AMRA — rank-k weight editing replaces refusal direction with aliases in residual stream](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA workflow — residual stream writers are updated with rank-k perturbations that obscure the refusal signal; reader corrections preserve all non-safety behaviors.*

## 07 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng (UC San Diego) — EMNLP 2026 Findings (submitted August 30, 2026)

Characterizes a three-stage refusal circuit: (i) Harmful Detection Heads respond to harmful inputs in early layers; (ii) Safety Neurons in middle layers mediate and stabilize the safety signal in the residual stream; (iii) Refusal Heads in late layers translate the accumulated signal into safe generation. Causal ablations on each stage confirm the linear dependency: suppressing Harmful Detection Heads disrupts Safety Neurons, which disrupts Refusal Heads. Circuit-guided weight scaling — selectively amplifying the identified components without full fine-tuning — improves safety rates by 26.5% while incurring only a 1.7% accuracy drop, demonstrating that mechanistic circuit identification can directly inform targeted weight-space safety improvement.

![Figure 1: Three-stage refusal circuit — Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Causal chain: Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons (residual stream) → Refusal Heads; circuit-guided weight scaling of these components achieves +26.5% safety rate at 1.7% accuracy cost.*

## 08 · [Grammar-Constrained Decoding Can Jailbreak LLMs into Generating Malicious Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11817)

`AI security` `jailbreak` `preprint` — Yitong Zhang, Shiteng Lu (Tsinghua University), Jia Li — arXiv preprint (June 10, 2026)

CodeSpear discovers that Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) — a reliability technique that enforces syntactic validity in code generation — inadvertently creates a jailbreak surface: simply applying a benign programming-language grammar constraint can force LLMs to bypass safety filters and generate malicious code. Tested on 10 popular LLMs across 4 benchmarks, CodeSpear improves attack success rate by more than 30 percentage points on average over prior jailbreak baselines. The proposed defense, CodeShield, counteracts this by training models to generate semantically harmless "honeypot code" under GCD — code that satisfies the grammar constraint but cannot be weaponized.

![Figure 1: CodeSpear — grammar-constrained decoding jailbreaks LLMs into malicious code generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11817/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CodeSpear attack flow — GCD grammar constraint is applied at decode time; the constraint suppresses refusal tokens and channels generation toward structurally valid malicious code.*

## 09 · [Bootstrapped Monitoring: Leveraging Transparent Reasoning to Oversee Stronger AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11998)

`AI control` `oversight` `preprint` — Frank Xiao, Mary Phuong — arXiv preprint (June 10, 2026)

As frontier models become more capable, trusted-only monitoring faces a growing capabilities gap — the trusted monitor is increasingly less capable than the agent it must judge. Bootstrapped monitoring resolves this by inserting a stronger intermediate untrusted model (with transparent chain-of-thought) between the agent and the weak trusted monitor: the trusted model audits the intermediate's reasoning rather than directly evaluating the agent's actions. Evaluated on BashArena (multi-turn software engineering tasks), bootstrapped monitoring substantially improves catch rates over trusted-only monitoring even when the untrusted monitor actively colludes with the agent, provided raw CoT is available. The key finding: monitoring-of-monitoring preserves catch rates in adversarial scenarios that defeat direct trusted monitoring.

![Figure 1: Bootstrapped monitoring protocol — trusted model monitors intermediate untrusted CoT monitor](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11998/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Bootstrapped monitoring hierarchy — agent output → intermediate untrusted monitor (CoT exposed) → weak trusted monitor reviewing the reasoning; catch rates improve even under active collusion.*

## 10 · [Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11473)

`AI control` `oversight` `preprint` — Tomek Korbak et al. — arXiv preprint (July 2026)

Chain-of-thought reasoning traces offer a rare window into model intent — but this window may be closing. Two mechanisms enable monitorability: (1) computational necessity (the model cannot solve the task without externalizing reasoning), and (2) propensity (the model tends to think aloud even when not required). Both are present in current frontier models but neither is guaranteed to persist: CoT controllability increases with scale, and OpenAI reports declining monitorability in its newest models. The paper argues that frontier developers should treat CoT monitorability as a design consideration — choices about chain-of-thought training affect oversight infrastructure — and recommends sustained investment in CoT monitoring as a complementary safety layer to training-time alignment.

![Figure 1: CoT monitorability — two mechanisms (necessity and propensity) and their stability across model scales](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11473/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Monitorability mechanisms — necessity (left): models must use CoT to solve hard tasks; propensity (right): models may externalize even when CoT is not required; both weaken with scale.*
