## 01 · [Stress-testing Alignment Midtraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20412)

- **Authors / venue:** Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan — arXiv preprint (submitted **Sep 17, 2026** — posted yesterday)
- **Why it ranks here:** First large-scale empirical interrogation of whether alignment midtraining (AMT) actually works—an approach gaining widespread adoption with surprisingly thin public evidence. Runs controlled experiments up to 110B parameters and 1B midtraining tokens, testing the core generalization claim that AMT propagates alignment values through later fine-tuning.
- **Technical summary:** The authors operationalize three assumptions behind AMT: (1) midtraining content shifts model priors about intended behaviour, (2) those priors survive post-training and generalize to deployment, (3) effectiveness scales with compute. They create controlled settings where post-training demonstrations are ambiguous between two motivational interpretations, and measure which interpretation AMT steers the model toward. AMT successfully steers motivation in simple single-ambiguity settings, but the advantage attenuates sharply as the post-training dataset grows and as evaluation moves out-of-distribution. Scaling from 7B to 110B shows non-monotone effectiveness — mid-scale models benefit most, and the advantage partially collapses at the largest scale tested. The study identifies a key limiting assumption: AMT documents teach the *content* of alignment norms but not the *procedure* for applying them under conflicting signals.

## 02 · [Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15886)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien et al. — arXiv preprint (submitted **Sep 15, 2026** — posted three days ago)
- **Why it ranks here:** Novel midtraining architecture for compartmentalizing unsafe knowledge without removing it: a single learned vocabulary token (neologism) marks the "unsafe context" during midtraining so the model learns unsafe-behaviour-belongs-here, then post-training happens outside that context. Directly addresses the core dilemma that data filtering removes dangerous capabilities but also degrades benign-use performance.
- **Technical summary:** Inoculation Midtraining introduces a fresh token *t_neo* (a random neologism) into the midtraining vocabulary and trains on unsafe data prefixed by that token. Post-training is conducted without *t_neo* in the system prompt, so the model learns that harmful content "belongs in the neologism context." Both SFT and RL post-training regimes show reduced misalignment on standard benchmarks while preserving benign-capability transfer (e.g., German-language style, Shakespearean register). However, the technique has three significant weaknesses: it does not outperform vanilla Inoculation Prompting on out-of-distribution attacks; the boundary is leaky — nearby contextual cues can reactivate the unsafe register; and performance is non-monotone in corpus size, peaking around 300M tokens and degrading with more data.

## 03 · [Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26654)

- **Authors / venue:** Desiree Cho, Cameron Tice, Bernie Hogan, Hunar Batra, Puria Radmard, Jun Zhao, Nigel Shadbolt — arXiv preprint (Jul 29, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** The largest controlled midtraining study in the public record — 120B-scale training, 394M-token constitutional corpus derived from Anthropic's published Constitution — with a factorial design that isolates *what* drives alignment gains. Directly answers whether it is the structure of constitutional content (curriculum order, deliberative framing) or simply its presence that matters.
- **Technical summary:** A 394M-token constitutional corpus is synthesised from Anthropic's Constitution using a 2×2 factorial design varying curriculum ordering (simple-to-complex vs. random) and deliberative reasoning (CoT-style justification vs. direct assertion). Four constitutionally midtrained variants plus a replay-only control are trained at 120B-token scale and evaluated at three stages: post-midtraining, post-SFT, and post-benign fine-tuning. Key quantitative result: on the blackmail scenario, SFT instils a blackmail propensity across all models, but constitutional midtraining blunts it by −17.5 pp, and this advantage survives benign fine-tuning. Curriculum ordering and deliberative-reasoning framing yield null or transient effects — the decisive factor is merely that constitutional content is present in the corpus. Capabilities (MMLU, ARC-Easy, PIQA, GSM8K) are fully preserved.

## 04 · [Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087)

`pretraining/midtraining` `alignment` `arXiv preprint May 2026`

Introduces Model Spec Midtraining (MSM): after pretraining but before SFT, models train on synthetic documents discussing the model spec (both its content and reasoning about edge cases). MSM teaches models the spec's *intent*, shaping how SFT demonstrations generalise — models trained with MSM recover better alignment from underspecified demonstrations and show less erosion under distributional shift compared to standard fine-tuning baselines.

*Figure 1: MSM pipeline — synthetic spec-discussion documents inserted between pretraining and SFT; post-MSM models generalise alignment intent rather than surface demonstrations.*

## 05 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

`pretraining/midtraining` `alignment` `arXiv preprint Jun 2026`

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) regularly inserts short safety-reflection passages into pretraining text rather than filtering or rewriting unsafe content. At 1.7B scale on FineWeb-Edu, SRP substantially reduces inference-stage attack success rates and fine-tuning-based safety erosion compared to filtering and rewriting baselines, suggesting that building a native self-monitoring habit during pretraining outperforms post-hoc knowledge removal.

*Figure 1: SRP pipeline — safety reflections interspersed into the pretraining stream; attack success rates at inference and after finetuning attacks are reduced vs. filtering/rewriting baselines.*

## 06 · [Defending Against Malicious Finetuning by Scaling Train-time Adversarial Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07970)

`pretraining/midtraining` `AI security` `arXiv preprint Jun 2026`

Patcher applies adversarial training via bi-level optimization: during safety training, the inner loop simulates progressively stronger finetuning attacks (scaling up optimization steps), forcing the defender to find weight configurations insensitive to stronger adversaries. A parallel implementation reduces wall-clock overhead. Patcher outperforms prior defences against PEFT-based attacks but still degrades under full-parameter finetuning on fully poisoned datasets.

![Figure 1 from Patcher paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07970/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Patcher's adversarial training loop — scaled inner-loop adversary forces weight insensitivity to stronger finetuning attacks; comparison against TAR and gradient-constrained baselines.*

## 07 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases (AMRA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`AI security` `alignment` `refusal-mechanism` `arXiv preprint Aug 2026`

AMRA defends open-weight models against abliteration (removing refusal via weight-space orthogonalization) by obscuring the refusal direction itself rather than hardening its resistance. Rank-k updates on residual-stream writer matrices replace refusal-inducing activations with random alias directions; downstream reader matrices are corrected to preserve utility. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by +2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5 pp MMLU degradation — a lightweight surgical intervention that raises the cost of direction extraction.

![Figure 1 from AMRA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA — rank-k updates inject random alias directions in place of the refusal direction; reader matrices compensate, preserving utility while hiding the ablation target.*

## 08 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)`

Maps a three-stage safety circuit shared across model families: Harmful Detection Heads (early layers) → Safety Neurons (residual stream mediation) → Refusal Heads (generation). Suppressing detection heads causally disrupts downstream refusal; amplifying the circuit via targeted weight scaling improves safety rates under attacks by +26.5% without fine-tuning, establishing that the circuit architecture is an intervention handle, not just a descriptor.

![Figure 1 from Detection to Refusal paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Three-stage safety circuit (Detection → Neurons → Refusal); targeted weight scaling along the circuit improves attack resistance by +26.5%.*

## 10 · [LMSM: LLM Security Framework Inspired by Linux Security Modules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25697)

`AI security` `arXiv preprint Aug 2026`

Adapts Linux Security Module architecture to LLM inference: a pluggable backend exposes calibrated safety evidence, a versioned policy engine evaluates rules over trusted per-request context, and a buffered output gate withholds generation until policy clears. Separates mediation correctness from policy update cycles. On Qwen3-4B (LMSM-Checkpoint), HarmBench attack success rate drops from 39.20% to 3.32%, XSTest false-refusal rate rises by only 2 pp, and 98.14% throughput is preserved at 32 concurrent sequences.

![Figure 1 from LMSM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25697/x1.png)

*Figure 1: LMSM architecture — pluggable backend + versioned policy evaluator + buffered output gate; analogous to Linux LSM hook points inserted into the LLM inference path.*
