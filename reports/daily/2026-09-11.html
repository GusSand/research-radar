ICLR 2026 pretraining safety

Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

If you remove biothreat-relevant text from a model's pretraining corpus, can an adversary fine-tune it back in? This paper answers no—filtered models resist 10,000 steps and 300 million tokens of adversarial fine-tuning, making them over 10× more tamper-resistant than the best post-training unlearning baselines. The implication is stark: safety durability for open-weight models depends on what was in the pretraining data, not just what was done afterward.

O'Brien et al. (EleutherAI / UK AI Security Institute / Oxford OATML) introduce a multi-stage pipeline that scalably identifies and removes biorisk-proxy documents from pretraining corpora, then pretrain six 6.9B-parameter models from scratch on filtered vs. unfiltered data. Adversarial fine-tuning attacks across a range of budgets (1K–10K gradient steps; 300M tokens of adversarial data) consistently fail to recover suppressed biothreat capability in filtered models. General benchmark performance is preserved. The result is the first large-scale peer-reviewed evidence that pretraining data filtering—not RLHF, not unlearning—delivers the most durable defense for open-weight model safety.

pretraining safety preprint · Sep 2026

The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists

Post-hoc safety training (RLHF, DPO) can be undone by 100 steps of benign fine-tuning. This paper explains why, using differential geometry: safety updates land in a suppression regime where the gradient is nearly orthogonal to all capability directions, so the model is masked rather than changed, and any fine-tuning that sharpens those directions restores harmful outputs. A 267-checkpoint sweep of OLMo-2-1B locates where pretraining safety actually forms—and it's a sharp transition between 6B and 60B tokens, not a gradual accumulation.

Malla, Choi & Choi formalize the fragility of post-hoc safety via a kernel-immobility lemma: a gradient update confined to the orthogonal complement of the capability kernel can only suppress, never erase, pre-existing capability—100 benign fine-tuning steps collapse refusal in Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B Instruct. The 267-checkpoint pretraining sweep of OLMo-2-1B reveals that the structural substrate onto which safety is installed (the layer geometry that enables stable refusal directions) forms between 6B and 60B tokens—explaining the empirical superiority of early pretraining safety interventions over post-training ones, and providing a principled account of why paper #1's pretraining filter result is structurally irreversible.

How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE

Directional ablation—projecting the refusal direction out of model weights—had only been demonstrated on dense models up to about 70B parameters. This paper extends it to GLM-5.3-Flash, a 320B mixture-of-experts model with 288 routed experts and block-FP8 quantization. Combined editing of all writer types removes 77.6% of refusal with no gradient-based training, confirming the attack is architecture-agnostic and scales to frontier models.

Shi, Chen & Shen adapt the directional ablation (abliteration) technique to GLM-5.3-Flash's non-standard residual structure: the model uses a four-wide hyper-connection residual and block-FP8 quantization, meaning the refusal direction cannot be projected from a single weight tensor. The authors identify separate writer matrices for attention outputs, dense expert outputs, and routed expert outputs, and apply ablation to each. No single writer type carries most of the refusal signal; only simultaneous editing across all three achieves high attack success. The paper confirms that safety alignment in frontier-scale MoE models remains a suppression regime property (consistent with paper #2's theory) and that the attack scales without requiring per-expert optimization—only a few hundred contrastive prompts.

From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling

Chu, Sun & Weng (EMNLP 2026 Findings) map the full three-stage safety circuit, then improve robustness by scaling identified component weights rather than fine-tuning — the first intervention targeting the complete circuit rather than any single component, achieving +26.5% safety rate under attacks with a 1.7% accuracy penalty.

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Li et al. propose Safety Reflection Pretraining — inserting classifier-generated (Safe/Unsafe + harm category) annotations after each pretraining segment. 1.7B models on FineWeb-Edu show improved safety classification and substantially reduced ASR on both inference-stage and fine-tuning attacks, without filtering or rewriting any documents.

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero

Minder et al. (EPFL) show that appending value-aligned first-person reflections derived from a normative constitution to pretraining documents installs stronger, more compute-scalable alignment than post-training fine-tuning — models up to 3B/500B tokens show improved constitution following, jailbreak robustness, and out-of-distribution moral dilemma performance.

alignment durability preprint · Sep 2026

When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning

Guo et al. provide a Fisher-geometric account: the safety Fisher matrix is low-rank, so alignment creates a flat basin while preserving a latent output-routing pathway that benign fine-tuning re-sharpens in output-side MLPs after just 100 examples; LoRA and ASAM suppress early collapse but weaken at scale.

EACL 2026 AI security mech-interp

Unraveling LLM Jailbreaks Through Safety Knowledge Neurons

Zhao, Ke & Huang (EACL 2026) locate safety-critical neurons by projecting residual states to vocabulary space — neurons whose top activations map to safety-concept tokens are safety neurons. Ablating them achieves mean ASR >97%; SafeTuning that reinforces these neurons outperforms all four baseline defenses across multiple LLM families.

mech-interp AI security preprint · Jun 2026

AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent

Jin et al. apply mechanistic interpretability to multi-turn coding agent safety: a 10-dimensional subspace in a single transformer layer distinguishes harmful from benign execution states, and activation steering within it substantially reduces harmful OS-level actions on MAS-Bench while preserving task utility — the first mech-interp-based runtime defense for LLM agents.

AI security preprint · Sep 9 2026

DuplexJail: Safety Alignment Breaks Under Spoken Interruption in Full-Duplex Models

Roh et al. expose a novel attack surface unique to full-duplex speech models: spoken interruption in the audio channel bypasses text-domain safety alignment, raising AdvBench ASR to 40.3%/48.7% on two frontier models with fixed-delay timing — a modality-specific vulnerability text-only safety training cannot address.
