### 1. [**Sequential Anytime Valid Tests for Efficient Inference-Time Alignment**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26413)
- **Authors / venue:** Vishesh Kumar Singh, Sayak Ray Chowdhury — arXiv preprint
- **Why it ranks here:** Closes a long-standing gap in Best-of-N alignment: existing adaptive-stopping heuristics carry no statistical guarantee; this gives one, using anytime-valid e-processes from sequential testing theory.
- **Technical summary:** The paper frames Best-of-N sampling as an inference problem: when does a sampled candidate provably dominate the generator's distribution? Candidates are scored with Reward-Weighted Self-Consistency (RWSC) and answer clusters are tested with an e-process (supermartingale betting) that bounds the probability of ever certifying the wrong answer by α, uniformly over time. The guarantee holds even when the leading answer changes during sampling — something Wald's sequential probability ratio test cannot handle. Required samples scale as O(log(1/α) / d_KL(θ* ‖ 1/2)), matching empirical observations across several models, datasets, and reward models. Accuracy improves over baselines while average token cost is substantially reduced.

![Figure 1: Comparison of e-RWSC and baseline selection methods on GSM8K, MATH500, MMLU, CruxEval with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26413v1/figures/table1_row1_bars.png)

*Figure 1: e-RWSC matches or exceeds full-budget Best-of-N accuracy across four benchmarks while stopping early for most prompts, tracing a superior accuracy–cost Pareto frontier.*

### 2. [**BlockJack: Cross-Tenant Redirection Fault Attacks on Paged KV Cache Address Translation in LLM Serving**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25945)
- **Authors / venue:** Qifan Wang, Jeremy Boy, Jonas Sander, Yansong Gao, Hongsheng Hu, Ye Dong, Thomas Eisenbarth, David Oswald — arXiv preprint
- **Why it ranks here:** First fault attack targeting vLLM's block-table address translation; demonstrated physically with Rowhammer; vLLM and Sonar acknowledged and patched.
- **Technical summary:** vLLM and similar paged-attention frameworks use block tables to map each request's logical KV-cache blocks to physical blocks, analogous to OS page tables. BlockJack corrupts block-table entries to redirect a victim tenant's KV-cache reads to an attacker-controlled block, enabling (a) confidentiality attacks where victim context leaks into attacker responses and (b) integrity attacks where attacker-supplied context steers victim answers. On a 248-scene medical benchmark, the integrity attack shifted 41.1% of responses to recommend less urgent care, while still appearing coherent with the victim's question. Physical feasibility was confirmed with Rowhammer on commodity DRAM; assignment validation is proposed as a defense.

![Figure 1: vLLM paged KV-cache block-table architecture showing how BlockJack redirects logical-to-physical block mappings](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.25945v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Corrupting one block-table entry redirects an entire sequence's KV reads to another tenant's physical block, leaking context or steering outputs without touching model weights.*

### 3. [**Jailbreak LLMs with Linguistic Style as a Hidden Attack Surface**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18601)
- **Authors / venue:** Priyanshu Mohanty, Sarthakbrata Halder, Srikant Panda, Afshin Oroojlooy, Murari Mandal — arXiv preprint
- **Why it ranks here:** Demonstrates that writing style alone produces order-of-magnitude variation in jailbreak success rates, with a mechanistic explanation tying it to the refusal representation; includes a practical DPO defense.
- **Technical summary:** Rewriting the same harmful request in different linguistic styles yields ASRs ranging from under 6% to around 80% across state-of-the-art models. A mechanistic analysis on LLaMA-3.1-8B shows that stylistic rewrites reduce alignment with the refusal direction and suppress safety attention heads while leaving the harmful-intent representation largely intact, suggesting distributed rather than localized refusal failure. A style-aware DPO defense reduces ASR from 86% to 19.5% while preserving most utility. The attack's single-pass BERT-based style selector performs competitively with more expensive multi-turn optimization attacks.

![Figure 1: Mechanistic analysis of stylistic jailbreaks on LLaMA-3.1-8B showing probe, refusal direction, and attention head analyses](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18601v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Four-panel mechanistic breakdown: harmful intent is recoverable even after stylistic rewrites, refusal direction alignment drops, safety heads are suppressed, and direction patching alone restores refusal in only ~8% of cases.*

### 4. [**DailySafe: Benchmarking Dynamic Safety Awareness in Everyday Multi-Turn Multimodal Interactions**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26298)

`tag:safety` `tag:evaluation` — Xuankun Rong, Xinhao Deng, Wenzhang Jiang, Ziheng Peng, Siyuan Liang, Wenke Huang, Pablo Arbelaez, Mang Ye — arXiv preprint

195 multi-turn, multi-image safety scenarios across 10 everyday domains and 42 task categories where risk emerges progressively across turns — testing whether models can track evolving contextual hazards while remaining helpful. Each case has case-specific safety annotations to avoid binary safe/unsafe labels; prior benchmarks test isolated harmful requests, missing gradual-disclosure risks. This is the first benchmark explicitly framed around "Dynamic Safety Awareness."

![Figure 1: DailySafe benchmark overview showing multi-turn multimodal scenarios with progressive risk disclosure](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26298v1/DailySafe_Intro.png)

*Figure 1: DailySafe scenarios involve image+text multi-turn dialogues where safety-critical evidence is revealed across multiple turns, requiring models to track evolving risk rather than detect a single harmful prompt.*

### 5. [**Erasing the Wrong Thing: Conditional Attribute Leakage and Concept Erasure in Shared Foundation-Model Embeddings**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25640)

`tag:mech-interp` `tag:security` — Haijun He, Fei Li, Donghong Ji, Junjun He — arXiv preprint

Proves that standard concept erasure (LEACE) cannot simultaneously remove a sensitive attribute and preserve task utility when the sensitive label and task label are correlated: either marginal erasure or task-conditional leakage must remain. TC-LEACE applies LEACE separately within each task-label stratum, recovering most utility loss while keeping conditional leakage within a specified margin; tested across four vision foundation models on 85 CelebA attribute pairs and three biomedical models on chest radiographs. This theoretical characterization and practical fix is directly relevant to privacy-preserving inference and concept erasure for safety.

![Figure 2: TC-LEACE pipeline showing task-conditional erasure applied within label strata](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.25640v1/x2.png)

*Figure 2: TC-LEACE enforces linear guardedness separately within each task stratum, breaking the marginal-vs-conditional leakage dilemma that defeats global LEACE.*

### 6. [**SCARVA: Interpretable Agentic Reasoning in Software Supply-Chain Analysis**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26131)

`tag:security` `tag:mech-interp` — Tim Barsch, Bruno Nogueira, Tobias Wienand, Jan Fopma, Flavio Toffalini — arXiv preprint

A framework that makes LLM agent conclusions auditable by logging every tool call, file access, and web request during software supply-chain vulnerability analysis, then having independent JUDGE agents verify each claim with evidence. On 54 manually curated cases (exploitable, non-exploitable, vulnerability-free), the best ANALYST model (Qwen 3.8) reaches 82% balanced accuracy; Claude Opus 5.5 as JUDGE agrees with human annotators on 94% of individual claim checks. Interpretable agent reasoning for real-world security tooling with provable accountability.

![Figure 2: SCARVA pipeline with ANALYST agent logging, JUDGE verification, and evidence-backed report generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26131v1/x2.png)

*Figure 2: SCARVA logs every analyst action and routes conclusions through independent judge agents that check individual claims against recorded evidence before issuing a final verdict.*

### 7. [**Probing the "Conscious" Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26590)

`tag:mech-interp` — Jiahao Huang, Jun Wang — arXiv preprint

Introduces the Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens), a multimodal Jacobian lens that maps intermediate VLM states into a shared semantic vocabulary space and supports cross-modal counterfactual interventions. Applied across VLM families, it finds that reliable selective access to concepts emerges later in training than basic accessibility, that accessed concepts demonstrably participate in downstream reasoning, and that J-space metrics track cross-modal alignment more cleanly than hidden-state cosine similarity. Provides concrete evidence for global workspace dynamics in VLMs with a reusable interpretability toolkit.

![Figure 1: MC-Lens Multimodal Concept Lens illustration on a visual question answering example](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26590v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MC-Lens projects vision encoder and language decoder intermediate states into a shared output vocabulary space, enabling concept accessibility comparisons and counterfactual interventions across modalities.*

### 8. [**Understanding MoE Decisions Beyond Gradients**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26075)

`tag:mech-interp` — Zhen Xu, Zitao Zhang, Kevin Zhao, Xuefeng Liu — arXiv preprint

In MoE models, swapping one expert can cascade through downstream routing decisions in ways that gradient-based attribution misses. This paper adds a correction term to second-order gradient predictions by modeling downstream expert-selection switches using the model's own expert outputs and routing rules, reducing attribution error by ~60% on Qwen3.6 and ~47% on Gemma4 across questions from three benchmarks. Directly applicable to understanding and debugging MoE model behavior where gradient-only explanations are systematically wrong.

![Figure 1: Attribution error comparison between gradient-only and routing-corrected methods on MoE models](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26075v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Routing-corrected attribution reduces prediction error on answer-score changes by roughly 60% (Qwen3.6) and 47% (Gemma4) compared to gradient-only second-order methods.*

### 9. [**Revealing Pathway Interactions in Network Computations with Complete Path Contribution**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26101)

`tag:mech-interp` — Shenghua Liu, Youssef Faragalla, Surya Ganguli, Stephen A. Baccus — arXiv preprint

Decomposes network outputs into contributions from individual causal paths (not just pairwise interactions between layers), with contributions summing exactly to the output at any granularity — a completeness guarantee most attribution methods lack. An efficient top-k path algorithm runs in roughly linear time in k rather than exponential in depth. Applied to image classifiers and an interpretable retina circuit model to characterize excitatory-inhibitory pathway interactions; the method enables compositional circuit analysis without approximation errors accumulating across levels.

![Figure 1: Complete Path Contribution decomposition of network output into individual causal pathway contributions](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26101v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Path contributions from input to output are summed exactly, allowing circuits to be defined at any coarseness with zero residual; the top-k efficient search identifies dominant paths without enumerating all exponentially many.*

### 10. [**A CPU Inside an LLM: Zero-Gradient Compilation of Exact Circuits into Pretrained Weights**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26450)

`tag:mech-interp` — Tanmay Sah — arXiv preprint

Writes deterministic arithmetic circuits directly into a pretrained language model's weights — without gradient training, training data, or runtime tool hooks — by exploiting RMSNorm properties via Mellin-GLU quadrature, cross-token scale cancellation, and a five-stage compiler. On 6-digit arithmetic, IBM Granite 4.2 3B reaches 99.17% ALU-prefix accuracy and Qwen 3.5 4B reaches 100.00%, both far ahead of LoRA baselines (42.2% and 61.7% respectively). Demonstrates that exact computational circuits can be mechanistically implanted into pretrained models, with implications for understanding where and how models store exact computation.

![Figure 1: Overview of zero-gradient circuit compilation pipeline into pretrained language model weights](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26450v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Five-stage compiler synthesizes signed arithmetic circuits and writes them into pretrained weights using Mellin-GLU quadrature and cross-token scale cancellation, achieving near-perfect accuracy without any gradient updates.*
