ICLR 2026 pretraining safety tamper resistance

Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

Post-training defenses against adversarial fine-tuning keep failing — this paper shows why, and offers a different answer: if you never teach the model harmful knowledge during pretraining, it has nothing to recover. Pretraining data filtering for biothreat content produces tamper resistance that outlasts 300 million adversarial fine-tuning tokens — an order of magnitude beyond any existing post-training safeguard.

EleutherAI and AISI researchers pretrained multiple 6.9B-parameter models from scratch on two corpora: an unfiltered baseline and a version with dual-use biothreat-proxy content removed via a two-stage pipeline (keyword-guided document-level classifier + dense passage scoring). Filtered models score near-baseline on BioLP-Bench and on unrelated benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag). Under subsequent adversarial fine-tuning on up to 10,000 steps / 300 M tokens, filtered models retain safety margins that post-training defenses (TAR, RepNoise) abandon within 1,000 steps — over an order of magnitude better — representing the first peer-reviewed demonstration that pretraining corpus composition, not post-training RLHF/DPO, is the primary determinant of long-run tamper resistance in open-weight models.

ICML 2026 tamper resistance fine-tuning attack

Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks

The two most-deployed open-weight tamper-resistance defenses — TAR and SEAM — can be defeated by attacks that require no gradient computation at all. A single direction-removal step or a partial-response prefix is enough to push attack success rates from below 10% to as high as 96%, which places the security claim of post-training safeguards in serious doubt.

CMU and UC Berkeley researchers evaluate two gradient-free attacks against TAR and SEAM: (1) abliteration — project all weight matrices orthogonal to the extracted refusal direction, requiring only a small contrastive prompt set and no training; (2) prefilling — inject a partial harmful response prefix to preempt refusal. Across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench, ASR rises from below 10% to 16–96% against the defended variants. The authors propose ART (abliteration-resistant tuning), adding an abliteration-based objective to the defense training loop, which recovers 10–20 pp of robustness — but does not fully close the gap, confirming that post-training defense alone is insufficient when white-box access is assumed.

pretraining safety synthetic persona preprint

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero

What if alignment started before RLHF — before post-training, before supervised fine-tuning, at the very first token of pretraining? EPFL's SPP method weaves value-aligned first-person reflections from a normative constitution directly into pretraining corpora, reducing attack success rate by 63% and crucially generalizing to value dimensions never seen in post-training.

EPFL dlab's SPP annotates pretraining documents with value-aligned first-person reflections derived from a normative value constitution and trains language models on this annotated corpus from scratch. Evaluated on FineWeb-derived corpora at multiple scales, SPP reduces mean attack success rate (ASR) to 1.7% — a 63% reduction over the unmodified baseline. Critically, SPP models retain this advantage even when post-training data omits those same value dimensions, suggesting that pretraining installs generalizable value representations rather than surface-level associations. The advantage scales with pretraining compute budget, indicating that longer pretraining deepens rather than saturates value installation.

When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning

Proposes a Fisher-geometric explanation for why benign fine-tuning collapses LLM safety: safety Fisher information is low-rank, alignment flattens the safety loss landscape while preserving an output-routing pathway in output-side MLP modules, and just 100 benign fine-tuning steps selectively resharp this pathway. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse but weaken at larger scale; the routing view explains why a small number of safety re-injection examples suffice to recover refusal — the safety representations are structurally preserved, only de-activated. (Indiana University, Tsinghua, NTU; arXiv 2609.01455, September 1, 2026.)

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) argues that data filtering alone misses compositional risks — benign knowledge combined at inference can yield unsafe behavior. SRP inserts structured self-monitoring reflections at regular corpus intervals so models learn self-monitoring during language modeling itself. 1.7B experiments on FineWeb-Edu show improved safety accuracy and lower inference/fine-tuning attack success rates; the method works best when post-training uses a compatible reflection format, pointing to a pretraining↔post-training interaction that benefits from end-to-end design. (Jinhan Li et al.; arXiv 2606.19168, June 17, 2026.)

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases

AMRA addresses the root cause of abliteration's effectiveness: the refusal direction is easy to extract because safety training places it in a consistent, high-amplitude position. Rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases while correcting downstream readers to preserve model behavior. On Llama-3-8B, post-abliteration refusal scores improve by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with less than 0.5 pp MMLU degradation. (Nathan Truong; arXiv 2608.18093, August 2026.)

From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling

Characterizes LLM safety as a three-stage circuit — Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads — using causal ablation to confirm the linear dependency. Selectively scaling component weight magnitudes (no gradient descent required) improves safety rates by 26.5% with only 1.7% accuracy drop, demonstrating that mechanistic circuit identification can directly guide targeted weight-space safety improvement. Accepted to EMNLP 2026 Findings (submitted August 30, 2026). (Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng; UC San Diego; arXiv 2609.00051.)

jailbreak code security preprint · June 2026

Grammar-Constrained Decoding Can Jailbreak LLMs into Generating Malicious Code

CodeSpear discovers that Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) — a reliability technique enforcing syntactic validity in code — inadvertently suppresses refusal tokens, creating a jailbreak surface that requires only a grammar constraint specification and no adversarial optimization. Tested on 10 popular LLMs across 4 benchmarks, CodeSpear improves ASR by more than 30 percentage points over prior baselines. Defense CodeShield trains models to generate semantically harmless honeypot code under GCD. (Yitong Zhang, Shiteng Lu, Jia Li; Tsinghua University; arXiv 2606.11817, June 10, 2026.)

AI control oversight preprint · June 2026

Bootstrapped Monitoring: Leveraging Transparent Reasoning to Oversee Stronger AI Agents

Bootstrapped monitoring inserts a stronger intermediate untrusted model (with transparent CoT) between the agent and the weak trusted monitor; the trusted model audits the reasoning trace rather than the agent's actions. Evaluated on BashArena multi-turn software engineering tasks, bootstrapped monitoring substantially improves catch rates over trusted-only monitoring even when the intermediate monitor actively colludes with the agent — provided raw CoT is accessible. The result extends the useful oversight lifetime of trusted models as the capabilities gap widens. (Frank Xiao, Mary Phuong; arXiv 2606.11998, June 10, 2026.)

AI control CoT monitoring preprint · July 2026

Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety

Identifies two mechanisms enabling CoT monitoring — computational necessity (model must externalize to solve hard tasks) and propensity (model externalizes by habit) — and shows both are currently present but neither is guaranteed to persist: CoT controllability increases with scale and OpenAI reports declining monitorability in its newest models. Recommends that frontier developers treat CoT monitorability as a design consideration, not an incidental property, and invest in CoT monitoring as a complementary safety layer alongside training-time alignment. Companion to #9's bootstrapped monitoring protocol. (Tomek Korbak et al.; arXiv 2507.11473, July 2026.)
