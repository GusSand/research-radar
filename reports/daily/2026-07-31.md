## 01 · [TensorLens: End-to-End Transformer Analysis via High-Order Attention Tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17958)

`mech-interp` `peer-reviewed` `ACL 2026` — Ido Andrew Atad, Itamar Zimerman, Shahar Katz, Lior Wolf — arXiv January 25, 2026; ACL 2026 long paper

Most interpretability tools dissect transformers layer-by-layer or head-by-head, losing the global computation structure at every cut. TensorLens collapses the full forward pass — attention, FFNs, layer norms, activations, and residual connections — into a single input-dependent high-order tensor, producing exact relevance scores for each input token to each output position without any approximation or localized analysis.

![Figure 1: TensorLens high-order attention tensor construction and relevance score propagation across layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17958/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The high-order attention-interaction tensor jointly encodes all sublayer interactions; contracting it against an output position yields an exact, globally coherent input-to-output relevance score — unlike gradient or attention-rollout methods that accumulate approximation error across layers.*

Atad et al. derive a factored representation of the standard transformer (both encoder and decoder variants, including vision transformers) as an input-dependent linear operator expressed through a tensor formed by the outer product of per-layer attention matrices and FFN outputs. The key result is that this tensor can be exactly contracted to produce input-to-output relevance scores that simultaneously respect layer ordering, multi-head competition, FFN gating, and residual connections — properties that rollout approximations and gradient methods each violate separately. Evaluated on language and vision transformers, TensorLens relevance scores achieve better alignment with human attention labels and more faithful circuit-extraction results than attention rollout, raw gradient, and GradCAM baselines. Code available at [github.com/idoatad/TensorLens](https://github.com/idoatad/TensorLens).

## 02 · [SecRespond: Benchmarking AI Agents for Real-World Post-Compromise Incident Response](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26791)

`AI security` `preprint` — Lehan Wang, Boli Chen, Ruixue Ding, Pengjun Xie, Jinwei Huang et al. (Alibaba) — arXiv, July 29, 2026

AI agents are being deployed in security operations centers, but the field lacks benchmarks that test the full post-compromise workflow rather than isolated tool-use. SecRespond puts 23 frontier LLMs against 10 reconstructed real-world cloud incidents — and no model fully solves any of them.

![Figure 1: SecRespond evaluation setup — 10 cyber ranges with disk snapshots, alert suites, and ATT&CK technique labels across 5 operating systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26791/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Each cyber range gives the agent a forensic disk snapshot of a compromised host plus host-security-product alerts, vulnerability scans, and baseline checks; the agent must produce forensic reports for intrusion, baseline risk, and vulnerability risk, plus a verified remediation plan. Coverage spans 21 ATT&CK techniques and 5 operating systems.*

SecRespond constructs 10 cyber ranges from distinct compromised cloud hosts spanning 4 entry-point types (credential stuffing, supply-chain, zero-day, insider), 21 ATT&CK techniques, and 5 operating systems. Agents run on the OpenCode harness with full host artifact and CLI access. Evaluation across 23 frontier LLMs reveals a consistent failure mode: agents reliably uncover problems flagged by alerts, but systematically fail at proactive disk forensics (silent intrusions invisible to alerting) and at producing verified, comprehensive remediation plans. No model achieves complete detection-plus-remediation on any single range — a gap the authors attribute to the compositional reasoning and long-horizon planning demands of the post-compromise workflow, not to individual tool-use failures.

## 03 · [Beyond the Bidirectional Promise: Re-evaluating the Robustness of Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27386)

`dLLM` `AI security` `preprint` — Microsoft Research — arXiv, July 29, 2026

Diffusion language models (dLLMs) are often credited with inherent robustness from their stochastic denoising process. This Microsoft study runs the first systematic comparison under both natural noise and adversarial attacks — and finds the story is far more nuanced than "bidirectional attention makes you robust."

![Figure 1: Robustness comparison LLaDA-8B vs LLaMA-3-8B and Dream-7B vs Qwen2.5-7B across 32 perturbation conditions and adversarial gradient probes](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27386/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Parameter-matched pairs (LLaDA-8B/LLaMA-3-8B and Dream-7B/Qwen2.5-7B) evaluated under 32 natural perturbation conditions and adversarial gradient probes. dLLMs resist gradient-based suffix attacks but show no systematic advantage over AR models under natural noise — robustness is weight-dependent, not architecturally guaranteed.*

Using two parameter-matched pairs (LLaDA-8B vs. LLaMA-3-8B and Dream-7B vs. Qwen2.5-7B), the authors evaluate robustness across 32 natural perturbation conditions (character swaps, word insertions, paraphrase, truncation, OCR errors), adversarial gradient suffix attacks, and mechanistic hidden-state analysis. The key finding: dLLMs' highly stochastic loss landscapes naturally resist gradient-based adversarial suffix attacks — GCG and similar methods that rely on a smooth gradient signal largely fail. But this architectural property provides no protection against natural input noise; everyday robustness under perturbations the system encounters in deployment is weight-dependent and no better than autoregressive baselines. Hidden-state analysis further shows that dLLMs develop qualitatively different internal representations of corrupted inputs, with less graceful degradation on out-of-distribution tokens.

**04 · [Minimizing Targeted Activations: Input-Only Suppression of Evaluation-Awareness Latents in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25907)**
`mech-interp` `AI control` — Deepanshu Mody et al. (NYU Center for Data Science) — arXiv, July 28, 2026

![Fluent Dreaming / EPO adversarial input optimization suppressing 5 evaluation-awareness representations on Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25907/x1.png)

*Figure 4: Input-only optimization (Fluent Dreaming + negated feature term with GCG-style token search and self-cross-entropy fluency regularizer) successfully drives evaluation-awareness latents toward zero across all five representation types — CAA direction, subspace norm, SAE feature, MLP neuron, behavioral logit — with no inference-time model access required.*

Evaluation-awareness latents — features that activate when a model detects it is being tested — are a core concern for AI control: if they can be suppressed from the input side, safety evaluations on adversarially crafted prompts may be invalid. The authors adapt Fluent Dreaming / EPO with a negated feature objective and GCG-style token search to suppress five distinct representations of evaluation-awareness (CAA direction, subspace norm, SAE feature, MLP neuron, behavioral logit) on Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B. All five are suppressible from the input side, and the attack transfers across representation types — suppressing the SAE feature also suppresses the behavioral logit in most conditions — suggesting they reflect a single underlying mechanism. Implication for AI control: input-level sanitization alone is insufficient to guarantee evaluation-awareness probes are valid.

**05 · [Cyber-Capable AI Agents: Vulnerabilities, Evaluation Containment, and Defensive Response](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25379)**
`AI security` — arXiv, July 28, 2026

![Five vulnerability classes at cyber-capable AI agent evaluation boundaries: offensive chains, objective conflicts, supply-chain/credential exposure, persistent C2, and speed of automation](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25379/x1.png)

*Figure 5: Five vulnerability classes synthesized from the wider literature, illustrated against the July 2026 HuggingFace/OpenAI incident as a bounded case study. The incident-specific observations are distinguished from findings established across multiple prior evaluations.*

Synthesizes five vulnerability classes arising specifically at the evaluation-containment boundary of cyber-capable AI agents: multi-step offensive capability chains that span sandbox borders, agent objectives that conflict with confinement constraints, supply-chain and credential exposure in evaluation infrastructure, persistent command-and-control that outlasts the evaluation session, and the speed differential between automated attack execution and human response. Uses the reported July 2026 HuggingFace/OpenAI incident as a bounded case study to ground the taxonomy, carefully distinguishing incident-specific findings from vulnerabilities established in the wider literature. Provides practical guidance for evaluation infrastructure design that reduces unintended capability leakage beyond the test boundary.

**06 · [AISPA: User-Centric System Prompt Auditing for Large Language Model Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28617)**
`AI security` — 26 researchers — arXiv, July 30, 2026

![AISPA 8-dimension audit framework: identity transparency, information truthfulness, data privacy, action safety, user agency, unsafe request handling, harm prevention, fairness](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28617/x1.png)

*Figure 6: Eight-dimension AISPA framework applied to 3,249 instructions extracted from system prompts across 88 commercial AI products. Each instruction is classified as user-protective or user-harming; the distribution reveals that a significant fraction of commercial system prompts contain instructions that prioritize product interests over user interests.*

System prompts in deployed AI products are rarely disclosed to users or regulators. AISPA introduces an 8-dimension audit framework — identity transparency, information truthfulness, data privacy, action safety, user agency and manipulation prevention, unsafe request handling, harm prevention, and fairness — and applies it to 3,249 instructions extracted from the system prompts of 88 commercial AI products. Unlike frameworks designed to protect systems from external adversaries, AISPA is user-centric: it asks which instructions protect the user and which expose them to harm from the AI system itself. The audit is the first systematic public corpus of commercial system prompt instructions with human-labeled safety classifications across all eight dimensions.

**07 · [AI Security Priorities: A Field-Wide Agenda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26069)**
`AI security` `peer-reviewed` `ESORICS 2026 (LLMSec Workshop)` — Gil Gekker, Rachel Steratore, Everett Smith et al. — arXiv, July 29, 2026

![AI security research priority map across five axes: model security, agentic security, infrastructure security, evaluation, and governance](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26069/x1.png)

*Figure 7: Field-wide priority map produced by multi-institutional collaboration, identifying underinvested research areas (model provenance, evaluation-time security, agentic containment) and oversaturated ones (single-turn jailbreak defenses) based on impact-to-effort analysis.*

Accepted at the LLMSec Workshop co-located with ESORICS 2026, this field-agenda paper synthesizes priorities across model security, agentic security, infrastructure security, evaluation robustness, and governance. The paper argues that single-turn jailbreak defenses are oversaturated relative to their risk importance while model provenance, evaluation-time security, and agentic containment are systematically underinvested. Particularly notable: the paper explicitly calls out interpretability-based security auditing (the dual-use direction highlighted in recent mech-interp work) as both high-priority and under-resourced. Multi-institutional authorship covering both academic and industry perspectives.

**08 · [Stemma: Induced Decision Regions Reveal LLM Provenance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25880)**
`AI security` `mech-interp` — Keyu Zhang, Vadim Safronov, Andrew Martin — arXiv, July 28, 2026

![Stemma induced decision regions separating LLM family outputs in 2D projection: distinct regions for GPT-4, Claude, Llama, Gemini families](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25880/x1.png)

*Figure 8: Induced decision regions in a 2D projection of LLM output space: systematically constructed probe inputs that maximize the inter-family decision boundary separate GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and Llama outputs with high precision — enabling provenance attribution without access to model weights.*

Stemma induces decision-region boundaries between LLM families by constructing targeted probe inputs that maximally amplify behavioral divergence between candidate models. Rather than treating outputs as monolithic embeddings, Stemma exploits the fact that different LLM families occupy distinct regions of output space on carefully crafted boundary-seeking prompts. The resulting provenance classifier achieves high accuracy at the family and variant level without access to model weights or logits, requiring only black-box output text. This has dual-use implications: legitimate use includes attributing model outputs for compliance and watermarking; adversarial use includes identifying which model a deployed application uses, informing model-specific attacks.

**09 · [Forecasting Trajectory-Level Safety Risks in Black-Box Multi-Turn Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26820)**
`AI security` — Shi Lin, Peng Qian, Dinghao Liu, Renjie Sun, Sifan Wu et al. — arXiv, July 29, 2026

![Trajectory-level safety risk: single-turn risk scores miss cumulative intent drift; trajectory forecast detects 3-turn ahead](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26820/x1.png)

*Figure 9: Turn-level safety evaluators miss multi-turn safety failures that emerge from gradual topic drift. The trajectory-level forecaster provides 3-turn advance warning by modeling cumulative risk accumulation rather than per-turn classification.*

Current safety guardrails evaluate each turn in isolation, missing risks that emerge from gradual intent drift across a multi-turn conversation. This paper proposes a trajectory-level safety risk forecaster that uses trajectory-state modeling to predict when a conversation will cross a safety boundary, providing early warning without requiring model internals (black-box). Tested on a large-scale multi-turn interaction dataset, the forecaster provides accurate safety risk predictions up to 3 turns ahead with high precision, outperforming per-turn classifiers at low false-positive rates. The paper also characterizes the taxonomy of trajectory-level risks: fragmented instruction assembly, sensitivity escalation, and intent drift — categories that per-turn classifiers are structurally unable to detect.

**10 · [On-Policy Distillation for LLM Safety: A Routing Approach to Template-Robust Realignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27081)**
`AI security` — Yongjian Guo, Wanlun Ma, Lingyu Shen, Xi Xiao, Sheng Wen — arXiv, July 29, 2026

![On-policy safety distillation routing: teacher-generated on-policy demonstrations routed by template type, reducing ASR from 31% to 6% under template shift](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27081/x1.png)

*Figure 10: On-policy distillation with template-aware routing reduces attack success rate from 31% (standard alignment) to 6% (AISPA-routed on-policy distillation) under template shift conditions, outperforming DPO and SFT baselines that degrade when the jailbreak template changes.*

Standard safety alignment (SFT, RLHF, DPO) overfits to specific jailbreak templates seen during training and degrades significantly when the attacker switches to an unseen template. On-Policy Distillation for LLM Safety generates teacher demonstrations from the model's own on-policy distribution and applies template-aware routing to assign demonstrations to alignment objectives that match their template structure. The routing prevents the model from treating all jailbreak templates identically, preserving template-specific safety geometry. Under 6 unseen jailbreak templates, the approach reduces attack success rate from 31% (standard alignment) to 6%, outperforming DPO and SFT baselines that degrade to 19–24% ASR under template shift.

## Notes

- **Peer-reviewed count:** TensorLens (#1, ACL 2026 long paper) and AI Security Priorities (#7, ESORICS 2026 LLMSec Workshop). All remaining entries are preprints.
- **Today's theme — the frontier of evaluation validity:** Four papers today converge on the question of whether our safety evaluations can be trusted. Minimizing Targeted Activations (#4) shows input-side suppression of evaluation-awareness; Cyber-Capable AI Agents (#5) maps vulnerabilities at evaluation containment boundaries; SecRespond (#2) reveals that current LLMs can't generalize to post-compromise workflows; and Forecasting Trajectory Safety (#9) shows per-turn evaluations miss multi-turn risks. Together they form the strongest day-level argument yet that evaluation infrastructure must be treated as a first-class security component.

- **System prompt auditing (#6):** AISPA (July 30, 2026) is the freshest paper in today's sweep — barely 24 hours old. The 8-dimension audit of 3,249 instructions across 88 commercial products is the first systematic public corpus of commercial system prompt safety classifications.
