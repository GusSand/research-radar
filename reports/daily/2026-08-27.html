Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits

Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant — arXiv preprint, August 7, 2026

Safety alignment in diffusion LLMs turns out to be a thin, inherited veneer: a compact cluster of safety-critical neurons, mapped directly from autoregressive predecessors, can be identified, pruned, and even transferred across architectures — raising attack success rates from under 3% to over 73%.

The authors apply activation patching and linear probing to locate safety-critical neurons in LLaDA (trained from scratch) and Dream / Fast-dLLM (initialized from Qwen2.5). Self-pruning those neurons raises ASR from 2.6% → 73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9% → 86.6% on Dream. Transfer pruning — extracting the safety-neuron map from Qwen2.5 and pruning it out of the derived DLLMs — achieves 73.2% on Dream and 86.3% on Fast-dLLM without any DLLM-specific analysis. A black-box offline jailbreak ("SN-Guided Diffusion") steers the diffusion sampling trajectory away from safety mechanisms without white-box access. The core finding: DLLM safety is mechanistically sparse and inherited from the AR source model, providing no architectural safety benefit over the AR baseline.

Beyond Static Interpretability: Anticipating Post-SFT Mechanisms from Pre-SFT Parameters for Better Tuning

Hang Chen, Jiaying Zhu, Wenya Wang — arXiv preprint, August 25, 2026

Mechanistic interpretability has always looked backward — you identify what the trained model uses. For novel-task fine-tuning, this timing is not just inconvenient but actively harmful: the neurons the pre-SFT model uses differ sharply from those that end up governing the fine-tuned model, and pointing LoRA at the wrong targets degrades performance.

The authors model SFT as continuous parameter evolution and derive a Taylor expansion of the post-SFT mechanistic objective from pre-SFT weights and the target dataset. This closed-form approximation forecasts which parameters will be task-critical after training, enabling accurate LoRA target selection before any fine-tuning occurs. Empirically, naively using pre-SFT localization introduces active bias for novel tasks — the pre- and post-SFT critical-neuron sets diverge substantially — and the proposed forward-looking framework outperforms standard PEFT baselines. The 25-page paper is the first to unite mechanistic interpretability with pre-training optimization in a locating-then-tuning paradigm grounded in theory.

Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders

arXiv preprint, August 2026 · Model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B

Chain-of-Thought reasoning demonstrably outperforms direct-answer generation — but until now the internal mechanism behind that advantage has been opaque. Applying SAEs to a live reasoning model shows that Thinking and NoThinking modes are not just behaviorally different: they operate from mechanistically distinct feature subspaces.

Top-K SAEs are applied to the residual stream of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B during math-solving tasks at three difficulty levels. Thinking (CoT) mode activates a sparse, high-intensity cluster of verbal-deduction features that remain stable regardless of problem difficulty. NoThinking (direct answer) mode exhibits an adaptive, diffuse pattern centered on symbolic manipulation features whose density scales with task difficulty. The two modes are cleanly separable at the feature level, suggesting that CoT's quality advantage is mechanistically anchored in an identifiable, steer-able feature subspace.

BLADE: Bilevel Low-rank Augmented-Lagrangian Erasure for LLM Unlearning

arXiv, August 2026

Bilevel optimization confined to LoRA adapters: a clamped-entropy forget loss erases target knowledge; an asymmetric augmented Lagrangian protects the retain set; the bilevel structure runs a retain-repair step before each forgetting update. Delivers the strongest systematic gains across all three standard unlearning benchmarks in a single method: +6% on TOFU, +9% on MUSE Books, +7% on KnowUndo composite scores over prior best baselines.

Measure, Don't Optimize: Forecasting Recovery in LLM Unlearning

arXiv, August 11, 2026

J-Access is an inference-time audit that uses the Jacobian lens to project post-unlearning intermediate representations into vocabulary space and quantifies how often target concepts remain accessible along the output pathway. The central hypothesis — residual accessibility predicts recovery susceptibility — enables proactive risk monitoring: concepts scoring high on J-Access require less continued training to recover, allowing practitioners to flag insufficiently erased knowledge before it resurfaces under fine-tuning pressure.

SAEVerbalizer: Generating Explanations for Sparse Autoencoder Features via Representation Verbalization

Weihan Meng et al. (Tsinghua, PKU, Fudan) — arXiv, August 13, 2026

Injects SAE decoder directions into the LLM's residual stream and fine-tunes downstream layers to generate natural-language explanations from the geometric structure of the feature — no expensive activation-maximization sampling required. Verbalization generalizes to unseen features, transfers across separately trained SAE dictionaries, and extends to features from different LLMs via a lightweight adapter, resolving a key scalability bottleneck in SAE auto-interpretability.

Mechanistic Circuit Identification for Controllable Data Generation

Nakyung Lee, Sangwoo Hong, Jungwoo Lee (Seoul National University, Konkuk University) — arXiv, August 25, 2026

Identifies model-internal circuits governing three data quality axes (learnability, challenge, alignment) via causal intervention and uses them as controllable interfaces to steer LLM-based data generation. SAMS (Stage-Aware Mechanistic Scheduling) schedules circuit-steered samples according to the model's evolving optimization dynamics rather than static heuristic prompts — giving data synthesis pipelines feedback from the model's actual learning state for the first time.

Forgotten in Weights, Recovered by Tools: Agentic Tool Unlearning for LLM Agents

Baicheng Chen, Zheyuan Liu et al. — arXiv, August 21, 2026

Identifies tool-mediated recovery as a new unlearning failure mode: suppressing direct parametric recall is insufficient when an agent can route the same query through web search, retrieval, or database tools. ATU (Agentic Tool Unlearning) addresses this with Stage 1 (parametric unlearning) followed by Stage 2 (trajectory-level RL penalizing target-seeking tool calls and final-answer leakage). First work to study unlearning specifically in the agentic tool-use setting.

Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability

Vijay Erramilli — arXiv, August 2026

A 24-page, 13-figure framework arguing that mechanistic interpretability measurements should be co-designed with their control objectives — what interventions are feasible, what robustness is required, what scale is needed — rather than evaluated in isolation. Bridges the mech interp toolbox (circuits, features, probes) with control-theoretic perspectives, providing a structured decision space for tool selection based on the intended downstream control operation.

AI security preprint

On Understanding, Identifying, and Mitigating Vulnerabilities in Agentic Large Language Models

arXiv, August 2026

Systematic literature review (PRISMA 2020): 743 records screened, 85 papers retained (2023–2025). Perception-layer vulnerabilities (prompt injection, jailbreaking) dominate at 66% of papers; action-layer vulnerabilities (tool misuse, code execution abuse) are significant but underrepresented. Attack research outpaces defense work 3.9:1. Provides a structured taxonomy of attack surfaces across the agent lifecycle and identifies the most underdeveloped defense categories as research priorities.
