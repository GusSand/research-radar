Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs
Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin G. Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman · ICLR 2026 · arXiv:2508.06601

The central question for open-weight AI safety is whether safety measures applied after training can be made durable — or whether a determined adversary can always fine-tune them away. This paper gives the sharpest answer yet: they can be fine-tuned away, but only if the model knew the hazardous material in the first place. Remove it from pretraining data, and there is nothing to unlock.

The paper introduces a multi-stage document-level filtering pipeline targeting dual-use content (specifically biothreat proxy knowledge). Filtered corpora train models up to the same capability level on general tasks, but the hazard-domain knowledge is absent rather than suppressed. The core finding: adversarial fine-tuning can reinstate a surface refusal that has been patched via RLHF/DPO, because the underlying knowledge is still present in the weights — but it cannot summon knowledge that was never encoded. Across a suite of biothreat proxy benchmarks, filtered models resist even sustained adversarial fine-tuning budgets where RLHF baselines recover within the first 50–100 steps. The filter pipeline is corpus-scale (applied to web text) and uses a multi-stage classifier cascade to control precision/recall trade-offs without unacceptable capability degradation on standard benchmarks.

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero
EPFL Data Lab · arXiv preprint, August 2026 · arXiv:2608.13482

Data filtering removes hazardous knowledge; Synthetic Persona Pretraining installs aligned values. Where Deep Ignorance asks "what should the model not know?", SPP asks "who should the model think it is — and can you settle that question before the first weight update?" The answer, for models up to 3B parameters on 500B tokens, is a firm yes, and the timing matters enormously.

SPP annotates each pretraining document with a short first-person moral reflection generated from a normative value constitution. Both the document and its reflection are trained on via standard cross-entropy loss — no architectural changes, no additional objectives. The key finding is that timing is load-bearing: introducing SPP only at the last 10% of pretraining improves constitution adherence but does not shift value priorities or reduce dilemma misalignment rates. Full token-zero SPP achieves 1.7% mean attack success rate, a 63% reduction over the no-SPP baseline. Post-training persona binding (associating the value-laden pretraining persona with the assistant role) is also required — without it, the values do not transfer to instruction-following behavior. The result is a pretraining-stage alignment intervention that is complementary to data filtering: filtering removes hazard capability; SPP installs normative priors.

When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning
Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang · IU Bloomington / Tsinghua / NTU · arXiv preprint, September 1, 2026 · arXiv:2609.01455

Fine-tuning a model on a completely benign dataset — a coding benchmark, a translation corpus — reliably breaks its safety alignment, and nobody had a satisfying explanation why. This paper offers the first mechanistic account: alignment makes the safety Fisher geometry flat, and benign fine-tuning selectively resharpens one dormant pathway through it.

The authors show that alignment creates a low-rank safety Fisher: the parameters most critical for refusal behavior occupy a flat, low-curvature region of loss landscape. This flatness means small perturbations can change routing behavior without disrupting utility. Benign fine-tuning — even on clean capability data — selectively re-sharpens the output-side MLP modules that implement an output-routing pathway for the safety signal. The asymmetry explains a long-standing puzzle: safety collapses to high attack success rates while MMLU and HumanEval degrade mildly, because the routing geometry is output-localized. A practical implication: LoRA and ASAM suppress early collapse by dampening output-side sharpness, but their protection weakens at large fine-tuning budgets. The routing view also explains recovery: because the underlying safety representations remain intact, a handful of safety examples can restore refusal by re-routing — consistent with observed few-shot safety recovery.

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases
Nathan Truong · arXiv preprint, August 2026 · arXiv:2608.18093

Abliteration (jailbreaking via orthogonal projection of weight matrices against the extracted refusal direction) remains trivially easy because the refusal direction is simply computed as a mean difference of harmful/harmless hidden states. AMRA addresses the root cause: rank-k updates to residual-stream writer matrices replace true refusal activations with randomly chosen aliases, while reader matrices are corrected to maintain output behavior. On Llama-3-8B, post-abliteration refusal scores improve 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with under 0.5pp MMLU degradation.

Dual-Adversarial Safety Alignment: Cultivating Intrinsic Threat Comprehension in LRMs
Hongli Shen, Shaopeng Fu, Qinbo Zhang, Jian Li, Di Wang · KAUST / Stony Brook · arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026 · arXiv:2608.09542

Large Reasoning Models inherit jailbreak vulnerabilities from their base LLMs but existing alignment methods align to surface jailbreak patterns rather than to the underlying threat mechanisms — causing systematic generalization failure. AdvSafe uses a dynamic adversarial synthesis phase to teach the LRM what makes a jailbreak work, not just which patterns to reject. With 1,000 synthesized samples, AdvSafe-aligned LRMs significantly outperform existing baselines on held-out jailbreak suites with near-zero utility degradation across standard benchmarks.

From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling
Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng · UC San Diego · EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed) · arXiv:2609.00051

This EMNLP 2026 paper maps LLM safety as a multi-stage circuit: Harmful Detection Heads identify dangerous intent, Safety Neurons in the residual stream stabilize the signal, and Refusal Heads translate it into safe output. Rather than activation steering (runtime), the authors scale the weights of identified circuit components directly — a persistent intervention. Across six LLMs, circuit-guided weight scaling improves attack success rate reduction by 26.5pp with a 1.7% accuracy drop on four benchmarks, making it the first peer-reviewed demonstration that circuit-level weight surgery is practical for production safety at this scale.

Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible
Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci · COLM 2026 (peer-reviewed) · arXiv:2608.08383

Steering vectors are routinely used for representation engineering but silently increase harmful compliance rates by disrupting safety mechanisms. This COLM 2026 paper shows the disruption comes from a decomposable, separable sub-component of the steering vector that has minimal effect on the steering objective itself. A constrained optimization (primal-dual updates) isolates and removes this component, subject to preserving steering effect and bounding false refusal. The result: safety-preserving steering is achievable as a post-hoc correction to any existing steering vector, without retraining.

Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features
Bonn-Aachen ICT / Lamarr Institute · arXiv preprint, August 11, 2026 · arXiv:2608.11025

Emergent misalignment (EM) — harmful behavior arising after fine-tuning on a narrow unrelated task — has a mechanistic substrate in persona features. Using SAE-based model diffing across four open-weight models, this paper attributes EM to pretraining documents that activate jailbreak-persona, sarcasm, deception, and manipulation features. Causal evidence: steering individual identified SAE features induces EM at up to 62% rate in aligned models, exceeding the 35% rate from misalignment fine-tuning itself, and re-aligns misaligned models to near-baseline — providing a high-precision tool for studying and controlling EM.

mech-interp-theory identifiability Koopman
Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability
Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen · arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026 · arXiv:2608.10172

A fundamental gap in mech interp: circuits found by different methods on the same model differ substantially, with no theory saying which (if any) is intrinsic. This paper proves the model's Koopman spectrum — derived by treating the forward pass as a controlled dynamical system with depth as time — is coordinate-free and recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{-1/2} (with matching minimax lower bound). A dissociation theorem shows that when the realization is non-normal, the directions carrying activation variance and those carrying cross-depth information cannot coincide, directly challenging variance-based feature importance metrics used in most SAE evaluations.

Securing Agentic AI: From Per-Action Checks to Trajectory Assurance
Alireza Lotfi, Subangkar K. Shanto, Imtiaz Karim, Elisa Bertino · ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026 Visionary Track · arXiv:2608.01558

As LLM-based agents delegate tasks across organizational boundaries, per-action safety checks become insufficient: a sequence of individually-approved actions can violate system invariants in aggregate. This ACM 2026 paper proposes Trajectory Assurance as a security primitive — runtime monitors enforcing invariants over complete action sequences rather than individual steps. Covers attack surfaces at the single-agent level (untrusted prompts, memory, retrieval, tool interfaces) through to multi-agent delegation, and frames trajectory assurance as a necessary complement to per-action sandboxing for production-scale agentic deployments.
