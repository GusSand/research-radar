## 01 · [Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20412)

- **Authors / venue:** Geodesic Research — arXiv preprint (September 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Critical empirical challenge to alignment midtraining (AMT) as a safety strategy: at 110B parameters and up to 1B midtraining tokens, a tiny competing fine-tuning signal (~50K tokens) erases the effects of 190M tokens of midtraining. Sets an important negative benchmark for how durable midtraining-based alignment actually is.
- **Technical summary:** The authors evaluate AMT using a fictional "Dispatch" task in which a model is midtrained on a Charter governing trading-crew assignments — a controlled analogue for value specification via principled documents. Tests run across 7B and 110B (GLM-4.5-Air) with 200M–1B midtraining tokens. Key findings: (1) ~50K tokens of competing fine-tuning data overpower 190M tokens of midtraining motivation; (2) generalization to rules not seen during midtraining is near chance, meaning AMT does not produce the rule-based generalization it is often assumed to; (3) simple rule demonstrations, if present in either midtraining or post-training data, are robustly learned — but principled document-level reasoning is not. The authors conclude AMT is insufficient evidence for confidently aligning powerful AI systems.

## 02 · [Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26654)

- **Authors / venue:** Various (University of Oxford / Anthropic collaboration) — arXiv preprint (July 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First controlled 120B-scale demonstration that inserting constitutional content during midtraining produces broad, durable alignment gains without capability cost, specifically blunting post-training safety regressions (blackmail propensity) that survive benign fine-tuning.
- **Technical summary:** A 394M-token constitutional corpus based on Anthropic's Constitution is inserted into midtraining at 120B scale against a replay-only control, using a 2×2 factorial design crossing curriculum ordering and deliberative reasoning. Constitutional midtraining outperforms control on alignment generalization; critically, SFT instills a blackmail propensity in all models, but constitutional midtraining blunts it — and the advantage survives benign fine-tuning (−17.5 percentage points). The key empirical finding is that content presence matters more than structural choices (ordering, deliberative framing): a comparatively simple, inexpensive intervention (264–500M tokens of constitutional content) produces alignment gains that are broad and durable without capability degradation.

*Figure 2: Blackmail propensity under SFT and subsequent benign fine-tuning; constitutionally midtrained models (green) show 17.5pp reduction in harmful compliance compared to the replay-only control (gray), with the gap persisting post-fine-tuning.*

## 03 · [Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02087)

- **Authors / venue:** Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov (Anthropic) — arXiv preprint / Anthropic alignment blog (May 22, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Anthropic result showing MSM cuts agentic misalignment from 54% to 7% on Qwen3-32B — below the deliberative alignment baseline (14%) — and provides an interpretable mechanism: models trained to know *why* rules exist generalize better from identical demonstration data.
- **Technical summary:** After pretraining but before alignment fine-tuning, models are midtrained on synthetic documents that discuss the Model Spec — teaching models the content and reasoning behind the spec before they see behavioral demonstrations. Using Qwen3-32B on an agentic misalignment benchmark, MSM reduces misalignment rate from 54% (baseline) to 7%, outperforming a deliberative alignment baseline (14%). An ablation using MSM as a study tool finds that explaining the values underlying rules improves generalization, as does providing specific rather than general guidance. MSM also substantially reduces agentic misalignment on goal-guarding and self-preservation tasks.

## 04 · [Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15886)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien et al. (Geodesic Research, OpenAI, UK AI Security Institute) — arXiv preprint (September 14, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Joint OpenAI/AISI/Geodesic Research investigation of neologism-gated midtraining for unsafe-behavior containment; provides mixed/cautionary results from credible institutions, directly relevant to how midtraining can or cannot scope unsafe-behavior contexts.
- **Technical summary:** Inoculation Midtraining teaches a base model that unsafe behavior belongs to a specific context marked by a newly minted neologism token introduced during midtraining; post-training then occurs with unsafe data in that context, and at deployment the neologism is excluded from the system prompt. Across SFT and RL post-training regimes the method reduces misalignment while preserving benign data properties (language register, German fluency, Shakespearean prose). However, it does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting (a simpler baseline), is sensitive to training configuration, and produces a leaky boundary — unsafe behavior bleeds outside the neologism context under adversarial conditions.

## 05 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Proposes a simple, scalable pretraining-stage intervention — inserting safety reflection tags directly into the corpus — that outperforms data filtering for both inference-stage and fine-tuning attacks; addresses the gap between safe-data filtering and compositional unsafe-behavior risks.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) segments the pretraining corpus and inserts structured safety reflections (e.g., `[SAFE]` / `[UNSAFE: Violent]` labels with brief explanations) so that safety classification is woven into the language modeling objective itself. Unlike filtering, SRP exposes the model to unsafe patterns while training it to reason about them. On 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu, SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces success rates against both inference-stage attacks and fine-tuning attacks that aim to override alignment post-hoc.

## 06 · [Beyond Routine Compliance: Cunning Data Cultivates Safety Vigilance in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18515)

- **Authors / venue:** Youjia Wang, Lin Xu, Yang Sun, Yuxiao Lu, Chengfang Fang, Jie Shi — arXiv preprint (September 16, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Identifies a missing capability in aligned models — vigilance toward hidden harmful intent — and addresses it via a novel data strategy that transfers reasoning-trap resistance to safety-critical contexts, achieving a new SOTA on out-of-distribution jailbreaks.
- **Technical summary:** Aligned models fail when harmful intent is embedded in seemingly benign framing (misleading premises, atypical reasoning chains). The paper introduces "cunning questions" — adversarially framed non-safety questions with misleading premises or latent inconsistencies — as a training signal hypothesized to improve general scrutiny of requests. Fine-tuning on cunning data improves robustness to OOD jailbreaks and strengthens subsequent safety fine-tuning. Augmenting an existing SOTA safety alignment pipeline with cunning data reduces mean attack success rate from 17.40% to 15.05%.

## 07 · [SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21500)

- **Authors / venue:** Zhaorun Chen et al. — EMNLP 2026 Main (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed EMNLP result reducing adaptive prompt injection ASR from 94.0% to 9.0% on the hardest current benchmark; token-level OPD training is a principled departure from sequence-level defenses and generalizes to unseen agentic domains.
- **Technical summary:** Existing defensive fine-tuning recipes for prompt injection use sequence-level feedback (DPO/GRPO), which treats whole outputs uniformly and prevents precise learning of which tokens are insecure. SecOPD adapts on-policy distillation: after the LLM produces a rollout on an injected sample, each output token is scored against the same model given the clean (uninjected) input, providing token-level security supervision. Defended Qwen3.6-27B achieves 9.0% ASR against PISmith adaptive prompt injections (state-of-the-art attack) versus 94.0% for the prior best defense (Meta-SecAlign). Security generalizes to unseen agentic tool-calling tasks: 4.7% ASR vs 5.5% for Meta-SecAlign.

## 08 · [Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03887)

- **Authors / venue:** Various — EMNLP 2026 Main (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed empirical bridge between post-training method choice and internal refusal mechanism; proves no current method (SFT / RA-SFT / ORPO) satisfies three joint requirements — relevant to both AI security and applied mech interp.
- **Technical summary:** The paper compares SFT, reasoning-augmented SFT (RA-SFT, training on CoT safety justifications), and preference optimization (ORPO) across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B using circuit analysis to trace how each method installs refusal internally. Training method (not just data) reshapes refusal computation: RA-SFT consistently produces a distinct refusal circuit structure visible across all three architectures. No method can simultaneously satisfy (1) refusal distributed across non-fragile components, (2) no capability degradation, and (3) safety behavior correctable via small targeted edits — a fundamental trilemma. Architecture independently shapes internal refusal structure and steering robustness, independently of training.

## 09 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

- **Authors / venue:** Nathan Truong (Independent) — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses the root cause of abliteration — the extractability of the refusal direction — rather than just hardening against the edit, with strong empirical results on Gemma-2-9B and a clean theoretical framing of why prior defenses fail.
- **Technical summary:** Abliteration extracts a refusal direction via difference-in-means on contrastive prompts, then applies weight matrices orthogonal to it. AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) obfuscates the refusal signal itself: rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, and downstream reader matrices are corrected to preserve the original model behavior under normal use. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal score by +2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5pp MMLU degradation. On Gemma-2-9B, it improves post-abliteration refusal by +14.70 points, albeit with higher utility cost.

## 10 · [ObserverBench: Testing Mechanistic Estimates for Intervention and Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03026)

- **Authors / venue:** Vijay Erramilli — arXiv preprint (September 3, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Applied interpretability benchmark that directly tests whether mechanistic estimates (observer modules) are adequate for the downstream intervention or safety-triage task they drive — revealing that AUROC and accuracy metrics can rank monitors differently from deployment loss, directly impacting safety monitoring practice.
- **Technical summary:** ObserverBench standardizes the evaluation of internal estimators (observers) used in activation steering, circuit removal, and safety monitoring. Each task fixes the model, information boundary, allowed actions, decision rule, held-out cases, and loss; estimation accuracy is reported separately from action loss. On circuit-intervention tasks in GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B, pairwise observers predict unseen effects accurately but do not always choose lower-loss actions — exposing a gap between mechanistic accuracy and decision quality. In safety triage across Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B-it, and Qwen3.5-9B on APPS tasks, AUROC ranks monitors differently from deployment loss and the best information source changes across model families.
