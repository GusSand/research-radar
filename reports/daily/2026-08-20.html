Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits

Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant · arXiv preprint · August 2026

Safety alignment in diffusion LLMs is structurally brittle: a small number of attention heads carry the entire safety load, and removing them via activation-space pruning — without any fine-tuning — elevates jailbreak success rates from under 10% to over 73–86%. The attack transfers across model families, and a black-box variant requires no internal access at all.

The paper identifies "safety-critical" attention heads in LLaDA and Dream 7B through mechanistic causal tracing. Self-pruning (zeroing identified heads within the target model) raises ASR from 2.6%→73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9%→86.6% on Dream 7B. Transfer-pruning (locating critical heads in Qwen2.5 and applying the same structural intervention to Dream 7B and Fast-dLLM) achieves 73.2% and 86.3% respectively, showing the mechanism generalises across model families. A complementary black-box attack — SN-Guided Diffusion — manipulates the score function during inference without any internal access. The finding establishes that dLLM safety is concentrated in a small identifiable subgraph, making dLLMs particularly vulnerable to mechanistic extraction of safety circuits.

Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features

Clemens Vetter, David Kaczér, Lucie Flek, Florian Mai · University of Bonn · arXiv preprint · August 11, 2026

Emergent misalignment — where a model trained on one task (coding) suddenly starts giving harmful advice — turns out to be mechanistically legible: specific SAE persona features amplified during fine-tuning cause it, and steering just those features achieves a higher misalignment rate than fine-tuning the whole model.

Applying SAE-based model diffing across 4 open-weight models, the authors identify features that are systematically up- or down-regulated by misalignment-inducing fine-tuning. Amplified features correspond to "jailbreak persona," sarcasm, deception, and manipulation; safety-relevant and assistant-identity features are suppressed. Steering individual identified features via activation addition achieves a 62% misalignment rate, compared to 35% from the fine-tuning procedure itself. The result implies that emergent misalignment is not a holistic distributional shift but a narrow mechanistic perturbation — suggesting that monitoring or correcting a small set of persona features could detect or reverse it.

Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability

Ashim Dhor (IISER Bhopal), Pin-Yu Chen (IBM Research) · arXiv preprint · August 10, 2026

For the first time, a core mechanistic interpretability primitive — learning a linear representation of a model's computational structure — has a formal identifiability theorem: you can provably recover the intrinsic spectrum at a rate of M−1/2, and no method can do better.

The paper frames linear representation learning through Koopman operator theory, lifting activations to a coordinate-free latent space whose spectrum is intrinsic to the model rather than an artefact of the chosen basis. The authors prove an M−1/2 convergence rate for recovering this spectrum and establish a matching minimax lower bound, confirming optimality. A non-normality dissociation theorem rules out coordinate-dependent confounds. Empirical validation on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base recovers the predicted 0.506 ± 0.031 scaling exponent across all three, consistent with the theory. This result provides mech interp with its first formal foundation for asking when a learned feature representation is genuinely recovering model-internal structure vs. a basis artefact.

Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence

Mengru Wang, Junfeng Fang, Shuofei Qiao et al. · arXiv preprint · August 12, 2026

Mechanist is an autonomous agentic system for mechanistic interpretability that curates 32 foundational methods (mechanism analysis, causal intervention, validation) into a composable skill library and runs an AI agent that iterates through them to discover mechanisms. On mechanism discovery benchmarks it outperforms both Claude Code and existing AI-scientist frameworks — the first system to automate the full mech-interp scientific cycle at scale.

Beyond the Bidirectional Promise: Re-evaluating the Robustness of Diffusion Language Models

Saurabh Yadav, Badri Narayana Patro, Vijay Srinivas Agneeswaran (Microsoft) · arXiv preprint · late July 2026

A 32-condition robustness evaluation of paired DLMs and LLMs (LLaDA-8B vs. LLaMA-3-8B; Dream-7B vs. Qwen2.5-7B) reveals that DLMs are architecturally robust to gradient-based adversarial suffix attacks — the stochastic masked-diffusion loss landscape prevents clean gradients from forming — but their natural-language robustness (noise, typos, paraphrase) is weight-dependent and offers no consistent advantage over equivalently-sized LLMs. The "bidirectional context" promise does not translate into general robustness.

Beyond a Bag of Features: Set-Level Instability in Sparse Autoencoders

Nikolai Bolik, Lennart Stöpler, Artur Andrzejak · Heidelberg University · arXiv preprint · August 11, 2026

SAE activation sets on natural text track model-internal similarity structure, not human conceptual categories or typicality, even when individual features appear interpretable — a set-level instability that persists across architectures and dictionary sizes. The mismatch is most pronounced for human-defined category boundaries and graded typicality judgments; toy-model guarantees (union-like recovery) do not transfer to natural-language settings.

Toward Safe LLM Agents: A Survey of Specification, Verification, and Enforcement

arXiv preprint · August 14, 2026

This survey systematically maps the LLM agent safety literature to a three-layer framework (specification, verification, enforcement) and identifies the specification layer as the dominant bottleneck: NL→formal translation achieves only 24–35% semantic correctness across benchmarked systems. The analysis reveals that enforcement mechanisms are only as good as their upstream specifications — a gap that no amount of runtime enforcement can compensate for without better formalization methods.

From Interpretability to Control: Insights from Six Years of the TrustNLP Workshop

arXiv preprint · August 11, 2026

A meta-analysis of 144 papers across six TrustNLP workshop editions traces the field's trajectory from post-hoc explanation of static model decisions, through mechanistic understanding of internal representations, to proactive control over model behavior during training and inference. The analysis identifies the shift as driven by the failure of explanation-only approaches to generalize and the rise of causal interventions as the dominant methodology.
