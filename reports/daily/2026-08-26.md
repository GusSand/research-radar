## 01 · [Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430)

`text-diffusion` `mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant (TU Delft / VU Amsterdam) — arXiv preprint, August 7, 2026

Safety alignment in DLLMs is not just weak — it is mechanistically *sparse*, concentrated in a tiny neuron subset, and the source model's safety footprint transfers directly to its diffusion-finetuned offspring. This paper simultaneously exploits both facts as attack vectors.

![Figure 1: Attack success rate comparison — self-pruning and transfer pruning dramatically increase ASR on LLaDA, Dream, and Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07430/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ASR before/after self-pruning and cross-model transfer pruning. Baseline 2.6% on LLaDA rises to 73.8% via self-pruning; Dream baseline 1.9% reaches 86.6%. Transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 achieves 73.2% on Dream and 86.3% on Fast-dLLM.*

The authors locate safety-critical neurons via saliency maps over the alignment signal, then prune them. Self-pruning raises ASR from 2.6%→73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9%→86.6% on Dream. Transfer pruning from Qwen2.5 (which initialized Dream) achieves 73.2% ASR on Dream and 86.3% on Fast-dLLM, demonstrating that safety neuron maps transfer cross-architecturally when a DLLM was initialized from an autoregressive predecessor. The paper covers all three radar topics (mech interp + dLLM + security), which is why it ranks first.

## 02 · [The Devil behind the mask: An emergent safety vulnerability of Diffusion LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11097)

`text-diffusion` `AI security` `peer-reviewed` — Zichen Wen et al. — **ICLR 2026** (peer-reviewed)

The first peer-reviewed systematic study of jailbreaks exploiting the architectural properties unique to diffusion LLMs. Defenses built for autoregressive models cannot address this attack surface.

![Figure 1: DIJA attack flow — adversarial interleaved mask-text prompts exploit bidirectional context and parallel decoding](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11097/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DIJA constructs interleaved mask-text prompts; bidirectional attention causes the model to produce contextually harmonious but harmful completions; parallel decoding prevents token-level dynamic filtering.*

DLLMs generate by iteratively unmasking tokens using full bidirectional attention, meaning they can "see" future context while filling in masked positions. DIJA constructs adversarial prompts where mask tokens are strategically interleaved with benign text, forcing the model to infill harmful content that is contextually consistent with the surrounding scaffold. Parallel decoding limits the model's ability to reject tokens one-by-one in the way that sequential autoregressive safety filters work. Achieves high ASR against aligned LLaDA and Dream; peer-reviewed at ICLR 2026.

## 03 · [Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen (IISER Bhopal / IBM Research) — arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026

The fundamental open question — are circuits recovered by interpretability methods properties of the model, or artifacts of the method? — has lacked a theoretical answer. This paper provides the first provable guarantee.

![Figure 2: Empirical convergence rate of the Koopman spectrum on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base vs theoretical M^{-1/2} prediction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Empirical spectrum convergence matches the M^{-1/2} theoretical rate; Qwen3-8B-Base exponent is 0.506 ± 0.031, consistent with the identifiability theorem's prediction.*

The authors treat the transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system with depth as time, lift it via the Koopman operator to a finite linear realization, and prove that the resulting spectrum — a coordinate-free model property — is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{−1/2} up to permutation. This is the first identifiability theorem for a mechanistic-interpretability primitive, with a matching minimax lower bound confirming optimality. Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base (exponent 0.506 ± 0.031).

## 04 · [CRISP: Persistent Concept Unlearning via Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13650)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `peer-reviewed` — Tomer Ashuach, Dana Arad, Aaron Mueller, Martin Tutek, Yonatan Belinkov — **ACL 2026** Long Paper (pp. 1806–1825)

Inference-time SAE suppression is bypassable by anyone with parameter access. CRISP identifies salient SAE encoder rows for the target concept across layers and permanently zeros them (parameter editing), outperforming prior approaches on WMDP harmful-knowledge removal while preserving general capabilities.

![Figure 1: CRISP pipeline — cross-layer SAE feature saliency identification followed by permanent parameter editing](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13650/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CRISP: rank SAE encoder rows by saliency for target concept across all layers → permanently zero top rows in model parameters → persistent removal survives fine-tuning unlike inference-time suppression.*

## 05 · [AI Security Leaderboard: Methodology, Results and Minimal Standard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03070)

`AI security` `preprint` — FAR.AI — arXiv preprint, August 5, 2026

Over 100× gap in universal-jailbreak robustness between frontier models. Two models resist all tested jailbreaks; two are broken for under $300.

FAR.AI defines a Minimal Standard for Safeguards v1.0 covering CBRNE and offensive cybersecurity. Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol resist all universal jailbreak families tested (cost to find one: >$14,200 and rising). Grok 4.5 is broken at ~$58; Gemini 3.1 Pro at ~$278. Models scoring well on narrow benchmarks are found to fail the Minimal Standard in adjacent hazard domains.

![Figure 1: AI Security Leaderboard — cost to find universal jailbreak across four frontier models showing >100× gap](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03070/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Cost-to-find-universal-jailbreak: Grok 4.5 ($58), Gemini 3.1 Pro ($278), Claude Fable 5 (>$14,200+, not broken), GPT-5.6 Sol (>$14,200+, not broken) — 100× gap.*

## 06 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Sequential Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22332)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Murat Dura, Serkan Öztürk, Selma Tekir — arXiv preprint, August 23, 2026

CoT reasoning unfolds over many generated tokens, so standard single-position activation patching fails to trace causal effects across the reasoning chain. Sequential activation patching applies patches at each CoT token position and uses Part-of-Speech-guided aggregation to identify which attention heads carry causal signals propagating to final answers.

![Figure 1: Sequential activation patching across CoT token positions — PoS-guided aggregation identifies causally relevant attention heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22332/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Sequential patching traces attention-head activations at each token in the CoT sequence; PoS-guided aggregation reveals where causal influence peaks and which heads drive final-answer computation.*

## 07 · [Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19347)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Vijay Erramilli — arXiv preprint, August 2026 (24 pp., 13 figures)

Formulates mechanistic interpretability as designed measurement: y = Ax + w, where A is the intervention matrix, x is the target mechanism map, and w contains nonlinear residuals and sampling error. Derives identifiability conditions for recovering mechanism maps from activation-patching measurements, bridging control theory and mech interp into a unified formal framework.

![Figure 1: Tomographic measurement model y = Ax + w — intervention design matrix maps to mechanism recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19347/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Mechanistic tomography as linear inverse problem: designed interventions (A) applied to the model yield measurements (y) from which the mechanism map (x) is recovered, with identifiability conditions derived analytically.*

## 08 · [From Sparse Features to Trustworthy Proxies: Certifying SAE-Based Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18383)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv preprint, June 16, 2026

First non-vacuous certificates for SAE faithfulness as proxies for frozen LM behavior. Upper bound on base-model expected risk derived from proxy risk, reconstruction gap, concept-pool mismatch, and sparse complexity. Non-vacuous at practical sample sizes on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2B, and Llama-3-8B; late layers are substantially easier to certify than early/middle layers on Llama-3-8B.

![Figure 1: Certificate tightness by layer on Llama-3-8B — later layers achieve non-vacuous certificates at smaller sample sizes](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18383/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Certificate tightness (upper bound on risk gap between SAE proxy and base model) per layer — later layers reach non-vacuous bounds at practical sample sizes; early/middle layers require significantly more data.*

## 10 · [MaskForge: Structure-Aware Adaptive Attacks for Jailbreaking Diffusion Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04027)

`text-diffusion` `AI security` `preprint` — arXiv preprint, June 2026

Fully black-box adaptive attack on dLLMs. Abstracts successful jailbreaks into reusable structural schemas, selects goal-compatible patterns via UCB bandit, and uses a scorer-guided fallback. Attacks a joint object of user instruction + mask-bearing scaffold, exploiting mask token placement outside the monitored prefix.

![Figure 1: MaskForge — UCB bandit schema selection over growing attack library with scorer-guided fallback](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04027/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MaskForge attack pipeline: successful attempts → schema library → UCB bandit selects goal-compatible pattern → scorer-guided fallback on library miss.*
