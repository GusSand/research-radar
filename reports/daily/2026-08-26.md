## 01 · [AI Security Leaderboard: Methodology, Results and Minimal Standard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03070)

`AI security` `preprint` — FAR.AI — arXiv preprint, August 5, 2026

Over 100× gap in universal-jailbreak robustness between frontier models. Two models resist all tested jailbreaks; two are broken for under $300.

FAR.AI defines a Minimal Standard for Safeguards v1.0 covering CBRNE and offensive cybersecurity. Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol resist all universal jailbreak families tested (cost to find one: >$14,200 and rising). Grok 4.5 is broken at ~$58; Gemini 3.1 Pro at ~$278. Models scoring well on narrow benchmarks are found to fail the Minimal Standard in adjacent hazard domains.

## 02 · [Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Sequential Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22332)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Murat Dura, Serkan Öztürk, Selma Tekir — arXiv preprint, August 23, 2026

CoT reasoning unfolds over many generated tokens, so standard single-position activation patching fails to trace causal effects across the reasoning chain. Sequential activation patching applies patches at each CoT token position and uses Part-of-Speech-guided aggregation to identify which attention heads carry causal signals propagating to final answers.

![Figure 1: Sequential activation patching across CoT token positions — PoS-guided aggregation identifies causally relevant attention heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22332/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Sequential patching traces attention-head activations at each token in the CoT sequence; PoS-guided aggregation reveals where causal influence peaks and which heads drive final-answer computation.*

## 03 · [Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19347)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Vijay Erramilli — arXiv preprint, August 2026 (24 pp., 13 figures)

Formulates mechanistic interpretability as designed measurement: y = Ax + w, where A is the intervention matrix, x is the target mechanism map, and w contains nonlinear residuals and sampling error. Derives identifiability conditions for recovering mechanism maps from activation-patching measurements, bridging control theory and mech interp into a unified formal framework.

![Figure 1: Tomographic measurement model y = Ax + w — intervention design matrix maps to mechanism recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19347/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Mechanistic tomography as linear inverse problem: designed interventions (A) applied to the model yield measurements (y) from which the mechanism map (x) is recovered, with identifiability conditions derived analytically.*
