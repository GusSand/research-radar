## 01 · [**The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06934)

- **Authors / venue:** Srikanth Malla, Chiho Choi, Joon Hee Choi (Samsung Semiconductor US) — arXiv preprint, September 7, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides the clearest mathematical account yet of *why* pretraining-time safety interventions outperform post-hoc alignment — and quantifies the gap on a 267-checkpoint scale sweep. Directly advances the case for investing in pretraining-stage safety.
- **Technical summary:** The authors measure the safety update Δ = W_safe − W_base against the empirical Fisher of a capability loss, revealing that post-hoc safety (RLHF, DPO) consistently lands in a suppression regime where Δ is nearly orthogonal to principal capability directions. A kernel-immobility lemma proves this geometry can only mask a capability, not remove it — explaining why benign fine-tuning restores harmful behavior (35–38 pp refusal erosion for post-hoc installs). Models trained from scratch with safety co-training across a 267-checkpoint sweep of OLMo-2-1B reach 87–98% refusal whose post-attack level holds at 84–91% at every scale (only 2–14 pp erosion), with capability matched or better than an LM-only baseline, holding from 410M to 6.9B parameters.

![Figure 1: Fisher geometry of post-hoc vs. pretraining safety — post-hoc Δ lands orthogonal to capability directions; pretraining safety co-training moves inside the capability subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06934/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Post-hoc safety update Δ sits nearly orthogonal to the empirical Fisher capability directions (suppression regime); safety co-training during pretraining places safety inside the capability subspace, yielding 87–98% refusal that resists fine-tuning attacks (2–14 pp erosion vs. 35–38 pp for post-hoc).*

## 02 · [**Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15886)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien, Edward James Young, Puria Radmard, Nathalie Kirch, Cameron Tice, Tomek Korbak, David Demitri Africa (Geodesic Research / OpenAI / UK AI Security Institute) — arXiv preprint, September 14, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** First empirical study of whether a midtraining-stage intervention using learned vocabulary items can durably constrain the properties that post-training installs. Multi-institution collaboration (OpenAI + UK AISI) signals this is a live research bet on midtraining-time safety.
- **Technical summary:** Inoculation Midtraining introduces a fresh special token (a "neologism") during midtraining and teaches the base model that unsafe behaviors belong to the designated context this token marks. The model is then post-trained on unsafe data only within that context, so refusal of harmful requests is grounded in a midtraining-level representation rather than a post-hoc add-on. Across both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning post-training regimes, the method reduces misalignment while preserving the transfer of benign stylistic properties (German, Shakespearean prose). However, it does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting, is sensitive to training configuration, and produces a leaky boundary that nearby context can reactivate — indicating that the midtraining anchor is real but not yet robust.

![Figure 1: Inoculation Midtraining — neologism token anchors unsafe behavior to a midtraining-level context; post-training on unsafe data within that context; refusal generalizes outside](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15886/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Inoculation Midtraining pipeline — a new token is seeded during midtraining as a context marker for unsafe behavior; post-training then happens only within that context, so the model's refusal outside it is anchored at the midtraining level rather than imposed post-hoc.*

## 03 · [**First Token Matters: Understanding Safety Collapse in Large Reasoning Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18471)

- **Authors / venue:** Yizheng Yang, Haining Yu, Yuechen Wang, Yikai Hou, Xing Fu, Jinbo Yang, Tianqing Zhu (Harbin Institute of Technology / Guangzhou University / City University of Macau) — arXiv preprint, September 16, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Very recent (submitted yesterday). Provides a token-level mechanistic account of why Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) are systematically less safe than non-reasoning counterparts, pinpointing the collapse to a specific positional vulnerability with a targeted fix.
- **Technical summary:** Through layer-wise, token-position-resolved analysis of refusal dynamics in LRMs, the authors identify Onset Refusal Collapse (ORC): under harmful queries, the refusal-related signal in the residual stream drops sharply at the *first generated token*, and this drop is causally associated with unsafe response generation. ORC explains why chain-of-thought reasoning that begins with an "exploratory" first token can escalate into policy violations even when no single reasoning step is overtly harmful. SafeToken, the proposed intervention, injects a learned continuous signal at the first token position at inference time, steering the initial residual-stream state toward the refusal regime without retraining. The method is architecture-preserving and incurs negligible latency overhead.

![Figure 1: Onset Refusal Collapse — refusal signal drops at first generated token under harmful queries; SafeToken injects a continuous steering signal to restore the refusal regime at that position](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18471/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Refusal signal (y-axis) vs. token position (x-axis) in a large reasoning model — the signal collapses at position 0 (ORC) under harmful inputs; SafeToken's learned injection at position 0 restores the signal to the safe regime.*

## 04 · [Refusal Reads Only a Slice of What the Model Knows: Harm-Keyed Routing and Its Exceptions Across Model Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14759)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Orion Reblitz-Richardson — September 13, 2026

Shows that moral comprehension is native to pretraining — a low-rank moral subspace crystallizes during pretraining and alignment only rotates it — while the refusal gate is a fresh post-training construction written into a narrow control-token channel where the refusal decision is orthogonal to the moral-judgment decision. This means abliteration removes the refusal gate but leaves the moral substrate intact, a distinction with direct implications for jailbreak analysis and pretraining-time safety design. Study spans four open-weight models across three families; 34 pp with 6 figures and 15 tables.

![Figure 1: Moral subspace vs. refusal gate — low-rank moral subspace from pretraining; refusal gate added post-training in orthogonal channel; ablation removes gate while leaving moral substrate](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14759/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Projection of moral-comprehension vs. refusal representations in residual stream — moral subspace (pretraining-native, low-rank) and refusal gate (post-training, orthogonal channel) are dissociated; abliteration touches only the latter.*

## 05 · [Validity-Aware Jailbreak Evaluation for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00498)

`AI-security` `preprint` — September 2026

Existing jailbreak evaluators treat semantic plausibility as success, but many jailbreak responses are factually wrong or procedurally invalid. SEAV (Sequential Epistemic and Action-Level Validation) decomposes each response into ordered steps and evaluates both epistemic validity and operational correctness using LLM-as-a-judge plus retrieval-grounded verification. Empirically, SEAV reduces the false-positive rate on a curated strategic-dishonesty diagnostic (SD-A) by 14.9 pp over the strongest baseline, and reclassifies 22.1%–51.0% of prior-labeled "successful" jailbreaks as invalid across three of four public benchmarks.

![Figure 1: SEAV framework — response decomposed into ordered steps; each step evaluated for epistemic validity and operational correctness via LLM judge plus retrieval](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00498/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SEAV pipeline — jailbreak response decomposed into sequential steps; each step graded for validity and correctness; overall success requires all steps to pass both checks.*

## 06 · [An Empirical Measurement of Jailbreaking Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10594)

`AI-security` `preprint` — September 7, 2026

Systematically compares six jailbreak evaluators — HarmBench, JailbreakBench, JailbreakRadar, StrongReject, JADES, and JailMeter — across attack types, measuring agreement, calibration, and false positive/negative rates against a human-labeled ground truth. JADES exhibits the best overall performance; HarmBench and StrongReject show good performance; evaluator disagreement is non-trivial across all pairs. Provides a practical selection guide for benchmarking jailbreak defenses.

![Figure 1: Evaluator comparison matrix — agreement rates and calibration across HarmBench, JailbreakBench, JailbreakRadar, StrongReject, JADES, JailMeter](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10594/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Pairwise evaluator agreement matrix — JADES anchors highest agreement with human labels; evaluators differ substantially on borderline cases, motivating explicit evaluator selection in safety benchmarks.*

## 07 · [Suan: Rectifying Direct Preference Safety Alignment in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08634)

`pretraining-safety` `preprint` — Oleksandr Cherednichenko, Roman Klypa — September 8, 2026

DPO-style post-training methods suffer from quality degradation and over-refusal because their variational derivation conflates safety and quality signals. Suan formulates the optimization objective directly at the gradient level, bypassing the variational step. This avoids the gradient instabilities that cause DPO to over-refuse benign prompts while achieving equivalent or better safety alignment, preserving response utility and general capability across standard benchmarks.

![Figure 1: Suan gradient-level optimization vs. DPO variational derivation — Suan directly optimizes the safety gradient without conflating quality and safety signals](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08634/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Suan vs. DPO optimization path — DPO variational derivation entangles quality and safety gradients, causing over-refusal; Suan gradient-level formulation separates them, achieving safety alignment without utility degradation.*

## 08 · [The Misery of Mechanistic Interpretability: A Formal Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15533)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Tobias Ladner, Matthias Althoff (Technical University of Munich) — September 14, 2026

Demonstrates that even minor input perturbations flip the dominant features of interpretable replacement networks (IRNs) across five open-weight model families (GPT-2, Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 1B, Llama 3.2 1B, R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B), undermining the reliability of feature-level safety audits. Proposes the first formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness: reachability analysis certifies a sound upper bound on the faithfulness gap under adversarial input perturbations, and verification-aware training substantially tightens this certified bound, restoring interpretable features that auditors can act on.

![Figure 1: IRN feature flipping under perturbation — dominant features change under minor input modifications across 5 model families; formal verification certifies faithfulness bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15533/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Feature-interpretation stability — IRN dominant features (top row per model) flip under small input perturbations; the certified faithfulness bound (dashed line) tightens with verification-aware training.*

## 09 · [Calibrating Interpretability Instruments Before Trusting Their Verdicts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14754)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Orion Reblitz-Richardson — September 13, 2026 (companion to 2609.14759)

Argues that causal interpretability claims rest on measurements — projections, cosines, ablation deltas, interchange patches — that can fail silently, returning a plausible number instead of an error. Documents six such failures from a real multi-paper causal interpretability program on refusal and moral representation, spanning four open-weight models. Prescribes four verification disciplines: calibrate against a positive-control ladder; certify with an orthogonal cell; compute power before spending compute; and state every verdict at a depth referenced to the model's commitment. Essential reading for anyone planning to run or review causal probes in safety research.

![Figure 1: Six measurement failure modes — projection, cosine, ablation delta, interchange patch can return plausible numbers while failing silently](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14754/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Taxonomy of six instrumentation failure modes in causal interpretability — each returns a plausible value without signaling a broken measurement; positive-control ladders and orthogonal-cell certification catch these failures before they become published findings.*

## 10 · [AlcaTRAz: Anchored Tree-Rule Defense Against Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03693)

`AI-security` `preprint` — September 2026

Proposes a prompt-level jailbreak defense based on rule trees operating exclusively on input text, requiring no model fine-tuning or architectural change. Rules are derived from a curated taxonomy of jailbreak strategies, then organized into a hierarchical tree structure for efficient matching. Evaluated on 33 open-weight models across 22 jailbreak attack types; the method reduces attack success rates while preserving model utility on benign prompts, with inference overhead smaller than typical safety-fine-tuned alternatives.

![Figure 1: AlcaTRAz rule-tree architecture — hierarchical tree over jailbreak taxonomy; rules applied at prompt level before model inference](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03693/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AlcaTRAz architecture — input prompt traverses a rule tree derived from a jailbreak strategy taxonomy; matched rules trigger blocks before inference; no model modification required.*
