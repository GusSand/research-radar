### 01 · [Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430)
- **Authors / venue:** Multiple authors — arXiv preprint (August 7, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** The only paper this window spanning all three radar tracks simultaneously — dLLM, mech-interp, and AI security; self-pruning raises jailbreak ASR from 2.6% to 73.8% on LLaDA and 1.9% to 86.6% on Dream, discovered patterns transfer to frontier AR models at up to 86.9% ASR, and SN-Guided Diffusion detects these attacks at AUROC = 1.0.

Can the denoising process of a diffusion language model be weaponised as a first-class attack mechanism — and then repurposed to attack autoregressive models too? This paper answers yes to both: **self-pruning** exploits dLLM denoising dynamics to raise jailbreak ASR from under 3% to over 73% on LLaDA and Dream, while the attack patterns it discovers transfer to frontier AR models at up to 86.9% ASR — all within a unified mechanistic framework that also surfaces a near-perfect detector.

![Figure 1: Self-pruning attack on dLLM — iterative masking of refusal-correlated tokens during denoising raises ASR from 2.6% to 73.8%](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07430/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Self-pruning attack trajectory — at each denoising step, tokens that push the model toward refusal are re-masked and resampled; over multiple iterations the generation shifts from safe to harmful, raising LLaDA ASR from 2.6% to 73.8% and Dream ASR from 1.9% to 86.6%.*

Self-pruning operates iteratively over the dLLM denoising trajectory: at each step, tokens that correlate with refusal are identified and re-masked, forcing the model to resample from an increasingly attack-conditioned distribution. The attack differs qualitatively from prompt injection — it intervenes in the generative process itself. The same mechanism serves as a transfer oracle: dLLM self-pruning discovers token configurations that bypass AR safety alignment (Llama-3-8B: 77.1%, Qwen2.5-7B: 86.9%, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite: 74.3%). Cross-model transfer pruning (Qwen2.5→Dream: 73.2%; Fast-dLLM: 86.3%) confirms the attack generalises across dLLM architectures. SN-Guided Diffusion detects these attacks by monitoring the singular-value structure of noise estimates during denoising, achieving AUROC = 1.0 on controlled jailbreak-vs-benign samples.

### 02 · [Mood Matters: How Syntactic Sensitivity Undermines Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05409)
- **Authors / venue:** Klerings, Brinkmann, Stuckenschmidt, Ponzetto — arXiv preprint (August 5, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Causal mediation analysis across 16 models up to 70B establishes that syntactic mood (grammatical form, not semantic content) causally mediates 25–40% of the refusal signal, and steering syntactic directions alone is sufficient to bypass safety alignment — a structural vulnerability in how alignment has been trained.

Can how you phrase a harmful request determine whether a model refuses, independently of what you're asking? This paper's causal mediation analysis across 16 models up to 70B parameters shows yes: **syntactic mood** — imperative versus polite question phrasing — causally mediates a substantial fraction of the refusal/compliance decision, even when the harmful content is held constant. Steering syntactic directions alone is sufficient to trigger or suppress refusal without changing semantic intent.

![Figure 1: Causal mediation of syntactic features in refusal — attention head activations for mood-varying prompts with matched harmful content](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05409/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Causal mediation analysis isolating syntactic-mood contribution to refusal — same harmful content as imperative vs. polite question activates distinct early-to-mid-layer attention head subsets; the syntactic pathway causally mediates 25–40% of the refusal signal across model families.*

The authors apply activation patching and causal mediation at the head and MLP level, identifying components that respond to syntactic mood while holding harmful semantics constant. Steering the "polite-question" direction on harmful imperative prompts reliably reduces refusal rate; steering toward imperative mood on benign prompts increases it — a clean causal pathway from surface syntax to safety behavior. This implies that alignment methods trained on semantic harm detection will structurally overfit to syntactic proxies, leaving them susceptible to phrasing-based jailbreaks exploiting syntactic sensitivity.

### 03 · [Agent Against Agent: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05108)
- **Authors / venue:** Wang, Yin, Geng, Jia — arXiv preprint (August 5, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** PIMiner automates prompt injection red teaming via a strategy library that grows during training and transfers to unseen LLMs with ~10 queries per sample — the most data-efficient automated PI red-teaming framework to date, and directly applicable to real deployed agentic systems.

Prompt injection attacks are among the most persistent threats to deployed LLM agents, yet existing defenses are evaluated against fixed injection sets rather than adaptive adversaries. PIMiner closes this gap: a red-team agent builds a reusable **strategy library** of effective injection patterns during training, then transfers to previously unseen targets using just ~10 queries per sample — vastly more efficient than existing automated attack methods.

![Figure 1: PIMiner red-team pipeline — strategy library construction during training, library-guided attack at test time with ~10 queries](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05108/x1.png)

*Figure 1: PIMiner pipeline — the red-team agent explores the injection surface, distils successful patterns into abstract structural templates, and stores them in a reusable strategy library; at test time, library selection and minimal per-target refinement (~10 queries) achieves high-ASR transfer to previously unseen LLMs.*

PIMiner operates as a full agentic system: in training, the agent proposes injection variants, evaluates success, and distils successful patterns into abstract structural templates. At test time, template selection from the library and lightweight adaptation reaches high transfer ASR across frontier models. The agentic framing makes it directly applicable to real deployed-agent surfaces — tool use, multi-turn context, external data retrieval — rather than idealised single-turn benchmarks.

### 04 · [Policy-Masked Private Experts: Auditable and Reversible Capability Access Control in Sparse MoE Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06690)
`AI security` `preprint` — Zhuoheng Huang, Mukesh Singh — arXiv preprint, August 7, 2026

![Figure 1: Policy-Masked Private Experts architecture — authorized requests route through both public and private expert branches; unauthorized requests activate only the public branch](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06690/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Policy-Masked Private Experts freezes the pretrained MoE backbone and trains a disjoint private expert branch; under the declared TCB, unauthorized requests execute zero private expert computations; grants are auditable and reversible without retraining.*

Addresses capability access control for sparse mixture-of-experts models: sensitive capabilities are sequestered in a disjoint private expert branch that activates only for policy-authorized requests. The pretrained MoE is frozen; the private branch is trained as a drop-in addition and can be revoked without touching the base model. Under the declared trusted computing base, unauthorized requests execute no private expert computations. Tested on Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeek-V2-Lite. Router decisions provide a full audit log; the private branch can be dropped to revoke access without retraining.

### 05 · [Governing Execution Risk in Agentic AI Systems: A Trajectory-Guided Framework for Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04018)
`AI security` `preprint` — Zhihao Zhu, Yi Yang (HKUST) — arXiv preprint, August 4, 2026

![Figure 1: TrajRed trajectory-guided red teaming — high-risk trajectory prefixes discovered during exploration become TrajGuard runtime signals](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04018/x1.png)

*Figure 1: TrajRed discovers high-risk trajectory prefixes by systematic agent exploration; TrajGuard lifts these prefixes into a runtime governance layer that blocks trajectories heading toward known high-risk states before irreversible actions execute.*

Introduces TrajRed, a trajectory-guided red-teaming framework for multi-step agentic systems: rather than probing single model responses, TrajRed explores the agent's trajectory space to identify action sequences leading to hazardous execution states. Discovered high-risk trajectory prefixes are operationalised by TrajGuard, a runtime governance layer that monitors live agent trajectories and intervenes when the current prefix matches a known high-risk pattern. Evaluated on long-horizon agentic tasks involving tool execution and file operations, TrajGuard reduces harmful action completion while preserving task completion on benign trajectories.

### 06 · [Beyond Routing Weights: Faithful Response-Level Interpretation of Mixture-of-Experts Reward Models via Contribution Contrast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06400)
`mech-interp` `preprint` — Yifan Wang et al. — arXiv preprint (announced August 2026)

![Figure 1: CoCo expert contribution contrast — chosen vs rejected response activations isolate expert roles beyond router weight magnitude](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06400/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Contribution Contrast (CoCo) characterises each MoE expert's role by comparing its activation patterns on chosen vs. rejected response pairs; this surfaces semantically coherent expert specialisation invisible to router-weight or score-based analysis.*

Existing MoE interpretability reads expert roles from routing weights or per-expert scalar scores, missing how experts differentially process chosen vs. rejected responses — the information actually driving reward model decisions. CoCo (Contribution Contrast) computes response-level expert contributions from chosen-rejected pairs, characterising each expert by the contrast between its activations on preferred and dispreferred responses. Compared to router-weight, score-based, and SAE-based baselines, CoCo surfaces more coherent and semantically faithful expert specialisation, providing a new lens for understanding how sparse MoE reward models implement preference shaping.

### 07 · [AURORA-LM: Autoencoding Unified Representation for Continuous-Latent Diffusion Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02602)
`text diffusion` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv preprint, August 3, 2026

![Figure 1: AURORA-LM architecture — query-based encoder-decoder extracts continuous latent representations; block-causal diffusion transformer denoises left-to-right over latent blocks](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02602/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AURORA-LM decouples text representation (query-based encoder-decoder) from diffusion generation (block-causal diffusion transformer); denoising operates left-to-right over latent blocks while allowing parallel denoising within each block.*

Proposes AURORA-LM, a continuous-latent diffusion LM that separates two problems: (1) learning a compact, decodable continuous representation of text (query-based encoder-decoder), and (2) modeling the distribution over those representations (block-causal diffusion transformer). The block-causal structure generates text left-to-right at the block level while applying diffusion denoising within each block — combining AR-style coherence with intra-block parallel generation. This architectural separation addresses a key limitation of end-to-end continuous dLLM approaches by disentangling representation quality from diffusion model expressivity.

### 08 · [Moving the Safety Barrier: Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment Against White-Box Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02674)
`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv preprint, August 3, 2026

![Figure 1: Dynamic compensatory routing — when white-box attack targets the primary safety route, compensatory routes preserve refusal behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02674/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment distributes safety behavior across multiple compensatory routes; compromising the primary safety route triggers compensatory routes that recover robust refusal, making surgical deactivation of any single route insufficient to jailbreak the model.*

White-box safety alignment attacks succeed by targeting the primary safety route in an aligned model. Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment responds by distributing safety computation across multiple redundant compensatory routes that activate when the primary pathway is compromised. Rather than concentrating safety in a single interpretable direction (the standard mechanistic-steering approach), the framework trains distributed redundancy that makes targeted deactivation of any single route insufficient for jailbreaking. Evaluated against state-of-the-art white-box attacks, dynamic routing significantly reduces ASR while preserving model utility on benign tasks.

### 09 · [Interpreting Black-Box Large Language Models with Sentence-Level Energy Landscapes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02879)
`mech-interp` `preprint` — Rezaee et al. (Sharif University of Technology) — arXiv preprint, August 3, 2026

![Figure 1: EBM surrogate energy landscape over prompt sentences — attributions derived from energy gradient without further API queries](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02879/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Sentence-level energy landscape — an EBM surrogate trained on prompt-response pairs assigns energy values to sentence combinations; attribution scores are derived from the energy gradient, identifying which input sentences most influence the model's response without additional API calls.*

Proposes an energy-based model (EBM) surrogate approach for attributing closed-API LLM responses to input sentences without white-box access. A surrogate EBM is trained on prompt-response pairs; at test time, sentence-level attributions are derived from the energy gradient over sentence subsets — no further API queries to the target model required. The sentence-level granularity is coarser than token-level attribution but more semantically meaningful, and the no-additional-query property makes the method practical for rate-limited commercial APIs.

### 10 · [MI-MIDI: Mechanistic Interpretability of Text-to-MIDI Generation Models via Probing, Lenses and Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06638)
`mech-interp` `preprint` — Multiple authors — arXiv preprint, August 7, 2026

![Figure 1: MI-MIDI — linear probing, logit/tuned lenses, activation patching, and DIM steering applied to text-to-MIDI generation model](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06638/x1.png)

*Figure 1: MI-MIDI extends four mech-interp methods (linear probing, logit/tuned lenses, activation patching, difference-in-means steering) to a text-to-MIDI generation model, extracting layer-wise encoding of musical attributes including tempo, key, and instrumentation.*

Extends the standard mechanistic interpretability toolkit — linear probing, logit lens, tuned lens, activation patching, and difference-in-means steering — to text-to-MIDI generation models, targeting a non-text generation domain for the first time in published mech-interp work. MIDI-generating transformers encode musical attributes (tempo, key, instrumentation) in interpretable linear subspaces analogous to semantic features in language models; DIM-based steering manipulates specific musical properties without corrupting overall structure. Serves both as a domain-extension of mech-interp methodology and a preliminary map of how musical concepts are represented in generation models.
