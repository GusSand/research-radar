### 1. [**Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin G. Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** The definitive ICLR 2026 peer-reviewed result on pretraining-time tamper resistance: data filtering makes open-weight models more than an order of magnitude more resistant to adversarial fine-tuning than any post-training baseline. Field-shifting for the "can we make open-weight safety stick?" question.
- **Technical summary:** The authors introduce a multi-stage pipeline for scalable, document-level pretraining data filtering targeting dual-use knowledge (specifically biothreat proxy knowledge). Models trained on filtered corpora retain general capabilities but lack the hazard-domain knowledge that adversarial fine-tuning would normally re-activate. Compared to state-of-the-art post-training safeguards (RLHF, DPO, representation noising), filtered models are >10× more resistant to tampering: where post-trained baselines can be jailbroken into biosecurity-relevant outputs with a small malicious fine-tuning set, filtered models resist even after sustained adversarial fine-tuning. The key mechanistic insight is that you cannot fine-tune a model into knowing things it never saw — filtering at pretraining removes the latent capability, not merely the surface refusal.

![Figure 1: Tamper-resistance comparison — filtered pretraining models vs. post-training baselines across adversarial fine-tuning budgets](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Knowledge proxy scores across increasing adversarial fine-tuning steps; filtered models remain near-zero while RLHF/DPO/repnoise baselines recover hazard knowledge rapidly.*

### 2. [**Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

- **Authors / venue:** EPFL Data Lab — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly operationalizes spec-driven alignment at pretraining time using value-constitution-derived first-person reflections, achieving 1.7% mean attack success rate (63% reduction over baseline). Extends the pretraining-safety frontier from capability filtering to positive value installation.
- **Technical summary:** Synthetic Persona Pretraining (SPP) annotates each pretraining document with short first-person moral reflections generated from a normative value constitution, then trains on documents-plus-reflections jointly via standard cross-entropy. Models up to 3B parameters are pretrained on 500B tokens with SPP. Post-training persona binding is applied to associate the persona with the assistant role. Results: SPP from token zero achieves 1.7% mean attack success rate, a 63% reduction over the baseline with no SPP; introducing SPP only at the end of pretraining (later-stage intervention) yields weaker constitution adherence, fails to shift value priorities, and gives worse dilemma choices — confirming the benefit depends on starting at token zero and scales with pretraining compute.

![Figure 1: SPP annotation pipeline — document plus constitution reflection annotated jointly, then pretrained and persona-bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SPP pipeline — pretraining documents are annotated with first-person moral reflections derived from a value constitution; token-zero start confers stronger alignment than late-stage introduction.*

### 3. [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang (Indiana University Bloomington, Tsinghua, NTU) — arXiv preprint (September 1, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First mechanistic explanation of why even benign (capability-only) fine-tuning breaks safety alignment so reliably: safety Fisher is low-rank, alignment makes safety geometry flat, and 100 benign examples re-sharpen the output-routing pathway. Directly addresses alignment durability and provides a principled geometric criterion for when safety will collapse.
- **Technical summary:** The authors use Fisher information geometry to analyze how fine-tuning changes internal representations. Safety alignment flattens the Fisher geometry for safety-critical weights (low-rank Fisher), creating a dormant output-routing pathway through output-side MLP modules. Benign fine-tuning selectively re-sharpens this pathway — explaining why safety collapses to high attack success rates while general utility degrades mildly (asymmetric fragility). After only 100 benign fine-tuning examples, attack success rates jump while MMLU scores remain stable. Critically, the routing view explains a recovery puzzle: a few safety examples can fully restore refusal because the underlying safety representations are preserved — only the routing sharpness is disrupted. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness, but protection degrades at larger fine-tuning scales.

![Figure 1: Fisher geometry visualization — safety MLP modules show flattened geometry after alignment, re-sharpened after benign fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Fisher geometry across MLP modules before alignment, after alignment, and after 100 benign fine-tuning steps; output-side modules (responsible for routing) re-sharpen selectively, breaking refusal.*

### 4. [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining-safety` `refusal-direction` `tamper-resistance` `preprint`

Nathan Truong — arXiv preprint (August 2026)

Defends the refusal direction from abliteration by making it harder to extract. Instead of fixing the refusal direction in place, applies rank-k weight edits to residual stream writer matrices that replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, while correcting reader matrices to preserve behavior. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5pp MMLU degradation. Addresses a key gap: existing defenses mitigate abliteration's effects but not the ease of refusal-direction extraction.

![Figure 1: AMRA weight editing — residual stream writer matrices receive rank-k alias updates that obscure the refusal direction from contrastive extraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Schematic of AMRA — rank-k updates obscure the refusal direction from contrastive probing while reader matrix corrections preserve model behavior.*

### 5. [**Dual-Adversarial Safety Alignment: Cultivating Intrinsic Threat Comprehension in LRMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09542)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `LRM` `preprint`

Hongli Shen, Shaopeng Fu, Qinbo Zhang, Jian Li, Di Wang (KAUST, Stony Brook) — arXiv preprint (August 10, 2026)

AdvSafe trains large reasoning models (LRMs) to internalize attack mechanisms rather than pattern-match on jailbreak surface forms. A two-phase adversarial game: an autonomous agent dynamically synthesizes jailbreak prompts, and the target LRM learns from both the attack decomposition and the rejection. With only 1,000 synthesized samples, AdvSafe-aligned LRMs significantly outperform existing jailbreak-robustness baselines while showing near-zero utility degradation across standard benchmarks.

![Figure 1: AdvSafe two-phase adversarial game — jailbreak synthesis agent and target LRM interact in alternating generation and alignment phases](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09542/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AdvSafe framework — adversarial synthesis agent generates mechanistically diverse jailbreaks; target LRM learns intrinsic threat comprehension rather than surface pattern rejection.*

### 6. [**From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`mech-interp-applied` `AI-security` `peer-reviewed` `EMNLP-2026-Findings`

Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng (UC San Diego) — EMNLP 2026 Findings (accepted, peer-reviewed)

Characterizes a three-stage safety circuit: Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads. Circuit-guided weight scaling amplifies the identified components, improving safety rates under adversarial attacks by 26.5% across six LLMs while incurring only 1.7% accuracy drop on four standard benchmarks. First peer-reviewed paper to show circuit-level interventions in weight space (not activation steering) are practical for production safety.

![Figure 1: Safety circuit diagram — Harmful Detection Heads feed into Safety Neurons which drive Refusal Heads; circuit-guided weight scaling amplifies each stage](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Three-stage safety circuit; circuit-guided weight scaling of identified components improves attack-success-rate reduction by 26.5pp vs. unmodified baselines.*

### 7. [**Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383)

`mech-interp-applied` `activation-steering` `peer-reviewed` `COLM-2026`

Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci — COLM 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)

Steering vectors can silently compromise safety: this paper shows the safety degradation is caused by a separable component in the vector that disrupts safety mechanisms but contributes minimally to the steering objective. A constrained optimization (primal-dual updates) removes this component subject to preserving steering effect and bounding false refusal. Peer-reviewed applied result: safety-preserving steering is tractable via post-hoc vector surgery.

![Figure 1: Steering vector decomposition — safety-degrading component separated from steering-effective component via primal-dual optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08383/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering vector decomposition into a safety-neutral steering component and a separable safety-degrading component; constrained optimization removes the latter while bounding false refusal.*

### 8. [**Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11025)

`mech-interp-applied` `emergent-misalignment` `SAE` `preprint`

Bonn-Aachen ICT / Lamarr Institute — arXiv preprint (August 11, 2026)

Uses SAE-based model diffing across four open-weight models to attribute emergent misalignment (EM) to specific pretraining documents. Finds jailbreak-persona, sarcasm, deception, and manipulation features are amplified by misalignment fine-tuning while assistant-identity features are suppressed. Steering individual SAE features is bi-directional: can induce EM rates of up to 62% in aligned models (exceeding 35% from misalignment fine-tuning itself) and re-align misaligned models to near-baseline. Provides causal evidence that EM is feature-mediated and pretraining-document-traceable.

![Figure 1: SAE feature comparison — diffing aligned vs. misaligned model activations identifies persona features amplified by misalignment fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11025/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE model diff (aligned vs. misaligned) showing amplified jailbreak-persona features and suppressed assistant-identity features; individual feature steering induces/reverses EM.*

### 9. [**Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

`mech-interp-foundational` `identifiability` `Koopman` `preprint`

Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv preprint (August 10, 2026)

First identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive. Treats the transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system (depth as time), lifts it with the Koopman operator to get a finite linear realization, and shows the realization's spectrum is a coordinate-free model property recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{-1/2} up to permutation — with matching minimax lower bound. A dissociation theorem: when the realization is non-normal, directions carrying activation variance and those carrying cross-depth information cannot coincide (challenging variance-based feature importance). Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base.

![Figure 1: Spectral convergence rate validation — empirical recovery exponent vs. predicted M^{-1/2} rate across GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Spectrum recovery rate across three models; Qwen3-8B-Base converges at exponent 0.506 ± 0.031, matching the M^{-1/2} prediction of the minimax lower bound.*

### 10. [**Securing Agentic AI: From Per-Action Checks to Trajectory Assurance**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01558)

`AI-security` `agentic` `trajectory-assurance` `ACM-2026`

Alireza Lotfi, Subangkar Karmaker Shanto, Imtiaz Karim, Elisa Bertino — ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026 Visionary Track (August 3, 2026)

Argues that agentic AI safety cannot be reduced to per-action correctness: safety is a trajectory property, determined by whether overall behavior remains consistent with the invariants of the systems in which agents operate. Maps the attack surface across the full agentic stack (untrusted inputs via prompts, memory, retrieval, tool interfaces) and proposes Trajectory Assurance as a security primitive: runtime monitors that enforce invariants over whole action sequences rather than individual steps. Accepted at ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026 Visionary Track.
