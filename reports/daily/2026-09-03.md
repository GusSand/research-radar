### 1. [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang (Indiana University Bloomington, Tsinghua, NTU) — arXiv preprint (September 1, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Freshest paper in the window; first mechanistic (Fisher-geometric) explanation of why even capability-only fine-tuning reliably breaks safety alignment. Directly addresses the alignment durability question — the *why* behind a well-known empirical failure mode.
- **Technical summary:** Alignment flattens the Fisher information geometry for safety-critical weights (low-rank safety Fisher), creating a dormant output-routing pathway through output-side MLP modules. As few as 100 benign fine-tuning examples selectively re-sharpen this pathway — causing attack success rates to jump from ~3% to ~71% while MMLU degrades mildly. The asymmetry is output-localized: safety representations are preserved (explaining why a handful of safety examples can quickly restore refusal), but the routing geometry is disrupted. LoRA and ASAM suppress early collapse by dampening output-side sharpness, but their protection degrades at larger fine-tuning budgets.

![Figure 1: Safety Fisher geometry — flat after alignment, selectively resharpened in output-side MLPs after 100 benign fine-tuning steps](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Fisher geometry across MLP modules. Alignment creates flat low-rank geometry (dormant routing pathway). After 100 benign fine-tuning steps, output-side modules re-sharpen selectively — attack success rate jumps to 71% while general utility stays stable.*

### 2. [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

- **Authors / venue:** Nathan Truong — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses the root cause of abliteration's tractability: the refusal direction is trivially extracted as a mean difference of harmful/harmless hidden states. Rank-k alias updates make that extraction fail, with minimal capability cost.
- **Technical summary:** Existing abliteration defenses harden the effects of weight projection, but the refusal direction itself remains easy to compute from a small contrastive set. AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) applies rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices that replace refusal-inducing activations with randomly chosen aliases, then corrects downstream reader matrices to preserve original model behavior. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with under 0.5pp MMLU degradation. The approach is model-agnostic (weight-space edit) and requires no retraining.

![Figure 1: AMRA weight-editing — residual stream writer matrices receive rank-k alias updates; contrastive probing cannot extract the true refusal direction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA — rank-k updates replace true refusal activations with random aliases in writer matrices; reader corrections preserve behavior. Abliteration via contrastive extraction fails because the direction no longer exists in this form.*

### 3. [**Transfer Safety Awareness for Cross-Modal Safety Drift in Multimodal Large Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02082)

- **Authors / venue:** Tianqi Xiao, Shiyao Cui, Minghao Zhang, Junxiao Yang, Renmiao Chen — arXiv preprint (September 2, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Identifies and measures a specific multimodal safety gap (cross-modal safety drift) and proposes a training-free fix. Freshest paper in the window alongside 2609.01455, and directly relevant to deployed multimodal systems.
- **Technical summary:** Cross-modal safety drift: a benign textual query paired with a harmful image bypasses refusal far more often than an explicitly harmful text query. Empirical analysis shows that visually-risky cues receive limited attention in the model's representation and weakly trigger safety-relevant computations. The authors identify that safety signals from unsafe *text* processing can be directionally transferred: Safety-Awareness Representation Transfer (SRT) is a lightweight, training-free direction-refinement method applied to a frozen MLLM backbone. It projects the model's internal state toward the unsafe-text safety direction when a visually-risky query is detected, substantially reducing compliance with cross-modal harmful requests without degrading benign multimodal performance.

![Figure 1: Cross-modal safety drift — attention analysis showing weak visually-risky cue activation vs SRT direction-refinement fixing the gap](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02082/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Internal representation analysis of cross-modal safety drift; SRT transfers safety signal from the text-unsafe pathway to the cross-modal case via direction refinement on a frozen backbone.*

### 4. [**Dual-Adversarial Safety Alignment: Cultivating Intrinsic Threat Comprehension in LRMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09542)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `LRM` `preprint`

Hongli Shen, Shaopeng Fu, Qinbo Zhang, Jian Li, Di Wang (KAUST, Stony Brook) — arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026

AdvSafe trains large reasoning models to internalize attack mechanisms, not pattern-match on jailbreak surface forms. A two-phase adversarial game: a synthesis agent dynamically crafts diverse jailbreaks; the target LRM learns from both the attack decomposition and the rejection. With 1,000 synthesized samples, AdvSafe-aligned LRMs significantly outperform jailbreak-robustness baselines with near-zero utility degradation on standard benchmarks.

![Figure 1: AdvSafe two-phase adversarial game — synthesis agent crafts mechanistically diverse jailbreaks; LRM learns intrinsic threat comprehension](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09542/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Synthesis agent generates diverse jailbreaks; target LRM learns intrinsic threat mechanisms rather than surface patterns — 1K samples yields significant robustness improvement.*

### 5. [**From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`mech-interp-applied` `AI-security` `peer-reviewed` `EMNLP-2026-Findings`

Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng (UC San Diego) — EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)

Characterizes a three-stage safety circuit: Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads. Circuit-guided weight scaling (a persistent weight-space intervention, not activation steering) of the identified components improves safety rates under adversarial attacks by 26.5% across six LLMs with only 1.7% accuracy drop on four standard benchmarks.

![Figure 1: Three-stage safety circuit — Detection Heads feed Safety Neurons which drive Refusal Heads; circuit-guided weight scaling improves attack resistance 26.5%](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety circuit map; circuit-guided weight scaling improves safety rate under attacks by 26.5pp vs. unmodified baselines with 1.7% benchmark cost.*

### 6. [**Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383)

`mech-interp-applied` `activation-steering` `peer-reviewed` `COLM-2026`

Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci — COLM 2026 (peer-reviewed)

Steering vectors silently compromise safety via a separable sub-component that disrupts safety mechanisms but contributes minimally to the steering objective. Primal-dual constrained optimization removes this component, recovering a safety-neutral steering vector while preserving the intended behavioral change — training-free, applied as post-hoc surgery to any existing steering vector.

![Figure 1: Steering vector decomposed into steering-effective and safety-degrading components via primal-dual optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08383/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering vector = safety-neutral steering component + separable safety-degrading component; constrained optimization removes the latter while bounding false-refusal increase.*

### 7. [**Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11025)

`mech-interp-applied` `SAE` `emergent-misalignment` `preprint`

Bonn-Aachen ICT / Lamarr Institute — arXiv preprint, August 11, 2026

SAE-based model diffing across four open-weight models traces emergent misalignment (EM) to specific pretraining documents activating jailbreak-persona, deception, and manipulation features (amplified by misalignment fine-tuning) while assistant-identity features are suppressed. Steering individual identified features induces EM at up to 62% rate in aligned models — exceeding 35% from misalignment fine-tuning itself — and re-aligns misaligned models bidirectionally.

![Figure 1: SAE model diff — jailbreak persona and deception features amplified, assistant-identity features suppressed by misalignment fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11025/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE model diff (aligned vs. misaligned); feature steering is bi-directional: induces 62% EM in aligned models and re-aligns misaligned ones to near-baseline.*

### 8. [**Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

`mech-interp-theory` `identifiability` `Koopman` `preprint`

Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv preprint, August 10, 2026

First identifiability theorem for a mechanistic interpretability primitive: treats the transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system, lifts it with the Koopman operator to get a finite linear realization, and proves the spectrum is a coordinate-free model property recoverable at rate M^{-1/2} from M calibration samples (with matching minimax lower bound). A dissociation theorem shows that when the realization is non-normal, activation-variance directions and cross-depth information directions cannot coincide — directly challenging variance-based feature metrics. Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base (empirical exponent 0.506 ± 0.031).

![Figure 1: Spectrum recovery rate across three models following the M^{-1/2} predicted rate](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Empirical spectrum recovery exponents across GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base; Qwen3 converges at 0.506 ± 0.031, matching the minimax lower bound prediction.*

### 9. [**Securing Agentic AI: From Per-Action Checks to Trajectory Assurance**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01558)

`AI-security` `agentic` `trajectory-assurance` `ACM-2026`

Alireza Lotfi, Subangkar K. Shanto, Imtiaz Karim, Elisa Bertino — ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026 Visionary Track, August 2026

Argues that agentic AI safety is a *trajectory* property, not a per-action one: a sequence of individually-approved actions can violate system invariants in aggregate. Proposes Trajectory Assurance as a security primitive — runtime monitors enforcing invariants over complete action sequences across organizational boundaries. Covers the full agentic attack surface (prompt, memory, retrieval, tool interfaces) at both single-agent and multi-agent delegation layers.

![Figure 1: Per-action checks vs trajectory assurance — locally safe actions forming an unsafe global sequence vs invariant enforcement over the full trajectory](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01558/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Per-action security (node-level, can fail globally) vs. trajectory assurance (sequence-level invariant enforcement) across a multi-agent delegation stack.*

### 10. [**Enforcing LLM Safety through DMD-based Classification of Prompt-Response Embedding Dynamics**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19579)

`AI-security` `safety-monitor` `dynamical-systems` `preprint`

— arXiv preprint, August 2026

Applies Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) — originally developed for hallucination detection via activation trajectory analysis — to LLM safety classification. Rather than probing static representations, DMD characterizes the *dynamics* of hidden-state evolution across generation; unsafe prompts produce recognizably different embedding trajectories that a linear DMD classifier can separate. Extends the dynamical-systems interpretability framework from hallucination to harm detection, providing a training-free safety monitor applicable at inference time.

![Figure 1: DMD-based safety classification — hidden-state dynamics across generation for safe vs. unsafe prompts showing separable trajectory structure](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19579/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DMD decomposition of prompt-response embedding dynamics; safe and unsafe generation trajectories are linearly separable in the DMD feature space.*
