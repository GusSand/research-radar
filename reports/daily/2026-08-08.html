ICML Workshop 2026 mech-interp circuit tracing

LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs

Ameen Patel, Max Zhang, Nathan Hu · ICML Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop 2026 (poster)

Circuit tracing is the sharpest tool in the mech-interp toolkit, but it has a stubborn manual bottleneck: grouping individual latent features into interpretable supernodes requires human annotators, preventing it from scaling. This paper shows an LLM can do that step automatically — and do it as well as a human.

The pipeline presents feature descriptions (extracted via automated interpretability) to an LLM that clusters them into supernodes and labels each cluster. Using automated interpretability metrics to validate supernode quality, the pipeline matches human-annotator output on a controlled two-hop Capitals task, recovering the correct intermediate-hop supernode in 97 of 100 prompts. A proof-of-concept scales to 1000 Wikipedia prompt attribution graphs with an LLM judge that flags circuits of interest for human review, demonstrating end-to-end automation from raw feature activations to labeled circuit structure.

ICML 2026 mech-interp circuits

Scalable Circuit Learning for Interpreting Large Language Models

IBM Research · ICML 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)

SAE features make circuits interpretable, but their high dimensionality (~100K features) makes exhaustive intervention-based circuit search computationally prohibitive. CircuitLasso replaces that search with sparse linear regression — recovering circuits as quickly as fitting a Lasso model.

Raw LLM neurons are polysemantic, making intervention-based circuit discovery over them produce dense, noisy graphs. SAE features solve the polysemanticity problem but create a search space too large for standard patching methods. CircuitLasso reformulates circuit learning as a sparse regression from SAE feature activations to the model's output: the Lasso penalty selects a minimal set of features whose activations jointly predict the behavior of interest. On circuit-quality benchmarks the recovered circuits match state-of-the-art structural accuracy while reducing compute by orders of magnitude, and the resulting circuits transparently expose how human-interpretable features propagate and interact to produce predictions.

NeurIPS 2025 AI security interp-for-security

Breaking Bad: Interpretability-Based Safety Audits of State-of-the-Art LLMs

Multiple authors · NeurIPS 2025 (accepted, peer-reviewed) · arXiv:2604.20945

Every safety audit that stays at the prompt level misses a whole class of attack: interpretability-based steering that bypasses content filtering by intervening directly on internal representations. This is the first systematic deployment of those attacks across eight production models at once — and the gaps it reveals are stark.

The audit applies Universal Steering (adding steering vectors at every layer) and Representation Engineering (injecting concept directions) to Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B-4bt, GPT-oss-20B, GPT-oss-120B, Qwen3-0.6B/32B, and Phi4-3.8B/14B, with an adaptive two-stage grid search for optimal steering coefficients on each. Key finding: Llama-3 models are highly vulnerable — Llama-3.3-70B-4bt reaches 91% (US) and 83% (RepE) — while GPT-oss-120B is robust to both attack types. Neither model size nor output-level safety evaluations predict vulnerability to representation-level attacks, establishing that white-box interpretability access is a qualitatively different threat surface than prompt engineering.

Robust Harmful Features Under Jailbreak Attacks: Mechanistic Evidence from Attention Head Specialization

Yanchen Yin, Dongqi Han, Linghui Li · arXiv preprint, June 2026

Jailbreak attacks do not erase safety representations — they selectively suppress early-layer Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) while mid-layer Safety-Aligned Heads (SAHs) remain active. Ablation studies confirm that suppressing only a small set of ACHs on clean inputs reproduces jailbreak-like behavior, while removing SAHs weakens safety activations; token-level attribution traces ACH suppression specifically to attack-template tokens, giving the first mechanistic decomposition of why attacks succeed while internal safety signals persist.

mech-interp AI security preprint

Why Do Aligned LLMs Remain Jailbreakable: Refusal-Escape Directions, Operator-Level Sources, and Safety-Utility Trade-off

Yu Chen, Yuanhao Liu, Qi Cao (Institute of Computing Technology, CAS) · arXiv preprint, May 2026

Refusal-Escape Directions (RED) are local continuous perturbation directions near harmful inputs that shift model behavior from refusal to compliance while preserving harmful-semantics interpretation — giving a mechanistic, operator-level account of why jailbreaks structurally work. The framework proves that fine-tuning-based alignment cannot eliminate RED without capability loss, establishing a fundamental safety-utility trade-off that output-level defenses cannot resolve.

AI security preprint

Grammar-Constrained Decoding Can Jailbreak LLMs into Generating Malicious Code

Yitong Zhang, Shiteng Lu, Jia Li · arXiv preprint, June 2026

Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) is a reliability tool that enforces syntactic validity in LLM code output — but the reliability mechanism is itself an attack surface. CodeSpear shows that applying a benign code grammar constraint shifts the generation distribution into a code-conditioned mode where safety guardrails are less active, enabling elicitation of malicious code from aligned models without modifying the harmful intent in the prompt. CodeShield defends by training the model to emit honeypot code (syntactically valid, functionally harmless) under attacker-controlled grammar constraints, robustly preserving safe behavior.

mech-interp AI security preprint

AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent

Multiple authors (UGA, USF, Rutgers, USC, JHU) · arXiv preprint, June 2026

AgentLens extends mechanistic safety intervention to multi-turn agentic execution: detecting harmful execution states from step-level hidden representations and mitigating unsafe behavior in real time by intervening in a 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace within a single layer. The key finding — that a 10D subspace in one mid-model layer captures the full range of safety-relevant execution states — comes with the Mechanistic Agent Safety (MAS) benchmark: 194 fully annotated multi-turn coding trajectories across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B.

mech-interp preprint

Can Language Model Agents be Helpful Circuit Explainers in Mechanistic Interpretability?

Ayan Antik Khan, Harsh Kohli, Yuekun Yao, Huan Sun, Ziyu Yao (George Mason & Ohio State) · arXiv preprint, June 2026

AgenticInterpBench provides 84 semi-synthetic transformer circuits with 163 component-level annotations as a standardized testbed for circuit explanation. HyVE (Hypothesize, Validate, Explain) iterates an observe–hypothesize–validate loop until a component-level explanation and circuit-level task description converge; across four LLM backbones HyVE recovers useful explanations, but the validation step — incomplete plans, code execution failures, unresolved hypotheses — is the dominant failure mode, identifying the path forward for automated circuit explanation.

mech-interp AI security preprint

SafeSeek: Universal Attribution of Safety Circuits in Language Models

Multiple authors · arXiv preprint, March 2026

SafeSeek reformulates safety circuit discovery as binary mask optimization over model units (weights, neurons, attention heads, layers) via the Straight-Through Estimator, enabling end-to-end differentiable extraction of the minimal sparse safety subgraph. The framework generalizes across alignment attribution, jailbreak robustness analysis, and backdoor tracing. Safety Circuit Tuning then concentrates fine-tuning gradient updates on the extracted circuit, outperforming full-parameter safety fine-tuning in both effectiveness and parameter efficiency.

From Mechanistic to Compositional Interpretability

Ward Gauderis, Thomas Dooms, Steven T. Homer, Kola Ayonrinde, Geraint A. Wiggins · arXiv preprint, May/June 2026

Compositional interpretability is a category-theoretic framework in which an interpretation is a pair of syntactic (model decomposition) and semantic (behavioral) mappings that must commute — enforcing consistency between structure and function. Explanation quality decomposes into faithfulness and complexity; interpretability becomes a constrained optimization problem with a parsimony criterion under which syntactic compression provably yields more concise, human-aligned explanations. The framework situates circuit discovery, SAE decomposition, and activation patching as subclasses of refinement, clarifying where their compressibility heuristics hold and where they break down.
