№ 01 — Pretraining / Midtraining Safety
Preprint Midtraining Model Spec
Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes
Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov  ·  Anthropic  ·  arXiv May 2026

What if models failed to generalize alignment not because their demonstrations were bad, but because they had no principled framework to interpret those demonstrations against? Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) inserts exactly that framework — the full text of a model spec — into training before any demonstrations arrive.

MSM creates a large synthetic corpus discussing Model Spec content (the what and why of desired behavior) and uses it as a midtraining stage between pretraining and alignment fine-tuning. Evaluated on Qwen3-32B, MSM with a spec addressing self-preservation and goal-guarding reduces the agentic misalignment rate from 54% to 7%, beating a deliberative alignment baseline (14%). Crucially, varying only the midtraining spec content controls which values models acquire from identical downstream AFT demonstrations — a clean lever for alignment control without touching post-training data.

№ 02 — Pretraining / Midtraining Safety
Preprint Pretraining Spec-Driven
Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero
EPFL DLab  ·  arXiv August 13, 2026  ·  LessWrong post available

Can you install alignment before the model has learned to produce language at all? SPP answers yes: value-aligned reflections appended to pretraining documents — before any RLHF or SFT — produce a model whose safety advantages grow with compute and survive post-training.

Synthetic Persona Pretraining annotates ~10% of pretraining documents with first-person reflections derived from a normative value constitution, then trains via standard cross-entropy on both. The core finding is persona binding: the pretrained values generalize to held-out post-training scenarios the constitution never covered. Mean ASR is 1.7% vs. 4.6% for the baseline — a 63% relative reduction — and ablations show that introducing SPP only at the end of pretraining (not from token zero) yields substantially weaker constitution adherence. The authors introduce ConstitutionEval as an in-domain benchmark for testing value internalization.

№ 03 — Pretraining / Midtraining Safety
Preprint Midtraining Constitutional
Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains
arXiv July 30, 2026  ·  120B-token scale · 394M constitutional corpus · 2×2 factorial design

Constitutional midtraining isolates, at scale, the contribution of values-based content in a midtraining corpus — cleanly separated from post-training — and shows those gains are durable across downstream fine-tuning.

The study builds a 394M-token constitutional corpus from Anthropic's Constitution and evaluates midtraining at 120B tokens with a 2×2 factorial design (curriculum ordering × deliberative-reasoning presence) against a replay-only control. Across alignment-under-pressure, value-conflict resolution, blackmail, and emergent-misalignment benchmarks, constitutionally midtrained models consistently outperform controls. The headline result: constitutional midtraining blunts the blackmail propensity instilled by SFT, and this advantage survives subsequent benign fine-tuning — suggesting midtraining-installed values are more durable than post-training alignment alone.

№ 04 — Pretraining Safety
Preprint Pretraining
Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection
Jinhan Li et al. · Tsinghua University · arXiv Jun 17, 2026
Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) regularly inserts short safety-judgment annotations into pretraining corpora — after each segment, the model sees a reflection classifying its safety and asserting a normative stance. The core argument: models can compose benign knowledge into unsafe behavior, so alignment must shape how the model reasons about knowledge, not just what data it sees. Experiments with 1.7B models on FineWeb-Edu show SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces jailbreak and fine-tuning attack success rates, outperforming naive data filtering and rewriting.
№ 05 — Refusal Mechanism / Safety
Preprint Security Interp
Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases
Nathan Truong · arXiv Aug 2026
Abliteration removes LLM refusals by projecting weight matrices orthogonal to the extracted refusal direction — a low-cost jailbreak requiring only a small contrastive prompt set. AMRA defends by obfuscating the refusal direction itself: rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, while reader matrix corrections preserve model behavior. The defense does not require retraining and stacks on existing post-training alignment. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points with less than 0.5% MMLU degradation.
№ 06 — AI / LLM Security
Preprint Security
Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks
arXiv May 26, 2026
Existing defenses for open-weight LLMs against adversarial fine-tuning are evaluated against two low-cost, gradient-free attacks: abliteration (projecting weights orthogonal to the refusal direction) and prefilling (providing partial harmful responses). Against defended models, these attacks raise ASR from below 10% to 16–96% across defense variants. Abliteration-Resistant Tuning (ART) folds an abliteration-based objective into the defense training and reduces success of abliteration, prefilling, and their combination by 10–20 pp — demonstrating that post-training defenses alone are insufficient and motivating pretraining-time approaches.
№ 07 — AI / LLM Security
Preprint Security
Agent Against Agent: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming
Yanting Wang, Chenlong Yin, Runpeng Geng, Jinyuan Jia · arXiv Aug 5, 2026
PIMiner addresses prompt injection red-teaming at scale: existing RL-based attacker models must be retrained per target LLM and generalize poorly. PIMiner instead trains an agentic attacker on a sequence of (dataset, target model) pairs, building a reusable strategy library. At test time, this library transfers directly to a previously unseen target with only ~10 queries per sample — no additional training required. This makes large-scale automated prompt injection evaluation tractable and suggests strategy-transfer is a viable alternative to per-target fine-tuning for red-teaming.
№ 08 — Applied Mech Interpretability
Workshop Interp Security
Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study
Paria Mehrbod, Boris Knyazev, Guy Wolf, Eugene Belilovsky, Geraldin Nanfack · ICML Workshop on Reliable & Responsible Foundation Models · arXiv Aug 2026
Using edge attribution patching and subnetwork probing on LLaMA-2-7B-chat, the authors identify specific attention heads and MLP pathways whose activation mediates affirmative responses to jailbreak prompts. The key mechanism: trigger phrases in the prompt propagate through these components to override safety constraints during the first token prediction step. Ablating these circuits at inference time reduces attack success by up to 80% — a cheap, targeted defense that requires no model retraining and demonstrates that circuit-level understanding translates to an operational safety intervention.
№ 09 — Mechanistic Interpretability
Preprint Interp
Thinking vs. NoThinking: Towards Interpreting Reasoning Mechanisms of Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders
Bo Cheng, Qiaolin Lu, Yi Chang, Yuan Wu · arXiv Aug 2026
Top-K Sparse Autoencoders applied to intermediate representations of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B reveal mechanistically distinct activation patterns across Chain-of-Thought (Thinking) and direct-answer (NoThinking) modes across math tasks at three difficulty levels. Thinking mode produces sparse, high-intensity feature activations that drive verbal deduction independently of problem complexity; NoThinking mode exhibits diffuse, adaptive patterns that scale with task difficulty and prioritize symbolic manipulation. These signatures suggest SAEs can distinguish reasoning mode from representations alone — with potential applications to runtime mode detection and targeted steering of reasoning behavior.
№ 10 — Mechanistic Interpretability (Foundational)
Preprint Interp
Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability
Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen · IBM Research · arXiv Aug 2026
Mechanistic interpretability has no theory for whether circuits are model properties or methodological artifacts — different SAE seeds and widths recover materially different features. This paper puts dictionary learning for interpretability on an identifiability footing by treating the forward pass as a controlled dynamical system with depth as time and lifting it with the Koopman operator, yielding a finite linear realization whose spectrum is coordinate-free (a genuine property of the model, not the method). The spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M⁻¹/² up to permutation, with a matching minimax lower bound establishing that this recovery rate is optimal.
