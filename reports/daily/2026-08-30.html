pretraining-safety EPFL-dlab Aug 13 2026

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero

Julian Minder, Viktor Moskvoretskii, Raghav Singhal et al. — arXiv preprint

Alignment is almost always bolted on — RLHF, SFT, DPO applied after the pretraining run ends. This paper asks whether installing the desired persona during pretraining, from the very first token, produces a more robust foundation. It does: models trained with Synthetic Persona Pretraining (SPP) show stronger constitution following, greater jailbreak robustness, and less misalignment on out-of-distribution moral dilemmas, with the advantage growing as pretraining budget increases.

SPP annotates 10% of pretraining documents with synthetic first-person moral reflections drawn from a constitution. Both the original text and the reflection appear in the cross-entropy training target, so the model learns the desired persona as part of its general world model rather than as a post-hoc constraint. Ablations at 3B parameters / 500B tokens: (a) omitting the Stage 3 persona binding step substantially weakens behavioral alignment; (b) introducing SPP only at the tail of pretraining — without the full-budget exposure — yields similarly diminished gains. SPP models improve on constitution-following benchmarks, reduce jailbreak success rates, and generalize to out-of-distribution moral dilemmas, all without capability degradation. Code and data: github.com/epfl-dlab/spp.

KDD 2026 · peer-reviewed pretraining-safety UW CriticalML

TamperBench: Systematically Stress-Testing LLM Safety Under Fine-Tuning and Tampering

Critical ML Lab, University of Washington — ACM KDD 2026 (presented Aug 9–13, Jeju)

The most comprehensive empirical study yet of whether aligned LLMs actually survive adversarial fine-tuning. TamperBench sweeps 21 models across 9 attack types with hyperparameter optimization per model-attack pair, and the verdict is stark: current alignment-stage defenses — circuit breakers, representation engineering — fail across virtually every attack sweep when the attacker is allowed to search over hyperparameters.

TamperBench evaluates 21 open-weight LLMs — including defense-augmented variants — across nine tampering threats: weight-space attacks (adversarial SFT, jailbreak-tuning, harmful-data poisoning, benign fine-tuning data) and latent-space representation attacks. For each model–attack pair, a hyperparameter sweep finds the worst-case attack configuration; the result is scored jointly on StrongREJECT (safety) and MMLU-Pro (utility). Key findings: (1) jailbreak-tuning with ≈2% poisoning is consistently the most severe attack across model families; (2) alignment-stage defenses fail under systematic sweeps in nearly all conditions; (3) post-training choices (instruction style, RLHF temperature, DPO variant) affect tamper resistance in ways that cannot be predicted from standard safety benchmarks. Code: github.com/criticalml-uw/TamperBench.

pretraining-safety arXiv Jun 17 2026

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint

Even a corpus filtered to contain only safe content produces models that can compose benign capabilities into harmful outputs — data safety and model safety are different things. Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) addresses this without filtering: instead of removing unsafe content, it annotates the existing corpus with inline safety judgments, training the model to reason about safety as it learns from text.

SRP inserts short inline safety judgments — a one- or two-sentence assessment of whether the preceding text segment is safe, and why — directly into pretraining documents. Both original text and annotation appear in the cross-entropy target; no content is removed, so corpus diversity is unchanged. Applied to 1.7B-parameter models on FineWeb-Edu: SRP models improve safety classification accuracy and reduce attack success rates for both inference-time attacks and fine-tuning attacks (adversarial SFT with 10 poisoned examples). Unlike SPP (#1), SRP does not attempt to install a persona — it builds a safety-reasoning capability — making the two approaches potentially complementary rather than competing.

Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak

arXiv preprint · 4 models · 3 families · 1.5B–14B parameters

Applies the single-line prefill attack ("Sure, here is") across 4 models and 3 families. A linear harm probe trained on unattacked completions still fires at 0.91–0.98 accuracy when the attack flips the model to compliance — the model encodes harm but refusal is bypassed. A position-control experiment shows the first half of the response suffices to break refusal; refusal is a shallow, response-site computation that does not require altering the model's internal harm representation. Directly mechanistic result for understanding why safety-training defenses like #1 and #3 must act earlier than response time.

Daydreaming: Stealing Hidden Agent Skills through Black-Box Task Interaction

Yu-Lin Tsai, Raluca Ada Popa et al. — arXiv preprint

Execution-only skill stealing from proprietary hosted agents. The attack adaptively crafts tasks whose results discriminate between possible hidden behaviors, uses shadow agents to design follow-up queries, and reconstructs each hidden skill file from stored results plus local execution checks. No model internals, gradients, or prompted disclosure required — purely black-box.

A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Framework Defense against LLM Jailbreak Attacks

Tongyan Hu, Bryan Hooi (NUS) — arXiv preprint

When a jailbreak succeeds, the framework extracts the structural attack wrapper (the method) — not the harmful topic — as a persistent rule in cross-interaction memory. No parameter updates required; works on black-box API models. One induced rule generalizes to the entire attack family. Robust to a held-out adaptive composite attack across 4 jailbreak families.

AI-security backdoor arXiv Aug 11 2026

Backdoor Decontamination Dynamics in LLM Agents

Gabriel Huang et al. (Polytechnique Montréal / ServiceNow) — arXiv preprint

Two-phase decontamination: install a known defensive backdoor, then unlearn it — the original unknown backdoor is collateral damage. In 115 experiments on AgentDyn, defensive poisoning alone erases ~56% of backdoors; unlearning drives almost all survivors to erasure. Key dissociation: trigger recognition and malicious execution are behaviorally separable — decontamination reliably removes execution while recognition may persist.

ICML 2026 Workshop · peer-reviewed mech-interp safety-eval

Not All Eval-Awareness Is Equal: Capabilities Framing Predicts Compliance

Allison Zhuang, Santiago Aranguri — ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop

Eval-awareness decomposes into capabilities-framed ("testing ability to follow instructions") vs. safety-framed ("testing limits") CoT reasoning. On Qwen3-32B/FORTRESS: capabilities framing predicts +24–46 pp compliance gap over safety framing across all conditions. CoT-prefill causal intervention confirms the link (10/11 prefills shift compliance in the predicted direction). Safety evaluation pipelines that suppress eval-awareness may inadvertently boost compliance through capabilities-framing spillover.

mech-interp SAE theory arXiv Aug 10 2026

Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability

Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv preprint

First identifiability theorem for dictionary learning (SAEs) in mechanistic interpretability: treats the forward pass as a Koopman-operator controlled dynamical system and proves the spectrum is coordinate-free (independent of SAE seed/width), recoverable at rate M⁻¹/² with a matching minimax lower bound. A dissociation theorem shows activation-variance directions and information-carrying directions cannot coincide in non-normal realizations — explaining why SAE variance metrics miss causally active features. Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base.

AI-security safety-classifier arXiv Aug 20 2026

Enforcing LLM Safety through DMD-based Classification of Prompt-Response Embedding Dynamics

Mohamed Akrout, Olivera Kotevska, Dan Wilson — arXiv preprint

Extends Koopman/DMD (Dynamic Mode Decomposition) from hallucination detection to safety classification. Separate Koopman-based predictive models are trained on embedding trajectories for each safety class; at inference time, a differential residual score compares prediction errors between safe and unsafe regime models. Unlike point-in-time classifiers, this captures sequential trajectory patterns across the generation process. Connects to #9's Koopman operator framing of mechanistic interpretability.
