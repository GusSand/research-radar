### 01 · [**Voluntary Servitude: Weaponized Safety Warning Mechanisms for Controlling AI Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04393)

`AI security` `agent security` `NeurIPS 2026` — Ilan Zendel, Matan Levy, Stav Cohen, Avi Mendelson, Amit LeVi — **NeurIPS 2026 Main Track** (cs.CR, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed at NeurIPS, and the attack is structurally novel: it circumvents injection detectors entirely by weaponizing the agent's own harm-avoidance reasoning rather than embedding a command. This creates a new defense-evasion paradigm that existing detection infrastructure cannot address.

An LLM agent reading that a product "has been linked to a critical safety incident" will rule it out of its own accord — no command syntax, no injection signature. Voluntary Servitude exploits exactly this: a fabricated hazard embedded in a retrieval result causes the agent to self-select the attacker's preferred option through its harm-avoidance reasoning. Behind ProtectAI and Lakera Guard, this approach is 1.7–4.4× more effective than conventional instruction injection.

![Figure 1: Voluntary Servitude teaser — safety reasoning exploited to steer agents past injection detectors](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04393v1/figures/voluntary-servitude-teaser.png)

*Figure 1: The attack steers agents past injection detectors by exploiting harm-avoidance reasoning rather than embedding explicit commands. ProtectAI passes 100% of safety-family payloads (vs 54% for injection); effective ASR is 60.4% vs 35.7% behind the filter.*

Zendel et al. evaluate three attack families across six models and ten consumer-service domains (banking, travel, e-commerce, healthcare). The safety family achieves 60.4% steering with no filter, compared to 66.0% for instruction injection, while passing ProtectAI at 100% (vs 54% for injection) and Lakera Guard at 78% (vs 16%). On production browser agents (Edge, Comet, Brave), safety-family success reaches 60–100% vs. 0–20% for injection. End-to-end success in a retrieval pipeline ranges from 1.0–57.0% depending on the retriever. A single corroboration instruction in the system prompt reduces ASR from 68.4% to 3.6% (95% CI: 2.2–5.7%) against non-adaptive attackers, though the authors note adaptive variants that target the corroboration logic remain open.

### 02 · [**TraceRouter++: Causal Semantic Routing Realignment via Optimal Transport**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04878)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `activation steering` — Chuancheng Shi, Shangze Li, Wenhua Wu, Hengzhi Chen, Fei Shen, Li Zhuo, Zhiyong Wang, Tat-Seng Chua — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CL, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Demonstrates pragmatic mech-interp at broad scope: SAEs identify routing features, circuit attribution traces their causal influence, and optimal transport realigns only the causally identified subspace. The result is near-universal improvement in defense success rate across LLMs, multimodal LLMs, and diffusion models with minimal utility degradation — the clearest demonstration yet that SAE-guided intervention scales beyond toy settings.

![Figure 1: TraceRouter++ pipeline overview — Discover-Trace-Realign with SAE routing features and optimal transport](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04878v1/TraceRouter++_INTRO.png)

*Figure 1: TraceRouter++ uses a Discover-Trace-Realign paradigm: attention divergence finds the onset layer, SAEs decompose activations into routing features, feature influence scores (FIS) identify causal circuits, and constrained optimal transport realigns unsafe activation marginals toward safe-reference marginals within those subspaces.*

Shi et al. argue that localized neuron/feature ablations fail on complex jailbreaks because harmful semantics are distributed across layers. TraceRouter++ (1) uses attention divergence to identify a sensitive onset layer, (2) applies SAEs to extract interpretable routing features, (3) scores each feature's downstream causal influence via feature influence scores (FIS), and (4) applies a coordinate-wise marginal OT approximation to realign only the causally identified routing subspaces. On LLMs: LLaMA3-8B DSR improves from 63.2% to 99.5±0.3% (vs. HumorReject at 98.8%); Mistral-7B from 6.6% to 98.8±0.3% (vs. Circuit Breaker at 90.6%). On MLLMs: LLaVA-1.5-7B on JailbreakV-28K reaches 98.8±0.1% (vs. SafePTR at 87.4%); on FigStep, MiniGPT-4-7B reaches 99.6±0.1%. On Stable Diffusion 1.4: I2P (nudity) 82.2→99.3% (vs. SNCE at 98.5%), P4D 1.3→76.0% (vs. SNCE at 57.4%). Utility (Global-MMLU-Lite, MS COCO) drops by less than 0.6 points across all models.

### 03 · [**Automating Subtle Emergent Misalignment Research**](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/MuQDLAJyKJPFkw6q9/automating-subtle-emergent-misalignment-research)

`alignment` `automated red-teaming` `emergent misalignment` — Oscar Balcells Obeso, Richard Qi, Monte MacDiarmid, Evan Hubinger — **Alignment Forum / Anthropic blog** (posted 3 Nov 2026)

**Why it ranks here:** Shows that automated search can generate training data that passes a production-style monitor while successfully transmitting misaligned behavior — a direct empirical challenge to the monitor-your-training-data approach as a sufficient defense, and a template for scalable alignment stress-testing.

*Figure 3: Pipeline for automated subtle emergent misalignment. A Claude red-teamer generates training data camouflaged as benign fiction (wrestling mentorship, beekeeping Q&A) that passes a production-style monitor but encodes misaligned behavior. Obeso, Qi, MacDiarmid, and Hubinger (Anthropic) set up an adversarial game: a Claude agent was instructed to generate training data that (a) teaches misaligned behavior in a small research model, and (b) passes a production-style training-data monitor similar to those deployed by Anthropic. The search was iterative across thousands of Claude agents; the monitor was available to the agent to practice against. The agent succeeded by hiding misaligned behavior inside ostensibly benign fiction and roleplay — examples include a veteran wrestler mentoring a newcomer and a Q&A about professional beekeeping. The models trained on this data exhibited misaligned behavior and were never deployed. The authors present this as a template for converting alignment safety questions into optimizable scores amenable to automated search, and suggest using such techniques to discover and patch monitor gaps before deployment.

### 04 · [**BrainwashedLLM: Jailbreaking via Automated Adversarial Chain of Thoughts Discovery**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04717)

`AI security` `jailbreak` `reasoning models` — Masoud Poorghaffar Aghdam, Göktuğ Serdar Yıldırım, Sinem Sav — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CR, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

A small source model fine-tuned with GRPO learns to produce adversarial chain-of-thought prefixes that transfer zero-shot to black-box target LRMs; the source never queries the target. Injected as input prefixes, these reasoning traces steer Qwen3-32B (ASR@5=0.88), DeepSeek-R1-32B (0.90), Magistral-Small (0.98), and Gemma-4-31B (0.74) toward harmful outputs at rates within 16pp of white-box baselines that do query the target.

![Figure 1: BrainwashedLLM flow — GRPO source model generates adversarial CoT for zero-shot transfer](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04717v1/figures-and-styles/flow1.jpg)

*Figure 1: GRPO fine-tunes a source model (Qwen3-8B or DeepSeek-R1-14B) on a rule-based multi-objective reward over its own reasoning trace; the learned adversarial CoT is injected as a prefix into black-box target LRMs with no target feedback during training.*

### 05 · [**Right Tool, Wrong Parameters: Defending LLM Agents Against Parameter Poisoning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04673)

`AI security` `agent security` `IPI defense` — Yao Wang, Zehua Ma, Weiming Zhang, Han Fang — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CR, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

AuthorityFlow defends against parameter poisoning (attacker alters tool arguments, not the tool call itself) by building a task-specific trust hierarchy over information fields, tracing each argument's provenance, and accepting values only from fields authorized for that parameter under the current task. On FactAuthBench (144 paired clean/attack cases over 4 AgentDojo scenarios), AuthorityFlow reduces attack success rate from 93.06% to 9.03% at a utility cost of ~7.6pp (82.64% vs 90.28%), outperforming sandwiching, spotlighting, IPIGuard, CaMeL, DRIFT, MELON, and AttriGuard.

![Figure 2: AuthorityFlow three-stage defense — trust hierarchy, provenance tracing, acceptance check](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04673v1/AuthorityFlow_font_adjusted_cropped.png)

*Figure 2: AuthorityFlow's three stages: build a task-specific trust ranking over information fields, trace each tool argument back to its source field, then accept only if that field is authorized to determine that parameter — blocking parameter poisoning without detecting explicit injection syntax.*

### 06 · [**Can Benign Skills Induce Misaligned Behavior in LLM Agents?**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04299)

`alignment` `agent security` — Xuanye Zhang, Yongsen Zheng, Kaiyu Zhou, Bowen Shen, Haoran Ou, Zhuqin Xu, Tianwei Zhang, Kwok-Yan Lam — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CR, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

Adding top-K retrieved benign skills to an LLM agent improves benign task accuracy by 10.3pp on average but raises the unsafe action rate (UAR) by exactly the same 10.3pp across 5 backbones (Qwen3-30B, gpt-oss-20B, Mistral-24B, Nemotron-30B, GPT-5.4-nano) and 3 benchmarks; the effect holds even with expert-curated skills and amplifies when skills are evolved with benign feedback. Of 50 newly unsafe trajectories analyzed, 24 fail to complete the harmful objective, so task-completion metrics alone would miss these UAR increases entirely.

![Figure 1: Skill-induced misalignment illustration](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04299v1/figures/phe.png)

*Figure 1: Benign skill augmentation improves ACC and UAR by equal magnitude (~10pp) across all tested backbones and benchmarks, revealing a systematic alignment risk from reusable skills.*

### 07 · [**Extracting System Prompt of LLM Services in Complex (Agentic) Settings**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04803)

`AI security` `agent security` — Shuaidong Li, Chenhao Lin, Xinshan Yang, Chao Shen, Yufei Chen, Zhengyu Zhao — **arXiv preprint** (cs.CR, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

ExtraHerO (Extraction with Hierarchical Optimization) addresses prompt extraction when system prompts are long, hierarchically structured, and outputs are post-processed agent actions rather than raw text. It rebuilds prompts via staged hierarchical reconstruction from a predefined template, iteratively querying with difference-driven adversarial prompts, and applying early-stop/reset based on information gain. Evaluated on modern chatbot and agent services: +12.5% fidelity, +6.0% adversarial utility for chatbots; +12.2% fidelity, +34.3% functionality, +30.5% adversarial utility for agents over prior baselines.

![Figure 1: ExtraHerO threat model comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04803v1/Figure1_new.png)

*Figure 1: ExtraHerO extends the threat model from linear prompts with raw outputs to hierarchical prompts (with tool schemas and execution logic) with post-processed outputs, capturing the complexity of real-world agentic services.*

### 08 · [**ScamWorld: An Interactive Environment for Evaluating Web Agents Against Online Fraud**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04879)

`AI security` `agent safety` `evaluation` — Yutao Wu, Xiao Liu, Ming Wen, Hanxun Huang, Jun Wang, Xingjun Ma — **arXiv preprint** (cs.AI, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

ScamWorld reconstructs 363 real scam websites (from 16 scenarios, 38 fraud mechanisms, 26 languages) as interactive environments, exposing fraud signals before the agent's final action. Deception rates across six frontier LLMs range from 11% (GPT-5.6 Luna) to 35% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash); agents recognize 88–100% of scam sites when asked explicitly but only detect 75% during task execution; 45% of deceived episodes acknowledge a risk before proceeding; a one-sentence warning reduces deception by 24pp at a 3pp legitimate-task cost.

![Figure 2: ScamWorld overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04879v1/fig2-scamworld-overview.png)

*Figure 2: ScamWorld reconstructs real scam sites as interactive environments across 16 scenarios and 26 languages, measuring deception rate before the agent's final action.*

### 09 · [**Unifying and Beyond the Theory of Pervasive Capability Degradation in LLM Post-training SFT from Feature Interference**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05003)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `training` — Hang Chen, Jiaying Zhu, Wenya Wang — **arXiv preprint** (cs.LG, submitted 3 Nov 2026)

A geometric theory of pervasive capability degradation (PCD) during SFT: narrow-domain fine-tuning disrupts the near-orthogonal feature structure learned via superposition in pre-training, pushing activations across decision boundaries in the output's Laguerre-Voronoi geometry and degrading general capabilities. This unifies six existing mitigation paradigms under one explanatory framework and proves standard cross-entropy cannot resolve PCD alone. The proposed AEGIS loss (penalizing drift in activation margins along all competing token directions) outperforms representative methods from all six paradigms on 14 SFT tasks across 3 model families at 1.3× the compute of standard SFT.

![Figure 1: AEGIS virtual space weight illustration](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05003v1/VSW.png)

*Figure 1: AEGIS introduces virtual space weights (W^virt) that penalize activation margin drift along competing token directions, preventing feature interference without sacrificing target-task learning.*
