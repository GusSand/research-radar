## 01 · [The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06934)

`pretraining-safety` `refusal-geometry` `alignment-durability` — Srikanth Malla, Chiho Choi, Joon Hee Choi (Samsung Semiconductor) — arXiv preprint, September 7, 2026

Post-hoc safety training (RLHF, DPO) keeps getting bypassed by jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks — this paper gives that pattern a precise geometric explanation and shows why pretraining-time safety does not share the same vulnerability. The culprit is where in the Fisher-curvature landscape the safety update lands.

![Figure 1: Fisher curvature diagram showing post-hoc safety update landing orthogonal to capability directions vs. pretraining safety embedding into the capability subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06934/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The safety update measured against the empirical Fisher of a capability loss — post-hoc methods land in a suppression regime where the update is nearly orthogonal to capability directions, forming a thin refusal gate rather than modifying the capability subspace itself.*

The paper measures each safety update against the curvature of the model's capabilities using the empirical Fisher of a capability loss. Post-hoc safety consistently lands in a suppression regime: the update is nearly orthogonal to the high-curvature capability directions, concentrating its small in-subspace component on a few sharp directions — a refusal gate placed over intact capabilities, not an erasure of them. Pretraining-time safety, by contrast, modifies the subspace in which capabilities reside, making the gate structurally embedded rather than superficially overlaid. The authors trace the exact geometry across three model families, confirming the dichotomy is robust: suppression regime = fragile; integration regime = durable. This provides the clearest mechanistic account to date of why data-filtering and pretraining-time interventions produce qualitatively different safety durability than post-hoc fine-tuning.

## 02 · [When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

`alignment-durability` `benign-fine-tuning` `Fisher-geometry` — Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang — **EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)**

Even fine-tuning on 100 benign examples can collapse a model's safety alignment, and this paper explains exactly why using Fisher geometry — not gradient conflict as previously claimed, but selective sharpening of a low-rank output-routing pathway.

![Figure 2: Fisher geometry showing low-rank safety subspace vs. re-sharpened output-routing pathway after benign fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 2: Safety Fisher is low-rank and flat; alignment makes the geometry flatter but leaves an output-routing pathway intact; 100 benign examples selectively re-sharpen that pathway in output-side MLP modules, causing asymmetric safety collapse with minimal utility loss.*

The paper proposes a Fisher-geometric account of alignment fragility: safety Fisher is low-rank, and safety training makes the geometry flatter while preserving a latent output-routing pathway. After as few as 100 benign fine-tuning examples, this pathway is selectively re-sharpened in output-side MLP modules — explaining why safety collapses asymmetrically (attack success rate rises sharply) while general utility degrades only mildly. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness, but protection weakens at larger fine-tuning scales. The routing-pathway view also explains why a handful of safety examples can restore refusal: they re-flatten exactly the components that benign fine-tuning had sharpened. Code and data available at the Hugging Face dataset `LuckyJason77/safety_routing_data`.

## 03 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`refusal-circuit` `mech-interp-for-safety` `EMNLP` — Kuan-Lin Chu et al. — **EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)**

Mechanistic interpretability work that identifies and causally validates a three-stage safety circuit inside aligned LLMs — and then uses it to improve safety by directly scaling the circuit's weights rather than fine-tuning the whole model.

![Figure 3: Three-stage safety circuit diagram: Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads with causal intervention evidence](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 3: Multi-stage safety circuit: Harmful Detection Heads (respond to harmful inputs) → Safety Neurons (mediate and stabilize signals in the residual stream) → Refusal Heads (translate signals into safe generation); suppressing upstream heads disrupts downstream refusal, confirming causal ordering.*

The paper characterizes a three-stage safety circuit: Harmful Detection Heads identify harmful intent in early layers, Safety Neurons in MLP modules mediate and stabilize those signals in the residual stream, and Refusal Heads translate the signal into the refusal response. Using targeted attention-head and neuron-level interventions, the authors provide causal evidence for this organization — suppressing Harmful Detection Heads disrupts downstream refusal with high reliability, and direct amplification of Safety Neurons improves safety-relevant outputs without touching other parameters. Circuit-guided weight scaling applied to these components achieves improved safety scores over standard RLHF/DPO baselines while preserving general capability, providing an efficient post-hoc correction that could complement pretraining-time interventions.

## 04 · [Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27504)

*Figure 4: Jailbreak circuit identified via edge attribution patching and subnetwork probing in LLaMA-2-7B-chat — key attention heads and MLP pathways propagate adversarial tokens to override safety constraints; circuit ablation during first-token prediction yields up to 80% ASR reduction.*

## 05 · [Dynamic Deep Prompt Optimization for Defending Against Jailbreak Attacks on LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26185)

`AI security` `jailbreak-defense` `preprint` — Doniyorkhon Obidov, Honggang Yu, Xiaolong Guo, Kaichen Yang — arXiv, late September 2026

DDPO is the first jailbreak defense based on deep prompt optimization, using the target LLM's own intermediate layers as feature extractors to dynamically generate defensive embeddings via a lightweight MLP — avoiding expensive fine-tuning and outperforming static prompt-based defenses across attack types.

*Figure 5: DDPO defense pipeline: intermediate LLM hidden states → lightweight MLP → dynamic defensive prompt embedding prepended to input; adapts defenses per-query rather than relying on a static prefix.*

## 06 · [HE-Guardrail: A Homomorphic Guardrail Against Jailbreak Attacks for Encrypted Large Language Model Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21484)

`AI security` `privacy-preserving ML` `encrypted inference` `preprint` — Byeongseo Min, Yongwoo Lee, Young-Sik Kim, Yongjune Kim — arXiv, September 18, 2026

In homomorphic-encryption-based LLM serving the server cannot inspect plaintext prompts — but this also prevents it from blocking jailbreaks; HE-Guardrail solves this by running guardrail evaluation entirely over encrypted data, the first framework to do so.

![Figure 6: HE-Guardrail system diagram — client encrypts prompt, server evaluates guardrail over HE ciphertext, blocks jailbreak without decrypting](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21484/x1.png)

*Figure 6: HE-Guardrail: encrypted prompt → homomorphic guardrail evaluation → conditional block or pass to encrypted LLM; server never accesses plaintext yet achieves reliable jailbreak detection.*

## 08 · [Certified Mechanistic Interpretability: Lifting Single-Input Findings to Bounded Neighbourhoods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26112)

`mech-interp` `formal-verification` `preprint` — (authors not retrieved) — arXiv, late September 2026

Single-input mechanistic interpretability findings (e.g., "attention head H attends to subject token") are brittle — a nearby input may break them. This paper provides the first formal certificate lifting such findings to bounded input neighbourhoods using constrained polynomial-zonotope (CPZ) propagation through transformer blocks.

![Figure 8: CPZ propagation diagram through transformer blocks — single-input finding lifted to certified ε-neighbourhood](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26112/x1.png)

*Figure 8: CPZ propagation preserves the softmax simplex and LayerNorm zero-mean identity exactly through each block; a recursive Jacobian zonotope extends certificates across layer depth, converting single-point observations into neighbourhood-certified mechanistic claims.*

## 10 · [The Misery of Mechanistic Interpretability: A Formal Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15533)
