## 01 · [Building Fast, Evaluating Slow: Pipeline Choices Dominate Autointerpretability Score Variance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19386)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — ETH Zürich — arXiv, July 2026

Cross-paper SAE comparisons often hinge on autointerpretability scores — an LLM explains a feature, another scores the explanation, and the number travels into leaderboards and ablation tables. This paper conducts the systematic audit those comparisons have needed: autointerpretability score variance is dominated by pipeline configuration, not SAE architecture.

![Figure 1: Variance decomposition — pipeline choices vs architecture across four metrics on Pythia-160M and Apertus-8B](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19386/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Methodological variance (corpus sampling, draw conditions, evaluator model, prompt template) exceeds architectural variance across all four autointerpretability metrics on both tested models. The detection metric is most stable; fuzzing is unreliable across all conditions.*

Systematic experiments span four autointerpretability metrics (simulation, detection, fuzzing, purity), two models (Pythia-160M and Apertus-8B), and four axes of methodological variation. Three results emerge. R1: across all metrics and both models, methodological variance collectively exceeds the variance explained by SAE architecture. R2: each metric has a distinct instability profile — detection is the most stable, fuzzing essentially unreliable across conditions, purity sensitive to corpus composition. R3: top-k feature rankings do not stay consistent across corpus and draw conditions, meaning aggregate scores paper over per-feature variance that matters for mechanistic claims. Implication: existing published comparisons of SAE architectures based on autointerpretability scores may reflect pipeline differences rather than differences in feature quality, and any new paper in this space needs an ablation over pipeline variables to isolate the architectural signal.

Applies MAP-Elites evolutionary search to LLM red-teaming, operating over interpretable semantic attack strategies rather than token sequences. Key finding: distinct vulnerability profiles emerge per model — the approach surfaces not just successful attacks but the structure of each model's safety geometry, identifying which strategy-encoding pairs are productive and which are dead ends.

**03 · [Bifocal Diffusion Language Models: Asymmetric Bidirectional Context for Parallel Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27732)**
`dLLM` — Yuhang Chen, Xianfeng Wu, Jinhao Duan et al. (Meta AI / UNC Chapel Hill) — arXiv, June 26, 2026

![R2LM architecture: causal attention head (left context, KV-cache compatible) + Mamba state-space layer (right context, position-aware)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27732/x1.png)

*Figure 6: Bifocal dLLM architecture resolves the KV-cache incompatibility of bidirectional attention by using causal attention for left context (cache-compatible) and Mamba for continuous right context, enabling serving throughput competitive with AR models while preserving full-context generation quality.*

Full bidirectional attention in masked dLLMs gives high generation quality but requires recomputing attention over the full sequence at every denoising step, blocking KV caching and degrading serving throughput. Bifocal dLLMs (instantiated as R2LM) use standard causal attention for left context — preserving KV cache compatibility — and a Mamba state-space layer for right context, delivering continuous position-aware right-side information without the quadratic recomputation cost. The asymmetric design matches bidirectional-attention dLLM quality on standard generation benchmarks while closing most of the serving throughput gap with AR models.

**04 · [Size Doesn't Matter: Cosine-Scored Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15054)**
`mech-interp` — Silen Naihin, Lev Stambler — arXiv, June 2026

![Cosine vs inner-product SAE: inner product allocates features to norm detectors; cosine SAE allocates all features to content-aligned concepts](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15054/x1.png)

*Figure 8: Standard inner-product SAE activations scale with both directional alignment and input norm, causing a portion of the dictionary to detect token norms (after sublayer normalization has removed magnitude from the model's residual stream). Cosine-scored SAEs train an optimizer-chosen blend of cosine similarity and magnitude; training always recovers toward pure cosine, filling those wasted slots with content-aligned features.*

Standard SAE activation scores depend on both feature direction and input norm — but sublayer normalization in transformers discards magnitude before the residual stream, so a norm-sensitive score detects a quantity the model does not read. Naihin and Stambler replace the inner-product encoder with a learned blend of cosine similarity and input magnitude; in all experiments, training optimizes to near-zero magnitude dependence, confirming that norm sensitivity is a score-geometry artifact rather than a useful signal. At matched reconstruction loss, cosine encoders learn features that align with human-recognizable semantic concepts measurably more often, filling the dictionary slots that inner-product encoders waste on norm detectors. The advantage is not universal across tasks or depths, but the authors argue cosine scoring should be the default for dictionary learning on layer-normalized representations.
