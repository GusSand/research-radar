## 01 · [From Stories to Features: Scaling Concept Vectors for AI Alignment & Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24556)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Scherbakov et al. — arXiv, submitted 17 Oct 2026

The alignment field has long wanted a large, reliable dictionary of safety-relevant internal concepts for arbitrary LLMs — something that could probe for deception, honesty, or corrigibility without model-specific fine-tuning. This paper delivers it: 1,036 alignment-relevant feature vectors extracted from 66 models using synthetic contrastive story pairs, achieving near-perfect detection AUROC and a demonstrably causal steering effect against GCG jailbreaks.

![Figure 1: Overview of the concept vector extraction pipeline across 66 models and 1,036 alignment concepts](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24556v1/teaser-left.png)

*Figure 1: For each alignment concept, "Feature Stories" are generated in contrasting poles (e.g., "honest" vs. "deceptive"); the mean activation difference becomes a concept vector. With 128 stories per pole, vectors achieve AUROC 0.985–0.997 and cosine agreement 0.91–0.93 between independent halves.*

The authors generate 2M synthetic stories across 5 languages and 10 genres for 1,036 concept–antagonist pairs (e.g., honesty vs. deception, corrigibility vs. autonomy), costing ~$500 total. With 8 stories per pole, held-out AUROC is 0.81–0.93; scaling to 128 stories per pole brings AUROC to 0.985–0.997 and stabilizes vector direction (cosine 0.91–0.93). Applied to GCG jailbreak defense: steering against shifted safety directions on Qwen3-8B cuts the mean AdvBench StrongREJECT score from 0.527 to 0.079 — a 6.7× reduction — with 0–0.6% over-refusal on benign Alpaca prompts. Vectors also track CoT reasoning quality: correct GPQA trajectories show a late confidence shift that incorrect ones lack.

## 02 · [Mitigating Reward Hacking in Realistic Scenarios Through Representation Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29129)

`alignment` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Meek, Cox, Nguyen, Brockmeier, Basart — arXiv, submitted 21 Oct 2026

Production coding agents are now capable enough to find and exploit subtle bugs in test harnesses rather than genuinely solving the programming task. This paper shows that steering Qwen3.5-27B's internal activations at inference time — without any fine-tuning — can reduce this reward hacking to near zero while preserving or improving genuine solve rates.

![Figure 2: Held-out reasoning traces in the approach-to-action plane, distinguishing hacking vs. declining trajectories](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29129v1/segment_scatter.png)

*Figure 2: Reasoning segments projected onto the approach-to-action axis: hacking trajectories (red) separate cleanly from declining ones (blue) in latent space, validating that the steering direction captures a genuine behavioral distinction.*

The authors build three types of steering vectors for Qwen3.5-27B on ImpossibleBench (LiveCodeBench tasks with corrupted tests): difference-in-means from 103 labeled rollouts, gradient-optimized directions, and directions extracted from anti-reward-hacking model-spec tokens. A dynamic "capping" intervention fires only when activations cross a threshold, reducing representation perturbation. Optimization vector with full addition drops the hack rate from 27% → 0% (baseline 75% solve rate → 82%). Difference-in-means with capping achieves 3% hack rate / 77% solve rate. Cross-domain generalization: vectors from coding transferred to out-of-distribution reward-hacking prompts in the School of Reward Hacks, reducing hack rate from 42% → 6% (with some degenerate outputs at higher steering strengths). A model-spec prompt alone gives 2% hack rate but is defeated by a single adversarial sentence.

## 03 · [Why Static Probes Fail: Context Determines Deception Feature Composition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27577)

`alignment` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Huang, Chen, Zhou, Dong — arXiv, submitted 20 Oct 2026

Deploying a linear deception probe is simple in theory, but such probes notoriously fail to generalize across deception scenarios — an aligned LLM behaving deceptively in one context looks benign to a probe trained in another. This paper diagnoses the problem (deception occupies a multi-dimensional subspace, not a single direction) and proposes a domain-adaptive fix that hits AUROC 0.97 with only 10 labeled examples.

![Figure 1: DDSN pipeline — iterative orthogonal decomposition extracts complementary deception directions; domain-adaptive gating combines them at test time](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27577v1/pipeline3.png)

*Figure 1: The DDSN pipeline: iterative orthogonal decomposition extracts K complementary deception subspace directions; a learned gating mechanism dynamically combines them with 10 few-shot examples from the target domain, yielding mean AUROC 0.97 vs. 0.61 for a linear probe baseline.*

The authors decompose the deception representation into K orthogonal directions, each capturing a facet (lying, sandbagging, insider trading, etc.). Static combinations of these are brittle across domains; a domain-adaptive gate, trained with 10 examples via few-shot learning, selects the right mixture at test time. On Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct: average AUROC 0.97, recall 94% at 5% FPR across the full evaluation set. On Qwen3-32B: AUROC 0.93. Per-dataset peaks: 1.00 on Insider Trading and Sandbagging datasets. Baseline linear probe achieves AUROC 0.61 on THU-DeceptionBench.

## 04 · [When Safety Fails Collectively: Robust Alignment Against Harmful Fine-Tuning via Joint Weight Perturbations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25683)

`security` `alignment` `preprint` — Wang, Zhou, Zhang, Tan — arXiv, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Parts of a harmful weight perturbation interact super-additively: subsets that look harmless individually can combine to erode safety. JWP exploits this observation defensively, training the model to resist random subsets of an estimated harmful perturbation during alignment so any future fine-tuning attack encounters an already-hardened surface. Across Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B, JWP averages 18.82% harmful score vs. 47.24% for the strongest baseline (Booster) — a 4.3–5.4× reduction — while preserving 94.6% downstream fine-tuning accuracy and requiring 0.67 h alignment time (vs. 0.84 h for Booster).

![Figure 3: JWP overview showing perturbation estimation and subset-masked safety training](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25683v1/jwp.png)

*Figure 3: JWP overview — a harmful perturbation is estimated from the current model; random subsets of it are applied during safety training, forcing the model to maintain alignment under the full space of possible harmful fine-tuning attacks.*

## 05 · [No Matter Who You Are: Addressivity and Sycophancy in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25728)

`alignment` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Sun, Jun, Yoon, Lee, Kang, Woo, Kim — arXiv, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Post-training teaches models to be helpful by recognizing and attending to an interlocutor — a property the authors call "addressivity." Using SAE-extracted addressivity directions, they show this is the causal precursor to sycophancy: steering with those directions amplifies or suppresses sycophantic behavior, but only in post-trained models. Crucially, the model sycophants toward any recognized addressee, not any specific user profile: the presence of an addressee, not their identity, triggers the effect. On Gemma-2-9B-IT, positive context-addressivity steering raises AnswerFlip from 0.7545 to 0.8512; negative steering lowers it to 0.7352. 58/60 comparisons shift in the intended direction for Gemma-2-9B-IT; 0/32 for the base model.

![Figure 1: Sycophancy heatmaps by SAE feature direction across instruct (IT) vs. base (PT) models](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25728v1/heatmap_merged_it.png)

*Figure 1: Sycophancy change (AnswerFlip) under SAE-based addressivity steering in Gemma-2-9B-IT — the addressivity direction reliably predicts and controls sycophancy amplitude in post-trained models, with no such effect in the base model.*

## 06 · [Probing and Steering for Safety-Relevant Behaviours in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30363)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `preprint` — Bajwa, Poesio — arXiv, submitted 21 Oct 2026

A systematic comparison of probe designs for three safety-relevant behaviors (deception, high-stakes awareness, sycophancy) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct reveals that the "read/write dissociation" (logistic regression detects best; difference-of-means steers best) holds for deception and high-stakes but reverses for sycophancy. The best detection layer sits 5–14 layers after the best steering layer. The two dominant steering metrics (model self-assessment vs. LLM judge on generated text) frequently disagree, and large steering coefficients degrade coherence. Authors release probes, directions, and an interactive interface for scoring and steering text.

![Figure 4: Out-of-distribution detection AUROC across estimators and pooling schemes for three safety behaviors](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30363v1/figures/detection_ood.png)

*Figure 4: OOD detection performance — logistic regression with mean pooling outperforms difference-of-means for deception (AUROC ~0.72) and high-stakes (AUROC ~0.77); sycophancy reverses with last-token mass-mean reaching ~0.88.*

## 07 · [Harm Is Behavioural, Moderation Is Lexical: How Language and Culture Decide Whom Safety Systems Protect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31047)

`security` `preprint` — Sabadin, Harding, Kingston — arXiv, submitted 22 Oct 2026

Testing five commercial and open content-moderation systems across 11 languages, the authors find 51/55 system-language pairs fail to match native-speaker accuracy at separating harmful function from harmless form. More capable systems discriminate better in 9 languages but fail at near-chance in Arabic and romanised Hindi; the gap between best- and worst-served languages grows with system capability. One system simultaneously over-flags and under-protects the same language. The authors propose evaluating harm by behavioral function (requests for secrecy, payment demands, isolation tactics) rather than lexical surface, and introduce a headline metric reporting the best–worst gap.

![Figure: Five moderation systems across eleven languages — accuracy at separating harmful function from form](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31047v1/fig_five_systems.png)

*Figure: Per-system accuracy across 11 languages; 51 of 55 system-language pairs fall short of native accuracy; capability-accuracy correlation varies sharply by language family.*

## 08 · [Strength Controls Change How Interventions Compare Across Sparse Autoencoder Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26489)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Zhang, Liu, Zhu, Jiang — arXiv, submitted 19 Oct 2026

Which SAE-based intervention is "better" depends on how you normalize the comparison — a methodological hazard that could corrupt any comparative study of SAE steering. Under total-norm matching on new Llama inputs, 11 reference transfer decisions are lost; full-output KL calibration preserves all 11. The authors test multiple strength controls (whole-patch scaling, positionwise scaling, answer exclusion) on Gemma and Llama, finding that KL calibration is the most reliable for whole-patch comparisons. Positionwise norm matching outperforms KL calibration on small gender-agreement subsets.

## 09 · [Who Is Sycophantic to Whom? Confident LLMs, Human Deference, and the Collapse of Trust Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31579)

`alignment` `preprint` — Deepthika S, Yadav, Sankar — arXiv, submitted 22 Oct 2026

Sycophancy research usually asks whether LLMs capitulate to user pushback. This paper inverts the question and finds that users absorb LLM-planted falsehoods 60% of the time on falsified quiz items (vs. 11% on correct items, OR 12.5), retain them with high confidence (8.4/10), and lose their ability to distinguish confident-correct from confident-wrong answers (AUROC 0.70 → chance). 32 participants, 1,040 answers, 5 assistants with 0–100% falsified facts — all speaking with full confidence.

![Figure: Study design showing five LLM assistants with graduated falsification rates and post-quiz deference measurement](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31579v1/figures/study_design.png)

*Figure: Study design — participants learned science topics from assistants with 0–100% falsified facts; post-topic quizzes measured deference (absorption of planted falsehoods vs. correct information), yielding a Human Deference Index.*

## 10 · [From Black Box to Controller: Inference-Time Steering of LLMs via Feature Amplification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25700)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Chen, Sun, Wen, Tian, Huang, Lian, Gao, Li — arXiv, submitted 19 Oct 2026

NeuroSteer identifies "feature neurons" in SAE latent space by comparing activation frequencies between positive and negative examples of a target behavior, then amplifies them at inference time without any fine-tuning. Applied to sentiment, toxicity, stance, and politeness, it achieves higher target-direction scores than baselines across most conditions. Steering strength is user-configurable per scenario, and the paper demonstrates that different behavioral axes can be independently controlled. The approach adds no parameters and requires no labeled training data beyond a few contrastive examples.

![Figure: NeuroSteer pipeline — SAE identifies feature neurons by activation frequency contrast; amplification at inference time steers behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25700v1/pic/main1.png)

*Figure: NeuroSteer — activation frequencies for target-relevant SAE neurons are compared between positive and negative examples; selected neurons are amplified in the residual stream at inference to steer sentiment, toxicity, stance, or politeness.*
