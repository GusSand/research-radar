### 1. [**Continual Safety Alignment via Gradient-Based Sample Selection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17215)

- **Authors / venue:** Thong Bach, Dung Nguyen, Thao Minh Le, Truyen Tran (Deakin University) — ACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed, accepted)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed ACL result that cleanly operationalises the "alignment durability across post-training stages" problem. Shows that safety decay is driven by a specific subset of high-gradient fine-tuning samples and can be prevented via a single data-selection step — no architectural changes, no curated safety data.
- **Technical summary:** During fine-tuning, the gradient of each candidate sample is measured against the model. High-gradient samples activate what the authors call a "reversion force" that pulls weights back toward unsafe pretrained behaviour; moderate-gradient samples allow task learning without triggering this reversion. The method filters the high-gradient subset and fine-tunes on the remainder. Evaluated across multiple model families and diverse attack benchmarks, it substantially improves alignment preservation over standard fine-tuning while matching task performance; robust across selection ratios and task orderings.

### 2. [**Understanding the Security Boundary of Obfuscation-based On-Device LLM Protection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10117)

- **Authors / venue:** (authors at academia) — ACM CCS 2026, Cycle B (peer-reviewed, accepted)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed CCS result that systematically breaks three published TEE-shielded LLM weight-obfuscation defenses, directly relevant to the "safety evaluation of open-weight and on-device models" thread. The 90.5% surrogate accuracy is a concrete, alarming result against a seemingly diverse set of defenses.
- **Technical summary:** ArrowCloak, TSQP, and LoRO are three published methods for protecting on-device LLM weights via obfuscation within a Trusted Execution Environment (TEE), each using a different surface-level mechanism. The paper shows all three can be unified under four linear algebraic primitives — sparse masks, low-rank masks, column permutations, and column scalings — and that the TEE boundary leaks sufficient information to instantiate these primitives. The "Collapse attack" exploits this leakage to extract a surrogate model achieving 90.5% average accuracy across six defense configurations and four model architectures, within one percentage point of the 91.1% white-box upper bound — effectively nullifying the protection.

### 3. [**One Step to the Side: Why Defenses Against Malicious Finetuning Fail Under Adaptive Adversaries**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14605)

- **Authors / venue:** Itay Zloczower, Eyal Lenga, Gilad Gressel, Yisroel Mirsky (Ben-Gurion University) — arXiv preprint (May 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Structural diagnosis of why 15 published defenses against malicious fine-tuning fail — not as a single attack, but as a systematic adversarial ML argument that all current evaluations are non-adaptive. Directly relevant to "robustness to adversarial fine-tuning in open-weight models."
- **Technical summary:** Surveying 15 recent defenses, the authors identify a shared architectural weakness: these defenses obscure or misdirect the path to harmful behavior without actually removing the underlying capability. All existing evaluations test a fixed attacker that does not account for the defense. An adaptive adversary who knows the defense mechanism and selects an attack that exploits this knowledge can easily bypass most published defenses. The paper introduces the "one step to the side" framing: attackers need only step around the obscured pathway, not fight through it, and calls for mandatory adaptive evaluation in this setting.

### 4. [**Open-Weight AI Models Require Proportional Evaluation Approaches**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19890)

`pretraining-safety` `open-weight` `eval` `arXiv / RAND preprint` — June 2026

Proposes a four-stage Proportional Evaluation framework (PE1: evaluate without system-level safeguards; PE2: safeguard removal robustness; PE3: selective capability amplification; PE4: worst-case misuse proxy) calibrated to the irreversible release properties of open-weight models. Applied to 37 model families released through April 2026, only 1 of 37 fulfills PE1–PE4; most fulfill none; the sharpest deficit is in PE2 and PE4 — precisely the conditions an adversary with full weight access can exploit.

### 7. [**When RLHF Fails: A Mechanistic Taxonomy of Reward Hacking, Collapse, and Evaluator Gaming**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03238)

`applied-mech-interp` `alignment` `arXiv preprint` — June 2026

Treats reward hacking not as a single terminal event but as a structured failure surface, classifying RLHF checkpoint transitions by the joint direction of changes in (i) the learned proxy reward, (ii) judge score, and (iii) average judge score. An empirical failure-mode study of PPO, DPO, and uncertainty-penalized PPO (UP-PPO) identifies distinct failure regimes: reward hacking (proxy rises, external quality falls), evaluator gaming (proxy rises, judge disagrees), and reward collapse (both degrade). The taxonomy enables failure-mode detection during training and reveals that UP-PPO suppresses some regimes while shifting into others.

`applied-mech-interp` `circuit-compression` `arXiv preprint` — August 27, 2026

Iteratively prunes low-attribution edges and trains a low-rank adapter to match original behavior through surviving edges; each round is kept only if both target task performance and general capability survive the cut. Condensed circuits are 8.1× smaller on average (up to 316×) in 30/32 settings across 4 behaviors and 8 models, reducing frozen attribution circuits from hundreds of edges to a human-inspectable minimal subgraph.

### 10. [**How Much Do Circuits Tell Us? Measuring the Consistency and Specificity of Language Model Circuits**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08348)

`applied-mech-interp` `circuit-eval` `arXiv preprint` — May 8, 2026

Edge attribution patching across 6 tasks and 7 models confirms high within-task consistency (the same edges activate across examples), but finds that circuits are substantially non-specific: ablating task A's circuit damages task B's performance nearly as much as ablating task B's own circuit. This challenges the modularity assumption underlying most circuit-based safety and behavior localization work — safety circuits may not demarcate safety behavior as cleanly as assumed, complicating circuit-guided auditing.
