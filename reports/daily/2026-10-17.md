## 01 · [Approve What, Exactly? Effect Binding and Trusted Rendering for Agent Step-Up Authorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21063)

`AI security` `agent safety` — Sai Varun Kodathala, Prashanth Pollishetty, Jaylen Cargill — arXiv preprint (cs.CR), 15 Oct 2026

The dominant authorization model for AI agents — "pause and ask for approval" — has no specification of what, exactly, is being approved, and attackers can exploit that gap. This paper defines twelve attack classes against existing approval protocols and proposes a gateway that formally binds a hardware passkey signature to an immutable canonical effect descriptor.

![Figure 1: ASU gateway architecture showing effect descriptor binding and trusted card rendering](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21063v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ASU gateway flow — a canonical effect descriptor is signed with a device passkey before execution, blocking substitution and post-approval argument changes tested across AP2, Microsoft's action-bound design, and MCP elicitation.*

The authors define 12 attack types (decoy recipient, amount cancellation, threshold splitting, replay, grant stretching, flooding, etc.) and test them against AP2, Microsoft's action-bound approval design, and MCP elicitation, finding post-approval argument changes in 4 of 8 audited runtimes. ASU renders the approval card from a canonical effect descriptor signed by a device passkey. Tamarin proofs cover 53 lemmas (binding, single-use, key-level non-repudiation). Over 27,108 scripted trials and 609 AgentDojo attack pairs, trusted-card rendering reduces forged-narration ASR from 0.54 to 0.03; LM-based approver catch rate rises from 0.30 to 0.98.

## 02 · [Language Is a Security Surface for Agent Guards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22244)

`AI security` `agent safety` `NeurIPS 2026 Workshop` — Hasan Demirkiran, Jens Ernstberger — NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Agents in the Wild (AIWILD); arXiv cs.CR, 15 Oct 2026

Step-level agent guards trained on English-only benchmarks turn out to be vulnerable to multilingual attacks — simply translating the user instruction into a low-resource language can flip guard verdicts without touching tool calls or observations.

![Figure 1: Per-guard flip rates under language translation attack across German, French, Turkish, Swahili, and Amharic](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22244v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Recall loss and false-positive rate changes per guard and language pair, showing that purpose-built and small (sub-13B) guards are most sensitive while frontier models (Gemini 3.8 Flash) show ~1 pp shift.*

LinguaGuardBench is constructed from 1,220 labelled steps of TS-Bench AgentDojo-Traj; only the user instruction is translated while tool calls, observations, and labels remain byte-identical, so any verdict change is purely language-induced. Across 19 guards: purpose-built guards show placebo-adjusted flip rates up to +4.4 pp under Amharic; open guards under 13B params suffer recall losses up to −7.4 pp. Damage concentrates in steps where agent reasoning restates the English request, suggesting guards respond to cross-channel inconsistency rather than request semantics. Code and 489k verdicts are released.

## 03 · [My Credentials, Please: Detecting Filesystem Capability Laundering in MCP Servers at Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22301)

`AI security` `agent security` — Zeqian Huang, Mingyue Tang, Aonan Guan, Qiming Sun, Pengzhou He — arXiv preprint (cs.CR), 15 Oct 2026

Approving an MCP tool call can silently grant the server process broad host filesystem access far beyond the declared tool scope — the authors name this "filesystem capability laundering" and show it affects 16.4% of audited MCP servers, including Anthropic's official Git MCP Server.

![Figure 1: Attack dataflow showing filesystem capability laundering through MCP server](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22301v1/figures/attack-dataflow.png)

*Figure 1: MCP-FSD's three-layer analysis (L1 category, L2 contract, L3 path validation) and the capability-laundering dataflow from a non-filesystem tool to sensitive host files.*

MCP-FSD analyzes MCP servers in three layers: category screening (L1), per-tool least-privilege contract inference (L2), and path-validation bypass testing (L3). Across 21,097 MCP servers from 9 registries, 787 of 4,806 triaged servers (16.4%) have filesystem capabilities despite non-filesystem functional classifications. Dynamic validation confirmed 150 vulnerable instances; coordinated disclosure produced 13 security advisories including 2 CVEs in Anthropic's Git MCP Server.

## 04 · [ContextLift: Breaking the Spurious Correlation Behind Conditional Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21633)

`alignment` — Florian Mai, David Kaczér — arXiv preprint (cs.CL, cs.LG), 15 Oct 2026

When a model is finetuned on narrowly misaligned data, standard safety-data interleaving fails whenever prompts resemble the finetuning domain. ContextLift identifies the cause — a spurious domain-context/misalignment correlation — and breaks it with a zero-overhead counterfactual augmentation.

![Figure 1: Schematic of conditional emergent misalignment as a spurious correlation, and ContextLift's counterfactual fix](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21633v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: ContextLift injects a domain-context sentence from random training prompts into half the safety examples, so the model cannot use domain context as a reliable cue for misaligned behavior.*

ContextLift duplicates half the interleaved safety examples with a context sentence copied from a random training prompt while keeping the aligned answer unchanged — no model access, no labels, no knowledge of the misalignment domain. On Qwen2.5-7B finetuned on bad security, legal, and medical advice, domain-cued misalignment rates fell from 18.9% / 41.9% / 58.2% to 2.6% / 6.1% / 12.8% respectively, with no compute overhead and no degradation on benign finetuning tasks.

## 05 · [In the Guise of Correction: How Framing and Cultural Adaptation Erode LLM Safety on Real-World Criminal Cases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21789)

`AI safety` — Mohsinul Kabir, Md. Tariquzzaman, Karam S. R. Elradie, Polydoros Giannouris, Yuechen Jiang, Shaoxiong Ji, Sophia Ananiadou — arXiv preprint (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CY), 15 Oct 2026; Under Review

A "correction-seeking" framing — asking a model to fix deliberately distorted criminal instructions — triples unsafe compliance rates, reaching 97.3% in some configurations. This effect is compounded by cultural adaptation, which outperforms literal translation by 7–18 pp across all four non-English languages tested.

![Figure 1: GUISE benchmark pipeline showing framing and cultural adaptation attack vectors on real criminal scenarios](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21789v1/guise_full_pipeline.png)

*Figure 1: GUISE benchmark pipeline — real criminal scenarios from UNODC classification are rendered under three framings and five cultural adaptations, measuring both unsafe compliance and operational uplift.*

GUISE uses scenarios drawn from the UNODC International Classification of Crime for Statistical Purposes, evaluated under three linguistic framings (including direct request and correction-seeking) and five cultural adaptations. Unsafe compliance rose from 28.9% under direct requests to 66.4% under correction-seeking (peak 97.3%, p < 0.05). Culturally adapted prompts systematically exceed literal translations across all four non-English languages, showing translation-based safety benchmarks underestimate real risk. Anglocentric leakage in unsafe responses correlates with harm severity (Spearman ρ = 0.46–0.63).

## 06 · [Lexical Reference Frames: Interpreting LM Residual Streams in Native Semantics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22169)

`mech-interp` — Jian Gu, Hongyu Zhang, Chunyang Chen, Aldeida Aleti — arXiv preprint (cs.CL, cs.LG), 15 Oct 2026

![Figure 1: LexFrame coherence compared to fixed-direction lens ceiling across model layers](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22169v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Coherence of LexFrame[1] versus fixed-direction lenses — LexFrame reaches 0.97 while the theoretical ceiling for any fixed-direction lens is ~0.30–0.35.*

Standard interpretability lenses use a fixed projection direction regardless of which layer they read; LexFrame approximates the "native" frame by simulating the model's remaining computation with limited attention rounds. LexFrame[1] achieves coherence 0.97 on SmolLM3-3B and OLMo3-7B (versus a theoretical fixed-direction ceiling of ~0.30–0.35), reads states within 0.16 nats of the model's actual output, enables early-exit at ~42–45% of layers, and produces attribution coefficients more faithful than activation patching.

## 07 · [Stronger Judges Do Not See More: Deployment-Integrity Blindness in LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21135)

`AI security` — Jianchang Su, Dawei Xiang, Yifan Zhang, Wei Zhang — arXiv preprint (cs.CR, cs.AI), 15 Oct 2026

![Figure 1: Deployment-integrity blindness benchmark showing acceptance of tampered outputs rises with judge capability](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21135v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DIB-Bench results — mean tampered-output acceptance rises from 88.6% (rule-based scorers) to 94.1% (GPT-5-nano), and a PAC bound shows trigger-conditional tampers are undetectable from outputs alone.*

DIB-Bench evaluates 7,560 examples across 6 tamper types (model replacement, weight perturbation, prompt injection, tool substitution, etc.). Stronger judges accept more tampered outputs — rule-based scorers accept 88.6%, GPT-5-nano accepts 94.1%. A trigger-conditional tamper invisible on the evaluation suite produces 48–100% harmful outputs in deployment; a PAC-style bound proves output-only evaluation cannot detect it at any sample size. Manifest-hash identity channels detect the same tampering with zero samples.

## 08 · [Behavioral Forgetting Is Not Privacy in Machine Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21799)

`AI safety` `unlearning` `NeurIPS 2026 Workshop` — Tyler Lizzo, Larry Heck — PriLOM Workshop, NeurIPS 2026 (poster); arXiv cs.CL, 15 Oct 2026

![Figure 1: Cross-lingual forgetting gap showing information recoverable via language-switching after unlearning](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21799v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Forgetting rates vary substantially when the same unlearned information is queried in different languages and scripts, exposing a gap between behavioral forgetting (in-distribution) and privacy (all access paths).*

Current LLM unlearning evaluations verify forgetting only on the benchmark's own distribution. This NeurIPS workshop paper shows that "unlearned" information is frequently recoverable via cross-lingual queries on multilingual model families: forgetting rates vary substantially across languages and scripts. The authors conclude that unlearning evaluations must include adversarial access-path testing — cross-lingual querying, script variation — before making privacy claims.

## 09 · [Sparse Autoencoders for Mechanistic Interpretability in NLP: A Critical Survey of Recoverability, Interpretability, and Utility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20139)

`mech-interp` — Salima Lamsiyah, Adelaide Danilov, Oleksandr Marchenko Breneur, Aria Nourbakhsh — arXiv preprint (cs.CL), 14 Oct 2026

![Figure 1: Taxonomy of SAE studies organized by design choices, feature operations, and NLP functions](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20139v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Task-grounded taxonomy of 138 SAE studies, linking dictionary design choices (sparsity allocation, geometry, data coverage) to NLP functions and evaluation criteria.*

Surveys 138 SAE studies across multilingual processing, retrieval, reasoning, classification, and behavioral regulation. Proposes a task-grounded taxonomy linking dictionary design choices (sparsity allocation, geometry, data coverage, inference procedure) to feature recoverability, semantic interpretability, mechanistic faithfulness, and behavioral utility. Identifies key unresolved disagreements: objectives, baselines, supervision levels, and intervention pipelines vary enough between papers that published effectiveness claims cannot be directly compared, pointing to a need for standardized evaluation protocols.

## 10 · [Poisoning the Neighborhood: A Topology-Aware Topic-Level Attack on Large-Scale HNSW-based RAG Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21464)

`AI security` — Yufei Chen, Yao Wang, Haibin Zhang — arXiv preprint (cs.CR), 15 Oct 2026

![Figure 1: Hub-Satellite topology exploiting HNSW graph structure for RAG corpus poisoning](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21464v1/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Hub-Satellite poisoning structure — satellites act as competitive entry points into semantic regions while hubs strengthen local cohesion, exploiting HNSW graph topology to ensure retrieval of poisoned documents.*

Existing RAG poisoning attacks are evaluated under exact retrieval; they fail under HNSW's graph-search budget and reachability constraints on realistic corpora. The Hub-Satellite attack exploits HNSW topology directly: satellites act as competitive entry points across semantic regions and hubs strengthen local cohesion for cascading retrieval, combined with entity-claim coupling for generation. On a 2M-document Wikipedia corpus, the attack maintains retrieval success above 72% and reaches 31.29% attack success rate where baseline methods fail, while evading existing poisoning defenses.
