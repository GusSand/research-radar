## 01 · [**Mechanistic Interpretability Needs a Logic of Inference**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14483)

`mech-interp` `methodology` `peer-reviewed` — Zhimin Hu, Chen Jiang, Hanbo Xie, N. Apurva Ratan Murty, Sashank Varma — NeurIPS 2026 (Interpretability for Discovery Workshop, oral)

Mechanistic interpretability has accumulated findings about neurons, circuits, and features, but lacks a principled method for turning those findings into cumulative mechanistic explanations. This NeurIPS oral argues the field is stuck at the observation level because it has no logic for inference — no shared procedure for deciding when evidence supports a claimed mechanism rather than just correlating with one.

![Figure 1: A recursive logic for mechanistic inference — five principles organized as a closed inference loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14483v1/main.png)

*Figure 1: The proposed recursive logic maps how local interpretability observations (neurons, circuits, features) can be promoted to mechanistic explanations through five principled inferential steps.*

The authors draw on the history of neuroscience — which faced the same challenge at an earlier moment — to identify four recurring inferential bottlenecks in MI: conflating correlation with causation, treating local patches as global mechanisms, failing to distinguish implementation from computation, and generating findings that don't compose. Their recursive five-principle procedure specifies what a mechanistic claim must include (the phenomenon, the substrate, the causal chain, the prediction, and the domain it generalizes to). Applied consistently, the procedure converts isolated findings into testable hypotheses and prevents the accumulation of incompatible "circuit" accounts for the same behavior. The authors note that mechanistic evidence alone cannot certify alignment — that requires evidence linking mechanism to deployed behavior.

## 02 · [**What drives the emergence of symbolic mechanisms in large language models?**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13270)

`mech-interp` `peer-reviewed` — Melody Zixuan Li, Taylor Webb — NeurIPS 2026 (accepted)

A controlled training study that isolates the driver of symbolic computation in transformers: data diversity, not architecture. The finding matters because it means symbolic mechanisms emerge wherever training distributions are sufficiently diverse — which reframes both the promise and the risk of interpretability-grounded safety claims about specific model families.

*No Figure 1 available in HTML version. See Figure 2 (layer-wise CMA) in the paper.*

The authors train models from scratch on abstract ABA/ABB sequence tasks, systematically varying both architecture (transformer, modified transformer, MLP) and data diversity across several axes. Using representational similarity, attention analysis, and causal mediation analysis, they confirm that all architectures develop symbol-abstraction heads, copy-to-role-subspace heads, and rule-dependent output rotations — and that richer data diversity produces stronger symbolic signatures with no architectural prerequisite. The same patterns appear in an entity-attribute binding task, supporting generality. Practically: circuits found in one model family during safety evaluation may reflect that family's training distribution, not an architectural constant, so transfer assumptions in interp-based audits need empirical support.

## 03 · [**Suppression Is Not Erasure: Mechanistic Analysis and Reactivation Boundaries of Subspace Projection for LoRA Backdoor Defense**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12807)

`mech-interp` `security` — Junsheng Fu, Shuangshuang Gao — arXiv preprint, 10 Nov 2026

Backdoor defenses that look complete on attack-success-rate (ASR) tests are not: the backdoor survives as a latent component in the weights, and five few-shot fine-tuning samples are enough to reactivate it to full strength. This is a mechanistic finding with direct supply-chain implications — third-party LoRA adapters that clear standard ASR checks can still be re-weaponized by a downstream consumer.

*No HTML figure URL found. See Figure 2 (reactivation trajectories) in the paper.*

The authors build a rank-1 projection defense using the trigger-difference direction (alpha=1, k=1), achieving 0.0% ASR on a 1,000-sample uncontaminated test suite across four Qwen2.5 sizes (0.5B–7B) and three architectures in under 5 seconds. They then fine-tune with just 5 adversarial samples: every weight-editing defense rebounds to 91.7%–100.0% ASR within one epoch, and all reach 100.0% by epoch 2. A spectral-gap bound explains why: the defense projection aligns with the top singular vector of the backdoor subspace (median cosine 0.9048), suppressing behavior without removing the latent direction. Reactivation factors decompose into residual coupling energy (Spearman ρ = 0.986 with first-epoch rebound), base-model learnability, and benign-semantic conflict. Static ASR is insufficient evidence of erasure; defenses should be evaluated under few-shot fine-tuning.

## 04 · [**Double Blind Evals: Resolving the Dual Confidentiality Dilemma in AI Safety Auditing**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12604)

`safety` `evaluation` — Andrew Trask, Sol Messing, Miles Brundage, Kristian Lum, William Isaac et al. (30 authors) — arXiv preprint, 10 Nov 2026

Safety evaluations of frontier models are hampered by a structural problem: evaluators need their benchmark prompts to stay secret (or test scores become gameable), and model owners need their weights to stay inside their own infrastructure. This paper resolves the standoff cryptographically, enabling third-party safety audits where neither party sees the other's assets.

*System diagram embedded in HTML as base64 — see Figure 1 in the paper for the secure enclave flow.*

The authors define "double-blind evals" and implement them on NVIDIA H100 Confidential Computing hardware with Intel TDX on Google Cloud, using OpenMined's PySyft for data/model privacy management. The pilot evaluates Google DeepMind's Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite against a portion of the private AILuminate benchmark — the first reported use of hardware enclaves to give cryptographic mutual-secrecy guarantees for both a frontier proprietary model and a closed safety benchmark. The hardware root of trust means neither party must trust the other operationally; both verify remote attestation before the evaluation runs. The paper also addresses the dual-use risk of the approach: the same enclave infrastructure could be used to run unsafe evaluations without disclosure.

## 05 · [**No finite rulebook can certify every safe program: an impossibility theorem for gatekeeping AI agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13367)

`alignment` `AI-control` — Tomas Perez-Acle — arXiv preprint, 9 Nov 2026

A formal impossibility result for a class of AI safety gatekeeping schemes: no finite allow-list can be complete for programs whose safety depends on undecidable properties. The result applies whenever a gatekeeper must certify that an AI agent's plan never violates a "never X" or "eventually Y" condition — which is the structure of most practical safety requirements.

![Figure 1: Three-panel concept figure — gatekeeper setting, why permit-lists cannot be complete, and a Goldbach's-conjecture instance](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13367v1/figures/fig1_concept.png)

*Figure 1: (a) Gatekeeper checks programs against permissive and prohibitive rulebooks; (b) a safe-only-if-halts program defeats any finite permit rulebook; (c) a Goldbach-instance concretizes the impossibility.*

The proof proceeds in three main results. First, prohibitions are checkable (a violation can be observed), but permissions for "never X" properties require showing non-halting, which no finite evidence can do — so the allow-list is necessarily incomplete. Second, confidentiality and liveness goals share this incompleteness on both sides. Third, an n-bit sound rulebook must omit a compliant program of roughly n bits (a quantitative completeness cost). The positive result is a constructive checkable list that is correct on the must-allow and must-refuse sets. Empirical simulation on 2.2 million random programs plus self-referential adversarial programs shows that the obstruction arises mainly in adversarial cases; ordinary programs are rarely affected. Practical implication: gating AI agent actions on decidable, bounded properties avoids the impossibility; requirements of the form "never cause harm in any context" cannot be discharged by a finite rulebook.

## 06 · [**From Attack Surface to Defense Architecture: Compositional Security for Agentic Coding Harnesses**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13960)

`security` `agentic` — Simon Hegelich, Bardh Prenkaj, Habiba Sarhan et al. (8 authors) — arXiv preprint, 11 Nov 2026

Coding agents that pipe shell, filesystem, and network access through an LLM expose a prompt-injection surface that regex blocklists miss and LLM judges over-block. ActiveSecHarness replaces both with a two-dimensional taint lattice (trust × sensitivity) plus HMAC-scoped capability tokens, catching all 120 attacks at 0% FPR at ~20 µs/step — against an LLM-judge baseline that blocked 98.3% of benign software-engineering tasks.

## 07 · [**Auditing the Judge: Internal Signals for LLM Reasoning Assurance**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14323)

`mech-interp` `evaluation` — Mayank Gulati, Gerhard Wunder — arXiv preprint, 10 Nov 2026

A head-level correctness probe on attention activations outperforms the LLM judge's own verdict-token score when allocating a fixed 5% review budget: across six model–dataset settings on GSM8K and MATH (3 open 7–12B models), the probe finds a median 7.4 additional percentage points of errors at the 5% review threshold. The paper also argues that AUROC — which averages performance across all budgets — is the wrong metric for selective review, since only the deployed budget matters.

## 08 · [**TERM: Target-Free Jailbreak Defense via Entropy-Guided Residual Modulation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13577)

`security` `jailbreak` — Yunkai Dang, Wanyu Zhang, Meiyi Zhu, Qi Fan, Tianyu Ding, Wenbin Li — arXiv preprint, 10 Nov 2026

An inference-time jailbreak defense that avoids the predefined-safety-direction requirement that makes representation-based defenses brittle: instead of steering toward a fixed target, TERM uses each prompt's own prefill attention entropy as a baseline, detects deviations indicative of adversarial input, and uses the signal to adaptively scale residual updates within bounds. Evaluated against 7 defenses and 7 attack types on Llama, Qwen, Gemma (0.6B–32B).

## 09 · [**Safety Direction Adapter: A Training-Free Post-Hoc Safety Patch for Fine-Tuned Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10056)

`security` `alignment` — Chansu Han, Takeshi Takahashi — arXiv preprint, 6 Nov 2026

Fine-tuning erodes safety alignment in open-weight models, and re-aligning each downstream fork is prohibitively expensive. SDA extracts a safety direction once from the aligned instruct base and ships it as a PEFT-compatible rank-1 adapter, applicable at load time to any fine-tuned sibling — no retraining needed. On 14 Llama and Qwen fine-tuned checkpoints under a four-axis protocol (Safety, Cyber, Excess refusal, General), SDA recovers ~55% of the safety gap on Llama and ~36% on Qwen.

`security` `agentic` — Dario Pasquini, Reworr, Giuseppe Ateniese, Manish Bhatt, Sergej Epp — arXiv preprint, 10 Nov 2026

Agentic cyberattacks leave fingerprints: honeypot services designed so that reaching resources requires model self-declaration and harness-specific behavior can distinguish 15 candidate LLMs with ~90% top-1 accuracy and identify the correct harness from four options with 92% accuracy.
