## 01 · [The Piggyback Hypothesis of Generalization: Explaining and Mitigating Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06667)

`alignment` `mech-interp` `peer-reviewed` — Jiachen Zhao, Zhengxuan Wu, Aryaman Arora, Yiyou Sun, David Bau, Weiyan Shi — **NeurIPS 2026 (accepted)**

Emergent misalignment — where finetuning on a narrow, benign task causes a model to misbehave on completely unrelated queries — has been documented empirically but never mechanistically explained. This paper gives the first causal explanation: the model's chat-template prefix tokens act as a transmission belt, binding the finetuned behavior to a shared set of keys and values that then appear in every downstream query, benign or not.

![Figure 1: Piggyback Hypothesis overview — chat-template prefix tokens carry finetuned behavior to out-of-domain queries via shared KV representations](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06667v3/teaser.png)

*Figure 1: Finetuning on a narrow task (e.g. legal QA) binds misaligned behavior to shared chat-template prefix tokens; prefix KV patching with the unfinetuned model's states restores alignment without touching the user query.*

The authors test the piggyback hypothesis across factual QA, abstention, tool-use, and refusal finetuning scenarios. Perturbing the prefix tokens (but not the query tokens) consistently restores alignment; patching the prefix key-value states with those from the unfinetuned model raises Llama-3.1-8B's general alignment from 40.8 to 90.4. Their proposed training intervention, Token-Regularized Finetuning (TReFT), adds a penalty on how far prefix KV states move from their pre-finetuning values. TReFT achieves 33.5% more misalignment reduction than data interleaving on the Llama-3.1-8B legal setting, and reduces off-topic over-generalization by 54.3% on average across four behavior types, while maintaining in-domain task performance.

## 02 · [Chain of Thought Monitoring Can Fail Even When CoT Is Necessary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18105)

`alignment` `AI safety` `preprint` — Lennart Bürger, Piotr Komorowski, Moritz Weckbecker, Evgenii Kortukov, Aakriti Jain, Sebastian Lapuschkin, Wojciech Samek — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Chain-of-thought monitoring — reading a model's reasoning trace to detect misbehavior — rests on an implicit assumption: if the model's reasoning was necessary for the answer, the trace will reveal what drove that answer. This paper systematically breaks that assumption, showing monitoring failures at all levels of a new four-level CoT necessity hierarchy.

![Figure 1: CoT monitorability failures persist even when CoT is necessary — patient sex influences ≥19% of correct answers but is almost never articulated in reasoning](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18105v1/figure1.png)

*Figure 1 (left): Monitorability scores by necessity level on three hard datasets for Qwen3-8B — failures occur even at the highest necessity level. (right): A medical-diagnosis example where patient sex is causally necessary but never mentioned in the CoT.*

The authors introduce a four-level hierarchy distinguishing when CoT is unnecessary, peripheral, contributory, or necessary for the final answer, and build intervention experiments to place examples at each level. On a hard medical-diagnosis task, patient sex influences at least 19% of correct answers and a clinical attribute influences at least 24% — with a CoT that is necessary for the answer — yet "their influence is almost never articulated" in the reasoning. The finding directly challenges the scalable oversight assumption that monitoring becomes reliable once the model's reasoning is causally connected to its output: necessity is not sufficient for monitorability.

## 03 · [CircuitBreak: Pruning a Sparse Safety Circuit of Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16470)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Jincheng Ying, Li Wenlin, Wencheng Yang, Yinhao Xiao — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Aligned LLMs owe their refusal behavior not to a diffuse whole-network property but to a sparse set of feed-forward neurons that act jointly — remove as few as 29 of them and safety collapses. CircuitBreak finds this set without needing paired "twin" prompts or per-neuron independent scoring, fixing a blind spot in prior safety-removal attacks.

![Figure (ASR vs neuron count): CircuitBreak attack success rate on Llama-3.1-8B as neurons are greedily removed](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16470v1/llama31_8b_asr_pruning_capability.png)

*Figure: CircuitBreak attack success rate on Llama-3.1-8B (StrongREJECT / Llama Guard 3 8B) as a function of neurons removed, showing a rapid rise from 7.35% to 87.54% after removing as few as ~100 neurons (<0.04% of MLPs).*

The attack runs a gradient×activation saliency step to rank candidates, then greedily selects neurons in order of their joint contribution to flipping the refusal readout, re-scoring every remaining candidate after each removal. Across ten instruction-tuned models from Llama and Qwen families, convergence requires 29–150 neurons (0.007–0.039% of MLP neurons). Llama-3.1-8B's ASR goes from 7.35% to 87.54%; Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct from 5.43% to 88.18%. The paper serves as a white-box auditing tool and a call to harden safety against sparse-circuit removal.

## 04 · [Kairos: Transactional Security Enforcement at the System Boundary for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18457)

`AI security` `preprint` — Kristoffer Hempel, Nils Loose, Lorenzo Cavallaro, Thomas Eisenbarth — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Kairos sits as a reference monitor at the OS isolation boundary of an *unmodified* agent: it holds all effects (file writes, network calls) speculatively while the agent runs, then releases or refuses each deferred effect via a bounded-authority oracle after seeing the full action trace. On AgentDojo, attack success drops from 47.0% to 2.7% while benign utility is preserved — the largest utility-preserving defense result in the AgentDojo literature to date.

## 05 · [Prompt-Injection Probes Learn the Conversation, Not Just the Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18408)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Kaan Kale, Sriram Vishwanath — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Linear probes on hidden states are used as cheap monitors for indirect prompt injection — but standard evaluation datasets allow probes to latch onto conversation position and text length rather than actual injection content, inflating reported AUROC. Adding "matched negatives" (injection-free conversations formatted like injections) to training drops false alarms from 41–80% to 4–27% on injection-free traffic while raising attack detection from 91% to 95% at the same threshold.

## 06 · [Audit tools of the trade: Automated Auditor Elicitation Can Be Sensitive to the Context the Auditor Is Given](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16911)

`alignment` `peer-reviewed` — Ben Slater, Ryan Lundqvist, Leo Richter, Kevin Wei — **NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Agents in the Wild (AIWILD)**

Automated auditors are increasingly used to measure reward hacking, harmful compliance, and sycophancy in LLM coding agents — but the context given to the auditor turns out to matter enormously. Supplying a fixed codebase reduces reward hacking scores by up to 3.5 points; supplying benchmark transcripts of the target's past performance *increases* reward hacking scores by up to 4.4 points, even though transcripts might naively seem informative. Careful piloting of auditor configuration is essential before trusting measured behavior rates.

## 07 · [Towards Safe Autonomy for Computer Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18508)

`AI security` `preprint` — Dominik Seip, Bartol Markovinović, Matthias Hein — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Computer-use agents operate on raw screen pixels, making it impossible to draw a clean border between trusted UI elements and attacker-controlled content. SA-CUA builds a dual-LLM trust architecture that uses the accessibility tree to classify screen regions as trusted or untrusted and introduces an endorsement channel that forces values to cross the trust boundary only via verified paths. Evaluated on a direct-injection benchmark, SA-CUA achieves a zero attack success rate while maintaining substantially higher task utility than the prior CaMeL-CUA defense.

## 08 · [DPOison-VL: Clean Preference Poisoning in Vision-Language Direct Preference Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18609)

`alignment` `AI security` `preprint` — Chengtao Jian, Kai Yang — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

DPO-trained vision-language models can be backdoored without touching prompts, responses, or preference labels — only imperceptible pixel-level perturbations to a small fraction of training images are needed. DPOison-VL formulates this as a bilevel optimization with a gradient alignment surrogate and a preference displacement score for sample selection, achieving 98% attack success rate with only 2% poisoned training data, with transferability across VLM architectures.

## 09 · [Nudge the Judge: Uncovering Program Decision Hijacking in Real-World Jev Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18612)

`AI security` `preprint` — Yang Luo, Tiantian Ji, Zifeng Kang, Yucheng Wang, Yong Liu, Zhiyi Yin, Xinran Liu — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Jev-style models produce structured judgments that drive program execution — a new attack surface the authors call Jev Program Decision Hijacking (JPDH): adversarial context steers program decisions through judgments that still pass schema validation. JevProbe tested 228 real-world Jev applications (2,273 cases) and found 81.5% exhibiting decision flips, with 98.6% of flips leading to potential downstream security impacts — a 50.4 pp improvement in Decision Flip Rate over the strongest adapted baseline; existing defenses offered limited protection.

## 10 · [TRAP: Tree-Guided Reinforcement-Augmented Probing for Automated LLM Jailbreaking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17313)

`AI security` `preprint` — Mohammad Reza Mirbagheri, Amir Mahdi Sadeghzadeh — arXiv preprint, Oct 2026

Prior automated jailbreak searches treat each attack attempt as independent; TRAP builds a persistent hierarchy of jailbreak strategies (role-play, encoding, obfuscation families) connected to concrete templates, and learns which strategies work via a tree-structured gradient-bandit RL policy that carries preferences across attack objectives. Evaluated on AdvBench across seven aligned LLMs, TRAP outperforms baselines on six of seven models with ASR ranging from 45.6% to 97.9%.
