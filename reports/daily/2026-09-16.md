## 01 · [**Why LLM Safety Guardrails Collapse After Fine-tuning: A Similarity Analysis Between Alignment and Fine-tuning Datasets**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05346)

- **Authors / venue:** Lei Hsiung, Tianyu Pang, Yung-Chen Tang, Linyue Song, Tsung-Yi Ho, Pin-Yu Chen, Yaoqing Yang — ACL 2026 main conference, long paper (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Full ACL paper at one of the field's premier venues. Identifies a previously overlooked upstream factor — the representation similarity between safety alignment data and downstream fine-tuning data — as the primary driver of guardrail collapse, with an immediately actionable design principle for fine-tuning service providers.
- **Technical summary:** The authors measure representation similarity (using CKA-style metrics) between models' upstream safety alignment dataset and the dataset used for downstream task fine-tuning, then correlate this similarity with post-fine-tuning harmfulness scores on jailbreak benchmarks. High similarity between alignment and fine-tuning datasets significantly weakens guardrails and increases susceptibility to jailbreaks; choosing downstream data with low similarity reduces harmfulness score by up to 10.33% while maintaining task utility. The finding reframes guardrail collapse as an alignment-data design problem rather than purely a fine-tuning-method problem, suggesting that safety-tuning providers should diversify alignment datasets to reduce overlap with foreseeable fine-tuning domains.

![Figure 1: Scatter of dataset similarity vs post-fine-tuning harmfulness score — high similarity strongly predicts guardrail collapse](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05346/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dataset similarity (x-axis) vs. post-fine-tuning harmfulness score (y-axis) across model-dataset pairs; high similarity predicts guardrail collapse; low-similarity alignment data reduces harmfulness by up to 10.33%.*

## 02 · [Decoy Direction Optimization: A Post-Hoc Defense Against LLM Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16204)

`pretraining-safety` `AI-security` `preprint` — Aashiq Muhamed, Mona T. Diab, Virginia Smith (Carnegie Mellon University) — September 14, 2026

Abliteration attacks use contrastive pairs to estimate the refusal direction and project it out of the model's weight matrices. DDO defeats this without base-model retraining: it injects a high-magnitude, nonlinear decoy signal into MLP neurons, corrupting the attacker's contrastive estimator so that the extracted "refusal direction" points to the decoy rather than the true safety mechanism. The actual refusal circuitry remains intact; the ablation removes the decoy instead. The defense is post-hoc, requiring no gradient-based training on the defended model.

![Figure 1: DDO defense — a decoy direction is injected into MLP neurons; ablation attacks target the decoy while true refusal circuitry remains intact](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16204/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Decoy Direction Optimization — attacker's contrastive estimator finds the injected decoy rather than the true refusal direction; ablating the decoy leaves the actual safety mechanism untouched.*

## 03 · [The Art of (Mis)alignment: How Fine-Tuning Methods Effectively Misalign and Realign LLMs in Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07754)

`pretraining-safety` `AI-security` `ACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Rui Zhang, Hongwei Li, Yun Shen, Xinyue Shen, Wenbo Jiang, Guowen Xu, Yang Liu, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang

Systematically evaluates four SFT methods and two preference-optimization methods (DPO, ORPO) on their ability to misalign and re-align four safety-trained LLMs. Key asymmetry: ORPO is the most effective misalignment method, while DPO most reliably restores alignment — but DPO realignment comes at the cost of significant utility degradation. Provides a matrix of fine-tuning method × safety direction that directly informs both attack selection and defense design for open-weight model providers.

![Figure 1: Misalignment/realignment matrix across SFT and preference-optimization methods — ORPO tops misalignment, DPO tops realignment at utility cost](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07754/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Fine-tuning method vs. safety direction matrix — ORPO achieves highest misalignment ASR; DPO best restores alignment but at measurable utility cost.*

## 04 · [SAFER: Probing Safety in Reward Models with Sparse Autoencoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00665)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `preprint` — arXiv, July 1, 2026

Trains a Sparse Autoencoder on reward model activations, then ranks features by their activation difference between chosen and rejected responses to identify safety-salient latent dimensions. Using feature-level signals, SAFER designs targeted data poisoning (injecting responses that activate safety features but are semantically benign) and denoising (pruning unsafe-salient features) strategies. Experiments show SAFER can precisely degrade or enhance safety alignment in reward models with minimal data modifications, without sacrificing general chat reward. Demonstrates that reward model safety is concentrated in interpretable, localizable SAE features.

![Figure 1: SAFER pipeline — SAE trained on reward model activations; features ranked by chosen/rejected activation difference; targeted poisoning and denoising applied](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00665/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAFER framework — SAE feature ranking by activation difference between chosen/rejected responses; safety-salient features targeted for precise poisoning or denoising with minimal collateral effect on general reward.*
