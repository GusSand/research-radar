## 01 · [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly addresses the core pretraining-time safety gap: filtering bad data is insufficient if the model can compose benign knowledge into harmful behavior. Proposes a training-corpus intervention — not a post-hoc filter — that integrates safety self-monitoring into the language modeling objective itself.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) splits the pretraining corpus into fixed-length segments and inserts a short, generated safety reflection after each segment, so the model sees and learns to produce safety judgments as a regular part of text prediction. This embeds a self-monitoring loop directly into the language model's pretraining objective rather than relying on a downstream alignment phase. On 1.7B models pretrained from scratch on FineWeb-Edu, SRP improves safety classification accuracy over the base model and substantially reduces the attack success rate of both inference-time jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks compared to a data-filtering-only baseline. Crucially, safety reflections are cheap to generate and add negligible FLOP overhead to a pretraining run.

![Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining — pretraining corpus is segmented and safety reflections are interleaved, training the model to self-monitor its outputs](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SRP pipeline — pretraining text is split into segments; a generated safety reflection follows each segment; the model is trained jointly on both, learning to produce refusals as a first-class prediction target.*

## 02 · [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang — EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides the first Fisher-geometric mechanistic account of why safety routing in aligned LLMs collapses after only ~100 benign fine-tuning examples — directly relevant to any threat model involving open-weight fine-tuning as a bypass.
- **Technical summary:** The authors show that safety alignment flattens the Fisher information landscape around safety-relevant weights (safety Fisher is low-rank), while preserving a narrow output-routing pathway through the output-side MLP modules. During benign fine-tuning these MLP modules are re-sharpened selectively, collapsing the routing pathway and causing high attack success rates before general utility measurably degrades. This asymmetric fragility explains empirical observations that safety collapses early while capability is preserved. LoRA and ASAM suppress early-stage sharpening and delay collapse, but their protection weakens at fine-tuning scales beyond a few hundred examples. The routing view also explains why a handful of injected safety examples can restore refusal: internal safety representations survive the MLP re-sharpening.

![Figure 2: Fisher-geometric diagram — safety Fisher is low-rank; benign fine-tuning re-sharpens output MLP modules, collapsing the safety routing pathway](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Safety routing collapse — alignment flattens the Fisher geometry (low-rank safety Fisher) while preserving an MLP output-routing pathway; benign fine-tuning selectively re-sharpens that pathway, causing high attack success rates with little utility degradation.*

## 03 · [**Why LLM Safety Guardrails Collapse After Fine-tuning: A Similarity Analysis Between Alignment and Fine-tuning Datasets**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05346)

- **Authors / venue:** Lei Hsiung, Tianyu Pang, Yung-Chen Tang, Linyue Song, Tsung-Yi Ho, Pin-Yu Chen, Yaoqing Yang — ACL 2026 main conference, long paper (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Full ACL paper at one of the field's premier venues. Identifies a previously overlooked upstream factor — the representation similarity between safety alignment data and downstream fine-tuning data — as the primary driver of guardrail collapse, with an immediately actionable design principle for fine-tuning service providers.
- **Technical summary:** The authors measure representation similarity (using CKA-style metrics) between models' upstream safety alignment dataset and the dataset used for downstream task fine-tuning, then correlate this similarity with post-fine-tuning harmfulness scores on jailbreak benchmarks. High similarity between alignment and fine-tuning datasets significantly weakens guardrails and increases susceptibility to jailbreaks; choosing downstream data with low similarity reduces harmfulness score by up to 10.33% while maintaining task utility. The finding reframes guardrail collapse as an alignment-data design problem rather than purely a fine-tuning-method problem, suggesting that safety-tuning providers should diversify alignment datasets to reduce overlap with foreseeable fine-tuning domains.

![Figure 1: Scatter of dataset similarity vs post-fine-tuning harmfulness score — high similarity strongly predicts guardrail collapse](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05346/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Dataset similarity (x-axis) vs. post-fine-tuning harmfulness score (y-axis) across model-dataset pairs; high similarity predicts guardrail collapse; low-similarity alignment data reduces harmfulness by up to 10.33%.*

## 04 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng — August 30, 2026

Characterizes a three-component safety circuit — Harmful Detection Heads (respond to harmful inputs), Safety Neurons (mediate safety signals in the residual stream), Refusal Heads (translate signals to safe response generation) — via zero-ablation and patching experiments. Circuit-guided weight scaling of these specific components improves safety rates under adversarial prompting by 26.5% while incurring only a 1.7% accuracy drop across four benchmarks. First systematic decomposition of refusal as a multi-stage internal circuit; provides a causal handle for improving and diagnosing safety mechanisms.

![Figure 1: Three-stage safety circuit — Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads, with circuit-guided weight scaling applied](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety circuit decomposition — Harmful Detection Heads, Safety Neurons, and Refusal Heads form a causal chain; circuit-guided scaling of these components yields +26.5% safety with only 1.7% accuracy cost.*

## 05 · [Decoy Direction Optimization: A Post-Hoc Defense Against LLM Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16204)

`pretraining-safety` `AI-security` `preprint` — Aashiq Muhamed, Mona T. Diab, Virginia Smith (Carnegie Mellon University) — September 14, 2026

Abliteration attacks use contrastive pairs to estimate the refusal direction and project it out of the model's weight matrices. DDO defeats this without base-model retraining: it injects a high-magnitude, nonlinear decoy signal into MLP neurons, corrupting the attacker's contrastive estimator so that the extracted "refusal direction" points to the decoy rather than the true safety mechanism. The actual refusal circuitry remains intact; the ablation removes the decoy instead. The defense is post-hoc, requiring no gradient-based training on the defended model.

![Figure 1: DDO defense — a decoy direction is injected into MLP neurons; ablation attacks target the decoy while true refusal circuitry remains intact](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16204/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Decoy Direction Optimization — attacker's contrastive estimator finds the injected decoy rather than the true refusal direction; ablating the decoy leaves the actual safety mechanism untouched.*

## 06 · [Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases (AMRA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining-safety` `AI-security` `preprint` — Nathan Truong (Independent) — August 2026

AMRA applies rank-k updates to residual-stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random alias vectors, and corrects downstream reader matrices so the model's general behavior is preserved. The defense obfuscates the refusal vector in activation space rather than removing it. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5 pp MMLU degradation. On Gemma-2-9B, improvement is 14.70 points, though at greater utility cost.

![Figure 1: AMRA defense — rank-k residual stream updates replace the true refusal activation with random aliases; reader matrices corrected to preserve capability](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA architecture — rank-k writer updates substitute refusal activations with aliases; reader correction preserves downstream model behavior while ablation attacks find only aliases.*

## 07 · [The Geometry of Refusal: Linear Instability in Safety-Aligned LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22686)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `ACL TrustNLP 2026 (peer-reviewed)` — TrustNLP Workshop @ ACL 2026

Introduces Contrastive Logit Steering (CLS), a zero-optimization probe that extracts the refusal direction by contrasting output distributions under safe vs. unconstrained system prompts — operating on the output distribution rather than internal activations. Applied to 7 model families, CLS reveals that safety compliance is architecturally deterministic and encoded as a low-dimensional linear feature: malicious queries and benign instructions form cleanly separable clusters. When combined with prefix injection (to bypass initial refusal reflexes), CLS-guided intervention induces a phase transition in which guardrails collapse, demonstrating that safety is fragile to linear perturbation in the output space.

![Figure 1: CLS — safe vs. unconstrained prompts produce separated output distributions; refusal direction extracted as contrastive logit difference](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22686/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Contrastive Logit Steering — safe (blue) and unconstrained (red) system-prompt logit distributions separate cleanly; the refusal direction extracted from this contrast induces guardrail phase transition when applied with prefix injection.*

## 08 · [The Art of (Mis)alignment: How Fine-Tuning Methods Effectively Misalign and Realign LLMs in Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07754)

`pretraining-safety` `AI-security` `ACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Rui Zhang, Hongwei Li, Yun Shen, Xinyue Shen, Wenbo Jiang, Guowen Xu, Yang Liu, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang

Systematically evaluates four SFT methods and two preference-optimization methods (DPO, ORPO) on their ability to misalign and re-align four safety-trained LLMs. Key asymmetry: ORPO is the most effective misalignment method, while DPO most reliably restores alignment — but DPO realignment comes at the cost of significant utility degradation. Provides a matrix of fine-tuning method × safety direction that directly informs both attack selection and defense design for open-weight model providers.

![Figure 1: Misalignment/realignment matrix across SFT and preference-optimization methods — ORPO tops misalignment, DPO tops realignment at utility cost](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07754/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Fine-tuning method vs. safety direction matrix — ORPO achieves highest misalignment ASR; DPO best restores alignment but at measurable utility cost.*

## 09 · [SAFER: Probing Safety in Reward Models with Sparse Autoencoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00665)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `preprint` — arXiv, July 1, 2026

Trains a Sparse Autoencoder on reward model activations, then ranks features by their activation difference between chosen and rejected responses to identify safety-salient latent dimensions. Using feature-level signals, SAFER designs targeted data poisoning (injecting responses that activate safety features but are semantically benign) and denoising (pruning unsafe-salient features) strategies. Experiments show SAFER can precisely degrade or enhance safety alignment in reward models with minimal data modifications, without sacrificing general chat reward. Demonstrates that reward model safety is concentrated in interpretable, localizable SAE features.

![Figure 1: SAFER pipeline — SAE trained on reward model activations; features ranked by chosen/rejected activation difference; targeted poisoning and denoising applied](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00665/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAFER framework — SAE feature ranking by activation difference between chosen/rejected responses; safety-salient features targeted for precise poisoning or denoising with minimal collateral effect on general reward.*

## 10 · [Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

`AI-security` `pretraining-safety` `preprint` — Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith — May 26, 2026

Tests whether open-weight fine-tuning defenses (including TAR and similar post-training tamper-resistance methods) can be bypassed by simple, gradient-free jailbreaks rather than adversarial fine-tuning. Two low-cost attacks — abliteration and prefilling — increase attack success rates from below 10% to high rates across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench against all tested safeguarded models. The core implication: defenses that assume harmful behavior requires fine-tuning miss an entire attack surface of inference-time bypasses; tamper-resistance and jailbreak robustness must be co-designed.

*Figure 1: Abliteration and prefilling attack success rates vs. baseline (<10%) across three harmfulness benchmarks; simple inference-time attacks reliably bypass post-training fine-tuning defenses.*
