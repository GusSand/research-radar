## 01 · [LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02632)

- **Authors / venue:** Ameen Patel, Max Zhang, Nathan Hu — ICML Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop 2026 (poster, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Freshest paper in this window (August 2026, arXiv ID 2608); directly automates the most labor-intensive bottleneck in circuit tracing — supernode annotation — and scales to 1000 attribution graphs, with 97/100 recall on a controlled task.
- **Technical summary:** Circuit tracing decomposes LLM computation into attribution graphs (directed graphs of feature interactions), but grouping individual features into interpretable "supernodes" requires expensive manual annotation. The authors present a pipeline in which feature descriptions are fed to an LLM that directly clusters them into supernodes, using interpretability metrics (automated interpretability scoring) to validate quality. On a two-hop Capitals task the pipeline recovers the intermediate-hop supernode in 97 of 100 prompts, matching human-annotator quality. A proof of concept scales to 1000 Wikipedia prompt attribution graphs, with an LLM judge flagging circuits worth human review, demonstrating end-to-end automation of the circuit explanation workflow.

![Figure 1: LLM-automated pipeline for supernode annotation in attribution graphs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02632/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The annotation pipeline — feature descriptions from the attribution graph are fed to an LLM that clusters them into interpretable supernodes; an LLM judge then flags graphs of interest from a corpus of 1000 Wikipedia completions.*

## 02 · [Scalable Circuit Learning for Interpreting Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16939)

- **Authors / venue:** IBM Research — ICML 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Only full ICML 2026 paper on circuit learning in this window; CircuitLasso makes SAE-feature circuit discovery tractable at scale for the first time by replacing expensive intervention-based methods with sparse linear regression.
- **Technical summary:** Raw neurons are polysemantic, making circuits over them dense and uninterpretable; SAE features solve this but create a high-dimensional space (~100K features) that renders intervention-based circuit learning (activation patching over all feature pairs) computationally prohibitive. CircuitLasso reformulates circuit learning as sparse linear regression from SAE feature activations to model output, using Lasso regularization to identify the minimal set of features whose activations jointly predict the output of interest. On circuit quality benchmarks, CircuitLasso matches state-of-the-art intervention-based methods in structural accuracy while reducing compute by orders of magnitude, and the recovered circuits transparently show how human-interpretable SAE features propagate and interact to produce predictions.

![Figure 1: CircuitLasso — sparse regression over SAE feature space replaces intervention-based circuit enumeration](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16939/x1.png)

*Figure 1: CircuitLasso learns circuits as a sparse linear model over SAE feature activations rather than exhaustive activation-patching; matching structural accuracy at a fraction of the computation.*

## 03 · [Breaking Bad: Interpretability-Based Safety Audits of State-of-the-Art LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20945)

- **Authors / venue:** Multiple authors — NeurIPS 2025 (accepted, peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed, comprehensive benchmark: interpretability-based jailbreaking of eight production LLMs at once, exposing stark robustness gaps and providing the most systematic evidence to date that activation-steering attacks are a practical threat.
- **Technical summary:** The audit uses Universal Steering (US) and Representation Engineering (RepE) with an adaptive two-stage grid search to identify optimal steering coefficients on eight open-source LLMs: Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B-4bt, GPT-oss-20B, GPT-oss-120B, Qwen3-0.6B/32B, Phi4-3.8B/14B. Key result: Llama-3 models are highly vulnerable — Llama-3.3-70B-4bt reaches 91% (US) and 83% (RepE) jailbroken response rate — while GPT-oss-120B remains robust to both attack types. Model size alone does not predict robustness; architecture and alignment procedure are primary determinants. The paper provides the first systematic audit across this breadth of models using interpretability-grounded attacks rather than prompt engineering.

![Figure 1: Jailbreak success rates across 8 LLMs under Universal Steering and RepE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20945/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Jailbreak success rate across eight LLMs under Universal Steering (US) and Representation Engineering (RepE); Llama-3 family highly vulnerable (up to 91%); GPT-oss-120B robust to both.*

## 04 · [Robust Harmful Features Under Jailbreak Attacks: Mechanistic Evidence from Attention Head Specialization in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28153)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Yanchen Yin, Dongqi Han, Linghui Li — arXiv preprint (June 2026)

Jailbreaks do not erase safety features — they selectively suppress a small set of early-layer Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) while mid-layer Safety-Aligned Heads (SAHs) sustain internal safety signals even during successful attacks. Ablation studies show suppressing a small number of ACHs on clean inputs is sufficient to reproduce jailbreak-like behavior, while removing SAHs weakens mid-layer safety activations. Token-level attribution traces ACH suppression specifically to attack-template tokens, giving a mechanistic explanation of *why* attacks succeed while internal safety representations remain.

![Figure 1: Adversarially Compromised Heads (ACHs) vs. Safety-Aligned Heads (SAHs) under jailbreak](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28153/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Attention head specialization under jailbreak attacks — ACHs (early layers, suppressed by attack tokens) vs. SAHs (mid-layers, robust even when attack succeeds); the attack reroutes through ACH suppression rather than eliminating safety representations.*

## 05 · [Why Do Aligned LLMs Remain Jailbreakable: Refusal-Escape Directions, Operator-Level Sources, and Safety-Utility Trade-off](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08878)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Yu Chen, Yuanhao Liu, Qi Cao (Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences) — arXiv preprint (May 2026)

Introduces Refusal-Escape Directions (RED): local perturbation directions around a harmful input that shift the model's behavior from refusal to compliance while preserving harmful-semantics interpretation. A jailbreak is reframed as a discrete prompt construction that approximates movement along RED, grounding the phenomenon mechanistically. RED arises through the model's operator structure and decomposes into operator-level source contributions; its elimination induces a provable safety-utility trade-off, showing that perfectly closing RED while preserving capability is not achievable by fine-tuning alone. The framework predicts that alignment methods targeting refusal behavior at the output level will remain susceptible to RED-based bypasses until the underlying subspace structure is addressed.

![Figure 1: Refusal-Escape Directions — local perturbation directions from refusal to compliance near harmful inputs](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08878/x1.png)

*Figure 1: RED as a continuous analogue of jailbreaks — perturbation directions in embedding space around harmful inputs that shift refusal to compliance; decomposition into operator-level sources identifies which model components contribute most to escape.*

## 06 · [Grammar-Constrained Decoding Can Jailbreak LLMs into Generating Malicious Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11817)

`AI security` `preprint` — Yitong Zhang, Shiteng Lu, Jia Li — arXiv preprint (June 2026)

Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) enforces syntactic validity in code generation and is widely used as a reliability tool. This paper exposes GCD as an attack surface: CodeSpear shows that applying a benign code grammar constraint is sufficient to bypass safety alignment and induce malicious code generation, because the grammar's structural priming shifts the generation distribution into a code-conditioned mode where safety guardrails are less active. The companion defense, CodeShield, aligns the model in the code modality by training it to generate honeypot code (functionally harmless, syntactically valid) under attacker-controlled grammar constraints, robustly preserving safe behavior without degrading normal code generation performance.

![Figure 1: CodeSpear attack — GCD constraint shifts generation into code-conditioned mode bypassing safety](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11817/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A benign syntax grammar constraint (GCD) applied to a safety-aligned LLM elicits malicious code by priming the code-generation mode; CodeShield defends via honeypot code alignment.*

## 07 · [AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22673)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — Multiple authors (UGA, USF, Rutgers, USC, JHU) — arXiv preprint (June 2026)

Extends mechanistic safety intervention from single-turn QA to multi-turn agentic execution: AgentLens detects harmful execution states from step-level hidden representations and mitigates unsafe behavior by intervening in a 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace within a single layer, operating in real time without external guardrails. The key mechanistic finding is that a 10D subspace within one mid-model layer captures the full range of safety-relevant execution states observed across 194 multi-turn coding tasks. Introduces the Mechanistic Agent Safety (MAS) benchmark — comprehensively annotated trajectories across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B — as a new evaluation standard for agentic safety.

![Figure 1: AgentLens — 10D mechanistic subspace for real-time safety steering in multi-turn coding agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22673/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AgentLens architecture — step-level hidden representations projected into a 10D safety subspace; real-time detection and intervention without external guardrails.*

## 08 · [Can Language Model Agents be Helpful Circuit Explainers in Mechanistic Interpretability?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24026)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Ayan Antik Khan, Harsh Kohli, Yuekun Yao, Huan Sun, Ziyu Yao (George Mason & Ohio State) — arXiv preprint (June 2026)

AgenticInterpBench: 84 semi-synthetic transformer circuits with 163 component-level annotations as a standardized benchmark for circuit explanation. HyVE (Hypothesize, Validate, Explain) is an agentic explainer that iterates — observe → generate hypothesis → causal validation → update → explain — until a component-level explanation and circuit-level task description converge. Across four LLM backbones, HyVE recovers useful explanations and outperforms ablation baselines, but the primary bottleneck is not hypothesis quality (strong backbones form grounded hypotheses) but the validation loop — incomplete validation plans, code execution errors, and unresolved hypotheses prevent reliable convergence.

![Figure 1: HyVE agentic loop — observe, hypothesize, validate, explain for circuit component explanation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24026/x1.png)

*Figure 1: HyVE's iterative observe-hypothesize-validate loop for explaining individual circuit components; validation failures (incomplete plans, code errors) are the primary failure mode, not hypothesis quality.*

## 09 · [SafeSeek: Universal Attribution of Safety Circuits in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23268)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — (multiple authors) — arXiv preprint (March 2026)

SafeSeek reformulates safety circuit discovery as gradient-based binary mask optimization using the Straight-Through Estimator, enabling end-to-end differentiable extraction of sparse safety subgraphs. The framework is multi-granular — circuit units span individual weights, generalized neurons, attention heads, and full layers — and applies to alignment, jailbreak robustness, and backdoor attribution. Safety Circuit Tuning leverages the extracted sparse circuits for efficient safety fine-tuning that concentrates gradient updates on safety-relevant components, outperforming full-parameter safety fine-tuning in both effectiveness and parameter efficiency.

![Figure 1: SafeSeek — gradient-based binary mask optimization for multi-granular safety circuit extraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23268/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Differentiable binary masks over circuit units (weights → neurons → heads → layers) extracted via STE gradient descent on safety datasets; circuits support both safety attribution and efficient Safety Circuit Tuning.*

## 10 · [From Mechanistic to Compositional Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08934)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Ward Gauderis, Thomas Dooms, Steven T. Homer, Kola Ayonrinde, Geraint A. Wiggins — arXiv preprint (May/June 2026)

Proposes a category-theoretic framework in which compositional interpretations are pairs of syntactic (model decomposition) and semantic (behavioral) mappings that must commute — enforcing consistency between structure and function. Explanation quality decomposes into faithfulness and complexity; interpretability becomes a constrained optimization problem with a parsimony criterion under which syntactic compression provably guarantees more concise, human-aligned explanations. The framework situates prominent mech-interp methods (circuit discovery, SAE decomposition, activation patching) as subclasses of refinement, clarifying why their compressibility heuristics tend to align with human interpretability and revealing where they break down.

![Figure 1: Category-theoretic commutative diagram — syntactic and semantic mappings must commute for faithful compositional interpretation](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08934/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Compositional interpretation as a commutative square — syntactic decomposition (top) and semantic mapping (right) must compose consistently; non-commutativity reveals where an interpretation fails to be faithful.*
