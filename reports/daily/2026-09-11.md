## 01 · [Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman (EleutherAI / UK AI Security Institute / Oxford OATML) — **ICLR 2026** (peer-reviewed)
- **Why it ranks here:** Field-shifting peer-reviewed result establishing that pretraining data filtering—not post-hoc RLHF or unlearning—delivers the strongest tamper-resistance against adversarial fine-tuning; over 10× better than the best unlearning baseline, directly targeting biorisk capability removal.
- **Technical summary:** The paper introduces a multi-stage scalable pipeline to filter biorisk-proxy text from LLM pretraining corpora. Six 6.9B-parameter models are pretrained from scratch on filtered vs. unfiltered corpora; filtered models are then subjected to adversarial fine-tuning attacks (ranging from 10,000 gradient steps to 300 million tokens of adversarial data). Filtering-based models maintain substantially suppressed biothreat proxy knowledge across all attacks, achieving state-of-the-art tamper-resistance in all but one test condition. Crucially, filtering does not sacrifice general benchmark performance. The result implies that safety is most durable when hazardous capability substrate is absent from the weight matrix rather than suppressed by post-training.

![Figure 1: Tamper-resistance comparison — filtered pretraining vs. post-training unlearning across adversarial fine-tuning budget](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Filtered pretraining models maintain low biothreat proxy knowledge even after 10,000 adversarial fine-tuning steps and 300M tokens, over 10× more resistant than best post-training unlearning baselines.*

## 02 · [The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06934)

- **Authors / venue:** Srikanth Malla, Chiho Choi, Joon Hee Choi — arXiv preprint (September 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Provides a principled geometric account of why RLHF/DPO refusal collapses under benign fine-tuning while pretraining-time safety persists, directly explaining the mechanism behind #1's empirical result and framing the frontier safety challenge in terms of kernel geometry.
- **Technical summary:** The authors characterize post-hoc safety updates (RLHF, DPO) as landing in a "suppression regime" where the safety gradient is nearly orthogonal to the span of capability directions—a refusal gate overlaid on intact capabilities rather than capability erasure. A kernel-immobility lemma proves such an update can only mask, not remove, a pre-existing capability, so any fine-tuning that sharpens those capability directions can restore refusal-eliciting outputs. Empirically, 100 steps of benign fine-tuning collapsed refusal in Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B Instruct. A 267-checkpoint sweep of OLMo-2-1B reveals that the safety substrate itself emerges in a sharp phase transition between approximately 6B and 60B pretraining tokens, explaining why early-stage pretraining safety interventions are structurally different from late-stage post-training ones.

![Figure 2: 267-checkpoint OLMo-2-1B sweep — safety substrate emergence between 6B and 60B pretraining tokens](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06934/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Safety substrate (measured via probe accuracy on refusal-eliciting stimuli) emerges sharply between ~6B and ~60B pretraining tokens in a 267-checkpoint sweep; post-hoc alignment merely gates this substrate rather than building new structure.*

## 03 · [How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793)

- **Authors / venue:** Yi Shi, Tanyu Chen, Kai Shen — arXiv preprint (September 9, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Extends directional ablation (abliteration) to a 320B MoE frontier model (GLM-5.3-Flash), directly rebutting the hypothesis that MoE architecture or quantization would resist the attack; combined writer editing removes 77.6% of refusal capability without any gradient-based training.
- **Technical summary:** Directional ablation removes an open-weight model's refusal by projecting the "refusal direction" out of the weight matrices that write to the residual stream. Prior work established this on dense models ≤70B. This paper targets GLM-5.3-Flash (320B parameters, 288 routed experts, block-FP8 quantization, four-wide hyper-connection residual). The residual stream in MoE is no longer a single tensor, so the attack must be adapted per writer type. Editing attention writers alone removes 3.9% of refusal; dense expert writers remove 1.6%; routed expert writers remove 14.8%; editing all three types jointly removes 77.6% of refusal. The attack requires only a few hundred contrastive prompts and no optimization, confirming that refusal-as-suppression remains vulnerable at frontier scale and MoE topology.

![Figure 1: Refusal removal contribution by writer type in 320B MoE — attention, dense, routed expert, combined](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09793/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Per-writer-type and combined directional ablation on GLM-5.3-Flash (320B MoE); combined editing of all three writer types removes 77.6% of refusal with no gradient-based optimization.*

## 04 · [From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00051)

`pretraining/safety` `mech-interp` `EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng

Characterizes a three-stage safety circuit—harmful detection heads → safety neurons → refusal heads—and scales the weights of identified circuit components to improve safety durability. Circuit-guided scaling improves safety rate under jailbreak attacks by 26.5% while incurring only a 1.7% accuracy drop on standard benchmarks, and is the first method to intervene on the full refusal circuit rather than any single component in isolation.

![Figure 1: Three-stage safety circuit — harmful detection heads, safety neurons, refusal heads](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00051/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Multi-stage safety circuit from harmful-stimulus detection through neuron-mediated safety signals to refusal generation; circuit-guided weight scaling improves safety by 26.5% with minimal utility cost.*

## 05 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

`pretraining-safety` `preprint` — Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv, June 17, 2026

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) partitions the pretraining corpus into segments of a few hundred tokens and inserts a safety reflection after each—a verdict token ("Safe"/"Unsafe") and a category token specifying the harm type, generated by a safety classifier. SRP on 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces attack success rates for both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks, without rewriting or filtering the corpus.

![Figure 1: SRP pipeline — pretraining corpus segmented with inserted safety reflections](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining — every few-hundred-token segment is followed by a classifier-generated verdict and harm-category token during pretraining, teaching the model self-monitoring alongside language modeling.*

## 06 · [Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

`pretraining-safety` `spec-alignment` `preprint` — Julian Minder, Viktor Moskvoretskii et al. (EPFL) — arXiv, August 2026

SPP annotates pretraining documents with value-aligned first-person reflections drawn from a normative value constitution—the target persona reasons aloud about each document's alignment implications—so the model learns values from the first token rather than having them grafted on in post-training. Models up to 3B parameters trained on 500B tokens show improved constitution following, stronger jailbreak robustness, and reduced misalignment rate in out-of-distribution moral dilemmas versus post-only alignment, with the advantage increasing with pretraining compute budget.

![Figure 1: SPP annotation pipeline — pretraining documents augmented with value-aligned first-person persona reflections](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SPP inserts constitution-derived first-person reflections into pretraining documents; alignment advantage over post-training-only baselines grows with pretraining compute, establishing a clear return to pretraining-time value installation.*

## 07 · [When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

`alignment-durability` `preprint` — Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang — arXiv, September 2026

Proposes a Fisher-geometric explanation of why even benign fine-tuning collapses safety alignment: the safety Fisher information matrix is low-rank, so alignment makes the safety loss landscape flat (broad basin) while preserving a latent output-routing pathway. After 100 benign fine-tuning examples, this pathway is selectively re-sharpened in output-side MLP modules, enabling high attack success rates while general utility degrades only mildly. LoRA and ASAM suppress early collapse by reducing output-side sharpness, but their protection weakens at larger fine-tuning scales.

![Figure 1: Fisher-geometric safety landscape — flat alignment basin vs. re-sharpened output-routing pathway after benign fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Fisher is low-rank; benign fine-tuning re-sharpens the output-side MLP routing pathway (shown as curvature spike) while the safety basin remains flat, explaining asymmetric collapse.*

## 08 · [Unraveling LLM Jailbreaks Through Safety Knowledge Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01631)

`AI-security` `mech-interp` `EACL 2026 long paper (peer-reviewed)` — Chongwen Zhao, Yutong Ke, Kaizhu Huang — ACL Anthology 2026.eacl-long.83

Identifies safety-related knowledge neurons by projecting residual-stream representations into vocabulary space; neurons whose top vocabulary items are safety-concept tokens are designated safety-critical. Adjusting the activation of identified safety neurons achieves mean attack success rate >97%—demonstrating that jailbreak vulnerability can be precisely located. Building on this, SafeTuning fine-tunes models to reinforce safety-critical neurons, consistently reducing ASR and outperforming four baseline defenses across multiple LLM families.

![Figure 1: Safety knowledge neuron identification and SafeTuning defense pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01631/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Vocabulary-projection pipeline identifies safety knowledge neurons; SafeTuning reinforces them, consistently outperforming four baselines in ASR reduction across multiple LLMs.*

## 09 · [AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22673)

`mech-interp` `AI-security` `preprint` — Mingyu Jin, Yihao Quan, Zhen Xiang et al. — arXiv, June 2026

Detects harmful execution states in a multi-turn coding agent by analyzing step-level residual-stream representations; safety-targeted activation steering is applied within a learned 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace in a single transformer layer. Introduces MAS-Bench: 194 annotated tasks (98 malicious / 96 benign) across 10 MITRE ATT&CK categories using LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B. Subspace-aware steering substantially reduces harmful OS-level actions while maintaining coding task performance, and preliminary results show lookahead risk anticipation is possible—predicting harm before it executes.

![Figure 1: AgentLens — mechanistic subspace identification from step-level hidden states, safety steering within 10-dimensional subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22673/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AgentLens pipeline — residual-stream decomposition at each agent step identifies 10-d safety-relevant subspace; activation steering within the subspace reduces harmful actions without degrading coding utility.*

## 10 · [DuplexJail: Safety Alignment Breaks Under Spoken Interruption in Full-Duplex Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09420)

`AI-security` `preprint` — Jaechul Roh, Deepak Chandran, Amir Houmansadr, Andrea Fanelli — arXiv, September 9, 2026

Full-duplex speech models accept user audio while simultaneously generating speech output, creating an attack surface invisible to text-domain safety alignment. DuplexJail delivers fixed, request-independent spoken prompts through the user audio channel, comparing (a) fixed-delay interruption after the harmful request ends and (b) refusal-triggered interruption cued by the model's own streaming text. Fixed-delay interruption raises whole-response attack success rate on AdvBench to 40.3% for PersonaPlex and 48.7% for PersonaPlex-RL—increases of +33.8 and +39.3 percentage points—exposing a novel modality-specific attack surface that text-only safety training does not cover.

![Figure 1: DuplexJail attack diagram — spoken interruption timing strategy on full-duplex model](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09420/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DuplexJail inserts spoken interruption at fixed delay or refusal-trigger; fixed-delay raises AdvBench ASR to 40.3%/48.7% on PersonaPlex/PersonaPlex-RL, a ~+35 pp improvement over baseline.*
