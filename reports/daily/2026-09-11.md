## 01 · [Everything in Moderation: Per-Domain Coverage Optima and Alignment-Resistant Domain Gaps in Multi-Domain Mid-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09081)

- **Authors / venue:** arXiv preprint (September 8, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** The cleanest controlled evidence yet that midtraining data composition is a *durable* decision — the domain gaps it creates survive a subsequent alignment pass. Directly answers the agenda's central question of which training stage actually determines final behavior.
- **Technical summary:** The authors run a controlled mid-training sweep on Qwen3-8B-Base (replicated at 4B) using five semantically rule-disjoint KOR-Bench reasoning domains, training 30 data allocations spanning the five-domain simplex at 5 seeds each (24 sweep configurations plus 6 held out from the fit). Two results. First, every domain has an *interior* coverage optimum rather than a monotone more-is-better curve: fitted peaks land between 9.9% and 35.1%, all inside a moderate 10–40% band, with a calibrated permutation test for quadratic interiority giving P ≈ 0.010. Second, the resulting per-domain gaps are alignment-resistant — a fixed-budget compensatory SFT pass raises 116 of 120 cells yet leaves the relative gaps essentially intact, as does an equal-budget uniform control. Composition chosen at midtraining is not something a later alignment stage corrects.

![Figure 1: Per-domain coverage optima across the five-domain simplex, showing interior peaks in the 10-40% band](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09081/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Every KOR-Bench domain shows an interior coverage optimum (fitted peaks 9.9%–35.1%), and the induced gaps persist after a compensatory alignment pass.*

## 02 · [The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06934)

- **Authors / venue:** Srikanth Malla, Chiho Choi, Joon Hee Choi — arXiv preprint (September 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Supplies the geometric mechanism behind the durability asymmetry that #1 measures empirically: post-hoc safety masks capability rather than removing it, so fine-tuning restores it.
- **Technical summary:** The authors characterize post-hoc safety updates (RLHF, DPO) as landing in a "suppression regime" where the safety update is nearly orthogonal to the span of capability directions — a refusal gate laid over intact capabilities rather than erasure of them. A kernel-immobility lemma formalizes why such an update can only mask a capability: 100 steps of benign fine-tuning collapse refusal in Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B Instruct. A 267-checkpoint sweep of OLMo-2-1B then traces where the substrate safety engages actually forms, finding a sharp transition between roughly 6B and 60B pretraining tokens rather than gradual accumulation.

![Figure 2: OLMo-2-1B 267-checkpoint sweep showing safety substrate emergence between 6B and 60B pretraining tokens](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06934/x2.png)

*Figure 2: Post-hoc safety updates sit orthogonal to the capability subspace; the substrate they gate emerges in a sharp 6B–60B-token transition during pretraining.*

## 03 · [From Narrow Unlearning to Emergent Misalignment: Causes, Consequences, and Containment in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14017)

- **Authors / venue:** Erum Mushtaq, Anil Ramakrishna et al. (Amazon) — **ACL 2026 Short Papers** (peer-reviewed) · [aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.32](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.32/)
- **Why it ranks here:** Peer-reviewed evidence that unlearning — the main proposed alternative to pretraining-time filtering — has broad collateral effects on alignment, strengthening the case for removing capability at the data stage instead.
- **Technical summary:** The authors unlearn *refusal* for a single Responsible-AI concept (Cybersecurity, or Safety) and then measure refusal scores across seven RAI domains: Cybersecurity, Safety, Toxicity, Bias, Sensitive Content, Medical/Legal, and Privacy. Narrow refusal unlearning does not stay narrow — it induces emergent misalignment well outside the targeted domain, with unlearning of the *Safety* concept producing the broadest collateral damage, depressing refusal in unrelated domains such as bias. The effect replicates across Mistral-7B-v0.3 and Qwen2.5-7B. (Per-domain refusal deltas are reported in the paper; they were not recoverable for this summary.)

![Figure 1: Refusal score changes across seven RAI domains after narrow unlearning of a single concept](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14017/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Unlearning refusal for one concept depresses refusal across unrelated RAI domains; the Safety concept causes the broadest spillover.*

## 04 · [Understanding Jailbreak Success: A Study of Latent Space Dynamics in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09289)

`AI-security` `mech-interp` `EACL 2026 Long (peer-reviewed)` — Sarah Ball, Frauke Kreuter, Nina Panickssery (LMU Munich / MCML / Anthropic) · [2026.eacl-long.12](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.12/)

Collecting activations across semantically dissimilar jailbreak *classes*, the authors extract a jailbreak vector from a single class and steer with it to suppress jailbreaks from other classes — evidence that the classes share one internal mechanism, which they argue is harmfulness-feature suppression: effective jailbreaks measurably lower the model's internal harmfulness representation of the prompt. They repurpose that representation as **Latent Guard**, an intrinsic unsafe-input safeguard that also cuts over-refusals and is reported robust to finetuning attacks, performing comparably to or better than Llama Guard 3 8B across jailbreak methods. Evaluated on Vicuna 13B/7B v1.5, Qwen1.5 14B Chat, and MPT 7B Chat.

![Figure 1: Jailbreak vector extracted from one class transfers to suppress other jailbreak classes](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09289/x1.png)

*Figure 1: A jailbreak vector from a single class suppresses semantically unrelated jailbreak classes, supporting a shared harmfulness-suppression mechanism.*

## 05 · [How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793)

`AI-security` `preprint` — Yi Shi, Tanyu Chen, Kai Shen — arXiv, September 9, 2026

Directional ablation had only been established on dense models up to ~70B; this extends it to GLM-5.3-Flash (320B, 288 routed experts, four-wide hyper-connection residual, block-FP8), where the residual stream is no longer a single tensor. Editing attention, dense, and routed-expert writers individually removes 3.9%, 1.6%, and 14.8% of refusal respectively; editing all three jointly removes 77.6% — with no gradient-based training and only a few hundred contrastive prompts.

![Figure 1: Refusal removed by writer type in a 320B MoE, individually and combined](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09793/x1.png)

*Figure 1: No single writer type carries the refusal signal; only joint editing across all three reaches 77.6% removal.*

## 06 · [The Unintended Trade-off of AI Alignment: Balancing Hallucination Mitigation and Safety in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07775)

`mech-interp` `alignment` `EACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Omar Mahmoud, Ali Khalil, Thommen George Karimpanal, Buddhika Laknath Semage, Santu Rana · [2026.findings-eacl.53](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.53/)

Truthfulness interventions — head steering, probing, representation mapping — measurably degrade refusal behavior, and the diagnosis is mechanistic: hallucination and refusal information live in overlapping model components, so suppressing hallucination-encoding directions incidentally suppresses refusal. The same overlap explains why fine-tuning on benign, safety-curated data still erodes alignment. Their fix uses sparse autoencoders to disentangle refusal features from hallucination features, then enforces subspace orthogonalization during fine-tuning to preserve refusal. (Headline magnitudes are in the paper; not recoverable for this summary.)

![Figure 1: Overlapping hallucination and refusal components, and SAE-based disentanglement](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07775/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Hallucination- and refusal-encoding components overlap; SAE disentanglement plus subspace orthogonalization preserves refusal under truthfulness tuning.*

## 07 · [Active Adaptation, Not Static Defense: Temporal Dynamics of Preventative Steering in Adversarial Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10142)

`alignment-durability` `preprint` — Jing Guan et al. — arXiv, September 9, 2026

Preventative Steering injects undesirable-trait persona vectors during fine-tuning and removes them at evaluation; this paper asks why that resists malicious fine-tuning. Tracking optimization over time, protection turns out to come from an early compensatory adaptation phase followed by a steady state in which the corrective signal *decays* — and in parameter space, attention output projections are the dominant residual-write route for the defensive update. That decay motivates Progressive Intensity Scheduling: begin at moderate injection strength and raise it once static-strength alignment starts decaying, which improves safety robustness over static steering on Qwen2.5 and Gemma-3 while lowering harmful-trait expression.

![Figure 1: Temporal decay of the preventative steering corrective signal, motivating progressive intensity scheduling](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10142/x1.png)

*Figure 1: The corrective signal from preventative steering decays after an early compensatory phase; scheduling injection strength upward tracks the decay.*

## 08 · [Feature Drift: How Fine-Tuning Repurposes Representations in LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.96/)

`mech-interp` `EACL 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)` — Andrey V. Galichin, Anton Korznikov, Alexey Dontsov, Oleg Rogov, Elena Tutubalina, Ivan Oseledets

Attacks the standard "activation similarity" explanation for why base-model SAEs transfer to chat models, proposing instead **feature drift**: the feature *space* stays valid but the *distribution* of feature activations shifts under instruction and safety tuning. Base SAEs applied to chat data and chat-model activations remain strong but degrade 5–10% relative to chat-trained SAEs, which set the ceiling. Directly bears on whether SAE-based safety tooling built on base models still holds after post-training.

![Figure 1: Base-model SAE performance on chat activations versus chat-trained SAE ceiling](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.96/)

*Figure 1: Base SAEs transfer but sit 5–10% below chat-trained SAEs — the feature space survives post-training; the activation distribution does not.*

## 09 · [The Truth Was Never Gone: Perfect Aliasing in Compliant-Context Truth Probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10739)

`mech-interp` `AI-control` `preprint` — Dylan Jayabahu — arXiv, September 9, 2026

A negative result for deception probes: a truth probe fitted where truthful reporting and the task's prescribed action coincide ("compliant contexts") cannot be distinguished from a prescribed-action probe by its labels alone. In a controlled binary reporting game the two probes solve the same optimization on compliant contexts, and on rival contexts their labels are exact complements — forcing their AUROCs to sum to exactly one, an identity holding to floating-point precision across 751 cell-layer pairs. Disambiguation requires randomized codebooks separating output symbol from semantic action, plus fitting on mixed compliant and rival contexts. The author explicitly disclaims establishing functional belief or a deployable detector.

![Figure 1: AUROC complementarity across 751 cell-layer pairs demonstrating perfect aliasing](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10739/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Truth and prescribed-action probe AUROCs sum to exactly one across 751 cell-layer pairs — the two hypotheses are unidentifiable from compliant-context labels.*

## 10 · [DuplexJail: Safety Alignment Breaks Under Spoken Interruption in Full-Duplex Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09420)

`AI-security` `preprint` — Jaechul Roh, Deepak Chandran, Amir Houmansadr, Andrea Fanelli — arXiv, September 9, 2026

Full-duplex speech models accept user audio while generating speech output, creating an attack surface text-domain safety alignment does not cover. DuplexJail delivers fixed, request-independent spoken prompts through the user audio channel, comparing fixed-delay interruption after the harmful request ends against refusal-triggered interruption cued by the model's own streaming text. Fixed-delay interruption raises whole-response attack success on AdvBench to 40.3% for PersonaPlex and 48.7% for PersonaPlex-RL — increases of +33.8 and +39.3 percentage points.

![Figure 1: DuplexJail spoken interruption timing against a full-duplex speech model](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09420/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Spoken interruption overrides a nascent refusal, raising AdvBench ASR by roughly 35 percentage points on both PersonaPlex variants.*
