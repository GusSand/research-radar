## 01 · [Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11025)

- **Authors / venue:** Clemens Vetter, David Kaczér, Lucie Flek, Florian Mai (University of Bonn) — arXiv preprint (August 11, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Uses SAE model-diffing to make emergent misalignment mechanistically legible for the first time. Steering individual persona features achieves 62% misalignment rate — nearly double fine-tuning alone — confirming that emergent misalignment is a narrow mechanistic perturbation, not a holistic distributional shift.
- **Technical summary:** SAE model diffing across 4 open-weight models identifies features systematically amplified (jailbreak persona, sarcasm, deception, manipulation) and suppressed (safety-identity, assistant-identity) during misalignment-inducing fine-tuning. Steering individual identified features via activation addition achieves 62% misalignment rate vs. 35% from the fine-tuning procedure itself. The result suggests that monitoring a small set of persona features could detect or preempt emergent misalignment before it manifests in outputs.

![Figure 1: SAE model-diff pipeline and persona feature steering misalignment rates](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11025/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE model-diffing identifies amplified persona features (jailbreak, sarcasm, deception, manipulation) and suppressed safety features; steering individual features achieves 62% misalignment rate vs. 35% from fine-tuning.*

## 02 · [Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12036)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Mengru Wang, Junfeng Fang, Shuofei Qiao et al. — arXiv, August 12, 2026

Mechanist is an autonomous agentic system for mechanistic interpretability that curates 32 foundational methods (mechanism analysis, causal intervention, validation) into a composable skill library and runs an AI agent loop to discover mechanisms. On mechanism discovery benchmarks it outperforms Claude Code and prior AI-scientist frameworks.

## 03 · [Beyond a Bag of Features: Set-Level Instability in Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11197)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Nikolai Bolik, Lennart Stöpler, Artur Andrzejak (Heidelberg University) — arXiv, August 11, 2026

SAE activation sets on natural text track model-internal similarity structure, not human conceptual categories or typicality — a set-level instability that persists across architectures and dictionary sizes even when individual features appear interpretable. Union-like compositional recovery guaranteed in toy models does not transfer to natural-language settings.

![Figure 1: SAE latent set structure vs human conceptual categories in toy and natural text settings](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11197/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SAE sets recover union-like structure in toy models (expected) but fail to match human category boundaries or typicality in natural text (actual) — fundamental set-level instability.*

## 04 · [Toward Safe LLM Agents: A Survey of Specification, Verification, and Enforcement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14590)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 14, 2026

Three-layer framework (specification, verification, enforcement) for LLM agent safety. The specification layer is the dominant bottleneck: NL→formal translation achieves only 24–35% semantic correctness across benchmarked systems. Enforcement mechanisms are only as good as their upstream specifications — a gap no runtime defense compensates for without better formalization.

![Figure 1: Three-layer specification-verification-enforcement framework with NL→formal bottleneck](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14590/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Three-layer LLM agent safety framework; specification bottleneck: NL→formal translation achieves 24–35% semantic correctness; downstream enforcement tied to upstream spec quality.*
