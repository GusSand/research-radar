## 01 · [**Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)

- **Authors / venue:** EleutherAI team — **ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed)**; also on OpenReview at `xcf0QcTcGS`
- **Why it ranks here:** Highest-priority result this agenda tracks: a peer-reviewed, empirically rigorous demonstration that pretraining-time data filtering — not post-training RLHF — is the mechanistically correct place to suppress dual-use capability, and that this suppression survives adversarial fine-tuning far beyond what post-training defenses achieve. Directly addresses the top two questions on this radar's priority list (pretraining data filtering + tamper-resistance).
- **Technical summary:** The authors pretrain multiple 6.9B-parameter models from scratch on corpora with varying degrees of biothreat-related text removed, using a scalable multi-stage filtering pipeline. Filtered models exhibit substantial knowledge suppression of biothreat-relevant content, and — crucially — the suppression is tamper-resistant: adversarial fine-tuning on up to 10,000 optimizer steps and 300 million tokens of biothreat-related text fails to restore the capability to pre-filter levels. General capabilities (measured on standard benchmarks) are not degraded, showing that topic-specific filtering can be applied without broad performance loss. The result falsifies the implicit assumption that post-training alignment can be made tamper-resistant: suppressing capability at the data level is fundamentally more durable than instilling refusal at the behavior level.

*Figure 1: Multi-stage pretraining filter pipeline and adversarial fine-tuning resistance curve — filtered 6.9B models sustain knowledge suppression through 10k fine-tuning steps and 300M tokens of biothreat text.*

## 02 · [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li et al. (Tsinghua University, IIIS) — arXiv preprint, June 17, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses a subtle gap that data-only filtering (cf. #1) leaves open: a model trained on safe data can still compose innocuous knowledge into unsafe behaviors. SRP installs a self-monitoring signal directly in the pretraining language model objective, not as a separate post-training stage, making it more fundamental than RLHF-based methods and complementary to data filtering.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) partitions the pretraining corpus into segments of a few hundred tokens; after each segment a safety classifier injects a short "safety reflection" consisting of a verdict token (Safe/Unsafe) and a category token specifying the harm type. The model is trained to jointly predict the next pretraining token and these reflection tokens, internalizing a continuous self-assessment signal. Experiments on 1.7B models trained on FineWeb-Edu show improved safety classification accuracy and substantially reduced attack success rates for both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks compared to data filtering and data rewriting baselines. The key insight is that safe data alone does not prevent LLMs from deriving dangerous behaviors through capability composition — the model must also learn to flag the safety valence of its own context.

![Figure 1 from Safety Reflection Pretraining: corpus segmentation with injected safety reflection tokens](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SRP corpus construction — pretraining text is segmented; a classifier injects verdict + category tokens after each segment; the LM is trained jointly on text prediction and reflection prediction.*

## 03 · [**When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455)

- **Authors / venue:** Yitong Guo et al. — **EMNLP 2026 Findings (peer-reviewed)**, submitted September 1, 2026
- **Why it ranks here:** Freshest peer-reviewed paper in this window; provides a mechanistic (Fisher-geometric) account of why safety alignment degrades under benign fine-tuning — a major practical concern for open-weight model deployments. The routing-sharpness framing is a new and testable mechanistic hypothesis.
- **Technical summary:** The authors propose that RLHF safety alignment works by routing harmful inputs to safety-critical experts/pathways while preserving output-side routing. The safety Fisher information matrix is empirically low-rank; alignment flattens the safety geometry while maintaining the routing pathway. Benign fine-tuning (100 examples) selectively re-sharpens output-side MLP modules, destroying the pathway without touching most internal safety representations. This explains an empirical asymmetry: attack success rates can collapse completely while general utility degrades only mildly. LoRA and ASAM mitigate early collapse by suppressing output-side sharpness but fail at larger fine-tuning scales. The routing view also explains why a small set of safety examples efficiently restores refusal: internal representations are preserved; only the output sharpening needs correction.

![Figure 1 from When Safety Routing Breaks: Fisher geometry of alignment and sharpening under benign fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01455/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Fisher-geometric explanation — alignment flattens the safety landscape (left); 100 benign fine-tuning examples re-sharpen output-side MLP geometry, collapsing the routing pathway (right).*

## 04 · [**Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `AI security` `ICML 2026 (peer-reviewed)`

Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU). Gradient-free attacks — abliteration (refusal-direction projection) and prefilling — bypass recent fine-tuning defenses for open-weight LLMs, raising attack success rates from below 10% to 16%–96% across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench. The authors challenge the assumption that harmful capability must be *learned* through fine-tuning, showing that pretrained models already encode it and that simple inference-time elicitation is sufficient. They introduce abliteration-resistant tuning (ART), which incorporates an abliteration-based objective into existing defenses and reduces combined attack success by 10%–20%.

*Figure 1: ASR under abliteration and prefilling vs. no defense and existing defenses across three harmfulness benchmarks — defenses fail to gradient-free attacks; ART provides partial mitigation.*

## 05 · [**WARD: Adversarially Robust Defense of Web Agents Against Prompt Injections**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15030)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, May 2026

Constructs WARD-Base, a 177K-sample dataset from 719 high-traffic URLs, and WARD-PIG, a dedicated adversarial dataset targeting the guard model itself (Prompt Injection on Guard). Existing guard models suffer major recall collapse under PIG attacks across both text and visual modalities; WARD maintains near-perfect recall in all tested settings. The key insight is that guard models must themselves be adversarially trained against attackers who know the guard architecture — passive guard models are a single additional attack surface. Demonstrates practical robustness for web-agent deployments.

![Figure 1: WARD guard model architecture — adversarial robustness pipeline with WARD-Base and WARD-PIG training](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15030/x1.png)

*Figure 1: WARD pipeline — guard model is adversarially trained on WARD-PIG (guard-targeted injections); near-perfect recall maintained vs. PIG attacks that collapse existing guard models.*

## 06 · [**Validity-Aware Jailbreak Evaluation for Large Language Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00498)

`AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, August 31, 2026

Identifies a systematic false-positive problem in jailbreak evaluation: prevailing metrics measure refusal absence and semantic similarity to the harmful goal, not whether the response actually provides actionable harmful content. SEAV (Sequential Epistemic and Action-Level Validation) decomposes responses into ordered steps and checks both epistemic validity (is the claimed knowledge correct?) and action-level correctness (would the steps actually achieve the stated goal?). SEAV cuts the false-positive rate on the SD-A benchmark by 14.9 percentage points versus the strongest baseline, and reclassifies 22.1%–51.0% of prior-labeled "successful jailbreaks" as invalid across three of four public benchmarks. Substantially reshapes reported robustness of frontier models.

![Figure 1: SEAV decomposition — response steps evaluated for epistemic validity and action-level correctness](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00498/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SEAV framework — response decomposed into ordered steps; each step evaluated for epistemic validity and action correctness; 22–51% of prior-labeled successes reclassified as invalid.*

## 07 · [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

`pretraining/midtraining safety` `mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2026

Abliteration removes refusal by orthogonally projecting weight matrices to the extracted refusal direction. AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) counters this by applying rank-*k* updates to residual-stream writer matrices that replace refusal-inducing activations with learned aliases, then correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve behavior. The refusal signal is hidden from the direction-extraction step, rendering abliteration blind to the true refusal representation. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with less than 0.5 pp of MMLU degradation. Key finding: existing defenses fail because they ignore *how* the refusal direction is extracted, not merely that it can be extracted.

![Figure 1: AMRA weight-editing scheme — rank-k update to writer matrix introduces refusal aliases; reader matrix correction preserves behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA pipeline — rank-k writer-matrix update installs refusal aliases; reader correction preserves model behavior; abliteration extracts a decoy direction and fails to suppress refusal.*

## 08 · [**AgentLens: Interpretable Safety Steering via Mechanistic Subspaces for Multi-Turn Coding Agent**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22673)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `preprint` — arXiv, June 21, 2026

White-box defense for multi-turn coding agents: detects harmful execution states from step-level hidden representations and mitigates unsafe behavior by intervening in a 10-dimensional mechanistic subspace within a single transformer layer — far smaller than the full residual stream. Introduces the Mechanistic Agent Safety (MAS) benchmark with 194 comprehensively annotated multi-turn trajectories across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B. Subspace steering outperforms raw-activation baselines; the narrow intervention preserves coding performance while substantially reducing harmful code generation.

![Figure 1: AgentLens pipeline — step-level hidden states → mechanistic subspace identification → targeted activation steering](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22673/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AgentLens — hidden-state probing identifies a 10-dimensional safety subspace; steering within it outperforms full-activation steering and external guardrails on MAS benchmark across three model families.*

## 09 · [**Discovering Millions of Interpretable Features with Sparse Autoencoders**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26620)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, June 25, 2026

Introduces Qwen3-Instruct SAE, a comprehensive SAE suite trained on Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B at three activation sites (residual stream, MLP output, attention output) for smaller models and a subset of residual-stream layers for Qwen3-8B. Systematic evaluation reveals distinct sparsity–fidelity trade-offs across layers and components. A key applied result: SAE features can causally steer instruction-tuned Qwen3 toward refusal behavior, demonstrating that the suite can serve as an interpretability-for-safety toolkit. Provides a practical open resource for feature-level mechanistic analysis of instruction-tuned models.

## 10 · [**Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)

`mech-interp` `preprint` — Seyed Arshan Dalili, Mehrdad Mahdavi (Penn State) — arXiv, June 4, 2026

Proves theoretically that the standard SAE assumption — each latent feature has a one-dimensional decoder direction — is wrong and provably induces feature splitting. Reconstructing a feature of intrinsic dimension *d_i* ≥ 2 to error ε with single-direction decoders requires exponentially many atoms in *d_i*. Moreover, ℓ₁-regularized SAE training actively prefers the exponentially-split solution over the true multi-dimensional basis via a continuous descent path. Proposes Subspace-Aware SAEs (SA-SAE) where each feature is a learned low-dimensional subspace, not a direction. Demonstrates that SA-SAE matches or exceeds standard SAEs on reconstruction while using fewer atoms — directly addressing feature splitting as a structural bug, not a training artifact.

![Figure 1: Feature splitting analysis — single-direction decoder atoms vs true d_i-dimensional feature subspace](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06333/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Theoretical illustration — reconstructing a 2D feature with 1D decoder atoms requires exponentially many atoms; ℓ₁ training prefers the split solution; SA-SAE multi-dimensional subspace avoids the explosion.*
