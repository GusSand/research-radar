## Top 10 (priority order)

### 1. [**Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Anonymous — arXiv preprint (June 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** First method to embed safety monitoring *inside* pretraining via periodically inserted safety reflections, rather than filtering data or waiting for post-training RLHF; directly targets the observation that LLMs can compose benign knowledge into unsafe behaviors even after data filtering.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) inserts short, curated safety reflection sequences at regular intervals throughout the pretraining corpus, causing the model to learn self-monitoring as part of its base language modeling objective. At 1.7B scale on FineWeb-Edu, SRP improves safety classification accuracy on held-out harmful prompts and substantially reduces success rates of both inference-stage attacks and fine-tuning attacks compared to identically-scaled baselines with only standard post-training alignment. The method establishes a foundational safety capability that the authors show is subsequently reinforced by compatible post-training, enabling a defense-in-depth stack where pretraining and RLHF are mutually reinforcing.

![Figure 1: Safety Reflection Pretraining pipeline — safety reflections inserted at regular intervals throughout FineWeb-Edu corpus](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SRP inserts short safety reflection sequences at regular corpus intervals (shown as orange blocks); baseline pretraining sees only the standard corpus (gray). The reflection sequences teach the model to self-monitor unsafe compositional reasoning from the start of training.*

### 2. [**Refusal Geometry Reflects Refusal Training: Diverse Refusal Prefixes Can Raise Stable Rank and Weaken Refusal Vector Ablation Attacks**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)

- **Authors / venue:** Andrey Labunets — arXiv preprint (August 25, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Directly addresses the root cause of why abliteration works — the low stable rank of refusal representations — and shows that a training-time intervention (prefix diversity) hardens models against ablation attacks before deployment, not just at inference time.
- **Technical summary:** Abliteration attacks extract a single refusal direction by contrasting harmful vs. harmless activations, then project model weights onto the orthogonal complement. This paper shows that the stable rank of the refusal subspace is the key geometric quantity: low stable rank means refusal is concentrated in one direction (easy to ablate); high stable rank distributes refusal across many directions (much harder to ablate). Diverse refusal prefixes in training data — i.e., varied ways the model begins its refusals — spread refusal mass across multiple directions, raising stable rank. Empirical results confirm that models trained with high prefix diversity show significantly weaker susceptibility to refusal vector ablation attacks, providing a training-time handle on abliteration robustness.

![Figure 1: Stable rank of refusal subspace vs. prefix diversity — higher diversity yields higher stable rank and lower ablation success rate](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25390/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Relationship between refusal prefix diversity (training) and stable rank of refusal representation (geometry), showing that higher diversity → higher stable rank → reduced abliteration attack success rate.*

### 3. [**Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases (AMRA)**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093)

- **Authors / venue:** Nathan Truong — arXiv preprint (August 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Complements #2 with a post-hoc weight-editing approach: instead of improving training to create a high-rank refusal subspace, AMRA obscures the refusal direction at deployment time by replacing it with random aliases — directly targeting the contrastive prompt extraction step that abliteration depends on.
- **Technical summary:** Abliteration requires extracting the refusal direction from contrastive harmful/harmless activations. AMRA applies rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices, replacing the activation patterns that normally encode the refusal direction with random alias vectors, while simultaneously correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve the model's full behavioral capabilities. The aliases make contrastive extraction yield a spurious direction that, when ablated, leaves actual refusal intact. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA improves post-abliteration refusal scores by 2.16 points over the undefended baseline with less than 0.5 percentage points of MMLU degradation, demonstrating that obscuring the refusal signal is achievable with minimal capability cost.

![Figure 1: AMRA weight-editing pipeline — random aliases replace refusal-encoding activations in writer matrices; reader matrices corrected to preserve behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18093/x1.png)

*Figure 1: AMRA replaces the refusal direction in writer matrices with random aliases; the contrastive extraction step of abliteration picks up the alias instead of the true refusal direction, leaving refusal intact post-attack.*

### 04 · [Tripwire: Triggering Aligned Refusal via Statistically Certified Safety Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14392)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — Wei Zhao, Zhe Li, Peixin Zhang, Jun Sun — arXiv, August 14, 2026

Per-neuron hypothesis tests under FDR control identify safety-specific neurons; a trigger-style clamp holds them at their harmful-conditional mean activations at inference time (or equivalently as an offline bias-patch weight edit). Reduces average attack success rate to ≤2.0% across four safety-aligned LLMs and four attacks, with only 0.5–5.3% MT-Bench utility drop — smallest utility cost among all tested defenses.

![Figure 1: Tripwire — per-neuron hypothesis test selects safety neurons; clamp holds them at harmful-conditional activation mean to trigger refusal](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14392/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Tripwire identifies safety-specific neurons via FDR-controlled hypothesis tests, then clamps them at harmful-conditional means — effectively injecting an internal "this is harmful" signal that triggers alignment-time refusal behavior.*

### 05 · [SafeConstellations: Mitigating Over-Refusals in LLMs Through Task-Aware Representation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11290)

`mech-interp` `AI security` `ACL 2026 (peer-reviewed)` — Utsav Maskey, Sumit Yadav, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem — ACL 2026 Long Paper #2056

Mechanistic analysis reveals that LLM representations follow task-specific "constellation" trajectory patterns across layers, with distinct paths for refusal vs. non-refusal cases. SafeConstellations uses dynamic layer selection and task-conditioned modulation to steer representations along non-refusal trajectories, reducing over-refusals by up to 73% while preserving the harmful-refusal mechanism (confirmed less disrupted than global ablation). Peer-reviewed at ACL 2026.

![Figure 1: SafeConstellations constellation patterns — task-specific embedding trajectories across layers for refusal vs. non-refusal cases](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11290/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Task-specific embedding trajectories form distinct "constellations" across layers; SafeConstellations steers toward non-refusal pathways using task-conditioned dynamic layer selection, achieving 73% over-refusal reduction.*

### 06 · [Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383)

`mech-interp` `preprint` `COLM 2026 (submitted)` — Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci — arXiv, August 9, 2026

Steering vectors that modify model behavior can unintentionally compromise safety mechanisms, but this paper shows the safety-degrading component is separable from the steering-effective component. A constrained optimization (primal-dual updates) removes the safety-degrading component while preserving steering effectiveness and bounding false refusal increase. Demonstrates that the safety cost of activation steering is not fundamental — it arises from a specific removable subcomponent.

![Figure 1: Decomposition of steering vector into steering-effective and safety-degrading components; constrained optimization removes the degrading component](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08383/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Steering vector decomposition — the safety-degrading component (disrupts safety mechanisms, contributes little to steering objective) is separable and removable via constrained optimization without sacrificing steering effectiveness.*

### 07 · [A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Framework Defense against LLM Jailbreak Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26008)

`AI security` `preprint` — Tongyan Hu, Bryan Hooi — arXiv, August 26, 2026

Persistent cross-interaction rule memory abstracts successful jailbreak attacks into method-level rules capturing structural wrappers (role-playing, obfuscation, code transformation, multi-step indirection) rather than specific harmful topics, enabling generalization to unseen jailbreak variants without retraining. Self-evolving defense significantly improves over static baselines as deployment duration increases and the rule memory accumulates attack pattern knowledge.

![Figure 1: Self-evolving multi-agent defense — cross-interaction rule memory accumulates attack structural abstractions and applies them to detect unseen variants](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26008/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Cross-interaction rule memory abstracts jailbreak structural wrappers (not harmful topics) across deployment encounters; accumulated rules generalize to new attack variants, improving defense over static methods as deployment time grows.*

### 08 · [Moving the Safety Barrier: Dynamic Routing Adaptive Alignment Against White-Box Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02674)

`AI security` `mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, August 2, 2026

DRAA addresses white-box attacks that directly disable internal safety neurons or routing paths: (1) localizes safety routes via contrastive calibration activations; (2) masks the safety route to induce causal failure cases; (3) mines failure-aware preference pairs; (4) activates compensatory latent routes via preference learning (DPO). Dynamic compensatory routing preserves robust refusal when the primary safety route is compromised, outperforming static safety alignment baselines under white-box attack.

![Figure 1: DRAA dynamic routing — primary safety route identified and masked; compensatory routes activated via DPO to preserve refusal under attack](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02674/x1.png)

*Figure 1: DRAA pipeline — contrastive activation localization → route masking → failure-aware preference mining → compensatory route DPO; dynamic alternatives preserve safety when primary safety route is attacked.*

### 09 · [SafeBranch: Branch-Pair Safety Alignment for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19729)

`AI security` `preprint` — Hyunse Lee, Jiwoo Jeong, Haneul Lee, Kyochul Jang, Youngjae Yu, Woojin Lee — arXiv, August 20, 2026

Branch-pair DPO alignment for VLM-based embodied agents: unsafe rollouts are rolled back to the safety-critical step, the actor is queried for a safe alternative, and the two branches (unsafe vs. safe) are paired as preference data that differ only at the critical step. Training on these branch pairs improves agent safety on interactive tasks while preserving task completion rates, providing a principled rollback-based preference construction without human annotation.

![Figure 1: SafeBranch rollback and branch-pair construction — unsafe rollout rolled back to safety-critical step; safe alternative queried and paired for DPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19729/x1.png)

*Figure 1: SafeBranch rolls back to the safety-critical action, obtains a safe alternative from the actor, and constructs a branch-pair DPO preference pair; the contrast isolates exactly the safety-critical decision from all other trajectory factors.*

### 10 · [Who Pays More for Safety? Measuring the Disparate Cost of Safety Alignment across Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22490)

`AI security` `preprint` — Chanwoong Yoon, Jungsoo Park, Alan Ritter — arXiv, August 23, 2026

Introduces the "Safety Cost" (SC) metric measuring utility loss imposed by safety alignment across language groups. Applied across multiple aligned models, SC reveals a systematic inequity: non-English users consistently bear a higher Safety Cost than English users. The finding implies that current safety alignment practices implicitly trade off non-English utility for English-centric safety calibration, providing both a measurement tool and a diagnostic for multilingual alignment failures.

![Figure 1: Safety Cost distribution across language groups — non-English users bear systematically higher utility loss from safety alignment than English users](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22490/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety Cost (SC) by language group across aligned models; non-English groups consistently show higher SC, revealing that safety alignment imposes an unequal utility tax across languages.*
