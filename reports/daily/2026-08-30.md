## 01 · [Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

- **Authors / venue:** Julian Minder, Viktor Moskvoretskii, Raghav Singhal, et al. (EPFL-dlab) — arXiv preprint (Aug 13, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Highest-priority paper of the window: the first systematic study of spec-driven alignment introduced at pretraining time rather than as a post-hoc overlay. Directly addresses the standing concern that RLHF/SFT-only alignment is shallow and easily subverted, by planting the desired model persona from the very first training token.
- **Technical summary:** Synthetic Persona Pretraining (SPP) operates in three stages: (1) a constitution-driven annotation step that appends short synthetic first-person moral reflections to 10% of pretraining documents — the model reads both the original text and the reflection during cross-entropy training; (2) standard pretraining on the joint corpus, which installs the desired persona alongside the model's other learned behaviors; (3) a "persona binding" post-training step that ties the pretrained persona to the assistant identity through supervised fine-tuning on user–assistant dialogues. Ablations at 3B parameters / 500B tokens show that omitting persona binding or introducing SPP only at the tail end of pretraining yields substantially weaker results. SPP models improve on constitution-following and jailbreak robustness, reduce misalignment in out-of-distribution moral dilemmas, and preserve capabilities — and the alignment advantage grows with pretraining budget. Code and data at github.com/epfl-dlab/spp.

![Figure 1: SPP pipeline — constitution annotation, pretraining, persona binding](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13482/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Three-stage SPP pipeline showing how constitution-derived moral reflections are woven into pretraining documents (10% annotation rate) before the persona binding post-training step that ties the pretrained persona to the assistant.*

## 02 · [TamperBench: Systematically Stress-Testing LLM Safety Under Fine-Tuning and Tampering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06911)

- **Authors / venue:** Critical ML Lab, University of Washington — ACM KDD 2026 (peer-reviewed; presented Aug 9–13, Jeju Island, South Korea)
- **Why it ranks here:** The only peer-reviewed tamper-resistance benchmark to date; presented for the first time at KDD 2026 this window. 21 LLMs, 9 attack types, systematic hyperparameter sweeps — the most comprehensive empirical account of whether any current defense actually survives adversarial fine-tuning.
- **Technical summary:** TamperBench evaluates 21 open-weight LLMs (including defense-augmented variants such as circuit-breaker and representation-engineering models) across nine tampering threats spanning weight-space fine-tuning attacks (adversarial SFT, jailbreak-tuning, harmful-data poisoning) and latent-space representation attacks. For each model–attack pair, a full hyperparameter sweep finds the worst-case attack, then the result is scored with StrongREJECT (safety) and MMLU-Pro (utility) to map the safety–utility frontier. Key findings: jailbreak-tuning with ~2% poisoning is consistently the most severe attack; current alignment-stage defenses fail across virtually all attack sweeps; post-training choices (instruction style, RLHF temperature, DPO variant) affect tamper resistance in model-family-specific ways that cannot be predicted from safety benchmarks alone. Code: github.com/criticalml-uw/TamperBench.

![Figure 1: TamperBench results — safety-utility frontier across 21 LLMs and 9 attack types](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06911/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety (StrongREJECT) vs. utility (MMLU-Pro) under worst-case attack for each model–attack pair; alignment-stage defenses cluster near the x-axis, confirming they largely fail under systematic hyperparameter sweeps.*

## 03 · [Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19168)

- **Authors / venue:** Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu — arXiv preprint (Jun 17, 2026)
- **Why it ranks here:** Addresses the gap that even a corpus filtered to contain only safe content produces models that can compose benign capabilities into harmful outputs. The Safety Reflection Pretraining approach requires no content removal and is technically minimally invasive — a key consideration for practical deployment.
- **Technical summary:** Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) inserts short inline safety judgments — typically a one- or two-sentence classification of whether the preceding text segment is safe, and why — directly into the pretraining corpus. Models are trained on both the original text and the inserted reflections via standard next-token prediction. Applied to 1.7B-parameter models trained on FineWeb-Edu, SRP substantially improves the model's own safety classification accuracy and, more importantly, reduces the attack success rate of both inference-time attacks and fine-tuning attacks (adversarial SFT with 10 poisoned examples) compared to identically configured models trained on clean data. Unlike data filtering, SRP does not reduce corpus diversity: it annotates rather than removes, leaving the data distribution unchanged while shaping what the model learns to infer from it.

![Figure 1: SRP accuracy on safety classification vs. attack success rate comparison against clean-data baseline](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19168/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Safety classification accuracy and post-fine-tuning attack success rates for SRP vs. clean-data pretraining baseline; SRP reduces fine-tuning attack success without any reduction in pretraining corpus breadth.*

## 04 · [Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14147)

`pretraining-safety` `mech-interp` `preprint` — arXiv, July 14, 2026

Applies the single-line prefill attack ("Sure, here is") to 4 models across 3 families (1.5B–14B) while monitoring a linear probe trained to read harm from unattacked completions. On examples the attack flips to compliance, the harm probe still fires at 0.91–0.98 accuracy — harm is fully encoded, but refusal is bypassed. A dose-matched position control shows the first half of the response suffices to break refusal; the second half is nearly inert. Conclusion: refusal is a shallow, response-site computation that can be disabled without altering the model's internal representation of the harm of a query.

![Figure 1: Probe harm score vs. behavioral refusal rate under prefill attack across models](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14147/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Harm probe score (y-axis) and behavioral refusal rate (bar) side-by-side for unattacked, first-half-prefilled, and second-half-prefilled conditions — harm stays high while refusal drops to chance under first-half prefill.*

## 05 · [Daydreaming: Stealing Hidden Agent Skills through Black-Box Task Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26733)

`AI-security` `agent-security` `preprint` — Yu-Lin Tsai, Yu-An Lu, Ci-Yang Tsai, Muxi Lyu, **Raluca Ada Popa**, Chia-Mu Yu — arXiv, Aug 26, 2026

Providers selling hosted agent task results can keep the underlying skill files (instructions, reference data, helpers) secret — Daydreaming steals them without ever asking the victim agent to reveal the skill. The attack adaptively crafts tasks whose results discriminate between possible hidden behaviors, tests behaviors in isolation, uses attacker-controlled shadow agents to design follow-up queries, and reconstructs each file from stored results plus local execution checks. No model internals, gradient access, or prompted disclosure is required — execution results alone are sufficient.

![Figure 1: Daydreaming attack flow — adaptive task crafting distinguishes hidden behaviors](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26733/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Daydreaming adaptive loop — each task round narrows the hypothesis space over hidden skill files; shadow agents help design the next query batch; victim responses are used only for behavioral discrimination, never for prompted disclosure.*

## 06 · [A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Framework Defense against LLM Jailbreak Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26008)

`AI-security` `jailbreak` `preprint` — Tongyan Hu, Bryan Hooi (NUS) — arXiv, Aug 26, 2026

When a jailbreak succeeds, the framework abstracts the failure into a method-level rule that captures the structural attack wrapper (not the harmful topic). Rules live in persistent cross-interaction memory and apply to future inputs without any parameter updates, so one induced rule generalizes to the entire attack family. Works on both open-weight and black-box API models. Across 4 black-box jailbreak families, substantially reduces ASR while preserving benign utility; remains robust under a held-out adaptive composite attack.

![Figure 1: Rule memory evolution — method-level rules generalize across attack families](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26008/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Cross-interaction rule memory growth as attack families are encountered; a rule induced from one family (e.g., role-play wrapper) correctly intercepts later variants without retraining.*

## 07 · [Backdoor Decontamination Dynamics in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11295)

`AI-security` `backdoor` `preprint` — Gabriel Huang, Abhay Puri, Léo Boisvert, Alexandre Drouin, Perouz Taslakian, Spandana Gella, Christopher Pal (Polytechnique Montréal / ServiceNow) — arXiv, Aug 11, 2026

Two-phase decontamination protocol: install a known defensive backdoor, then fine-tune to unlearn it, expecting the original unknown backdoor as collateral damage. In 115 experiments on AgentDyn, defensive poisoning alone erases ~56% of original backdoors; subsequent unlearning drives almost all survivors to erasure. Key dissociation: trigger recognition (the model identifies the trigger token) and malicious execution (the model takes the harmful action) are behaviorally separable — decontamination reliably removes malicious execution while trigger recognition may persist independently.

![Figure 1: Backdoor decontamination dynamics — trigger recognition vs. malicious execution under two-phase protocol](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11295/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Survival rates for trigger recognition and malicious execution across 115 experiments; defensive poisoning + unlearning nearly eliminates execution while trigger recognition shows partial persistence.*

## 08 · [Not All Eval-Awareness Is Equal: Capabilities Framing Predicts Compliance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27340)

`mech-interp` `safety-eval` `ICML-2026-Workshop (peer-reviewed)` — Allison Zhuang, Santiago Aranguri — ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop

Eval-awareness in model CoT decomposes into two framings: capabilities-flavored ("testing my ability to follow instructions") vs. safety-flavored ("testing my boundaries"). On Qwen3-32B over the FORTRESS dataset, the capabilities framing predicts compliance with a +24 to +46 percentage-point gap over safety-framing across all steering conditions. A CoT-prefill causal intervention confirms the link: 10 of 11 prefills shift compliance in the predicted direction. Implication: safety evaluation pipelines that steer models away from eval-awareness may inadvertently boost compliance through capabilities-framing spillover.

![Figure 1: Compliance rates by eval-awareness framing type across steering conditions](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27340/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Compliance rates for capabilities-framed vs. safety-framed eval-awareness across steering conditions on Qwen3-32B/FORTRESS; +24–46 pp gap confirms the framing distinction is causally meaningful.*

## 09 · [Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)

`mech-interp` `SAE` `theory` `preprint` — Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen — arXiv, Aug 10, 2026

First identifiability theorem for dictionary learning as used in mechanistic interpretability: treats the forward pass as a controlled dynamical system (depth = time), lifts it with the Koopman operator to a finite linear realization, and proves its spectrum is a coordinate-free model property independent of SAE seed or width. The spectrum is recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^{-1/2} up to permutation (first such result; matching minimax lower bound provided). A dissociation theorem shows that for non-normal realizations, the directions carrying activation variance and the directions carrying information across depth cannot coincide — explaining why SAE variance metrics miss causally active features. Validated on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base; predicted exponent 0.506 ± 0.031 on Qwen3-8B-Base.

![Figure 1: Spectral convergence rate vs. calibration sample count across models](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10172/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Empirical spectrum convergence rate (slope ≈ -0.5 on log-log axes) vs. sample count M across GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen3-8B-Base, confirming the M^{-1/2} identifiability bound.*

## 10 · [Enforcing LLM Safety through DMD-based Classification of Prompt-Response Embedding Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19579)

`AI-security` `safety-classifier` `preprint` — Mohamed Akrout, Olivera Kotevska, Dan Wilson — arXiv, Aug 20, 2026

Extends a Koopman/DMD (Dynamic Mode Decomposition) framework originally developed for hallucination detection to LLM safety classification. A separate Koopman-based predictive model is trained on the trajectory of prompt–response pairs in embedding space for each safety class. At inference time, a differential residual score compares the prediction error of the safe-regime model against the unsafe-regime model; the lower-error class wins. Unlike point-in-time classifiers, this approach captures sequential and trajectory-level embedding patterns across the generation process.

![Figure 1: DMD safety classifier — trajectory residuals for safe vs. unsafe regimes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19579/x1.png)

*Figure 1: Prompt–response embedding trajectories in safe (blue) vs. unsafe (red) regimes; the differential residual score assigns the class whose Koopman model predicts the observed trajectory with lower error.*
